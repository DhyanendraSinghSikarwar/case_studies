## Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured)
Evidence is thin: DS is named as appearing in AMCAT, TCS NQT and some company rounds only by prep sites, and the more commonly named topic for TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture is data interpretation. So no question is claimed as "asked at" a company. Stars are therefore judgement: 3 stars for the answer-option key, the elimination method, the "evaluate, do not compute" rule and the classic one- or two-step problems (age, profit, time-work, percent, average, ratio) which any DS item would use; 2 stars for yes/no handling, linear-equation dependency, integer constraints, number properties and trap awareness; 1 star for differentiators (geometry, divisibility tricks, business-judgement framing, why DS is used). Roughly 75% of entries are fully specified problems with a single checkable option (A to E). Unverified: that Indian analyst online tests use the GMAT five-option wording; some use "Yes/No/Can't say" or only two-option variants, so the part states the five-option key in its scope line.
