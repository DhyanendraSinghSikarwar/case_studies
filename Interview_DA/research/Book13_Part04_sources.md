Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency; sources are prep sites)
- 3 stars: method questions and 5 to 7 person linear, circular and floor puzzles. Prep pages for TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture and Mu Sigma list seating or arrangement puzzles as a standard section, so core formats get the top star.
- 2 stars: 8-person circular, mixed-facing, two-row, alternating-group, month and weekday puzzles, roster and flat puzzles, counting seatings. Common in CAT-style and harder online tests; less certain in any single analyst gate.
- 1 star: 8-item hardest variants, ménage and block counting, 13th-Friday dates, HR-style roster modelling. Differentiators, not reported in analyst gates.

Limitations
- Sample: 6 searches, all prep and coaching sites; no official test-maker material. Company claims (counts per test, which company asks what) are single third-party statements and were not used inside any question.
- Gap-fill status: all 74 questions are original constructions on topics the sources name; none was seen in a source, so all are gap-fills at the question level. Only topic coverage and star levels trace to sources.
- Verification: the 43 generated puzzles (linear, circular, mixed-facing, alternating, floor, stack, weekday, month, two-row) were produced by a solver that adds clues until exactly one full arrangement remains, then removes redundant clues, and requires that no single clue alone fixes the asked answer. The 4 handcrafted arrangement puzzles (roster, flats, rectangular table, row swap) were brute-forced the same way. The 7 counting items were brute-forced by enumeration; the 4 date items were checked with Python's calendar (30 Jan, 27 Feb, 31 Mar 2026; 2nd and 4th Tuesday 10 and 24; 1, 13, 25 March; 13th Fridays in Feb, Mar, Nov 2026). The circular-table convention (facing the centre, right-hand side is anticlockwise; facing outside, right-hand side is clockwise) is stated inside each question.
