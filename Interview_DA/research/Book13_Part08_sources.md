Star-assignment reasoning (judgement; no source gives frequency for data-analyst roles)
- 3 stars: assumption, strengthen/weaken, inference, main conclusion, and the basic cause-versus-correlation and percentage-versus-number traps. These appear in every service-MNC critical-reasoning topic list found (Accenture, Wipro, Infosys, TCS) and are the core of CR practice in general.
- 2 stars: flaw, resolve-the-paradox, parallel reasoning, confounder and Simpson-style data arguments, claim-evaluation items. Common in CR practice and analytics interviews but not seen as a named gate item.
- 1 star: regression to the mean, A/B-test evaluation, out-of-scope traps, "evaluate the claim" items. Differentiators at analytics firms and product companies.

Limitations
- Sample: 6 searches, all returning commercial prep sites or general guides; no official test-maker material. The Mu Sigma/Fractal query returned nothing specific to either company (stated plainly). Question counts and timings differ between sources and are used only as hedged prep-site claims.
- All 62 questions are original constructions on topics the sources name, so all are gap-fills at the question level. Only topic coverage and star levels trace to sources.
- The numeric items (growth and decline, mix and Simpson effects, weighted averages, accident rates, promotion and hiring tables, the Fisher exact p-value of about 0.83 for 10/500 versus 12/500, the axis-ratio item and the headcount items) were checked with Python. Multiple-choice critical-reasoning items have one intended best answer by design; that is judgement, not machine-checkable, so the answer agent should state the reason for the choice.
