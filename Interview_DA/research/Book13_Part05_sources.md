Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency; all sources are prep sites)
- 3 stars: basic who-owns-what matching grids, simple selection/panel puzzles, short knight/knave puzzles, league match counting, and the grid/truth-teller method questions. Naukri, PrepInsta and Talent Battle pages list puzzle and matching-grid items for TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture and Cognizant, and ASD/Aon show the grid format in analyst-style assessments.
- 2 stars: larger or ordered grids, scheduling and precedence, digit and code puzzles, knockout and points-table items, and most method questions. These recur in puzzle collections but the evidence for any specific gate is thin.
- 1 star: 4-attribute and cryptarithm-style puzzles, hat-logic, Mastermind-style codes, bin packing, rooms/intervals, top-two guarantee, and meta questions (brute-force check, contradictions). They fit analytics-firm interviews (Mu Sigma, Fractal, Novartis per interview_processes.md) rather than service-MNC online tests.

Limitations
- Six searches, all returning prep, tutorial or media pages; no official Indian-company test material. Test patterns (question counts, timings) conflict between sources and are not used in any question.
- No question appeared verbatim in a source; all 53 puzzles are original constructions, so all are gap-fills at the question level. Only topic coverage and star levels trace to sources. The Aon result suggests that "deductive grid" there means shape grids, so word-puzzle grids are supported by the ASD and prep-site pages only.
- Uniqueness checks (Python brute force, scratch code): 45 of the 53 puzzles were enumerated (all grid puzzles over every candidate assignment with greedy-then-pruned clue sets, all knight/knave puzzles over every type assignment, selection/panel counts, Mastermind codes, DATA+DATA=SMART, digit puzzles, 3x3 and 4x4 grids, task orderings, interval overlap, critical path, assignment cost, bin packing, round-robin results and the top-two guarantee). The other 8 (knockout and round-robin counts, 10-player remaining matches, pigeonhole, chess score total, courier trip check against its weight bound, and the two-guards question) have closed-form answers checked by hand.
- Conventions used in the wording: knights always tell the truth and knaves always lie; in the "top-two guarantee" item ties are assumed to be broken against the team; a room is free the moment an interview ends.
