Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency)
- 3 stars: basic percentage and percentage change, profit/loss on cost price, marked price and discount, successive discounts, net effect of +x% then -x%, markup + discount profit, same-SP gain/loss pair, percentage points vs percent. Every prep source lists percentages and profit-loss as the highest-frequency quant topics at TCS/Infosys/Wipro/Accenture-type gates.
- 2 stars: false-weight dealer, GST, reverse percentage, buy-get-free, weighted pass rates, revenue = price x quantity, funnel/attrition chains, overtime/commission payroll problems. Common in question banks, less certain in a single data-analyst loop.
- 1 star: multi-step hard items (markup + discount + false weight, cost-up/price-up, doubled SP, equal successive discount from final price), aimed at analytics-firm aptitude gates (Fractal, Mu Sigma) and as differentiators.

Limitations
- Sample size: 8 searches, 7 productive; all evidence is third-party prep material that agrees on topics but not on test formats or counts. No source gives an interview frequency and none is a data-analyst-specific candidate report for this topic; company attributions are unverified.
- Question wording follows standard aptitude patterns; the numbers are original or standard and every problem was checked for a single answer (no source answer key was copied, since one source had an arithmetic mismatch).
- Roughly 70 of 82 questions are gap-fills from standard practice rather than directly seen in a source; about 12 are patterns directly seen in search snippets (successive discount formula, 30% vs 20%+10%, 40%/20% markup-discount, +/-25% pair or same-SP pair, 900 g false weight, markup 50%/discount 20%/900 g, compound population, percentage-change base, GST-free price). HR/business-flavoured items (attrition chain, funnel, absentees, overtime, CTC split, commission) are my additions, written to fit the book's HR-to-analyst reader.
