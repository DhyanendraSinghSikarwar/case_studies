Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency; sources are prep sites)
- 3 stars: blood relations, syllogism, seating, coding-decoding, direction, ranking and alphabet items. These topics recur in every company page found (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Mu Sigma) and form the usual reasoning section.
- 2 stars: clocks and calendars, Venn counting, inequalities, statement-assumption and weaken questions (Accenture critical reasoning), analogies, scheduling grids, cube counting, and lighter puzzles. Common in lists but less certain in any one gate.
- 1 star: classic brain-teaser puzzles (eggs, bridge, horses, 12 balls) and the "why test reasoning" question. They appear mainly in analytics-firm interviews (Mu Sigma, Fractal, Flipkart, per interview_processes.md), not in service-MNC online tests.

Limitations
- Sample: 6 successful searches, all returning commercial prep sites; no official test-maker material. Test patterns (question counts, timings) conflict between sources and are not used for any claim in the questions.
- All 80 questions are original constructions on topics the sources name; none was seen verbatim in a source, so all are gap-fills at the question level. Only the topic coverage and star levels trace to sources.
- The multi-step seating, floor, grid and scheduling puzzles were checked by brute-force enumeration to have a unique answer to the question asked. Circular seats use the stated convention that a person facing the centre has their right-hand side in the anticlockwise direction. The 2026 calendar items (1 Jan 2026 Thursday, 15 Aug 2026 Saturday, next same calendar year 2037) were checked with Python.
