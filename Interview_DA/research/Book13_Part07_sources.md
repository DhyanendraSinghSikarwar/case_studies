Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency; sources are prep sites)
- 3 stars: basic letter series, alphanumeric series, analogy and odd-one-out. TCS NQT guides list series, analogy and classification, and these are the most standard reasoning items; they are quick scoring items in a timed gate.
- 2 stars: wrap-around and two-track series, alphabet-test counting, number-letter analogies, odd-one-out with clusters, grid rules, plus method questions on ambiguity and time. Common in practice sets but less certain at any one employer.
- 1 star: harder compound series, shifting-rule analogies, and the link to analyst trend/anomaly work. Differentiators only; evidence of use at analytics firms is thin.

Limitations
- Sample: 4 searches, all returning commercial prep sites and some exam-prep pages; no official test-maker material, no first-hand 2025-26 DA candidate report. Question counts quoted by prep sites conflict and are not used.
- All 50 questions are original constructions on topics the sources name; none was seen verbatim in a source, so all are gap-fills at the question level.
- 42 of 50 are fully specified problems with single answers (the rest are method and "why" questions). The computed ones (series values with wrap-around, alphabetical-order letter, pair counting in PIVOTS, consonant-before-even-digit count, 50th letter of the grouped pattern, 100th term of the repeating pattern) were checked with Python; the remaining series, analogies and odd-one-out items were checked by hand against the stated rule. Rule-based analogies state "same rule for both" where needed, and the two grid questions give enough rows to rule out an alternative rule. Pure number series are left to Book 6 Part 5.
