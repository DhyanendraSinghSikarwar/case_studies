Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency)
- 3 stars: items every candidate needs before any test: what the gate looks like, reading the rules, the time and scoring arithmetic that sets strategy, reading stems, topic order, and mock review. These apply to every gate found in the sources.
- 2 stars: employer-type calibration, mapping to analyst work, test-day hygiene, sectional versus total timers, mixed reasoning+SQL tests, game-based rounds, interview puzzle behaviour. Common but tied to specific employers.
- 1 star: Mu Sigma observational rounds, adaptive tests, fatigue, practice-log analysis, 7-day plan, expected-value triage. Differentiators or thinly sourced.

Limitations
- Evidence is prep sites, vendor pages and anonymous candidate snippets; no official company test specification was found. Question counts and timings conflict between sources and are used only as labelled, unverified examples, never as facts in the questions (the 90-minute campus pattern is marked "unverified reported pattern").
- The claim that adaptive tests exist for these employers is not sourced; that question is conditional ("if you are told the test is adaptive").
- All 40 questions are original constructions on topics the sources name; none appeared verbatim in a source, so all are gap-fills at question level. About 10 (mark-weighting items, expected-value triage, practice-log analysis, 7-day plan, sunk-cost rule, brute force vs deduction) go beyond named topics.
- The 12 worked arithmetic items (marking-scheme, time-budget, attempt-count, practice-log and expected-value questions) were verified with Python scratch code; there are no seating/grid puzzles in this part.
