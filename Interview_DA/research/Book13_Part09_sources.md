Star-assignment reasoning (judgement, not measured frequency)
- 3 stars: NULL/UNKNOWN semantics, NOT IN, basic IF/AND/OR and pseudocode or flowchart tracing. The NULL and NOT IN pages are consistent and widely taught; tracing items are reported across TCS, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant prep pages. SQL/Excel are the core DA screening skills (market_skills_2026.md).
- 2 stars: set operators, join and group semantics with NULLs, decision-table completeness, boundary tests, recursion tracing. Common in SQL interview lists, but the logic-puzzle framing is less often reported.
- 1 star: Excel dialect quirks, XOR/combinatorial counts, rule-conflict puzzles and edge-case lists; differentiators for strong analytical candidates, not reported as gate items.

Limitations
- All sources are search snippets from commercial prep and documentation sites; no official test-maker material. Nothing says Boolean or NULL puzzles appear in Mu Sigma, Fractal or service-MNC aptitude tests; their inclusion is a design choice for DA interviews (SQL/Excel rounds, case discussions), not a verified gate pattern.
- Excel behaviours (blank cell comparisons, COUNTIF with "<>", AND over ranges, text-versus-number comparison, IFS without default) and PostgreSQL arithmetic are standard documented behaviour but were not executed in Excel or PostgreSQL here; the answer agent should confirm against vendor docs.
- All 60 questions are original constructions (gap-fills at question level); only topic coverage traces to sources.
- Checks run: 28 SQL and pseudocode items were executed in scratch code (SQLite for NULL, join, group, set-operator, CASE and AVG items; Python for traces, truth-table counts and the unique-answer puzzle) and the expected results matched the intended answers. SQLite follows the standard NULL rules used here.
