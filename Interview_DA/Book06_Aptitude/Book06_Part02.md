### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is a ratio, and how do you reduce the ratio 48:72:120 to its simplest form?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); ratio is listed as a recurring topic in prep-site material for IT-services gates (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know a ratio compares sizes and can simplify it with the highest common factor (HCF).
**Say this in the interview:** "A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. I divide every term by their HCF, which is 24, so 48:72:120 becomes 2:3:5."
**Answer:** **2:3:5**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. A ratio tells you how many parts of one thing there are for each part of another (for example 2 men for every 3 women).
2. Find the HCF (the biggest number that divides all the terms): 48 = 24 x 2, 72 = 24 x 3, 120 = 24 x 5. So the HCF is 24.
3. Divide each term by 24: 48/24 = 2, 72/24 = 3, 120/24 = 5.
4. Simplest form: 2:3:5.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Halve repeatedly while all terms are even: 48:72:120 gives 24:36:60, then 12:18:30, then 6:9:15, then divide by 3 to get 2:3:5. It works because dividing every term by the same number never changes the comparison.
**Check:** 2 x 24 = 48, 3 x 24 = 72, 5 x 24 = 120 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- A ratio has no units; it only shows relative size.
- Simplest form means no number (other than 1) divides all terms.
- Multiplying or dividing all terms by the same number gives an equal ratio.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) Simplify 36:60:84? HCF 12, so 3:5:7. 2) Is 2:3 the same as 3:2? No, order matters.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Stopping at a common factor that is not the highest (for example 6:9:15) and calling it simplest; reversing the order of terms.

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 A bonus pool of Rs 63,000 is to be shared among three employees in the ratio 2:3:4. How much does each employee get?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** The "one part" method for sharing in a ratio.
**Say this in the interview:** "Total parts are 2+3+4 = 9, so one part is 63,000 / 9 = 7,000. The shares are Rs 14,000, Rs 21,000 and Rs 28,000."
**Answer:** **Rs 14,000, Rs 21,000 and Rs 28,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Add the ratio terms: 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 parts.
2. One part = 63,000 / 9 = Rs 7,000.
3. First employee: 2 x 7,000 = Rs 14,000.
4. Second: 3 x 7,000 = Rs 21,000.
5. Third: 4 x 7,000 = Rs 28,000.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Find one part first, then multiply. Each share is (its term / total terms) of the pool, for example 2/9 of 63,000.
**Check:** 14,000 + 21,000 + 28,000 = 63,000 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- A "part" is just one unit of the ratio; the pool is cut into equal parts.
- Each person gets as many parts as their ratio number.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same pool in ratio 1:2:6? 9 parts again, one part Rs 7,000: Rs 7,000, 14,000 and 42,000.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by 3 (the number of people) instead of by 9 (the total parts).

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 The ratio of male to female employees in a department is 7:5. If there are 35 male employees, how many female employees are there, and what is the total headcount?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a 7:5 unitary-method example appears in a prep blog on ratio questions (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Unitary method (find one part, then scale).
**Say this in the interview:** "7 parts equal 35 men, so one part is 5 people. Women are 5 parts, so 25, and the total headcount is 60."
**Answer:** **25 female employees; total headcount 60**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Male = 7 parts = 35 people.
2. One part = 35 / 7 = 5 people.
3. Female = 5 parts = 5 x 5 = 25.
4. Total = 35 + 25 = 60 (or 12 parts x 5 = 60).
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Multiplier = 35 / 7 = 5; multiply every term by 5: 7:5 becomes 35:25.
**Check:** 35:25 divided by 5 gives 7:5 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- The multiplier that turns the ratio number into the real number is the same for all terms.
- Headcount is the sum of all parts, not just one group.
**Likely follow-ups:** If there were 42 men? Multiplier 6, women 30, total 72.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 35 + 5 = 40, or forgetting the question also asks for the total.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the formula for the average of a set of numbers, and what is the average of 12, 15, 18, 22 and 28?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); averages are described as standard in placement tests (prep-site claim, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Basic formula and clean arithmetic.
**Say this in the interview:** "Average equals the sum of the values divided by how many values there are. The sum is 95 and there are 5 numbers, so the average is 19."
**Answer:** **19**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Add: 12 + 15 = 27; 27 + 18 = 45; 45 + 22 = 67; 67 + 28 = 95.
2. Count the numbers: 5.
3. Average = 95 / 5 = 19.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Guess 18 and note the gaps: -6, -3, 0, +4, +10 add to +5; 5/5 = 1, so the average is 18 + 1 = 19. This works because gaps from any guess average out to the true gap.
**Check:** 19 x 5 = 95 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Average (also called mean) is the value each item would have if all were equal and the total stayed the same.
- Rearranged: Sum = Average x Count. This version solves most average problems.
**Likely follow-ups:** Average of 10, 20, 30, 40? 100/4 = 25.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by the wrong count; arithmetic slips while adding.

### Q5 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 The average monthly salary of 8 employees is Rs 36,000. What is the total payroll, and what does the average become if a ninth employee earning Rs 45,000 joins?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Using Sum = Average x Count, then recomputing.
**Say this in the interview:** "Total payroll is 8 x 36,000 = Rs 2,88,000. Adding Rs 45,000 gives Rs 3,33,000 for 9 people, so the new average is Rs 37,000."
**Answer:** **Total payroll Rs 2,88,000; new average Rs 37,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total = 8 x 36,000 = 2,88,000.
2. New total = 2,88,000 + 45,000 = 3,33,000.
3. New count = 9.
4. New average = 3,33,000 / 9 = 37,000.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The newcomer earns 45,000 - 36,000 = 9,000 above the old average. Spread over 9 people that is 1,000 each, so the average rises by Rs 1,000 to 37,000.
**Check:** 37,000 x 9 = 3,33,000 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- You cannot average with the old average alone; you must go back to the total.
- The excess over the old average, shared across the new headcount, is the rise.
**Likely follow-ups:** If the ninth earned Rs 27,000? 9,000 below, so the average falls by 1,000 to Rs 35,000.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Averaging 36,000 and 45,000 to get 40,500.

### Q6 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is a weighted average, and why can you not simply average two group averages when the groups have different sizes?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Understanding that bigger groups count for more.
**Say this in the interview:** "A weighted average gives each value a weight, usually its group size. You cannot just average two averages because a group of 90 people should influence the result more than a group of 10."
**Answer:** **Weighted average = (sum of value x weight) / (sum of weights); a plain average of averages is only right when group sizes are equal.**
**Step-by-step working (mini-example):**
1. Group A: 10 people, average salary Rs 40,000. Group B: 90 people, average Rs 60,000.
2. Plain average of averages: (40,000 + 60,000) / 2 = 50,000 (wrong).
3. Weighted: (10 x 40,000 + 90 x 60,000) / 100 = (4,00,000 + 54,00,000) / 100 = Rs 58,000.
4. 58,000 sits near 60,000 because Group B is far bigger.
**Check:** 58,000 x 100 = 58,00,000, which equals 4,00,000 + 54,00,000 (the two group totals).
**Explain simply:**
- Weight means "how much this group counts"; here it is the headcount.
- An average hides the total; to combine groups you must rebuild totals (average x count).
- With equal sizes the weights cancel, so the plain average works.
**Likely follow-ups:** When is a simple average correct? When groups are the same size.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Averaging the averages (the test setter expects Rs 50,000 as the tempting wrong option).

### Q7 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, find A:B:C.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Making the common term (B) equal in both ratios.
**Say this in the interview:** "B is 3 in one ratio and 4 in the other, so I make B equal to 12 in both. That gives A:B:C = 8:12:15."
**Answer:** **8:12:15**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. B appears as 3 (in A:B) and 4 (in B:C). Their LCM (lowest common multiple, the smallest number both divide into) is 12.
2. Scale A:B = 2:3 by 4: 8:12.
3. Scale B:C = 4:5 by 3: 12:15.
4. Join them: A:B:C = 8:12:15.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Multiply A:B by B's number in the second ratio (4) and B:C by B's number in the first (3): A = 2 x 4 = 8, B = 3 x 4 = 12, C = 5 x 3 = 15 (valid here because 12 is the LCM of 3 and 4; in general, use the LCM).
**Check:** 8/12 = 2/3 and 12/15 = 4/5 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- B is the bridge between the two ratios, so it must show the same number in both.
- Scaling a ratio by one number keeps its meaning.
**Likely follow-ups:** If A:B = 3:4 and B:C = 6:7? Make B = 12: 3:4 x 3 = 9:12 and 6:7 x 2 = 12:14, so A:B:C = 9:12:14.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing 2:3:5 or 2:3:4:5 by just gluing numbers without matching B.

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 How do you tell whether two quantities are in direct proportion or inverse proportion? Give one business example of each.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Conceptual clarity before applying the unitary method.
**Say this in the interview:** "If one goes up and the other goes up in the same ratio, it is direct, and their ratio y/x stays fixed. If one goes up and the other comes down, so their product stays fixed, it is inverse."
**Answer:** **Direct: the ratio stays constant (more of one means proportionally more of the other). Inverse: the product stays constant (more of one means proportionally less of the other).**
**Step-by-step working (mini-examples):**
1. Direct: pay for hours worked at Rs 500 an hour. 4 hours gives Rs 2,000; 8 hours gives Rs 4,000. Pay / hours = 500 always.
2. Inverse: a recruitment drive needs 120 person-days of screening. 4 recruiters take 30 days; 8 recruiters take 15 days. Recruiters x days = 120 always.
3. Test: double one quantity; if the other doubles it is direct, if it halves it is inverse.
**Check:** Direct: 2,000 / 4 = 4,000 / 8 = 500. Inverse: 4 x 30 = 8 x 15 = 120.
**Explain simply:**
- Direct: same direction, same rate (cost per item).
- Inverse: opposite direction, total work fixed (workers and days).
- Always ask "what stays the same, the ratio or the product?"
**Likely follow-ups:** Is salary proportional to years of service? Only if a fixed rate applies; proportion needs a constant ratio.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating "more workers, fewer days" as direct; assuming any two rising things are proportional.

### Q9 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the rule of alligation, and in what kind of problem do you use it?
**Asked at:** prep sites list alligation and mixture questions as recurring at IT-services gates (a single unverified prep-site claim); no company-specific candidate report found.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Knowing the fast route for mixing two things of different strength or price.
**Say this in the interview:** "Alligation finds the ratio in which two ingredients are mixed to get a target average price or strength. The ratio is the cheaper's distance to the mean versus the dearer's distance to the mean, crossed over."
**Answer:** **Quantity of cheaper : quantity of dearer = (dearer price - mean) : (mean - cheaper price).**
**Step-by-step working (mini-example):** Mix tea at Rs 200/kg and Rs 300/kg to get Rs 240/kg.
1. Gap of dearer from mean: 300 - 240 = 60.
2. Gap of mean from cheaper: 240 - 200 = 40.
3. Cheaper : dearer = 60 : 40 = 3:2.
4. Check: 3 x 200 + 2 x 300 = 1,200; 1,200 / 5 = 240.
**Check:** 3 kg at Rs 200 + 2 kg at Rs 300 = Rs 1,200 for 5 kg, which is Rs 240 per kg.
**Explain simply:**
- The mean sits between the two prices, closer to the one you use more of.
- The ratio is crossed because the cheaper one is used more when the mean is near it.
- Use it for price mixtures, concentration (acid, milk and water) and group-average problems.
**Likely follow-ups:** Can it be used for the average marks of two groups? Yes: the same cross method gives the ratio of group sizes.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Not crossing the gaps; using it with three ingredients directly.

### Q10 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Eight workers are paid Rs 12,000 in total for 15 days of work at a fixed daily rate per worker. How much would 10 workers be paid for 24 days at the same rate?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Unitary method with two directly proportional variables.
**Say this in the interview:** "Rate per worker per day is 12,000 / (8 x 15) = Rs 100. Then 10 workers x 24 days x 100 = Rs 24,000."
**Answer:** **Rs 24,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Worker-days paid: 8 x 15 = 120.
2. Rate per worker-day = 12,000 / 120 = Rs 100.
3. New worker-days: 10 x 24 = 240.
4. Pay = 240 x 100 = Rs 24,000.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Pay grows with both workers and days: 12,000 x (10/8) x (24/15) = 12,000 x 1.25 x 1.6 = 24,000.
**Check:** 240 worker-days is double 120, so pay doubles (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- A "worker-day" is one person working one day; pay is proportional to worker-days.
- This is direct proportion on both factors (unlike work-done-in-days, which is inverse).
**Likely follow-ups:** 5 workers for 9 days? 45 worker-days x 100 = Rs 4,500.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating days as inverse because "work and time" problems usually are; here money is being paid, not work completed.

### Q11 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 The average score of a candidate in four tests is 72. What must the candidate score in the fifth test to bring the average up to 75?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Working with totals rather than averages.
**Say this in the interview:** "Four tests total 288. Five tests at 75 need 375. The difference, 87, is the fifth score."
**Answer:** **87 marks**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total of four tests = 4 x 72 = 288.
2. Needed total for five tests = 5 x 75 = 375.
3. Fifth score = 375 - 288 = 87.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Target 75 plus the 4 earlier tests each need to rise 3 points: 75 + 4 x 3 = 87. The new score must cover its own 75 and make up the shortfall of the old four.
**Check:** (288 + 87) / 5 = 75 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Averages cannot be added; totals can.
- Required last score = new total minus old total.
**Likely follow-ups:** Target average 80? 400 - 288 = 112, impossible if the maximum is 100; always check feasibility.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 75 or 78 by only adding the target gap once, instead of making up 3 marks for each of the four earlier tests.

### Q12 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The average salary of 40 employees in Department A is Rs 50,000 and of 60 employees in Department B is Rs 65,000. What is the average salary of all 100 employees?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a weighted-average pattern common in prep material.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Weighted average, not a simple average of averages.
**Say this in the interview:** "Total pay is 40 x 50,000 + 60 x 65,000 = 20,00,000 + 39,00,000 = 59,00,000. Divided by 100 people, the average is Rs 59,000."
**Answer:** **Rs 59,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Department A total = 40 x 50,000 = 20,00,000.
2. Department B total = 60 x 65,000 = 39,00,000.
3. Combined = 59,00,000.
4. Average = 59,00,000 / 100 = Rs 59,000.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The gap is 15,000. B has 60% of staff, so the average is 50,000 + 0.6 x 15,000 = 50,000 + 9,000 = 59,000.
**Check:** Alligation: 59,000 is 9,000 above A and 6,000 below B; ratio 6:9 = 2:3 = 40:60 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Combined average lies closer to the bigger department's average.
- Weight each average by its headcount.
**Likely follow-ups:** If B had 40 people too? Equal sizes, so plain average Rs 57,500.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking Rs 57,500 (simple average of the two averages).

### Q13 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The average age of 39 employees in a team is 30 years. When their manager, aged 70 years, is included, what is the new average age?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Inclusion of one extra value into an average.
**Say this in the interview:** "Team total is 39 x 30 = 1,170. Adding 70 gives 1,240 for 40 people, so the average is 31 years."
**Answer:** **31 years**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Team total age = 39 x 30 = 1,170.
2. Add the manager: 1,170 + 70 = 1,240.
3. Count = 40.
4. New average = 1,240 / 40 = 31.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Manager is 70 - 30 = 40 years above the old average. Shared among 40 people that is 1 year each, so 30 + 1 = 31.
**Check:** 31 x 40 = 1,240 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- The newcomer's excess over the old average is spread across the new total headcount.
**Likely follow-ups:** Manager aged 50? 20 above, spread over 40 gives 0.5, so 30.5.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by 39 instead of 40.

### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The average weight of 8 people increases by 2.5 kg when one person weighing 65 kg is replaced by a new person. What is the weight of the new person?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** The replacement shortcut: total change = change in average x count.
**Say this in the interview:** "The average rose 2.5 for 8 people, so the total rose by 20 kg. The new person is 20 kg heavier than the one who left, so 85 kg."
**Answer:** **85 kg**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Rise in average = 2.5 kg for 8 people.
2. Rise in total = 8 x 2.5 = 20 kg.
3. This rise came entirely from the swap, so new person = 65 + 20 = 85 kg.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** New = old + (count x change in average). It works because only one value changed, so the whole shift in the total comes from it.
**Check:** Total before is 8A; after it is 8A - 65 + 85 = 8A + 20, so the average rises 20/8 = 2.5 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- You do not need the original average.
- If the average falls, subtract instead.
**Likely follow-ups:** Average falls by 1.5 when a 70 kg person leaves? New = 70 - 12 = 58 kg.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding only 2.5 to 65 to get 67.5.

### Q15 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The monthly salaries of two employees are in the ratio 3:5. After each gets an increment of Rs 4,000, the ratio becomes 7:11. What were their original salaries?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Setting up an equation from a ratio before and after.
**Say this in the interview:** "Let salaries be 3x and 5x. Then (3x + 4,000)/(5x + 4,000) = 7/11. Cross-multiplying gives x = 8,000, so the salaries were Rs 24,000 and Rs 40,000."
**Answer:** **Rs 24,000 and Rs 40,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Let the salaries be 3x and 5x.
2. 11(3x + 4,000) = 7(5x + 4,000).
3. 33x + 44,000 = 35x + 28,000.
4. 16,000 = 2x, so x = 8,000.
5. Salaries: 3 x 8,000 = 24,000 and 5 x 8,000 = 40,000.
**Check:** 28,000 / 44,000 = 7/11 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- "x" is the size of one ratio part.
- The same amount added to both keeps the difference fixed (16,000 before = 16,000 after), which is another route: before, difference is 2 parts; after, 4 parts.
**Likely follow-ups:** Using the difference: after increment 11 - 7 = 4 parts = 16,000, so one part is 4,000; 7 x 4,000 = 28,000, minus 4,000 = 24,000. Same answer.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding 4,000 only to one salary; forgetting to subtract the increment at the end.

### Q16 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 In what ratio must rice costing Rs 40 per kg be mixed with rice costing Rs 55 per kg so that the mixture is worth Rs 48 per kg?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); price-mixture alligation is a staple of IT-services quant sections (prep-site claim, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Alligation with a cross.
**Say this in the interview:** "Dearer minus mean is 55 - 48 = 7; mean minus cheaper is 48 - 40 = 8. So cheaper to dearer is 7:8."
**Answer:** **7:8 (Rs 40 rice : Rs 55 rice)**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Cheaper = 40, dearer = 55, mean = 48.
2. Distance of dearer from mean = 55 - 48 = 7 (this becomes the quantity of the cheaper rice).
3. Distance of cheaper from mean = 48 - 40 = 8 (this becomes the quantity of the dearer rice).
4. Ratio = 7:8.
**Check:** 7 kg at 40 = 280; 8 kg at 55 = 440; total 720 for 15 kg = Rs 48 per kg (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- 48 is 8 away from 40 and 7 away from 55, so it is slightly closer to the dearer rice; that is why the cheaper rice gets the smaller number (7).
- Crossing: each ingredient's quantity is the other's distance.
**Likely follow-ups:** Mean Rs 50? Gaps 5 and 10, ratio 5:10 = 1:2.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing 8:7 (not crossing).

### Q17 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A mixture of 60 litres contains milk and water in the ratio 2:1. How much water must be added to make the ratio of milk to water 1:1?
**Asked at:** milk/water mixtures appear in Accenture-tagged prep material (unverified prep-site report); no candidate report found.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Keeping the unchanged component fixed.
**Say this in the interview:** "Milk is 40 litres and water 20. For a 1:1 ratio water must also be 40, so add 20 litres."
**Answer:** **20 litres of water**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 3 parts = 60 litres, so 1 part = 20 litres.
2. Milk = 2 x 20 = 40 L; water = 20 L.
3. Milk stays at 40 L. For 1:1, water must be 40 L.
4. Water to add = 40 - 20 = 20 L.
**Check:** 40 : (20 + 20) = 1:1 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Adding water changes only the water, so milk is the anchor.
**Likely follow-ups:** To reach 2:3 milk:water? Water = 60 L, add 40 L.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding water to both sides, or answering 30 L.

### Q18 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The present ages of a father and his son are in the ratio 5:2. After 10 years the ratio becomes 2:1. What are their present ages?
**Asked at:** commonly asked; age problems with a "ratio now, ratio later" pattern are reported as recurring Wipro-style items in prep material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Ages as equations, remembering that both people age by the same number of years.
**Say this in the interview:** "Let ages be 5x and 2x. After 10 years, (5x + 10) = 2(2x + 10), so x = 10. Father is 50, son is 20."
**Answer:** **Father 50 years, son 20 years**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Present ages: 5x and 2x.
2. After 10 years: 5x + 10 and 2x + 10, in the ratio 2:1.
3. 5x + 10 = 2(2x + 10) = 4x + 20.
4. x = 10.
5. Father = 50, son = 20.
**Check:** After 10 years: 60 and 30, ratio 2:1 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- The gap between two ages never changes (here 30 years).
- Fast route: the gap is 3 parts now (5x - 2x = 3x). After 10 years the father is twice the son, so the gap equals the son's age then: 3x = 2x + 10, so x = 10 (son 20 now, 30 then).
**Likely follow-ups:** Ratio 7:3 now, 2:1 after 10 years? Father 70, son 30 gives 80:40, yes 2:1.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding 10 only to one person; solving x as the answer.

### Q19 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The ratio of the present ages of A and B is 4:5. After 8 years the ratio will be 6:7. Find their present ages.
**Asked at:** the "4:5 now, 6:7 later" age pattern is reported as a recurring Wipro NLTH topic in prep material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** The same age-ratio setup with unequal bases.
**Say this in the interview:** "Ages are 4x and 5x. (4x + 8)/(5x + 8) = 6/7 gives x = 4, so A is 16 and B is 20."
**Answer:** **A = 16 years, B = 20 years**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Present ages: 4x and 5x.
2. 7(4x + 8) = 6(5x + 8).
3. 28x + 56 = 30x + 48.
4. 8 = 2x, so x = 4.
5. A = 16, B = 20.
**Check:** After 8 years: 24 and 28, ratio 6:7 (verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- The difference between two ages never changes: it is 1 part now (5x - 4x = x). Later the ratio 6:7 also has a difference of 1 part, so the later ages are 6x and 7x.
- Fast route: later A = 6x = 4x + 8 (A's age now plus 8 years), so x = 4. Later ages are 24 and 28; now they are 16 and 20.
**Likely follow-ups:** Ratio 3:4, after 6 years 4:5? x = 6: 18 and 24.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting to subtract the years when working from the later ratio.

### Q20 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 The average of 10 observations is 25. It is later found that two entries were recorded wrongly: 15 instead of 45, and 40 instead of 20. What is the correct average?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Correcting a total, with signs.
**Say this in the interview:** "Wrong total is 250. First error: recorded 15 where it should be 45, so add 30. Second: recorded 40 where it should be 20, so subtract 20. Correct total is 260, average 26."
**Answer:** **26**

### Q25 ⭐⭐ 🟢 How do you find the fourth proportional to 6, 9 and 14, and the mean proportional between 4 and 25?

**Answer:** **Fourth proportional = 21; mean proportional = 10.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. The fourth proportional to a, b, c is the number x with a : b = c : x, so x = (b x c) / a.
2. x = (9 x 14) / 6 = 126 / 6 = 21.
3. The mean proportional m between a and b satisfies a : m = m : b, so m x m = a x b.
4. m x m = 4 x 25 = 100, so m = 10.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 6 to 9 is a multiplier of 1.5, so x = 14 x 1.5 = 21. For the mean proportional, take the square root of the product: the square root of 100 is 10.

**Explain simply:**
- A proportion means two ratios are equal: a : b = c : d, which means a x d = b x c (cross-multiplication).

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Third proportional to 4 and 6?* 6 x 6 / 4 = 9. 2) *Mean proportional of 9 and 16?* The square root of 144 = 12.

### Q29 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the average of the first 20 even numbers and of the first 25 odd numbers? State the shortcut formulas you used.

**Say this in the interview:** "The sum of the first n even numbers is n(n+1), so their average is n+1, which is 21. The sum of the first n odd numbers is n squared, so their average is n, which is 25."

3. Sum of the first n odd numbers (1, 3, 5, ...) = n x n. For n = 25: 25 x 25 = 625.
4. Average = 625 / 25 = 25.

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Average of the first 50 even numbers?* 51. 2) *Sum of the first 15 odd numbers?* 225.

### Q37 ⭐⭐ 🟡 In a class the average mark of boys is 72 and of girls is 78. The average of the whole class is 74. In what ratio are boys and girls? Use alligation.

**What the interviewer is checking:** Applying the rule of alligation to averages.
**Say this in the interview:** "The mean 74 sits between 72 and 78. Boys : girls is the distance of the mean from the girls' average over its distance from the boys' average: (78 - 74) : (74 - 72) = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1."

3. The quantity of the lower group (boys) is proportional to the gap on the other side (4), and girls to 2.
4. Boys : girls = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The mean 74 is closer to 72 (gap 2) than to 78 (gap 4), so there are more boys, in the inverse ratio of the gaps: 4 : 2 = 2 : 1.
**Check:** Say 20 boys and 10 girls: (20 x 72 + 10 x 78) / 30 = (1,440 + 780) / 30 = 2,220 / 30 = 74. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- Alligation is a shortcut for weighted averages: the larger group pulls the overall mean toward its own average.
- The ratio of quantities is the inverse of the ratio of the distances from the mean.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *If the class average were 75?* Gaps 3 and 3, so 1 : 1. 2) *If there are 30 students in all with mean 74?* 20 boys and 10 girls.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Giving 1 : 2 (the gaps in the wrong order); the larger group is the one nearer the mean.

### Q39 ⭐⭐ 🟡 20 litres of a 20% acid solution is mixed with 10 litres of a 50% acid solution. What is the percentage of acid in the final mixture?

2. Acid in the second solution = 50% of 10 = 5 litres.
3. Total acid = 4 + 5 = 9 litres; total volume = 20 + 10 = 30 litres.
4. Concentration = 9 / 30 x 100 = 30%.

### Q40 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How much water must be added to 40 litres of a 30% acid solution to reduce the concentration to 20%?

**Answer:** **20 litres of water.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Acid now = 30% of 40 = 12 litres.
2. After adding water the acid is still 12 litres, and it must be 20% of the new total.
3. New total = 12 / 0.20 = 60 litres.
4. Water to add = 60 - 40 = 20 litres.

- Adding water dilutes but does not change the quantity of acid, so the acid is the fixed anchor.
- Water is a 0% solution, which makes it a case of alligation.

### Q41 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Vessel 1 holds 20 litres of a milk-water mixture in the ratio 3:2 and vessel 2 holds 30 litres in the ratio 4:1. If both are poured into one container, what is the ratio of milk to water?

### Q42 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A container holds 40 litres of pure milk. 4 litres are removed and replaced with water, and the process is repeated once more. How much milk is left in the container?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); replacement problems are listed as a recurring pattern in prep sources (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Applying the replacement formula or tracking the milk step by step.
**Say this in the interview:** "Each round keeps 36/40 = 90% of the milk. After two rounds, 40 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 32.4 litres of milk remain."
**Answer:** **32.4 litres of milk.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Round 1: remove 4 L of pure milk, leaving 36 L milk; add 4 L water. The mixture is 36 L milk in 40 L, so milk is 90%.
2. Round 2: remove 4 L of the mixture; it carries 4 x 0.9 = 3.6 L milk. Milk left = 36 - 3.6 = 32.4 L.
3. So 32.4 L of milk and 7.6 L of water are in the container.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Milk left = 40 x (1 - 4/40) x (1 - 4/40) = 40 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 40 x 0.81 = 32.4 litres.
**Check:** 32.4 / 40 = 0.81 and 0.9 squared is 0.81. Same.
**Explain simply:**
- Each time you take out a portion, the same fraction of milk leaves with it, so what remains is multiplied by (1 - fraction removed).
- The second removal takes out some milk too, because the mixture is no longer pure.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Litres of water in the container now?* 7.6 L. 2) *Three rounds?* 40 x 0.9 cubed = 29.16 L.

### Q43 ⭐⭐ 🟡 What is the formula for the quantity of pure liquid left after n replacements in a container (replacement problems)? When does it apply?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); replacement problems are listed as a recurring pattern in prep sources (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the formula, its conditions, and why it works.
**Say this in the interview:** "Pure liquid left = Original quantity x (1 - x/V) to the power n, where V is the container's total volume and x is the amount removed and replaced each time, n times."
**Answer:** **Pure liquid left = V x (1 - x/V)^n** (V = total volume of the container, x = quantity drawn out and replaced each time, n = number of repetitions).
**Step-by-step working (mini-example):**
1. A 50-litre container of pure milk; 5 litres are removed and replaced with water; repeat 3 times in all.
2. Each round keeps 1 - 5/50 = 0.9 of the milk.
3. After 3 rounds: 50 x 0.9 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 50 x 0.729 = 36.45 litres of milk.
4. Water now = 50 - 36.45 = 13.55 litres.
**Check:** Round by round the milk is 50, 45, 40.5, 36.45 litres, matching 50 x 0.729.
**Explain simply:**
- It applies when the same quantity is removed from a well-mixed container and replaced by the other liquid (or by water), repeatedly, with the total volume constant.
- Why it works: each removal takes out the fraction x/V of whatever is there, so the pure liquid is multiplied by (1 - x/V) every round.
- The fraction (1 - x/V)^n is also the fraction of pure liquid left, so the ratio of pure to total after n rounds is (1 - x/V)^n.
- If the container starts with a mixture (not pure), apply the formula to the quantity of the component you are tracking.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Q42 with the formula?* 40 x (1 - 4/40)^2 = 32.4 L. 2) *Does it work if the volume changes?* No; the denominator must stay the total volume.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using x/V of the original amount repeatedly (subtracting n times x), using the removed quantity instead of the container volume, or applying it to non-mixed or changing-volume containers.

### Q44 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A team of 40 employees has an average salary of Rs 45,000. After 8 employees with an average salary of Rs 30,000 resign, what is the average salary of the remaining team?

### Q46 ⭐⭐ 🟡 What are componendo and dividendo? If (x+y)/(x-y) = 7/3, find x:y.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the ratio shortcut that avoids cross-multiplying.
**Say this in the interview:** "Componendo-dividendo says if a/b = c/d then (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d). Here (x+y)/(x-y) = 7/3 means x/y = (7+3)/(7-3) = 10/4, so x:y = 5:2."
**Answer:** **x:y = 5:2**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Rule: if (x+y)/(x-y) = p/q, then x/y = (p+q)/(p-q).
2. Here p = 7, q = 3, so x/y = (7+3)/(7-3) = 10/4.
3. 10/4 reduces to 5/2, so x:y = 5:2.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Add the top and bottom of the given ratio for x, subtract them for y: 7+3 = 10, 7-3 = 4, then halve to 5:2.
**Check:** Put x = 5, y = 2: (5+2)/(5-2) = 7/3. Correct (also verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Componendo: add 1 to both sides of a ratio (a/b = c/d gives (a+b)/b = (c+d)/d).
- Dividendo: subtract 1 from both sides (gives (a-b)/b = (c-d)/d).
- Dividing the componendo result by the dividendo result cancels b and d, giving (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d).
- Worked mini-example: if (a+b)/(a-b) = 5/1, then a/b = 6/4 = 3:2.
**Likely follow-ups:** If (x+y)/(x-y) = 9/5, find x:y. Answer: (9+5)/(9-5) = 14/4 = 7:2.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting in the wrong order (getting 4:10 flipped), or forgetting to reduce 10:4 to 5:2.

### Q47 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A vehicle travels a distance at 60 km/h and returns the same distance at 40 km/h. Why is the average speed not 50 km/h, and what is it?

**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you use total distance over total time, not the simple average of speeds.
**Say this in the interview:** "Average speed is total distance divided by total time. The slower leg takes more time, so it counts more. For equal distances it is 2ab/(a+b) = 2 x 60 x 40 / 100 = 48 km/h."

**Step-by-step working:**
1. Take the one-way distance as 120 km (any common multiple works).
2. Time going: 120 / 60 = 2 hours. Time returning: 120 / 40 = 3 hours.
3. Total distance = 240 km; total time = 5 hours.
4. Average speed = 240 / 5 = 48 km/h.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For equal distances use 2ab/(a+b) = 4800/100 = 48.
**Check:** At 50 km/h the trip would take 240/50 = 4.8 hours, but the real trip takes 5 hours, so 50 is too high.
**Explain simply:**
- Average speed = total distance / total time, never the plain mean of speeds.
- More time is spent at the slow speed, so the answer sits closer to 40 than to 60.

- Mini-example: 30 and 60 km/h over equal distances gives 2 x 30 x 60 / 90 = 40 km/h.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same trip at 50 and 75 km/h? 2 x 50 x 75 / 125 = 60 km/h.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking 50 km/h (the plain average), the tempting wrong option.

### Q52 ⭐⭐ 🔴 A and B earn incomes in the ratio 4:5 and their expenditures are in the ratio 3:4. If each saves Rs 2,000, what are their incomes?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a classic two-equation ratio problem.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can set up two unknowns from income minus expenditure equals savings.
**Say this in the interview:** "Let incomes be 4k and 5k, expenditures 3m and 4m. Then 4k - 3m = 2,000 and 5k - 4m = 2,000. Solving gives k = 2,000, so incomes are Rs 8,000 and Rs 10,000."
**Answer:** **A = Rs 8,000 and B = Rs 10,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Incomes 4k and 5k; expenditures 3m and 4m.
2. A: 4k - 3m = 2,000. B: 5k - 4m = 2,000.
3. Multiply the first by 4: 16k - 12m = 8,000. Multiply the second by 3: 15k - 12m = 6,000.
4. Subtract: k = 2,000.
5. Put back: 4 x 2,000 - 3m = 2,000, so 3m = 6,000 and m = 2,000.

**Check:** Expenditures 6,000 and 8,000 (ratio 3:4); savings 8,000 - 6,000 = 2,000 and 10,000 - 8,000 = 2,000. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Income minus expenditure equals savings, so each person gives one equation.
- Use different letters (k and m) because the "parts" of income and expenditure are different sizes.
- Eliminating m (making its coefficient equal) leaves one unknown.
**Likely follow-ups:** If each saves Rs 3,000, incomes scale by 1.5: Rs 12,000 and Rs 15,000.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using the same letter for both ratios, which gives a wrong or impossible answer.

**Answer:** **45 more days**

1. Total provisions = 60 x 40 = 2,400 person-days (one person-day is one person's food for one day).
2. Used in first 10 days = 60 x 10 = 600.
3. Left = 2,400 - 600 = 1,800.
4. Employees now = 60 - 20 = 40.
5. Days = 1,800 / 40 = 45.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 30 days were left for 60 people; fewer people means more days: 30 x 60/40 = 45.
**Check:** 40 people x 45 days = 1,800 = what was left. Verified.
**Explain simply:**
- Provisions are fixed, so people and days are inversely proportional (more people, fewer days).
- Person-days turns the problem into plain subtraction and division.
- Mini-example: food for 10 people for 6 days lasts 20 people for 3 days.
**Likely follow-ups:** If 20 more joined instead? 1,800 / 80 = 22.5 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 40 - 10 = 30 (ignoring the change in headcount) or 60 (just scaling 40 by 60/40).

### Q54 ⭐⭐ 🔴 How many kg of rice costing Rs 30 per kg must be mixed with 20 kg of rice costing Rs 45 per kg so that the mixture costs Rs 38 per kg?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); alligation questions are tagged to IT-services tests on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can use alligation (the cross-difference method) quickly.
**Say this in the interview:** "The mean price is 38. The dearer rice is 45 - 38 = 7 above it and the cheaper rice is 38 - 30 = 8 below it, so crossing over, cheap : dear = 7 : 8. For 20 kg dear, cheap = 20 x 7/8 = 17.5 kg."
**Answer:** **17.5 kg**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Cheap rice Rs 30, dear rice Rs 45, mean Rs 38.
2. Cheap rice is 38 - 30 = 8 below the mean; dear rice is 45 - 38 = 7 above.
3. Quantities are in the opposite ratio: cheap : dear = 7 : 8.
4. Dear = 20 kg is 8 parts, so one part = 2.5 kg.
5. Cheap = 7 x 2.5 = 17.5 kg.
**Check:** Cost = 17.5 x 30 + 20 x 45 = 525 + 900 = 1,425 for 37.5 kg; 1,425 / 37.5 = 38. Verified.
**Explain simply:**
- Alligation: the distance of each price from the mean, crossed over, gives the quantity ratio.
- The cheap rice has a bigger gap (8), so you need less of it relative to the gap on the other side.
- Equivalent check: total gap below must equal total gap above (17.5 x 8 = 140 = 20 x 7).
**Likely follow-ups:** Mean Rs 40? Gaps 10 and 5, ratio 5:10 = 1:2, so cheap = 10 kg.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Not crossing the differences (getting 8:7, hence 22.86 kg).

### Q59 ⭐ 🟡 A is 25% more than B and C is 20% less than A. Find A:B:C.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you pick a base of 100 for B and chain the percentages.
**Say this in the interview:** "Take B = 100. A is 25% more, so 125. C is 20% less than A, so 100. A:B:C = 125:100:100 = 5:4:4."
**Answer:** **A:B:C = 5:4:4**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Let B = 100.
2. A = 100 + 25% of 100 = 125.
3. C = 125 - 20% of 125 = 125 - 25 = 100.
4. A:B:C = 125:100:100; divide by 25 to get 5:4:4.
**Check:** 5 is 25% more than 4; 4 is 20% less than 5. Verified.
**Explain simply:**
- "25% more than B" means A = 1.25 x B; "20% less than A" means C = 0.8 x A.
- 1.25 x 0.8 = 1, so C equals B exactly; the increase and decrease cancel here because 25% up and 20% down are exact reverses.
**Likely follow-ups:** If C were 10% less than A? C = 112.5, giving 125:100:112.5 = 10:8:9.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Assuming 25% up and 20% down give net 5%, or taking 20% of B instead of A.

### Q60 ⭐ 🟡 A sales executive's average over 12 months is Rs 48 lakh. In the 13th month the sales are Rs 100 lakh. What is the new average over 13 months?

### Q62 ⭐ 🟡 Two numbers are in the ratio 3:4 and their LCM is 180. What is their sum?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you link a ratio to the HCF/LCM relationship.
**Say this in the interview:** "The numbers are 3k and 4k. Since 3 and 4 share no factor, their LCM is 12k. 12k = 180 gives k = 15, so the numbers are 45 and 60, sum 105."
**Answer:** **105**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write the numbers as 3k and 4k, where k is their HCF (highest common factor, the biggest number dividing both).
2. 3 and 4 have no common factor, so LCM (smallest number both divide into) = 3 x 4 x k = 12k.
3. 12k = 180, so k = 15.
4. Numbers: 3 x 15 = 45 and 4 x 15 = 60.
5. Sum = 45 + 60 = 105.
**Check:** LCM of 45 and 60 is 180, and 45:60 = 3:4. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- If two numbers are in ratio a:b (in lowest terms), their HCF is the multiplier k and the LCM is a x b x k.
- Product of the two numbers = HCF x LCM: 45 x 60 = 2,700 = 15 x 180.
- Mini-example: ratio 2:5 with LCM 100 gives k = 10, numbers 20 and 50.
**Likely follow-ups:** Ratio 3:4 and HCF 15? Same numbers, 45 and 60.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 7k = 180 or reading 180 as the product.
