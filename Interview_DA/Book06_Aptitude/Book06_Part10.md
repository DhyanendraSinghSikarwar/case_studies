### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Without a calculator, write the percentage equivalents of 1/8, 1/6, 1/12 and 1/16 (to two decimals where needed).
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the fraction-to-percentage table is a standard item in the exam-prep guides listed in the sources file (unverified prep-site claims).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you have the common fraction-percentage pairs memorised, so Data Interpretation (DI) sums need no long division.
**Say this in the interview:** "I know 1/8 is 12.5%, so 1/16 is half of that, 6.25%. 1/6 is 16.67% and 1/12 is half of that, 8.33%."
**Answer:** **1/8 = 12.50%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/12 = 8.33%, 1/16 = 6.25%**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 1/8: 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5, so 12.50%.
2. 1/6: 100 ÷ 6 = 16.666..., rounded to 16.67%.
3. 1/12 is half of 1/6, so 16.67 ÷ 2 = 8.33% (exactly 8.333...).
4. 1/16 is half of 1/8, so 12.5 ÷ 2 = 6.25%.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Learn only the "anchors" 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/8 = 12.5%. Halving a denominator-doubled fraction halves the percentage (1/6 to 1/12, 1/8 to 1/16). This works because doubling the denominator halves the fraction.
**Check:** Multiply back: 8 x 12.5 = 100 and 16 x 6.25 = 100 exactly; 6 x 16.67 = 100.02 and 12 x 8.33 = 99.96 (small rounding gaps). Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- A percentage is "out of 100"; 1/n as a percentage is simply 100 divided by n.
- Memorising a few anchors lets you build the rest by halving, doubling or multiplying (5/8 = 5 x 12.5 = 62.5%).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What is 5/6 as a percentage?* 5 x 16.67 = 83.33%. 2) *What is 7/8?* 100 minus 12.5 = 87.5%.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing 1/6 as 16.6% or 17% when two decimals are asked; mixing up 1/12 (8.33%) and 1/16 (6.25%).

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Using the 10% + 5% method, find 15% of Rs 640 without long multiplication.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the 10% + 5% split appears in exam-prep guides in the sources file (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can break a percentage into easy blocks.
**Say this in the interview:** "10% of 640 is 64, 5% is half of that, 32, so 15% is 96."
**Answer:** **Rs 96**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 10% of 640: move the decimal one place left, giving 64.
2. 5% is half of 10%: 64 ÷ 2 = 32.
3. Add: 64 + 32 = 96.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Say "ten percent, then half of it, then add". It works because 15% = 10% + 5%, and 10% needs no multiplication at all.
**Check:** 96 ÷ 640 = 0.15, so 15%. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- "Percent" means per hundred, so 10% is one tenth, found by shifting the decimal.
- Any percentage that is a multiple of 5 can be built from 10% and 5% blocks (35% = 3 x 10% + 5%).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Find 35% of Rs 640.* 192 + 32 = Rs 224. 2) *Find 17.5% of Rs 640.* 64 + 32 + 16 = Rs 112.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Doing 5% as half of 64 but forgetting to add the 10% block back (answering 32).

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Using the 'ends in 5' rule n(n+1) followed by 25, find 85² and 115².
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); squares of numbers ending in 5 are covered by FACE Prep and GfG tutorials listed in the sources file (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the squaring shortcut and why it works.
**Say this in the interview:** "For 85, take the tens part 8, multiply by the next number 9 to get 72, and attach 25: 7225. For 115, take 11 x 12 = 132, attach 25: 13225."
**Answer:** **85² = 7,225 and 115² = 13,225**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Any number ending in 5 is written as (10n + 5), where n is everything before the final 5.
2. 85: n = 8. n x (n + 1) = 8 x 9 = 72. Attach 25 to get 7225.
3. 115: n = 11. 11 x 12 = 132. Attach 25 to get 13225.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Multiply the leading part by its next whole number, then write 25 at the end. Why: (10n + 5)² = 100n² + 100n + 25 = 100 x n(n+1) + 25, so "n(n+1)" lands in the hundreds and 25 sits at the end.
**Check:** 85 x 85 = 85 x 80 + 85 x 5 = 6800 + 425 = 7225; verified by script for both.
**Explain simply:**
- The rule is just algebra written as a pattern: the last two digits are always 25.
- It only works for numbers ending in 5.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Find 65².* 6 x 7 = 42, so 4225. 2) *Find 105².* 10 x 11 = 110, so 11025.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Multiplying n by n instead of n by (n + 1); dropping the 25.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 How do you multiply 468 by 25 quickly using division by 4? Compute 468 × 25.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a standard prep-site trick (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you replace a hard multiplication by an easy one using a friendly number.
**Say this in the interview:** "25 is 100 divided by 4, so I divide 468 by 4 to get 117 and multiply by 100: 11,700."
**Answer:** **11,700**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write 25 as 100 ÷ 4.
2. 468 ÷ 4 = 117 (468 ÷ 2 = 234, then 234 ÷ 2 = 117).
3. 117 x 100 = 11,700.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Halve twice, then add two zeros. It works because 468 x 25 = 468 x 100 ÷ 4, and the order of multiplying and dividing does not change the result.
**Check:** 468 x 25 = 468 x 20 + 468 x 5 = 9360 + 2340 = 11,700.
**Explain simply:**
- Multiplying by 25 is the same as taking a quarter of 100 times the number.
- If the number is not divisible by 4, you get a decimal: 450 ÷ 4 = 112.5, so 450 x 25 = 11,250.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Multiply by 125.* Divide by 8, multiply by 1000: 468 x 125 = 58,500. 2) *Multiply by 5.* Halve and add a zero: 2340.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding only one zero; dividing by 4 wrongly (468 ÷ 4 is 117, not 107).

### Q5 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Using the 'split the digits and add in the middle' trick, compute 73 × 11.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a standard Vedic-maths style trick (unverified prep-site material).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the multiply-by-11 pattern.
**Say this in the interview:** "Write 7, then 7 + 3 = 10, then 3. The 10 carries, so it becomes 803."
**Answer:** **803**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Split 73 into 7 and 3.
2. Add the two digits: 7 + 3 = 10.
3. Write first digit, middle sum, last digit: 7 | 10 | 3.
4. The middle 10 is two digits, so carry the 1 to the left: 7 + 1 = 8, middle 0, last 3. Result: 803.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Why it works: 73 x 11 = 73 x 10 + 73 = 730 + 73. Adding column by column gives 7, (3 + 7), 3, which is exactly the "add in the middle" pattern.
**Check:** 73 x 11 = 730 + 73 = 803.
**Explain simply:**
- Multiplying by 11 means "the number shifted left once, plus the number itself".
- If the middle sum is 10 or more, carry the tens digit to the left.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Compute 45 x 11.* 4 | 9 | 5 = 495. 2) *Compute 123 x 11.* 1 | 3 | 5 | 3 = 1353.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing 7103 by forgetting to carry the extra 1.

### Q6 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Using the identity (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b², compute 52 × 48 without long multiplication.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a standard algebra shortcut (unverified prep-site material).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you spot two numbers equally spaced around a round number.
**Say this in the interview:** "52 and 48 are 2 above and 2 below 50, so the product is 50² minus 2², which is 2500 minus 4, so 2496."
**Answer:** **2,496**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Find the midpoint of 52 and 48: 50.
2. Distance from the midpoint: 2. So 52 = 50 + 2 and 48 = 50 − 2.
3. Apply (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b² with a = 50, b = 2.
4. 50² = 2500; 2² = 4; 2500 − 4 = 2496.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Midpoint squared minus distance squared. It works because the cross terms +2ab and −2ab cancel when you expand.
**Check:** 52 x 48 = 52 x 50 − 52 x 2 = 2600 − 104 = 2496.
**Explain simply:**
- The identity says the product of two numbers equally far from a middle number is the middle's square minus the gap's square.
- It is handy for pairs like 99 x 101, 38 x 42, 63 x 57.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Compute 63 x 57.* 60² − 3² = 3600 − 9 = 3591. 2) *Compute 101 x 99.* 10000 − 1 = 9999.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting to square the gap (answering 2500 − 2 = 2498).

### Q7 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Find 37.5% of Rs 880 by converting the percentage to a fraction.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the fraction table (37.5% = 3/8) appears in prep-site guides in the sources file (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know 37.5% = 3/8 and use it.
**Say this in the interview:** "37.5% is 3/8. One eighth of 880 is 110, so three eighths is 330."
**Answer:** **Rs 330**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 12.5% = 1/8, so 37.5% = 3 x 12.5% = 3/8.
2. 880 ÷ 8 = 110.
3. 110 x 3 = 330.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Divide by 8, then multiply by 3. It is quicker than 880 x 37.5 ÷ 100 because both steps are single-digit.
**Check:** 10% of 880 = 88, so 30% = 264; 7.5% = 66; 264 + 66 = 330.
**Explain simply:**
- Some percentages are neat fractions: 12.5% = 1/8, 25% = 1/4, 37.5% = 3/8, 62.5% = 5/8, 87.5% = 7/8.
- Working with the fraction avoids decimals.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Find 62.5% of 880.* 5 x 110 = Rs 550. 2) *Find 87.5% of 880.* 880 − 110 = Rs 770.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 1/3 or 3/10 for 37.5%; dividing by 3 instead of 8.

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 An online assessment has 25 quantitative questions in 40 minutes. How do you split the time across questions, and what do you do when a question is taking more than two minutes?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); 75-100 seconds per quant question and skip-and-return advice appear in the prep sources in the sources file (unverified; test patterns differ between sources).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you have a time-management plan, not just maths skill.
**Say this in the interview:** "40 minutes is 2,400 seconds, so about 96 seconds a question. I do easy ones in under a minute, bank the saved time for harder ones, and if I cross two minutes without a clear path I mark it and move on."
**Answer:** **About 96 seconds per question on average (2,400 s ÷ 25). Skip any question stuck past about 2 minutes and return to it after a first pass.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Convert to seconds: 40 x 60 = 2,400 s.
2. Divide by 25: 2,400 ÷ 25 = 96 s per question.
3. Plan a split: roughly 60 s for easy, 90-100 s for medium, 120-150 s for hard ones.
4. Keep the last 2-3 minutes for review and for unattempted questions.
5. Rule for slow questions: if after about 20 seconds you have no method, or at 2 minutes you are not close, flag it, move on and come back.
**Check:** 25 questions x 96 s = 2,400 s = 40 minutes.
**Explain simply:**
- Every question is usually worth the same marks, so one stuck question costs you two or three easy ones.
- Check whether wrong answers lose marks (negative marking) before deciding to guess.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if there are 30 questions in 40 minutes?* 80 s each. 2) *When do you guess?* Only after eliminating options, and only if negative marking is small (see Q17).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Spending 4-5 minutes to "win" one hard question; not checking the final minutes for unanswered items.

### Q9 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Mock Set A, Q1 (target 60 s): An employee's salary of Rs 48,000 per month is raised by 12.5%. What is the new monthly salary?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); original mock question.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Percentage increase using the 1/8 fraction.
**Say this in the interview:** "12.5% is one eighth. An eighth of 48,000 is 6,000, so the new salary is 54,000."
**Answer:** **Rs 54,000 per month**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 12.5% = 1/8.
2. 48,000 ÷ 8 = 6,000 (the raise).
3. New salary = 48,000 + 6,000 = 54,000.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** New salary = 9/8 of the old: 48,000 ÷ 8 x 9 = 6,000 x 9 = 54,000. Raising by 1/8 means the new value is 9/8 of the old.
**Check:** (54,000 − 48,000) ÷ 48,000 = 0.125 = 12.5%. Target-time check: 3 short steps, about 20 seconds, well inside 60 s. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- An increase of p% multiplies the old value by (1 + p/100).
- Using a fraction (1/8) avoids decimal multiplication.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Raise Rs 48,000 by 25%?* Rs 60,000. 2) *By 6.25%?* 3,000 more, so Rs 51,000.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 6,000 (the raise, not the new salary).

### Q10 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Mock Set A, Q2 (target 60 s): A company's headcount fell from 800 to 680 in a year. What is the percentage decrease?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); original mock question.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Percentage change with the old value as the base.
**Say this in the interview:** "The fall is 120. Dividing by the old headcount, 800, gives 15%."
**Answer:** **15% decrease**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Fall = 800 − 680 = 120.
2. Divide by the old value: 120 ÷ 800 = 0.15.
3. 0.15 x 100 = 15%.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 10% of 800 is 80, 5% is 40, so 15% is 120. The drop equals 120, so the answer is 15%.
**Check:** 800 x 0.85 = 680. Target-time check: about 20-25 seconds. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Percentage change = (change ÷ starting value) x 100; the starting value is always the base.
- The same drop of 120 measured against 680 would give 17.6%, which is wrong.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *By what percent must 680 rise to return to 800?* 120 ÷ 680 = 17.65%. 2) *Headcount falls 15% from 800 again?* 680.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by 680 (the new value) and choosing 17.6%.

### Q11 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Using the base-100 (deviation) method, compute 98 × 103.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); FACE Prep base-method articles in the sources file (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can multiply numbers near 100 using their gaps.
**Say this in the interview:** "98 is 2 below 100 and 103 is 3 above. Cross-add: 98 + 3 = 101. Multiply the deviations: −2 x 3 = −6. So 101 hundreds minus 6, which is 10,100 − 6 = 10,094."
**Answer:** **10,094**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Deviations from 100: 98 is −2; 103 is +3.
2. Cross-add: 98 + 3 = 101 (or 103 − 2 = 101). This is the "hundreds" part.
3. Product of deviations: (−2) x (+3) = −6.
4. Result = 101 x 100 + (−6) = 10,100 − 6 = 10,094.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Cross-add, put two zeros, then add or subtract the product of the gaps. Why: (100 + x)(100 + y) = 10,000 + 100(x + y) + xy = 100 x (100 + x + y) + xy.
**Check:** 98 x 103 = 98 x 100 + 98 x 3 = 9,800 + 294 = 10,094.
**Explain simply:**
- Write each number as 100 plus or minus a small gap, then expand.
- When both numbers are below 100, both gaps are negative and their product is positive.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Compute 97 x 96.* Cross: 97 − 4 = 93; gaps (−3)(−4) = +12; 9,300 + 12 = 9,312. 2) *Compute 104 x 107.* 111 hundreds + 28 = 11,128.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding the product when it is negative (answering 10,106).

### Q12 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A salary is raised by 20% and then cut by 20%. Using the shortcut a + b + ab/100, what is the net percentage change?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); successive-change formula in exam-prep guides in the sources file (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know that +20% then −20% does not cancel.
**Say this in the interview:** "Net change is 20 − 20 + (20 x −20)/100 = −4. So the salary ends 4% below where it started."
**Answer:** **A net decrease of 4%**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. a = +20, b = −20.
2. ab/100 = (20 x −20) ÷ 100 = −400 ÷ 100 = −4.
3. Net = 20 + (−20) + (−4) = −4%.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Take a starting salary of 100: after +20% it is 120; 20% of 120 is 24; 120 − 24 = 96. A fall from 100 to 96 is 4%. The formula works because the multipliers are 1.2 x 0.8 = 0.96.
**Check:** 1.2 x 0.8 = 0.96, so 4% lower. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- The second percentage is taken on the new, bigger figure, so the 20% cut removes more than the 20% raise added.
- Successive changes multiply; they do not add.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Raise 25% then cut 20%?* 25 − 20 − 5 = 0% net. 2) *Is the order important?* No, multiplication is order-independent.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering "no change" because +20 − 20 = 0.

### Q13 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Using (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b² with a round number, compute 47².
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a standard squaring trick (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you use a round number near the target.
**Say this in the interview:** "47 is 50 minus 3. So 47 squared is 2500 minus 300 plus 9, which is 2209."
**Answer:** **2,209**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write 47 = 50 − 3, so a = 50, b = 3.
2. a² = 2,500.
3. 2ab = 2 x 50 x 3 = 300.
4. b² = 9.
5. 2,500 − 300 + 9 = 2,209.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Square the round number, subtract twice (round number x gap), add the gap squared. Alternative: 47² = (47 − 3) x (47 + 3) + 3² = 44 x 50 + 9 = 2,209.
**Check:** 47 x 47 = 47 x 40 + 47 x 7 = 1,880 + 329 = 2,209; last digit 7 x 7 = 49 gives 9. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- (a − b)² is "a² minus twice a times b, plus b²"; the last term is added, not subtracted.
- Pick the nearest multiple of 10 as a.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Compute 53².* 2500 + 300 + 9 = 2,809. 2) *Compute 38².* 40² − 2 x 40 x 2 + 4 = 1600 − 160 + 4 = 1,444.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting the +b² term (answering 2,200).

### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Without computing decimals, which is larger: 7/9 or 11/14? Show the cross-multiplication trick.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); comparing fractions is a standard approximation/DI skill (unverified prep-site material).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can compare fractions without long division.
**Say this in the interview:** "Cross-multiply: 7 x 14 = 98 and 11 x 9 = 99. The side with the bigger product wins, so 11/14 is larger."
**Answer:** **11/14 is larger than 7/9**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write the fractions side by side: 7/9 and 11/14.
2. Multiply the top of the first by the bottom of the second: 7 x 14 = 98.
3. Multiply the top of the second by the bottom of the first: 11 x 9 = 99.
4. The fraction whose numerator gave the bigger product is bigger: 99 belongs to 11/14.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Cross-multiply and compare the two products. It works because multiplying both fractions by 9 x 14 (a positive number) keeps their order and turns them into 7 x 14 and 11 x 9.
**Check:** 7/9 = 0.7778 and 11/14 = 0.7857; verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Bringing fractions to a common denominator is the safe way to compare; cross-multiplying does it in one step.
- The products are close (98 against 99), which tells you the fractions are close too.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Which is larger, 5/8 or 8/13?* 5 x 13 = 65, 8 x 8 = 64, so 5/8 is larger. 2) *Does this work for negatives?* Not directly; compare the positive versions and reverse.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Comparing the larger numerator only, or cross-multiplying in the wrong direction.

### Q15 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Using the assumed-mean method, find the average of the monthly sales figures 62, 58, 65, 55 and 70 (Rs lakh).
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a standard averages shortcut (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you average through small deviations instead of adding big numbers.
**Say this in the interview:** "I assume 60 as the mean. The gaps are +2, −2, +5, −5, +10. They add to +10; divided by 5 that is +2, so the average is 62 lakh."
**Answer:** **Rs 62 lakh**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Assume a mean of 60.
2. Deviations: 62 − 60 = +2; 58 − 60 = −2; 65 − 60 = +5; 55 − 60 = −5; 70 − 60 = +10.
3. Sum of deviations: 2 − 2 + 5 − 5 + 10 = +10.
4. Average deviation = 10 ÷ 5 = +2.
5. Average = 60 + 2 = 62.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Pick a round figure in the middle, add only the small gaps, divide by the count, add back. It works because the average of (x − 60) equals (average of x) − 60.
**Check:** Direct sum 62 + 58 + 65 + 55 + 70 = 310; 310 ÷ 5 = 62.
**Explain simply:**
- Average = total ÷ count. Shifting every number by the same amount shifts the average by the same amount.
- Pairs that cancel (+2 with −2, +5 with −5) save work.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Average of 102, 98, 105, 95?* Assume 100: gaps +2, −2, +5, −5 = 0, so 100. 2) *If the sixth month is 74?* Total 384 ÷ 6 = 64.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting to add the assumed mean back (answering 2).

### Q16 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Why is the average speed for a round trip not the simple average of the two speeds? Find the average speed if a delivery van goes at 40 km/h one way and returns the same distance at 60 km/h.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a classic time-speed-distance trap (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you average over time (total distance ÷ total time), not over speeds.
**Say this in the interview:** "The van spends more time at the slower speed, so the average is pulled toward 40. Total distance over total time gives 48 km/h, not 50."
**Answer:** **48 km/h**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Assume a one-way distance of 120 km (the LCM of 40 and 60, to keep numbers whole).
2. Time going: 120 ÷ 40 = 3 hours. Time returning: 120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours.
3. Total distance = 240 km; total time = 5 hours.
4. Average speed = 240 ÷ 5 = 48 km/h.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For equal distances, average speed = 2xy ÷ (x + y) = 2 x 40 x 60 ÷ 100 = 4,800 ÷ 100 = 48. This is the harmonic mean, which is correct because the van spends unequal times at the two speeds.
**Check:** The simple average 50 would need equal times at each speed; here more time is at 40, so the answer must be below 50, and 48 is. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Average speed means total distance divided by total time.
- Slow stretches take longer, so they weigh more in the average.
- The distance itself cancels out; it does not matter whether it is 120 km or 10 km.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Speeds of 30 and 60 for equal distances?* 2 x 30 x 60 ÷ 90 = 40 km/h. 2) *Equal times at 40 and 60?* Then the simple average, 50.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 50 km/h.

### Q17 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A test awards +1 for a correct answer and −0.25 for a wrong answer, with four options per question. Is it worth guessing blindly? What if you can eliminate two options? Compute the expected marks per guess in both cases.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); negative-marking strategy is advised by test-prep vendors in the sources file (unverified; many tests have no negative marking, so check the rules).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can use expected value to decide on guessing.
**Say this in the interview:** "A blind guess has a one in four chance of +1 and three in four of −0.25, so expected value is +0.0625, slightly positive. After removing two options it is +0.375, clearly worth it."
**Answer:** **Blind guess: +0.0625 marks per guess (marginally worth it). After eliminating two options: +0.375 marks per guess (definitely worth it).**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Blind guess: P(correct) = 1/4 = 0.25; P(wrong) = 0.75.
2. Expected marks = 0.25 x (+1) + 0.75 x (−0.25) = 0.25 − 0.1875 = +0.0625.
3. Two options eliminated: P(correct) = 1/2; P(wrong) = 1/2.
4. Expected marks = 0.5 x (+1) + 0.5 x (−0.25) = 0.5 − 0.125 = +0.375.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** A guess breaks even when P(correct) = 0.25 ÷ 1.25 = 20%. With four options a blind guess is 25%, just above break-even; with two options left it is 50%, well above.
**Check:** Over 100 blind guesses you expect 25 right (+25) and 75 wrong (−18.75), net +6.25 = 0.0625 each. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Expected value is the average result if you repeated the same gamble many times.
- The 0.0625 gain is tiny and has high variance, so a blind guess is only slightly better than skipping.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if the penalty is −0.5?* Blind: 0.25 − 0.375 = −0.125, so do not guess blindly. 2) *With one option eliminated?* P = 1/3: 0.333 − 0.1667 = +0.167.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating the penalty as −1, or assuming guessing always loses with negative marking.

### Q18 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Mock Set A, Q3 (target 90 s): A retailer marks goods 40% above cost price and then allows a 10% discount. What is the profit percent?

### Q33 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Using the alligation (cross) method, in what ratio should rice at Rs 40 per kg and rice at Rs 60 per kg be mixed so that the mixture is worth Rs 52 per kg?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); alligation is a standard mixture shortcut.
**What the interviewer is checking:** applying the cross-difference rule correctly and not swapping the ratio.
**Say this in the interview:** "Mean price 52. Distance of 60 from 52 is 8; distance of 40 from 52 is 12. The cheaper rice gets the larger distance's opposite: 8 : 12, which is 2 : 3."
**Answer:** **Rs 40 rice : Rs 60 rice = 2 : 3**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write the cheap price (40), dearer price (60) and the mean (52).
2. Cross-subtract: (dearer - mean) = 60 - 52 = 8, which is the weight of the cheaper rice; (mean - cheaper) = 52 - 40 = 12, which is the weight of the dearer rice.
3. Ratio cheaper : dearer = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3.

- Alligation finds the mixing ratio from the distances of the two prices to the mean.

### Q42 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Mock Set B, Q6 (target 60 s): Find the HCF and the LCM of 36 and 48.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); basic number system.
**What the interviewer is checking:** prime factorisation and the HCF x LCM = product identity.
**Say this in the interview:** "36 = 2 squared x 3 squared and 48 = 2 to the 4 x 3. HCF takes the smaller powers: 4 x 3 = 12. LCM takes the larger: 16 x 9 = 144."
**Answer:** **HCF = 12; LCM = 144**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Prime factors: 36 = 2 x 2 x 3 x 3 = 2^2 x 3^2; 48 = 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 3 = 2^4 x 3.
2. HCF (highest common factor, the biggest number dividing both) uses the lowest power of each prime: 2^2 x 3 = 12.
3. LCM (lowest common multiple, the smallest number both divide) uses the highest power: 2^4 x 3^2 = 16 x 9 = 144.

**Fast method (30 seconds):** Subtract: 48 - 36 = 12, and 12 divides both, so HCF = 12. Then LCM = (36 x 48) / 12 = 1,728 / 12 = 144.
**Check:** 12 x 144 = 1,728 = 36 x 48. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- HCF x LCM = product of the two numbers (true for two numbers only).
- HCF is used for splitting into equal groups (for example, 36 and 48 employees into equal teams: largest team size 12).
- LCM is used for "when will they coincide" (for example, two shift patterns repeating every 36 and 48 days align every 144 days).

**Likely follow-ups:** (1) HCF and LCM of 24 and 60? 12 and 120. (2) Smallest number of equal teams if 36 and 48 staff are split with no mixing? 3 + 4 = 7 teams of 12.
**Common mistakes / traps:** mixing up HCF and LCM; picking the highest powers for HCF; using the product identity for three numbers.

### Q54 ⭐⭐ 🔴 Using cyclicity of powers of 2 modulo 7, find the remainder when 2^50 is divided by 7.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Cyclicity (repeating remainders) so you never compute a huge power.
**Say this in the interview:** "Powers of 2 mod 7 repeat as 2, 4, 1 every three steps. 50 leaves remainder 2 when divided by 3, so the remainder is the second in the cycle, which is 4."
**Answer:** **4**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 2^1 = 2, remainder 2. 2^2 = 4, remainder 4. 2^3 = 8, remainder 1.
2. Then 2^4 = 16, remainder 2; the pattern 2, 4, 1 repeats, cycle length 3.
3. 50 divided by 3 gives 16 groups and remainder 2 (3 x 16 = 48).
4. 2^48 = (2^3)^16 leaves remainder 1, so 2^50 = 2^48 x 2^2 leaves the same remainder as 2^2 = 4.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Find where the remainder first hits 1 (2^3). Then 2^50 = 2^48 x 2^2, and the first part is 1.
**Check:** Verified by script: 2^50 mod 7 = 4.
**Explain simply:**
- When a power's remainder equals 1, the cycle restarts.
- Only the leftover exponent (50 mod 3 = 2) matters.
**Likely follow-ups:** Remainder of 2^100 mod 7? 100 mod 3 = 1, so 2.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking the wrong position in the cycle and answering 1 or 2.

### Q59 ⭐ 🟡 Using the formula for the sum of an arithmetic series, find the sum of all multiples of 7 from 7 to 100.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Counting the terms of an arithmetic progression (AP) and using the sum formula.
**Say this in the interview:** "Multiples of 7 up to 100 are 7 to 98, that is 14 terms. Sum = 14 x (7 + 98) / 2 = 735."
**Answer:** **735**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 100 / 7 = 14.28, so there are 14 multiples; the last is 7 x 14 = 98.
2. Sum of an AP = number of terms x (first + last) / 2.
3. Sum = 14 x (7 + 98) / 2 = 14 x 105 / 2 = 7 x 105 = 735.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Sum = 7 x (1 + 2 + ... + 14) = 7 x 105 = 735, using 14 x 15 / 2 = 105.
**Check:** Verified by script: 7 + 14 + ... + 98 = 735.
**Explain simply:**
- An AP has a constant gap (here 7); the average of the terms is the average of the first and last.
**Likely follow-ups:** Sum of multiples of 5 up to 100? 20 terms, 20 x (5 + 100) / 2 = 1,050.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 100 as the last term, or counting 15 terms.

### Q60 ⭐ 🟡 1 January 2026 is a Thursday. Using odd days, what day of the week is 1 January 2028?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Odd days: the leftover days after removing complete weeks.
**Say this in the interview:** "2026 and 2027 are both normal years with 1 odd day each, so 2 odd days. Thursday plus 2 is Saturday."
**Answer:** **Saturday**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. From 1 Jan 2026 to 1 Jan 2028 is two full years, 2026 and 2027.
2. Neither is a leap year (2028 is, but its extra day falls after 1 Jan, in February).
3. A normal year has 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 day, so 1 odd day each. Total = 2 odd days.
4. Thursday + 2 days = Saturday.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** A normal year shifts the weekday by 1, a leap year by 2. Count the years passed and add.
**Check:** Verified by script: 1 January 2028 is a Saturday.
**Explain simply:**
- 365 = 52 x 7 + 1, so each year pushes the weekday ahead by one (two if the year is a leap year).
**Likely follow-ups:** What day is 1 Jan 2029? 2028 is a leap year, so +2: Saturday + 2 = Monday.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Counting 2028 as part of the span and answering Sunday or Monday.

### Q61 ⭐ 🟡 Use the square of a number near 1000 trick to find 1012².
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** The base-1000 trick for (1000 + d) squared.
**Say this in the interview:** "1012 is 12 above 1000. Add 12 to 1012 to get 1024, then append the square of 12 as three digits, 144. So 1,024,144."
**Answer:** **10,24,144 (1,024,144)**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 1012 = 1000 + 12.
2. (1000 + 12)^2 = 1000^2 + 2 x 1000 x 12 + 12^2 = 1,000,000 + 24,000 + 144.
3. Total = 1,024,144.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Left part = number + deviation = 1012 + 12 = 1024; right part = deviation squared (12^2 = 144) written in 3 digits. Join: 1024 | 144.
**Check:** Verified by script: 1012^2 = 1,024,144.
**Explain simply:**
- The trick is (a + b)^2 with a = 1000, so 2ab and b^2 line up with the place values.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1007^2? 1014 | 049 = 1,014,049.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing the right part in two digits; for deviations under 10, forgetting the leading zeros.
