### Q46 ⭐⭐ 🟡 What are componendo and dividendo? If (x+y)/(x-y) = 7/3, find x:y.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); listed as a 2-star shortcut topic in prep material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the ratio shortcut that avoids cross-multiplying.
**Say this in the interview:** "Componendo-dividendo says if a/b = c/d then (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d). Here (x+y)/(x-y) = 7/3 means x/y = (7+3)/(7-3) = 10/4, so x:y = 5:2."
**Answer:** **x:y = 5:2**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Rule: if (x+y)/(x-y) = p/q, then x/y = (p+q)/(p-q).
2. Here p = 7, q = 3, so x/y = (7+3)/(7-3) = 10/4.
3. 10/4 reduces to 5/2, so x:y = 5:2.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Add the top and bottom of the given ratio for x, subtract them for y: 7+3 = 10, 7-3 = 4, then halve to 5:2.
**Check:** Put x = 5, y = 2: (5+2)/(5-2) = 7/3. Correct (also verified by script).
**Explain simply:**
- Componendo: add 1 to both sides of a ratio (a/b = c/d gives (a+b)/b = (c+d)/d).
- Dividendo: subtract 1 from both sides (gives (a-b)/b = (c-d)/d).
- Dividing the componendo result by the dividendo result cancels b and d, giving (a+b)/(a-b) = (c+d)/(c-d).
- Worked mini-example: if (a+b)/(a-b) = 5/1, then a/b = 6/4 = 3:2.
**Likely follow-ups:** If (x+y)/(x-y) = 9/5, find x:y. Answer: (9+5)/(9-5) = 14/4 = 7:2.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting in the wrong order (getting 4:10 flipped), or forgetting to reduce 10:4 to 5:2.

### Q47 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A vehicle travels a distance at 60 km/h and returns the same distance at 40 km/h. Why is the average speed not 50 km/h, and what is it?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a classic speed-average trap.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you use total distance over total time, not the simple average of speeds.
**Say this in the interview:** "Average speed is total distance divided by total time. The slower leg takes more time, so it counts more. For equal distances it is 2ab/(a+b) = 2 x 60 x 40 / 100 = 48 km/h."
**Answer:** **48 km/h**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Take the one-way distance as 120 km (any common multiple works).
2. Time going: 120 / 60 = 2 hours. Time returning: 120 / 40 = 3 hours.
3. Total distance = 240 km; total time = 5 hours.
4. Average speed = 240 / 5 = 48 km/h.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For equal distances use 2ab/(a+b) = 4800/100 = 48.
**Check:** At 50 km/h the trip would take 240/50 = 4.8 hours, but the real trip takes 5 hours, so 50 is too high.
**Explain simply:**
- Average speed = total distance / total time, never the plain mean of speeds.
- More time is spent at the slow speed, so the answer sits closer to 40 than to 60.
- The 2ab/(a+b) formula (the harmonic mean, a special average for rates) holds only when the distances are equal.
- Mini-example: 30 and 60 km/h over equal distances gives 2 x 30 x 60 / 90 = 40 km/h.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same trip at 50 and 75 km/h? 2 x 50 x 75 / 125 = 60 km/h.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking 50 km/h (the plain average), which is the option the test setter places first.

### Q48 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, and the total of their monthly salaries is Rs 1,40,000. What is each person's salary?

**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can join two ratios by making the common term (B) equal.
**Say this in the interview:** "B is 3 in the first ratio and 4 in the second, so I make B equal to 12 in both. That gives A:B:C = 8:12:15, 35 parts, so one part is Rs 4,000."

1. A:B = 2:3. Multiply by 4: 8:12.
2. B:C = 4:5. Multiply by 3: 12:15.

4. Total parts = 8 + 12 + 15 = 35.
5. One part = 1,40,000 / 35 = Rs 4,000.
6. A = 8 x 4,000 = 32,000; B = 12 x 4,000 = 48,000; C = 15 x 4,000 = 60,000.

- Worked mini-example: A:B = 1:2 and B:C = 3:4 gives B = 6, so A:B:C = 3:6:8.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same ratios, total Rs 70,000? One part = 2,000, so A = 16,000, B = 24,000, C = 30,000.

1. Alloy 1 (3:2): copper = 3/5, zinc = 2/5 of its weight.
2. Alloy 2 (1:4): copper = 1/5, zinc = 4/5 of its weight.

### Q52 ⭐⭐ 🔴 A and B earn incomes in the ratio 4:5 and their expenditures are in the ratio 3:4. If each saves Rs 2,000, what are their incomes?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a classic two-equation ratio problem.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can set up two unknowns from income minus expenditure equals savings.
**Say this in the interview:** "Let incomes be 4k and 5k, expenditures 3m and 4m. Then 4k - 3m = 2,000 and 5k - 4m = 2,000. Solving gives k = 2,000, so incomes are Rs 8,000 and Rs 10,000."
**Answer:** **A = Rs 8,000 and B = Rs 10,000**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Incomes 4k and 5k; expenditures 3m and 4m.
2. A: 4k - 3m = 2,000. B: 5k - 4m = 2,000.
3. Multiply the first by 4: 16k - 12m = 8,000. Multiply the second by 3: 15k - 12m = 6,000.
4. Subtract: k = 2,000.
5. Put back: 4 x 2,000 - 3m = 2,000, so 3m = 6,000 and m = 2,000.
6. Incomes: A = 4 x 2,000 = 8,000; B = 5 x 2,000 = 10,000.
**Check:** Expenditures 6,000 and 8,000 (ratio 3:4); savings 8,000 - 6,000 = 2,000 and 10,000 - 8,000 = 2,000. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Income minus expenditure equals savings, so each person gives one equation.
- Use different letters (k and m) because the "parts" of income and expenditure are different sizes.
- Eliminating m (making its coefficient equal) leaves one unknown.
**Likely follow-ups:** If each saves Rs 3,000, incomes scale by 1.5: Rs 12,000 and Rs 15,000.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using the same letter for both ratios, which gives a wrong or impossible answer.

### Q54 ⭐⭐ 🔴 How many kg of rice costing Rs 30 per kg must be mixed with 20 kg of rice costing Rs 45 per kg so that the mixture costs Rs 38 per kg?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); alligation questions are tagged to IT-services tests on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can use alligation (the cross-difference method) quickly.
**Say this in the interview:** "The mean price is 38. The dearer rice is 45 - 38 = 7 above it and the cheaper rice is 38 - 30 = 8 below it, so crossing over, cheap : dear = 7 : 8. For 20 kg dear, cheap = 20 x 7/8 = 17.5 kg."
**Answer:** **17.5 kg**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Cheap rice Rs 30, dear rice Rs 45, mean Rs 38.
2. Cheap rice is 38 - 30 = 8 below the mean; dear rice is 45 - 38 = 7 above.
3. Quantities are in the opposite ratio: cheap : dear = 7 : 8.
4. Dear = 20 kg is 8 parts, so one part = 2.5 kg.
5. Cheap = 7 x 2.5 = 17.5 kg.
**Check:** Cost = 17.5 x 30 + 20 x 45 = 525 + 900 = 1,425 for 37.5 kg; 1,425 / 37.5 = 38. Verified.
**Explain simply:**
- Alligation: the distance of each price from the mean, crossed over, gives the quantity ratio.
- The cheap rice has a bigger gap (8), so you need less of it relative to the gap on the other side.
- Equivalent check: total gap below must equal total gap above (17.5 x 8 = 140 = 20 x 7).
**Likely follow-ups:** Mean Rs 40? Gaps 10 and 5, ratio 5:10 = 1:2, so cheap = 10 kg.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Not crossing the differences (getting 8:7, hence 22.86 kg).

**Likely follow-ups:** If B stayed 10 months? 9,00,000 : 7,20,000 = 5:4, B gets Rs 1,00,000.

### Q64 ⭐ 🔴 A vessel holds 60 litres of milk. 12 litres are removed and replaced with water, and this is repeated once more. What is the final ratio of milk to water?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); milk-and-water replacement examples appear in Accenture-tagged prep guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you remember the second removal takes out some water too.
**Say this in the interview:** "Each round keeps 4/5 of the milk (48 out of 60). After two rounds milk = 60 x (4/5)^2 = 38.4 litres, water = 21.6, ratio 16:9."
**Answer:** **Milk : water = 16:9 (38.4 L milk, 21.6 L water)**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Fraction removed each time = 12 / 60 = 1/5, so 4/5 of the milk stays.
2. After round 1: milk = 60 - 12 = 48 L, water = 12 L.
3. Round 2: the 12 L removed is a mix, 4/5 milk (9.6 L) and 1/5 water (2.4 L).
4. Milk = 48 - 9.6 = 38.4 L.
5. Water = 12 - 2.4 + 12 = 21.6 L.
6. Ratio 38.4 : 21.6 = 384 : 216 = 16:9.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Milk left = 60 x (4/5) x (4/5) = 38.4 L; water is the remainder 60 - 38.4 = 21.6 L.
**Check:** 38.4 + 21.6 = 60 L; 38.4 / 21.6 = 1.777..., which is 16/9. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- After the first swap the vessel is no longer pure milk, so the second 12 L carries some water out.
- Each round multiplies the milk by (1 - removed/total).
- Mini-example: 10 L, replace 5 L once leaves 5 L milk; twice leaves 2.5 L.
**Likely follow-ups:** Only one round? 48:12 = 4:1.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Taking out 12 L of pure milk twice (36 L left, ratio 3:2).

### Q65 ⭐ 🔴 A cask holds 80 litres of wine. 8 litres are withdrawn and replaced with water, and this is repeated three times in all. How much wine remains?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); replacement (repeated dilution) problems are listed in company-tagged prep banks (unverified).

**Say this in the interview:** "Each time 1/10 of the wine goes out, so 9/10 stays. After three rounds, wine = 80 x (9/10)^3 = 58.32 litres."

1. Fraction removed each time = 8 / 80 = 1/10, so 9/10 remains.
2. After round 1: 80 x 0.9 = 72 L.
3. After round 2: 72 x 0.9 = 64.8 L.
4. After round 3: 64.8 x 0.9 = 58.32 L.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Formula: remaining = original x (1 - removed/total)^n = 80 x 0.729 = 58.32.
**Check:** 80 x 729 / 1000 = 58.32. Verified. Water = 80 - 58.32 = 21.68 L.
**Explain simply:**
- Each round the well-mixed cask loses the same fraction of whatever is in it, so wine shrinks by a constant factor.
- The formula works for any number of rounds n.
- Mini-example: 100 L, replace 20 L twice leaves 100 x 0.8 x 0.8 = 64 L.
**Likely follow-ups:** After 2 rounds? 80 x 0.81 = 64.8 L.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting 8 x 3 = 24, giving 56 L.

### Q66 ⭐ 🔴 A and B start a business with investments in the ratio 3:5 (Rs 30,000 and Rs 50,000). After 6 months A withdraws Rs 10,000. If the annual profit is Rs 1,50,000, how much does each get?

### Q67 ⭐ 🔴 The average of 50 numbers is 38. If two numbers, 45 and 55, are discarded, what is the average of the remaining numbers?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a "discard values" average problem.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you adjust both the total and the count.
**Say this in the interview:** "Total is 50 x 38 = 1,900. Remove 45 + 55 = 100 to get 1,800 across 48 numbers: 37.5."
**Answer:** **37.5**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total of 50 numbers = 50 x 38 = 1,900.
2. Discarded sum = 45 + 55 = 100.
3. Remaining total = 1,900 - 100 = 1,800.
4. Remaining count = 50 - 2 = 48.
5. New average = 1,800 / 48 = 37.5.
**Check:** 37.5 x 48 = 1,800. Verified.
**Explain simply:**
- Rebuild the total from the average, change it, then divide by the new count.
- The discarded numbers average 50, above 38, so removing them lowers the average a little.
- Mini-example: average 10 of 5 numbers; drop 20 and 10 gives (50 - 30)/3 = 6.67.
**Likely follow-ups:** Discard only 55? (1,900 - 55)/49 = 37.65.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing 1,800 by 50 (36) or forgetting to reduce the count.
