### Q21 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the 6th letter to the right of the 12th letter from the right end of the English alphabet?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); reasoning topic listed in TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture and Cognizant prep pages (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can convert "from the right" to "from the left" with the 27 rule.
**Say this in the interview:** "The 12th from the right is the 15th from the left, which is O. Six letters to the right of O is U."
**Answer:** **U**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. The alphabet has 26 letters. Position from left = 26 + 1 - position from right = 27 - 12 = 15.
2. The 15th letter is O.
3. "To the right" means towards Z (the alphabet is written A to Z, left to right). 15 + 6 = 21.
4. The 21st letter is U.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Left position + right position always equals 27, so "12th from the right" is 15th from the left. Then just add 6.
**Check:** U is 21st from the left, so 27 - 21 = 6th from the right end; O is 12th from the right end, and 12 - 6 = 6 places apart. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Counting from one end of a line of 26 and counting from the other end always add up to 27, because the person is counted in both.
- "Right" in an alphabet question means the Z side.
**Likely follow-ups:** What is the 5th letter to the left of the 9th from the left? 9 - 5 = 4th letter, D.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 26 - 12 = 14 (gives N, then T). The correct conversion is 27 - 12 = 15. Also adding the 6 in the wrong direction.

### Q22 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 In a row, Rita is 14th from the left and Sunil is 18th from the right. When they interchange positions, Rita becomes 22nd from the left. How many people are in the row, and how many people stand between Rita and Sunil?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); reasoning topic listed in TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture and Cognizant prep pages (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Position-swap logic: the swap reveals the other person's original position.
**Say this in the interview:** "Rita moves to 22nd from the left, so Sunil was 22nd from the left. With Sunil 18th from the right, the row has 22 + 18 - 1 = 39 people. Between them are 7."
**Answer:** **39 people in the row; 7 people stand between Rita and Sunil.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. After the swap Rita sits where Sunil sat. So Sunil's original place is 22nd from the left.
2. Sunil is 18th from the right (his original place). Total = 22 + 18 - 1 = 39 (the person is counted from both ends, so subtract 1).
3. Rita is 14th from the left, Sunil 22nd from the left. People between = 22 - 14 - 1 = 7.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Total = left position + right position - 1 for the same seat; between = difference of positions - 1.
**Check:** Sunil is 18th from the right = 39 - 18 + 1 = 22nd from the left, matches. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Rita's new seat is Sunil's old seat, so the swap gives you Sunil's position from the left for free.
- Two counts from opposite ends of the same person overlap by that one person, hence the minus 1.
**Likely follow-ups:** If Rita were 10th from the left and Sunil 20th from the left in a row of 40, how many are between? 20 - 10 - 1 = 9.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding 22 + 18 without subtracting 1 (gives 40). Forgetting the minus 1 when counting people between.

### Q23 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Six friends A, B, C, D, E and F sit around a circular table facing the centre (so a person's right-hand side is the anticlockwise direction). A sits opposite D. ### Q24 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Statements: All pens are books. Some books are bags. No bag is a box. Conclusions: I. Some books are not boxes. II. Some pens are bags. III. No pen is a box. Which conclusion(s) follow?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); reasoning topic listed in TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture and Cognizant prep pages (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Syllogism: accept only what is true in every possible picture (Venn diagram thinking).
**Say this in the interview:** "Only conclusion I follows. Some books are bags, and no bag is a box, so those books are not boxes. The pen-bag link and the pen-box link are not forced."
**Answer:** **Only I follows.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. All pens are books: the Pen circle sits fully inside Book.
2. Some books are bags: Book and Bag overlap.
3. No bag is a box: Bag and Box are completely apart.
4. Conclusion I (Some books are not boxes): the books that are bags are not boxes. These books exist, so some books are not boxes. Follows.
5. Conclusion II (Some pens are bags): the bag-books might be outside the Pen circle. Not forced. Does not follow.
6. Conclusion III (No pen is a box): a pen could be a box-book that is not a bag, since only Bag and Box are separated. Not forced. Does not follow.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** A conclusion follows only if you cannot draw any valid picture that breaks it. Draw one picture where it fails: Pen overlapping Box (outside Bag) breaks III; Pen away from Bag breaks II.
**Check:** Tested against all possible set-combinations by script: only I holds in every case.
**Explain simply:**
- "Some" means at least one. It does not say which group.
- "Follows" means true in every possible picture, not just in the picture you drew first.
**Likely follow-ups:** If the statement were "All books are bags", would II follow? Yes: pens are books, books are bags, so some pens are bags (given pens exist).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Choosing III because pens "look" far from boxes. Two groups both linked to a third need not be linked to each other.

### Q25 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Statements: No clerk is a manager. All managers are graduates. Conclusions: I. No clerk is a graduate. II. Some graduates are not clerks. Assuming every named group has at least one member, which conclusion(s) follow?

### Q26 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 In a code language, "good sales team" is written "mo ka re", "team is good" is written "ka pi mo", and "sales team lead" is written "re ka du". What is the code for "good"?

### Q27 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A man starts from point P, walks 6 km north, turns east and walks 8 km, turns south and walks 6 km, then turns west and walks 3 km. How far is he from P and in which direction?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); direction sense is listed in Wipro and similar prep pages (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Coordinate thinking: net east-west and north-south displacement, then straight-line distance.

**Answer:** **5 km, due East of P.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Treat P as (0, 0) with east as x and north as y.
2. North 6: (0, 6). East 8: (8, 6). South 6: (8, 0). West 3: (5, 0).
3. He ends at (5, 0): no north-south shift, 5 km east.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Add opposite moves: north 6 minus south 6 = 0; east 8 minus west 3 = 5.
**Check:** Verified by script (coordinates add to (5, 0)).
**Explain simply:**
- Only the net movement in each direction matters, not the route.
- If both net moves are non-zero, use Pythagoras for the straight-line distance (a 3-4-5 triangle is common).
**Likely follow-ups:** If the last move were west 11, where would he end? (-3, 0): 3 km west of P.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding all distances (23 km) or giving the distance walked rather than the distance from P.

### Q28 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 In a relation code, A + B means A is the mother of B, A - B means A is the brother of B, and A x B means A is the father of B. What does P x Q - R mean for P and R?

2. All positions go up by 2 in turn: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 and next 19, 21, 23.
3. 19 = S, 21 = U, 23 = W. Answer SUW.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Skip a letter each time: A_C_E, then G (next), I, K... It is simply every other letter of the alphabet, taken three at a time.
**Check:** Each group's first letter jumps by 6 (A, G, M, S): consistent.
**Explain simply:**
- Replace letters by numbers 1 to 26; the pattern becomes an ordinary number series.
**Likely follow-ups:** What comes after SUW? Y, then A, C (wrapping around) -> YAC.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Stepping by one letter instead of two.

### Q38 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What formula gives the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at H hours and M minutes?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); clock angles appear in aptitude prep lists (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know both hand speeds and can explain the formula, not just recite it.
**Say this in the interview:** "Angle = absolute value of 30 times H minus 5.5 times M. If it is more than 180, subtract from 360."
**Answer:** **Angle = |30H - 5.5M| degrees, and if the result is above 180 take 360 minus it.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. The minute hand moves 360 degrees in 60 minutes: 6 degrees per minute.
2. The hour hand moves 30 degrees per hour, so 0.5 degrees per minute.
3. At H hours M minutes: hour hand = 30H + 0.5M; minute hand = 6M.
4. Gap = (30H + 0.5M) - 6M = 30H - 5.5M; take the absolute value.
5. Worked example 3:20: |90 - 110| = 20 degrees. Another: 9:30: |270 - 165| = 105 degrees. 7:45: |210 - 247.5| = 37.5 degrees.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Imagine the hour hand sits at H x 30, then slide it forward by half a degree per minute.
**Check:** At 3:00 the hands are 90 degrees apart: |90 - 0| = 90. Correct. Verified by script for the examples.
**Explain simply:**
- The minute hand is 12 times faster than the hour hand; it gains 5.5 degrees on it every minute.
- Use H on a 12-hour scale (13:00 is H = 1).
**Likely follow-ups:** Angle at 4:10? |120 - 55| = 65 degrees. When are the hands together between 3 and 4? 16 4/11 minutes past 3.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting the hour hand moves with the minutes, or reporting a reflex angle above 180.

### Q39 ⭐⭐ 🟢 How many odd days are there in 100 years, and what does an odd day mean?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); calendar odd days are a 2-star topic in prep lists (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Calendar basics: odd days as the leftover after full weeks.
**Say this in the interview:** "An odd day is a day left over after dividing the days by 7. 100 years has 5 odd days."
**Answer:** **5 odd days. An odd day is the remainder when the number of days is divided by 7.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 100 years contain 24 leap years (1900 is not a leap year, so the century has 24, not 25) and 76 ordinary years.
2. Days = 76 x 365 + 24 x 366 = 27,740 + 8,784 = 36,524.
3. 36,524 / 7 = 5217 weeks and 5 days remaining (7 x 5217 = 36,519).
4. So 5 odd days.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** An ordinary year has 1 odd day (365 = 52 weeks + 1), a leap year 2. 76 x 1 + 24 x 2 = 124; 124 mod 7 = 5.
**Check:** Verified by script. The standard rule: 100 years = 5, 200 = 3, 300 = 1, 400 = 0 odd days.
**Explain simply:**
- Weeks repeat every 7 days, so only the leftover days shift the weekday.
- If today is Monday, after 5 odd days it is Saturday.
**Likely follow-ups:** How many odd days in 400 years? 0 (400 years is an exact number of weeks, 20,871 weeks).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Counting 25 leap years in 100 years (gives 6), forgetting the century year is not leap.

### Q40 ⭐⭐ 🟢 Which of the years 1900, 2000, 2024 and 2100 are leap years, and what is the rule?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); calendar rules are a 2-star topic in prep lists (prep-site claim, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** The exact leap-year rule including the century exception.
**Say this in the interview:** "A year is a leap year if divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400. So 2000 and 2024 are leap; 1900 and 2100 are not."
**Answer:** **Leap years: 2000 and 2024. Not leap: 1900 and 2100.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Rule: divisible by 4 is a leap year. Century years (ending 00) are leap only if divisible by 400.
2. 1900: ends in 00, 1900 / 400 = 4.75: not leap.
3. 2000: 2000 / 400 = 5: leap.
4. 2024: 2024 / 4 = 506, not a century: leap.
5. 2100: 2100 / 400 = 5.25: not leap.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For centuries, drop the two zeros and check divisibility by 4 (19 no, 20 yes, 21 no).
**Check:** Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- A real year is slightly under 365.25 days, so the extra day every 4 years overcorrects; dropping 3 leap days every 400 years fixes it.
- A leap year has 366 days (February has 29).
**Likely follow-ups:** Is 2400 leap? Yes (24 is divisible by 4).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating 1900 or 2100 as leap because they are divisible by 4.
