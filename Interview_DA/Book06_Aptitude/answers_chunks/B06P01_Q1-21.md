### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 How do you calculate percentage increase and percentage decrease between two numbers, and which value goes in the denominator?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss among the most frequent quant topics at service-company aptitude gates (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you divide by the starting value and can say whether the change is up or down.
**Say this in the interview:** "Change divided by the original value, times 100. The denominator is always the starting number, whichever direction it moves."
**Answer:** **% change = (New - Old) / Old x 100; the denominator is the OLD (starting) value.** Positive means increase, negative means decrease.
**Explain simply:**
- Step 1: find the change, new minus old. Step 2: divide by the old value, because the question is "how big is the move compared with where we began". Step 3: multiply by 100.
- Percentage increase = (increase / old) x 100. Percentage decrease = (decrease / old) x 100. In both, "old" is the earlier figure.
- Worked example: headcount 200 falls to 150. Change = 50, base = 200, 50/200 = 0.25, so a 25% decrease. Going back from 150 to 200 is a 33.3% increase (50/150), because the base is now 150. The two are different, which is why the base matters.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if the old value is zero?* The percentage is undefined; quote the absolute change. 2) *80 to 100, then 100 to 80?* Up 25%, down 20%.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by the new value; dividing by the bigger number; forgetting that "A is x% more than B" uses B as the base.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 On what base are profit percentage, loss percentage and discount percentage each calculated (cost price or marked price)?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss among the most frequent quant topics at service-company aptitude gates (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Knowing which base goes with which term.
**Say this in the interview:** "Profit and loss percentages are on cost price; discount percentage is on marked price, unless the question says otherwise."
**Answer:** **Profit % and loss % use cost price (CP). Discount % uses marked price (MP).**
**Explain simply:**
- Profit % = profit / CP x 100; Loss % = loss / CP x 100. The cost price is what the seller started with, so it is the base for how well or badly the seller did.
- Discount % = discount / MP x 100, because the discount is a cut from the printed (marked) price.
- Worked example: CP Rs 200, MP Rs 300, SP Rs 240. Profit = 40, so 40/200 = 20% profit. Discount = 60, so 60/300 = 20% discount. Same 20%, different bases.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if the question says "profit on selling price"?* Then divide by SP (that is margin, see Q25). 2) *Is a discount on CP ever used?* Only if the question states it.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Calculating discount on cost price, or profit on selling price, because the SP is the number in front of you.

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the difference between cost price, marked price, selling price and discount, and how are they linked by formulas?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss among the most frequent quant topics at service-company aptitude gates (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Clear vocabulary and the formula chain.
**Say this in the interview:** "Cost price is what the shop paid, marked price is the label, discount is the cut from the label, and selling price is what the customer actually pays."
**Answer:** **SP = MP - Discount; Profit = SP - CP (or Loss = CP - SP); Discount % = Discount / MP x 100; Profit % = Profit / CP x 100.**
**Explain simply:**
- Cost price (CP): the price the seller paid to buy or make the item.
- Marked price (MP, also list price): the price printed on the item.
- Discount: the reduction given off MP. Selling price (SP): the price the customer finally pays.
- Chain: CP -> (add markup) -> MP -> (give discount) -> SP -> (compare with CP) -> profit or loss.
- Worked example: CP 800, MP 1,000, discount 10% = 100, so SP = 900. Profit = 900 - 800 = 100, which is 100/800 = 12.5% on cost.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can SP be above MP?* Not with a discount; that would be a surcharge. 2) *If MP = CP x 1.25 and discount is 20%?* SP = CP x 1.25 x 0.8 = CP, no profit, no loss.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Mixing MP with CP; treating MP as what the customer pays.

- Check: 0.8 x 0.9 = 0.72, so 1 - 0.72 = 0.28.

### Q18 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Two articles are each sold for Rs 1,200. One is sold at a 20% gain and the other at a 20% loss. What is the overall profit or loss, and what percentage of the total cost price is it?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss among the most frequent quant topics at service-company aptitude gates (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Calculating each cost price separately, and knowing that equal % gain and loss on equal SP always means a net loss.
**Say this in the interview:** "The gain article cost 1,000 and the loss article cost 1,500, so total cost is 2,500 against 2,400 received: a loss of Rs 100, which is 4%."
**Answer:** **Overall loss of Rs 100, which is 4% of the total cost price.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Article 1 (20% gain): SP = 1.2 x CP, so CP = 1,200 / 1.2 = 1,000.
2. Article 2 (20% loss): SP = 0.8 x CP, so CP = 1,200 / 0.8 = 1,500.

4. Loss = 2,500 - 2,400 = 100. Loss % = 100 / 2,500 x 100 = 4%.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** When equal SP gives x% gain and x% loss, the net loss is always x squared over 100 percent: 20 x 20 / 100 = 4%. Why: the loss article had a bigger cost, so the 20% loss is on a larger base than the 20% gain.
**Check:** Verified by script (exact fractions).
**Explain simply:**
- Gain and loss percentages are on cost price, and the two cost prices differ, so they do not cancel.
- A common wrong shortcut is "20% gain and 20% loss cancel, so no profit or loss".
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Each sold at Rs 1,200 with 10% gain and 10% loss?* Loss 1%. 2) *Same CP, 20% gain and 20% loss?* Then they do cancel (no net change).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Assuming break-even; taking 20% of 1,200 for both articles.
