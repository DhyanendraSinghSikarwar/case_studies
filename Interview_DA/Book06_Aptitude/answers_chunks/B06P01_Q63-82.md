### Q70 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Why is 8% of 25 equal to 25% of 8, and how can this save time in calculations?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss at TCS, Infosys, Wipro and Accenture-type gates (unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Understanding the symmetry of percentages and using it for mental maths.
**Say this in the interview:** x% of y equals y% of x, because both are x times y divided by 100. I flip to whichever is easier.
**Answer:** **Both equal 2, because x% of y = (x x y)/100 = y% of x**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 8% of 25 = (8 x 25) / 100 = 200 / 100 = 2.
2. 25% of 8 = (25 x 8) / 100 = 200 / 100 = 2.
3. Multiplication does not care about order, so the two always match.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Flip to the friendlier one: 4% of 75 is hard, 75% of 4 = 3 is instant; 16% of 50 = 50% of 16 = 8.
**Check:** 8% of 25 = 0.08 x 25 = 2 and 25% of 8 = 0.25 x 8 = 2. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- 'Percent' means 'per hundred': x% of y = x/100 x y.
- Because x/100 x y and y/100 x x contain the same product x x y, they are equal.
- Worked example: 12% of 50 headcount = 50% of 12 = 6 people.
**Likely follow-ups:**
- What is 36% of 25? 25% of 36 = 9.
- Does the trick work for 'percent more than'? No, only for 'percent of'.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Assuming symmetry holds for percentage change or increase (e.g. +x% then -x%), where it does not.

**Fast method (30 seconds):** Recognise 0.64 as 0.8 x 0.8 (8 x 8 = 64).
**Check:** 2,000 x 0.8 = 1,600; 1,600 x 0.8 = 1,280. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Successive discounts multiply, so the net effect is not 2d.
- The single equivalent discount is 36%, not 40%.
**Likely follow-ups:**
- Final price Rs 1,620 from 2,000? 0.81 = 0.9^2, so 10% twice.
- Two equal discounts total 51% off? 0.49 gives 30% each.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 18% (36% / 2 is wrong) or 36%, the net discount rather than each one.

### Q81 ⭐ 🔴 A shopkeeper sells sugar at Rs 40 per kg, which he bought at Rs 36 per kg, but his weighing machine shows 1 kg for only 800 g. What is his actual profit percentage, correct to 2 decimal places?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss at TCS, Infosys, Wipro and Accenture-type gates (unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Combining a price premium with a false weight, with two-decimal accuracy.
**Say this in the interview:** Each 'kg' costs him Rs 28.80 (800 g at 36/kg) and brings in Rs 40, so profit is 11.20/28.80 = 38.89%.
**Answer:** **38.89%**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Cost of the 800 g he actually gives = 0.8 x 36 = Rs 28.80.
2. Revenue = Rs 40 (price per 'kg').
3. Profit = 40 - 28.80 = Rs 11.20.
4. Profit % = 11.20 / 28.80 x 100 = 38.888...%, rounded to 38.89%.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Price factor 40/36 = 1.1111; weight factor 1,000/800 = 1.25; product = 1.3889, so 38.89%.
**Check:** Customer pays 40 for 800 g; cost of 800 g = 28.8; profit 11.2 on 28.8 = 38.89%. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Two gains: honest price markup (11.11%) and the 20% shortfall in weight (a 25% gain).
- They multiply: 1.1111 x 1.25 = 1.3889.
**Likely follow-ups:**
- Only the weight trick? 25%.
- If the weight was 900 g? 1.1111 x 1.1111 = 1.2346, so 23.46%.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by Rs 36 (11.11% + 25% = 36.11%) or using 20% in place of 25% for the weight gain.

### Q82 ⭐ 🔴 The price of a product is first increased by x% and then decreased by x%, resulting in a net 4% decrease from the original. Find x.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); prep sites list percentages and profit-loss at TCS, Infosys, Wipro and Accenture-type gates (unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Spotting that +x% then -x% equals a loss of x^2/100 percent.
**Say this in the interview:** The net change is minus x squared over 100, so x^2/100 = 4, giving x = 20.
**Answer:** **x = 20**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Take 100. After +x%: 100 + x.
2. After -x% on that: (100 + x)(100 - x)/100 = (10,000 - x^2)/100.
3. Net 4% decrease means result = 96, so 10,000 - x^2 = 9,600.
4. x^2 = 400, so x = 20 (the positive root).
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Memorise: +x% then -x% gives a loss of x^2/100 percent; set it to 4, so x = 20.
**Check:** x = 20: 1.2 x 0.8 = 0.96. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- The decrease is taken on a larger base, so the final value is always below the start.
- The negative root -20 is rejected because x is a percentage rise.
**Likely follow-ups:**
- Net 1% decrease? x = 10.
- Net 9% decrease? x = 30.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering x = 4 (assuming the changes cancel to the net figure) or forgetting to take the positive root.
