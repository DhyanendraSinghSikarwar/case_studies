### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 A can complete a job in 12 days and B in 15 days. Working together, how many days do they take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); "A and B together" is the standard opener in time-and-work lists on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you add daily rates, not days.
**Say this in the interview:** "A does 1/12 of the job a day and B does 1/15, so together they do 9/60 = 3/20 a day. One job takes 20/3 days."
**Answer:** **6 2/3 days (20/3 days, about 6.67 days)**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. A's one-day work = 1/12; B's one-day work = 1/15.
2. Together in one day = 1/12 + 1/15 = 5/60 + 4/60 = 9/60 = 3/20.
3. Days for the whole job = 1 divided by 3/20 = 20/3 days = 6 2/3 days.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Product over sum: (12 x 15)/(12 + 15) = 180/27 = 20/3. It works because adding the fractions 1/12 + 1/15 always gives (12+15)/(12x15).
**Check:** In 20/3 days A does (20/3)/12 = 5/9 and B does (20/3)/15 = 4/9; 5/9 + 4/9 = 1. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- A "rate" is the share of the job done in one day. Rates add when people work at the same time.
- Time for the whole job = 1 divided by the combined rate.
**Likely follow-ups:** If A takes 10 days and B 15 days? (10 x 15)/25 = 6 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Averaging the days ((12+15)/2 = 13.5) or adding them (27). Both are wrong; together must be faster than the faster person alone (under 12 days).

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 If A alone takes a days and B alone takes b days to finish a job, what is the formula for the time they take together, and why do you add their rates rather than their times?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a core concept item in time-and-work syllabi (Byju's concept notes, prep sites; unverified for any company).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you understand the idea behind the formula, not only the memorised shortcut.
**Say this in the interview:** "Together time = ab/(a+b). I add rates because people working at the same time each contribute part of the job every day, and contributions per day add up; days do not add because the two people are not working one after the other."
**Answer:** **Time together = ab/(a + b) days**
**Explain simply:**
- Rate = job done per day. A's rate = 1/a, B's rate = 1/b.
- Combined rate = 1/a + 1/b = (a + b)/(ab). Time = 1 divided by the combined rate = ab/(a + b).
- Rates add because they are "amount per day" and both are measured in the same unit (jobs per day). Times (days per job) cannot be added: two people sharing a job do not take longer than one.
- Sanity test: the answer is always smaller than the smaller of a and b.
**Worked mini-example:** Two HR coordinators take 6 and 3 hours to screen a batch of CVs. Rates 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 per hour, so together 2 hours; formula: (6 x 3)/(6 + 3) = 18/9 = 2.
**Likely follow-ups:** What if B undoes the work (an outlet pipe)? Subtract the rate: ab/(a - b). For 6 and 8 hours: 48/2 = 24 hours.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing (a + b)/2 or a + b. Also forgetting that the formula is only for two workers; for three, add three rates.

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Convert a delivery van's speed of 54 km/h into m/s.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); km/h to m/s is the first step of train problems listed on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Unit conversion with the 5/18 factor.
**Say this in the interview:** "Multiply km/h by 5/18 to get m/s: 54 x 5/18 = 15 m/s."
**Answer:** **15 m/s**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 1 km = 1,000 m, 1 hour = 3,600 seconds.
2. 54 km/h = 54 x 1,000 / 3,600 m/s.
3. = 54,000 / 3,600 = 15 m/s.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 1,000/3,600 simplifies to 5/18. Divide 54 by 18 = 3, then multiply by 5 = 15.
**Check:** Reverse: 15 x 18/5 = 54 km/h. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18. m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5.
- Speed numbers divisible by 18 (36, 54, 72, 90) give clean answers, which is why exams love them.
**Likely follow-ups:** 72 km/h in m/s? 72 x 5/18 = 20 m/s.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 18/5 instead of 5/18, or dividing by 3.6 and multiplying by it the wrong way round. A m/s figure bigger than the km/h figure is a red flag.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 An employee commutes 30 km at an average speed of 40 km/h. How many minutes does the trip take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Time = distance/speed, and converting hours to minutes.
**Say this in the interview:** "Time = 30/40 = 0.75 hours, which is 45 minutes."
**Answer:** **45 minutes**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Time = distance / speed = 30 / 40 hours.
2. 30/40 = 3/4 hour.
3. 3/4 x 60 = 45 minutes.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** At 40 km/h you cover 2 km every 3 minutes (40/60 km per minute = 2/3 km). 30 km / (2/3) = 45 minutes.
**Check:** 45 minutes = 0.75 h; 0.75 x 40 = 30 km. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- The triangle: distance = speed x time; time = distance / speed; speed = distance / time.
- The answer comes out in the unit of the speed (hours here); convert at the end.
**Likely follow-ups:** 50 km at 60 km/h? 5/6 h = 50 minutes.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Giving 0.75 as "75 minutes", or answering in hours when minutes are asked.

### Q5 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Pipe A fills a tank in 20 minutes and pipe B fills it in 30 minutes. If both are opened together, how long does the tank take to fill?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); pipes are treated as a time-and-work variant in Infosys-branded practice sets (prep sites, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Pipes are just workers: add the fill rates.
**Say this in the interview:** "Rates are 1/20 and 1/30 per minute, together 5/60 = 1/12, so 12 minutes."
**Answer:** **12 minutes**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. A fills 1/20 of the tank per minute; B fills 1/30.
2. Together: 1/20 + 1/30 = 3/60 + 2/60 = 5/60 = 1/12.
3. Time = 12 minutes.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** (20 x 30)/(20 + 30) = 600/50 = 12. Or LCM method: take the tank as 60 units; A gives 3 units/min, B gives 2; together 5; 60/5 = 12.
**Check:** In 12 minutes A fills 12/20 = 3/5 and B fills 12/30 = 2/5; total 1. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- A pipe filling a tank is the same maths as a worker doing a job.
- Fill rates add; the tank is "1 whole job".
**Likely follow-ups:** Pipes of 15 and 10 minutes? 150/25 = 6 minutes.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding times (50) or averaging them (25). Together must be faster than 20.

### Q6 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 A 150 m long train runs at 54 km/h. How many seconds does it take to cross a telegraph pole?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); train problems are reported for TCS NQT, Cognizant and Wipro tests on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** That for a pole (a point), the distance to cover is just the train's own length.
**Say this in the interview:** "Speed is 54 x 5/18 = 15 m/s. The train must cover its own length, 150 m, so 150/15 = 10 seconds."
**Answer:** **10 seconds**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Convert: 54 km/h = 54 x 5/18 = 15 m/s.
2. Distance to cover = length of train = 150 m (the pole has no length).
3. Time = 150 / 15 = 10 seconds.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 54 km/h = 15 m/s at once; 150/15 = 10.
**Check:** 15 m/s x 10 s = 150 m. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- "Crossing" means the whole train passes the object: from the engine reaching it to the last coach leaving it.
- For a point object (pole, signal, man standing), distance = train length only.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same train, 100 m platform? (150 + 100)/15 = 16 2/3 s.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Leaving the speed in km/h while the length is in metres.

### Q7 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 A can finish a job in 10 days, and B is twice as efficient as A. How many days does B alone take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); efficiency-ratio items appear on practice sites (PlacementPreparation; unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Efficiency and time are inversely related.
**Say this in the interview:** "Twice as efficient means half the time: 5 days."
**Answer:** **5 days**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. A's rate = 1/10 job per day.
2. B is twice as efficient, so B's rate = 2 x 1/10 = 1/5 per day.
3. Days = 1 / (1/5) = 5.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Efficiency ratio A:B = 1:2, so time ratio A:B = 2:1. A takes 10, so B takes 10/2 = 5.
**Check:** B does 1/5 x 5 = 1 job. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- More efficient = more work per day = fewer days for the same job.
- For the same job: time is inversely proportional to efficiency.
**Likely follow-ups:** B three times as efficient? 10/3 = 3 1/3 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 20 (multiplying instead of dividing).

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Data-entry operator A enters 30 records per minute and operator B enters 20 records per minute. Working together, how many minutes do they need to enter 1,500 records?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the data-entry framing is a gap-fill added for this book, not seen in a source.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Rates already given as quantities per minute; add and divide.
**Say this in the interview:** "Together they enter 50 records a minute, so 1,500/50 = 30 minutes."
**Answer:** **30 minutes**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Combined rate = 30 + 20 = 50 records per minute.
2. Time = total work / rate = 1,500 / 50.
3. = 30 minutes.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 1,500/50 = 150/5 = 30.
**Check:** 30 x 30 = 900 and 20 x 30 = 600; 900 + 600 = 1,500. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- When the work is a count (records) and rates are counts per minute, you do not need fractions: time = work / combined rate.
**Likely follow-ups:** If B takes a 10-minute break? A alone does 300 in those 10 minutes; remaining 1,200 / 50 = 24 minutes, so 34 minutes in all.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Computing only one operator's rate, or dividing 1,500 by 30 and 20 and adding.

### Q9 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 12 clerks process 600 forms in 10 days. How many days would 20 clerks take to process the same 600 forms, assuming equal efficiency?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the clerks and forms wording is a gap-fill added for this book.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Man-days: more workers, fewer days (inverse proportion).
**Say this in the interview:** "Total work is 12 x 10 = 120 clerk-days. With 20 clerks that is 120/20 = 6 days."
**Answer:** **6 days**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Work in "clerk-days" = 12 x 10 = 120.
2. The same work with 20 clerks: days = 120 / 20.
3. = 6 days.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Workers x days stays constant: 12 x 10 = 20 x d, d = 6.
**Check:** 20 x 6 = 120 = 12 x 10. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- A man-day (here clerk-day) is one person working one day. A job has a fixed number of them.
- More people share the same fixed total, so each needs fewer days.
**Likely follow-ups:** 15 clerks? 120/15 = 8 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** The 600 forms is a distraction; since the forms are the same, you do not need it. Do not treat this as direct proportion (which would give 16.7).

### Q10 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 8 men can finish a job in 15 days. How many men are needed to finish the same job in 10 days?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the classic man-days item in prep lists (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Inverse proportion between men and days.
**Say this in the interview:** "The job is 8 x 15 = 120 man-days. In 10 days I need 120/10 = 12 men."
**Answer:** **12 men**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total work = 8 x 15 = 120 man-days.
2. Men needed = 120 / 10.
3. = 12 men.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Days dropped from 15 to 10 (ratio 2/3), so men must rise by 3/2: 8 x 3/2 = 12.
**Check:** 12 x 10 = 120. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Finishing in fewer days needs more workers, in exact inverse ratio.
- Extra men needed here: 12 - 8 = 4, in case the question asks "how many more".
**Likely follow-ups:** In 20 days? 120/20 = 6 men.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 4 (the additional men) when the total is asked, or 5.33 (direct proportion).

### Q11 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A car travels from home to office at 60 km/h and returns along the same road at 40 km/h. What is the average speed for the whole round trip?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a return-trip average-speed item style appears in a GfG Wipro mock question (practice site, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** That average speed is total distance over total time, not the simple average.
**Say this in the interview:** "Equal distances means the harmonic mean: 2 x 60 x 40/(60 + 40) = 48 km/h. The simple average 50 is the trap."
**Answer:** **48 km/h**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Pick a distance that suits both speeds: say 120 km each way (LCM of 60 and 40).
2. Time going = 120/60 = 2 h; time returning = 120/40 = 3 h; total 5 h.
3. Total distance = 240 km.
4. Average speed = 240/5 = 48 km/h.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For equal distances: 2xy/(x + y) = 2 x 60 x 40/100 = 48. It works because average = total distance/total time, and the distance cancels out.
**Check:** The car spends more time at the slow speed, so the answer must sit below 50 and closer to 40: 48 does. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Average speed = total distance / total time, always.
- You spend longer at the slower speed, so it counts for more in the average.
**Likely follow-ups:** 30 km/h up and 60 km/h back? 2 x 30 x 60/90 = 40 km/h.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking 50 km/h (arithmetic mean). That is always listed as an option.

### Q12 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A and B together finish a job in 8 days. A alone can finish it in 12 days. How many days does B alone take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Subtracting a known rate from the combined rate.
**Say this in the interview:** "B's rate = 1/8 - 1/12 = 1/24, so B takes 24 days."
**Answer:** **24 days**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Together: 1/8 of the job a day. A alone: 1/12 a day.
2. B's rate = 1/8 - 1/12 = 3/24 - 2/24 = 1/24.
3. B takes 24 days.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** LCM method: job = 24 units. Together 3 units/day, A 2 units/day, so B 1 unit/day; 24/1 = 24 days.
**Check:** 1/12 + 1/24 = 3/24 = 1/8 which is 8 days together. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Combined rate = A's rate + B's rate, so B's rate = combined minus A's.
- Convert back to days by taking 1 over the rate.
**Likely follow-ups:** Together 6 days, A alone 10? 1/6 - 1/10 = 1/15, so 15 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting days (8 - 12 is negative; 12 - 8 = 4 is wrong).

### Q13 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A, B and C can finish a job alone in 10, 15 and 30 days respectively. How many days do they take working together?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); three-worker combinations are standard in prep lists (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Adding three rates, preferably with the LCM method.
**Say this in the interview:** "Take the job as 30 units: A does 3, B does 2, C does 1 a day, 6 together, so 5 days."
**Answer:** **5 days**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. LCM of 10, 15, 30 = 30, so let the job be 30 units.
2. A: 30/10 = 3 units/day; B: 30/15 = 2; C: 30/30 = 1.
3. Together: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 units/day.
4. Days = 30/6 = 5.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The LCM trick above. Fraction form: 1/10 + 1/15 + 1/30 = (3 + 2 + 1)/30 = 6/30 = 1/5.
**Check:** In 5 days: A 1/2, B 1/3, C 1/6; 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Choosing the job size as the LCM makes every daily rate a whole number.
- The units are imaginary; only the ratio matters.
**Likely follow-ups:** If C leaves? A and B: 5 units/day, so 6 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 1/10 + 1/15 + 1/30 and then forgetting to invert.

### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A can do a job in 20 days and B in 30 days. They start together, but A leaves after 4 days and B finishes the rest alone. In how many days from the start is the job completed?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the "one worker leaves" pattern is a listed variant (Byju's concept notes; unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Splitting the work into phases and tracking the remaining fraction.
**Say this in the interview:** "Job = 60 units. Together they do 5 a day for 4 days = 20 units. 40 units left at 2 a day for B = 20 days. Total 24 days from the start."
**Answer:** **24 days**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Job = LCM(20, 30) = 60 units. A does 3 units/day, B does 2.
2. Together 5 units/day for 4 days = 20 units done.
3. Remaining = 60 - 20 = 40 units.
4. B alone at 2 units/day: 40/2 = 20 days.
5. Total = 4 + 20 = 24 days.
**Check:** A works 4 days: 12 units; B works 24 days: 48 units; 12 + 48 = 60. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Phase 1 (both working) and phase 2 (B alone) each use their own rate.
- Ask "from the start", so add the 4 days back; "after A leaves" would give 20.
**Likely follow-ups:** A leaves after 6 days? Done 30; left 30; B takes 15; total 21 days.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 20 (the days B works alone), forgetting the first 4 days.

### Q15 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A 120 m long train travelling at 72 km/h crosses a 280 m long platform. How many seconds does it take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the platform length trap is described in Careers360 and Naukri Code360 trains material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Distance = train + platform length.
**Say this in the interview:** "72 km/h is 20 m/s. Distance is 120 + 280 = 400 m, so 400/20 = 20 seconds."
**Answer:** **20 seconds**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Speed = 72 x 5/18 = 20 m/s.
2. Distance to clear the platform = 120 + 280 = 400 m.
3. Time = 400/20 = 20 seconds.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 72 km/h = 20 m/s at once (72/18 x 5). 400/20 = 20.
**Check:** 20 m/s x 20 s = 400 m. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- The train starts crossing when its front reaches the platform edge and finishes when its tail leaves the other edge. The front has travelled the platform plus the train's own length.
**Likely follow-ups:** Speed if it took 25 s? 400/25 = 16 m/s = 57.6 km/h.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using only 280 m (forgetting the train's length) giving 14 s.

### Q16 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Two trains of lengths 100 m and 140 m run on parallel tracks in opposite directions at 60 km/h and 48 km/h. How many seconds do they take to cross each other completely?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); opposite-direction crossing is a standard train pattern on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Relative speed = sum of speeds; distance = sum of lengths.
**Say this in the interview:** "Closing speed is 60 + 48 = 108 km/h = 30 m/s. Distance is 240 m, so 8 seconds."
**Answer:** **8 seconds**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Opposite directions: relative speed = 60 + 48 = 108 km/h.
2. In m/s: 108 x 5/18 = 30 m/s.
3. Distance = 100 + 140 = 240 m.
4. Time = 240/30 = 8 seconds.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 108 km/h is a neat 30 m/s; 240/30 = 8.
**Check:** In 8 s the trains close 30 x 8 = 240 m, exactly the two lengths. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Opposite directions: the gap between them closes at the sum of the speeds.
- "Cross completely" means front meets front until tail clears tail, i.e. both lengths.
**Likely follow-ups:** Same direction? Relative 12 km/h = 10/3 m/s, 240/(10/3) = 72 s.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting the speeds, or using only one train's length.

### Q17 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A boat's speed in still water is 12 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h. How long does it take to go 45 km downstream and come back the same 45 km upstream?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); boats and streams are a listed pattern in Naukri Code360 and Careers360 material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Downstream = boat + stream; upstream = boat - stream.
**Say this in the interview:** "Downstream 15 km/h gives 3 hours; upstream 9 km/h gives 5 hours; total 8 hours."
**Answer:** **8 hours**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Downstream speed = 12 + 3 = 15 km/h; time = 45/15 = 3 h.
2. Upstream speed = 12 - 3 = 9 km/h; time = 45/9 = 5 h.
3. Total = 3 + 5 = 8 hours.
**Check:** Round-trip distance 90 km over 8 h means average speed 11.25 km/h, below 12 as expected (harmonic effect). Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Stream helps going downstream and hinders going upstream.
- Still-water speed = (down + up)/2; stream speed = (down - up)/2.
**Likely follow-ups:** Stream 4 km/h? Down 16 (2.8125 h), up 8 (5.625 h); total 8.4375 h.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using 12 km/h for both legs (7.5 h), or averaging speeds.

### Q18 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A tank is filled by pipe A in 6 hours and emptied by outlet pipe B in 8 hours. If both are opened together on an empty tank, how many hours does it take to fill?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); outlet as "negative work" is described in GfG and Careers360 pipes material (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Subtract the outlet's rate from the inlet's rate.
**Say this in the interview:** "Net rate = 1/6 - 1/8 = 1/24, so 24 hours."
**Answer:** **24 hours**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Inlet A: +1/6 of the tank per hour. Outlet B: -1/8 per hour.
2. Net = 1/6 - 1/8 = 4/24 - 3/24 = 1/24 per hour.
3. Time = 24 hours.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Take the tank as 24 litres: A adds 4 per hour, B removes 3, net +1 per hour, so 24 hours. Or ab/(b - a) = 48/2 = 24.
**Check:** In 24 h A fills 4 tanks, B empties 3, net 1 tank. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- An outlet is a worker who undoes work, so its rate is negative.
- The tank only fills if the inlet is faster than the outlet; here 6 h is faster than 8 h.
**Likely follow-ups:** Outlet of 4 hours (faster than the inlet)? It never fills; it empties.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding the rates: 1/6 + 1/8 = 7/24 gives 24/7 = 3.43 h, which is wrong because the outlet drains the tank.

### Q19 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Two people start 90 km apart and travel towards each other at 25 km/h and 20 km/h. After how many hours do they meet?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); meeting-point items are a standard relative-speed pattern.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Closing speed is the sum of speeds.
**Say this in the interview:** "They close the gap at 45 km/h, so 90/45 = 2 hours."
**Answer:** **2 hours**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Towards each other: relative speed = 25 + 20 = 45 km/h.
2. Time = gap / relative speed = 90/45 = 2 hours.
**Check:** In 2 h they cover 50 km and 40 km; 50 + 40 = 90. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Facing each other, the gap shrinks by both speeds each hour.
- Distances covered at the meeting point are in the ratio of their speeds (25:20 = 5:4).
**Likely follow-ups:** How far from the 25 km/h person's start? 50 km.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Dividing by one speed, or subtracting the speeds.

### Q20 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A thief runs at 30 km/h. A police officer starts chasing from a point 20 km behind at 40 km/h in the same direction. After how many hours does the officer catch the thief?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); chase problems are standard relative-speed items.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Same direction means subtract speeds.
**Say this in the interview:** "The officer gains 10 km every hour, and the gap is 20 km, so 2 hours."
**Answer:** **2 hours**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Same direction: relative speed = 40 - 30 = 10 km/h.
2. Gap to close = 20 km.
3. Time = 20/10 = 2 hours.
**Check:** In 2 h the officer runs 80 km and the thief 60 km; 60 + 20 head start = 80. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Chasing: the faster one eats the gap at the difference of speeds.
**Likely follow-ups:** Distance the officer covers? 40 x 2 = 80 km.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding the speeds (20/70).

### Q21 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A man walking at 5/6 of his usual speed reaches the office 12 minutes late. What is his usual time to reach the office?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the "usual speed" late-arrival item is standard on prep sites.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Speed and time are inversely proportional for a fixed distance.
**Say this in the interview:** "Speed 5/6 means time 6/5 of usual. The extra 1/5 of his usual time is 12 minutes, so usual time = 60 minutes."
**Answer:** **60 minutes (1 hour)**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Fixed distance: new time = usual time x 6/5.
2. Extra time = 6/5 T - T = T/5.
3. T/5 = 12 minutes, so T = 60 minutes.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Speed ratio 5:6 means time ratio 6:5. The one-unit difference equals 12 minutes, so the usual time (5 units) = 5 x 12 = 60.
**Check:** 60 x 6/5 = 72 minutes, which is 12 more. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- Slower speed = more time, in the exact flipped ratio.
- The "difference of ratio parts = the delay" trick finds the unit.
**Likely follow-ups:** At 4/5 speed, 15 minutes late? Time ratio 5:4, 1 unit = 15, usual = 4 x 15 = 60 minutes.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 72 (the new time) or treating 5/6 as a time ratio.

### Q22 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 A is 3 times as fast as B. Together they finish a job in 12 days. How many days does A alone take?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); efficiency-ratio problems are a listed pattern (PlacementPreparation; unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Turning an efficiency ratio into a rate split.
**Say this in the interview:** "A:B efficiency is 3:1, so A does 3/4 of the work. A alone takes 12 x 4/3 = 16 days."
**Answer:** **16 days** (B alone takes 48 days)
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Efficiency A:B = 3:1, so together they have 4 parts.
2. Together they take 12 days, so job = 4 x 12 = 48 part-days.
3. A alone does 3 parts a day: 48/3 = 16 days.
4. B alone does 1 part a day: 48 days.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Together time x (total parts / A's parts) = 12 x 4/3 = 16.
**Check:** 1/16 + 1/48 = 3/48 + 1/48 = 4/48 = 1/12, so 12 days together. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- "3 times as fast" means 3 times the rate, so a third of the time.
- A does 3/4 of the combined work each day, so A alone needs the combined time divided by 3/4, that is 12 x 4/3 = 16 days.
**Likely follow-ups:** A twice as fast, together 12 days? 12 x 3/2 = 18 days for A.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 36 (12 x 3) or 4 (12/3).
