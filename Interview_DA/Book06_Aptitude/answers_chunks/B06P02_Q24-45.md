### Q24 ⭐⭐⭐ 🔴 In a company the ratio of male to female employees is 5:3. After 20 more female employees are hired, the ratio becomes 5:4. How many male and female employees were there originally?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); ratio questions are frequently tested in numerical reasoning per prep blogs (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Setting up a ratio equation when only one side changes.
**Say this in the interview:** "Males do not change, so I keep males as 5 parts and write females as 3 parts plus 20, which must equal 4 parts of the new ratio. That gives 100 males and 60 females."
**Answer:** **100 male and 60 female employees originally.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Let males = 5k and females = 3k (k is the value of one "part").
2. After hiring, females = 3k + 20; males are unchanged at 5k.
3. New ratio 5k : (3k + 20) = 5 : 4, so 4 x 5k = 5 x (3k + 20).
4. 20k = 15k + 100, so 5k = 100 and k = 20.
5. Males = 5 x 20 = 100; females = 3 x 20 = 60.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Males stay the same and the male term is 5 in both ratios, so one unit is the same size before and after. Female units rose from 3 to 4, so 1 unit = 20 people. Then males = 5 x 20 = 100 and females = 3 x 20 = 60.
**Check:** New females = 60 + 20 = 80; 100 : 80 = 5 : 4. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- The quantity that does not change (males) is the anchor; build both ratios around it.
- When the anchor has the same ratio number in both ratios, the change in the other term (4 - 3 = 1 unit) equals the people added (20).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Same ratio change 5:3 to 5:4 but 10 females hired?* 1 unit = 10, so 50 males and 30 females. 2) *What if both groups change?* Write two equations, one per group.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating 5 and 3 as the actual headcounts, or adding 20 to the ratio number 3 instead of to the real number of females.

### Q25 ⭐⭐ 🟢 How do you find the fourth proportional to 6, 9 and 14, and the mean proportional between 4 and 25?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the definitions of fourth and mean proportional and can cross-multiply.
**Say this in the interview:** "Fourth proportional x means 6 : 9 = 14 : x, so x = 9 x 14 / 6 = 21. The mean proportional is the square root of 4 x 25, which is 10."
**Answer:** **Fourth proportional = 21; mean proportional = 10.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. The fourth proportional to a, b, c is the number x with a : b = c : x, so x = (b x c) / a.
2. x = (9 x 14) / 6 = 126 / 6 = 21.
3. The mean proportional m between a and b satisfies a : m = m : b, so m x m = a x b.
4. m x m = 4 x 25 = 100, so m = 10.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** 6 to 9 is a multiplier of 1.5, so x = 14 x 1.5 = 21. For the mean proportional, take the square root of the product: the square root of 100 is 10.
**Check:** 6 : 9 = 2 : 3 and 14 : 21 = 2 : 3. Also 4 : 10 = 2 : 5 and 10 : 25 = 2 : 5. Both hold.
**Explain simply:**
- A proportion means two ratios are equal: a : b = c : d, which means a x d = b x c (cross-multiplication).
- The fourth proportional is the missing fourth number; the mean proportional is the middle number that makes a : m = m : b (it is the geometric mean, not the ordinary average).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Third proportional to 4 and 6?* 6 x 6 / 4 = 9. 2) *Mean proportional of 9 and 16?* The square root of 144 = 12.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using the ordinary average (4 + 25) / 2 = 14.5 as the "mean" proportional; that is the arithmetic mean, a different thing.

### Q26 ⭐⭐ 🟢 Which ratio is greater, 3:5 or 4:7? How do you compare ratios quickly without converting to decimals?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Comparing fractions quickly by cross-multiplication.
**Say this in the interview:** "I cross-multiply: 3 x 7 = 21 and 4 x 5 = 20. Since 21 is bigger, 3 : 5 is the greater ratio."
**Answer:** **3:5 is greater than 4:7.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write each ratio as a fraction: 3/5 and 4/7.
2. Cross-multiply: 3 x 7 = 21 and 5 x 4 = 20.
3. The fraction whose numerator gave the bigger product is bigger: 21 > 20, so 3/5 > 4/7.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Cross-multiplication is the same as putting both fractions on the common denominator 35: 3/5 = 21/35 and 4/7 = 20/35. So you only compare 21 with 20.
**Check:** In decimals, 3/5 = 0.600 and 4/7 = about 0.571. The first is larger.
**Explain simply:**
- A ratio a : b behaves like the fraction a / b.
- To compare a / b with c / d (all positive), compare a x d with b x c; the side with the larger product is the larger ratio.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Compare 5:8 and 7:11.* 5 x 11 = 55 and 8 x 7 = 56, so 7:11 is greater. 2) *Order 2:3, 3:4 and 4:5.* Each is 1 minus 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 respectively, so 4:5 is the greatest.

### Q27 ⭐⭐ 🟢 If A:B = 2:3, A is what percentage of B, and B is what percentage more than A?

**What the interviewer is checking:** Seeing that "percentage of" and "percentage more than" use different bases.
**Say this in the interview:** "A is 2 out of B's 3, so 66.67% of B. B exceeds A by 1 on a base of A = 2, so B is 50% more than A."
**Answer:** **A is 66 2/3% (about 66.67%) of B; B is 50% more than A.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. A : B = 2 : 3, so take A = 2 and B = 3.
2. A as a percentage of B = 2 / 3 x 100 = 66.67%.
3. B is more than A by 3 - 2 = 1.
4. Percentage more = 1 / 2 x 100 = 50% (the base is A, the quantity we compare with).
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Ask "percent of what?" The word after "of" or "than" is the base: B in the first part, A in the second.

- "A is what percent of B" divides by B.
- "B is what percent more than A" divides the difference by A.
- The two answers are not mirror images, because the base changes.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *A is what percent less than B?* The gap 1 over base B = 3 gives 33.33%. 2) *A : B = 3 : 5, A is what percent of B?* 60%.

### Q29 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What is the average of the first 20 even numbers and of the first 25 odd numbers? State the shortcut formulas you used.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); averages are a standard placement-test topic per prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Knowing the sum formulas and turning them into averages.
**Say this in the interview:** "The sum of the first n even numbers is n(n+1), so their average is n+1, which is 21. The sum of the first n odd numbers is n squared, so their average is n, which is 25."
**Answer:** **Average of the first 20 even numbers = 21; average of the first 25 odd numbers = 25.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Sum of the first n even numbers (2, 4, 6, ...) = n x (n + 1). For n = 20: 20 x 21 = 420.
2. Average = 420 / 20 = 21.
3. Sum of the first n odd numbers (1, 3, 5, ...) = n x n. For n = 25: 25 x 25 = 625.
4. Average = 625 / 25 = 25.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** For any evenly spaced list, average = (first + last) / 2. The evens run from 2 to 40: (2 + 40) / 2 = 21. The odds run from 1 to 49: (1 + 49) / 2 = 25.
**Check:** The two evenly spaced checks above give the same 21 and 25.
**Explain simply:**
- Evenly spaced numbers have their average at the middle, which equals (first + last) / 2.
- The two formulas to memorise: first n evens sum to n(n+1); first n odds sum to n squared.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Average of the first 50 even numbers?* 51. 2) *Sum of the first 15 odd numbers?* 225.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Giving n for the even average (forgetting the +1), or swapping the two formulas.

### Q30 ⭐⭐ 🟢 How can the assumed-mean method be used to find the average of 118, 122, 119, 125 and 121 quickly? What is the average?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Mental-maths speed using deviations from a convenient number.
**Say this in the interview:** "I assume the mean is 120 and add the small deviations: -2, +2, -1, +5, +1 gives +5. Divided by 5 that is +1, so the average is 121."
**Answer:** **Average = 121.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Pick an assumed mean close to the data: 120.
2. Deviations from 120: 118 is -2; 122 is +2; 119 is -1; 125 is +5; 121 is +1.
3. Sum of deviations = -2 + 2 - 1 + 5 + 1 = +5.
4. Average deviation = 5 / 5 = +1.
5. Average = 120 + 1 = 121.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** This works because the average of (value - 120) equals the average minus 120. You add tiny numbers instead of three-digit ones, so slips are rarer.
**Check:** Direct: 118 + 122 + 119 + 125 + 121 = 605; 605 / 5 = 121.
**Explain simply:**
- The assumed mean is a round guess near the middle of the data.
- Average = assumed mean + average of the deviations. Any assumed mean gives the same answer; a close one is just easier.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Average of 98, 102, 105, 99, 101?* Assume 100: deviations -2, +2, +5, -1, +1 sum to +5, so 101. 2) *Does the choice of assumed mean change the answer?* No, only the effort.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Forgetting to add the assumed mean back, or dividing the deviation sum by the wrong count.

### Q32 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What is a compound ratio? Find the compound ratio of 2:3, 4:5 and 6:7 in simplest form.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Knowing that a compound ratio multiplies the terms.
**Say this in the interview:** "A compound ratio multiplies the first terms together and the second terms together. 2 x 4 x 6 = 48 and 3 x 5 x 7 = 105, which reduces to 16 : 35."
**Answer:** **Compound ratio = 16 : 35.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. The compound ratio of a : b, c : d and e : f is (a x c x e) : (b x d x f).
2. First terms: 2 x 4 x 6 = 48.
3. Second terms: 3 x 5 x 7 = 105.
4. 48 : 105; divide both by 3 to get 16 : 35 (no further common factor).
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Cancel before multiplying: (2/3) x (4/5) x (6/7). The 6 on top cancels with the 3 below to leave 2 on top, so the top is 2 x 4 x 2 = 16 and the bottom is 5 x 7 = 35.
**Check:** In decimals, 0.6667 x 0.8 x 0.8571 = 0.457, and 16 / 35 = 0.457.
**Explain simply:**
- A compound ratio chains several ratios by multiplying their fractions.
- Do not confuse it with the duplicate ratio (a squared : b squared) or the sub-duplicate ratio (square roots of a and b).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Duplicate ratio of 3:4?* 9 : 16. 2) *Compound ratio of 1:2 and 3:5?* 3 : 10.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding the terms instead of multiplying them, or forgetting to reduce to simplest form.

### Q33 ⭐⭐ 🟡 The average of 11 numbers is 50. The average of the first six is 49 and the average of the last six is 52. What is the sixth number?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); averages are a standard placement-test topic per prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Handling overlapping groups in an average problem.
**Say this in the interview:** "The two groups of six overlap at exactly the sixth number. Adding their totals counts it twice, so 294 + 312 - 550 = 56."
**Answer:** **The sixth number is 56.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total of all 11 = 11 x 50 = 550.
2. Total of the first six = 6 x 49 = 294.
3. Total of the last six (numbers 6 to 11) = 6 x 52 = 312.
4. Together the two groups count the sixth number twice: 294 + 312 = 606 = 550 + sixth number.
5. Sixth number = 606 - 550 = 56.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Add the two group totals, subtract the whole total; the surplus is the number counted twice.
**Check:** First five sum = 294 - 56 = 238; last five sum = 312 - 56 = 256; 238 + 56 + 256 = 550. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- Average x count = total; this is the base tool for every average problem.
- 6 + 6 = 12 but there are only 11 numbers, so exactly one number belongs to both groups.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Same data but the average of all 11 is 51?* Total 561, so the sixth = 606 - 561 = 45. 2) *Why is the shared number the sixth?* It is the only position in both groups.

### Q35 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Two numbers are in the ratio 5:7. If 12 is subtracted from each, the ratio becomes 3:5. What are the numbers?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Forming a ratio equation after the same amount is subtracted.
**Say this in the interview:** "I call them 5k and 7k, subtract 12 from each and cross-multiply with 3 : 5. That gives k = 6, so the numbers are 30 and 42."
**Answer:** **The numbers are 30 and 42.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Let the numbers be 5k and 7k.
2. After subtracting 12: (5k - 12) : (7k - 12) = 3 : 5.
3. Cross-multiply: 5(5k - 12) = 3(7k - 12).
4. 25k - 60 = 21k - 36, so 4k = 24 and k = 6.
5. Numbers = 5 x 6 = 30 and 7 x 6 = 42.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Use the difference, which does not change when the same number is subtracted. Old difference = 2k. In the new ratio the difference is 2 parts, so 2 new parts = 2k, giving one new part = k. Then 3k = 5k - 12, so k = 6.
**Check:** 30 - 12 = 18 and 42 - 12 = 30; 18 : 30 = 3 : 5. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- Subtracting the same amount from both terms changes the ratio, so you cannot simply subtract 12 from 5 and 7.
- The actual numbers are the ratio numbers times k; the equation finds k.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What is the difference between the numbers?* 12, unchanged by the subtraction. 2) *Two numbers in ratio 3:4; subtract 6 from each and the ratio becomes 2:3?* 3(3k - 6) = 2(4k - 6) gives k = 6, numbers 18 and 24.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting 12 from the ratio numbers directly, or forgetting that k multiplies both terms.

### Q37 ⭐⭐ 🟡 In a class the average mark of boys is 72 and of girls is 78. The average of the whole class is 74. In what ratio are boys and girls? Use alligation.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); alligation questions are listed in IT-services aptitude prep banks (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Applying the rule of alligation to averages.
**Say this in the interview:** "The mean 74 sits between 72 and 78. Boys : girls is the distance of the mean from the girls' average over its distance from the boys' average: (78 - 74) : (74 - 72) = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1."
**Answer:** **Boys : girls = 2 : 1.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Write the lower average (boys, 72) on the left, the higher (girls, 78) on the right, the mean (74) in the middle.
2. Cross-subtract: girls' side gives 78 - 74 = 4; boys' side gives 74 - 72 = 2.
3. The quantity of the lower group (boys) is proportional to the gap on the other side (4), and girls to 2.
4. Boys : girls = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The mean 74 is closer to 72 (gap 2) than to 78 (gap 4), so there are more boys, in the inverse ratio of the gaps: 4 : 2 = 2 : 1.
**Check:** Say 20 boys and 10 girls: (20 x 72 + 10 x 78) / 30 = (1,440 + 780) / 30 = 2,220 / 30 = 74. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- Alligation is a shortcut for weighted averages: the larger group pulls the overall mean toward its own average.
- The ratio of quantities is the inverse of the ratio of the distances from the mean.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *If the class average were 75?* Gaps 3 and 3, so 1 : 1. 2) *If there are 30 students in all with mean 74?* 20 boys and 10 girls.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Giving 1 : 2 (the gaps in the wrong order); the larger group is the one nearer the mean.

### Q39 ⭐⭐ 🟡 20 litres of a 20% acid solution is mixed with 10 litres of a 50% acid solution. What is the percentage of acid in the final mixture?

### Q40 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How much water must be added to 40 litres of a 30% acid solution to reduce the concentration to 20%?

**Step-by-step working:**
1. Acid now = 30% of 40 = 12 litres.
2. After adding water the acid is still 12 litres, and it must be 20% of the new total.
3. New total = 12 / 0.20 = 60 litres.
4. Water to add = 60 - 40 = 20 litres.

**Check:** 12 litres in 60 litres = 20%. Correct. Alligation: water is 0% and the solution is 30%, mean 20%, so solution : water = (20 - 0) : (30 - 20) = 2 : 1, and 40 L of solution needs 20 L of water.
**Explain simply:**
- Adding water dilutes but does not change the quantity of acid, so the acid is the fixed anchor.
- Water is a 0% solution, which makes it a case of alligation.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Water to reduce 30% to 15%?* New total 80, add 40 litres. 2) *Pure acid to add to 40 L of 30% solution to make it 40%?* Water stays 28 L, which is 60%, so the total is 46.67 L and the acid to add is 6.67 L.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 10 litres (the percentage drop) or treating the 20% as being of the 40 litres.

### Q41 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Vessel 1 holds 20 litres of a milk-water mixture in the ratio 3:2 and vessel 2 holds 30 litres in the ratio 4:1. If both are poured into one container, what is the ratio of milk to water?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); milk-water mixture examples appear in Accenture prep guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Finding actual quantities before combining; never adding ratios.
**Say this in the interview:** "Vessel 1 has 12 litres milk and 8 water; vessel 2 has 24 milk and 6 water. Together that is 36 milk and 14 water, so 18 : 7."
**Answer:** **Milk : water = 18 : 7.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Vessel 1 (20 L, ratio 3 : 2): milk = 20 x 3/5 = 12 L; water = 20 x 2/5 = 8 L.
2. Vessel 2 (30 L, ratio 4 : 1): milk = 30 x 4/5 = 24 L; water = 30 x 1/5 = 6 L.
3. Total milk = 12 + 24 = 36 L; total water = 8 + 6 = 14 L.
4. Ratio = 36 : 14 = 18 : 7.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Convert each vessel to litres of milk and water first; add milk with milk and water with water; reduce at the end.
**Check:** 36 + 14 = 50 = 20 + 30. Milk fraction 36/50 = 72%.
**Explain simply:**
- Ratios from different vessels cannot be added because each is based on a different total.
- Convert to real litres (the common unit), add, then form the new ratio.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What percentage of the final mixture is milk?* 72%. 2) *Water to add to make milk : water = 1 : 1?* 22 litres.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Adding the ratios directly (3 + 4) : (2 + 1) = 7 : 3, which ignores the different volumes.

### Q42 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A container holds 40 litres of pure milk. 4 litres are removed and replaced with water, and the process is repeated once more. How much milk is left in the container?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); replacement problems are listed as a recurring pattern in prep sources (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Applying the replacement formula or tracking the milk step by step.
**Say this in the interview:** "Each round keeps 36/40 = 90% of the milk. After two rounds, 40 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 32.4 litres of milk remain."
**Answer:** **32.4 litres of milk.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Round 1: remove 4 L of pure milk, leaving 36 L milk; add 4 L water. The mixture is 36 L milk in 40 L, so milk is 90%.
2. Round 2: remove 4 L of the mixture; it carries 4 x 0.9 = 3.6 L milk. Milk left = 36 - 3.6 = 32.4 L.
3. So 32.4 L of milk and 7.6 L of water are in the container.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Milk left = 40 x (1 - 4/40) x (1 - 4/40) = 40 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 40 x 0.81 = 32.4 litres.
**Check:** 32.4 / 40 = 0.81 and 0.9 squared is 0.81. Same.
**Explain simply:**
- Each time you take out a portion, the same fraction of milk leaves with it, so what remains is multiplied by (1 - fraction removed).
- The second removal takes out some milk too, because the mixture is no longer pure.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Litres of water in the container now?* 7.6 L. 2) *Three rounds?* 40 x 0.9 cubed = 29.16 L.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 40 - 4 - 4 = 32 litres (forgetting that the second removal also takes out some water).

### Q43 ⭐⭐ 🟡 What is the formula for the quantity of pure liquid left after n replacements in a container (replacement problems)? When does it apply?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); replacement problems are listed as a recurring pattern in prep sources (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know the formula, its conditions, and why it works.
**Say this in the interview:** "Pure liquid left = Original quantity x (1 - x/V) to the power n, where V is the container's total volume and x is the amount removed and replaced each time, n times."
**Answer:** **Pure liquid left = V x (1 - x/V)^n** (V = total volume of the container, x = quantity drawn out and replaced each time, n = number of repetitions).
**Step-by-step working (mini-example):**
1. A 50-litre container of pure milk; 5 litres are removed and replaced with water; repeat 3 times in all.
2. Each round keeps 1 - 5/50 = 0.9 of the milk.
3. After 3 rounds: 50 x 0.9 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 50 x 0.729 = 36.45 litres of milk.
4. Water now = 50 - 36.45 = 13.55 litres.
**Explain simply:**
- It applies when the same quantity is removed from a well-mixed container and replaced by the other liquid (or by water), repeatedly, with the total volume constant.
- Why it works: each removal takes out the fraction x/V of whatever is there, so the pure liquid is multiplied by (1 - x/V) every round.
- The fraction (1 - x/V)^n is also the fraction of pure liquid left, so the ratio of pure to total after n rounds is (1 - x/V)^n.
- If the container starts with a mixture (not pure), apply the formula to the quantity of the component you are tracking.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Q42 with the formula?* 40 x (1 - 4/40)^2 = 32.4 L. 2) *Does it work if the volume changes?* No; the denominator must stay the total volume.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using x/V of the original amount repeatedly (subtracting n times x), using the removed quantity instead of the container volume, or applying it to non-mixed or changing-volume containers.

### Q44 ⭐⭐ 🟡 A team of 40 employees has an average salary of Rs 45,000. After 8 employees with an average salary of Rs 30,000 resign, what is the average salary of the remaining team?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); averages are a standard placement-test topic per prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Removing a group from an average using totals.
**Say this in the interview:** "Total payroll is 18,00,000. The 8 leavers took 2,40,000, leaving 15,60,000 for 32 people, which is Rs 48,750 each."
**Answer:** **Average salary of the remaining 32 employees = Rs 48,750.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total payroll = 40 x 45,000 = Rs 18,00,000.
2. Payroll of the 8 who left = 8 x 30,000 = Rs 2,40,000.
3. Remaining payroll = 18,00,000 - 2,40,000 = Rs 15,60,000.
4. Remaining people = 40 - 8 = 32.
5. New average = 15,60,000 / 32 = Rs 48,750.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The leavers are Rs 15,000 below the old average, and 8 of them left. Their shortfall (8 x 15,000 = 1,20,000) is spread over the 32 stayers: 1,20,000 / 32 = 3,750. So the new average is 45,000 + 3,750 = Rs 48,750.
**Check:** 32 x 48,750 = 15,60,000. Matches.
**Explain simply:**
- Average x number of people = total. Subtract the total of the people who leave, then divide by who is left.
- Low earners leaving pushes the average up.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if the 8 who left averaged Rs 60,000?* Remaining total = 18,00,000 - 4,80,000 = 13,20,000, average Rs 41,250. 2) *Is the average of the stayers higher than before?* Yes, because the leavers earned less than the team average.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Subtracting the averages (45,000 - 30,000) or dividing the remaining total by 40 instead of 32.

### Q45 ⭐⭐ 🟡 The average age of a class of 25 students is 14 years. When the teacher's age is included, the average rises by 1 year. What is the teacher's age?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); inclusion-of-one-member averages are a standard pattern in prep sources (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Using totals before and after one person joins.
**Say this in the interview:** "The 25 students total 350 years. With the teacher there are 26 people at an average of 15, so 390 years in total. The teacher is 390 - 350 = 40."
**Answer:** **The teacher is 40 years old.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total age of the students = 25 x 14 = 350 years.
2. New average = 14 + 1 = 15, with 26 people.
3. New total = 26 x 15 = 390 years.
4. Teacher's age = 390 - 350 = 40 years.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** The teacher must lift all 25 students' average by 1 (25 extra years) and also cover the new average 15 for themselves: 15 + 25 x 1 = 40.
**Check:** (350 + 40) / 26 = 390 / 26 = 15. Correct.
**Explain simply:**
- Adding one person at age A changes the total from 350 to 350 + A, and the head-count from 25 to 26.
- The new member's value = new average + (old group size x rise in average).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *If the average rises by 2 instead?* Teacher = 16 + 25 x 2 = 66 (an unrealistic age, but it shows the method). 2) *What if the teacher were 30?* New average = 380 / 26, about 14.62.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Answering 15 (the new average) or 14 + 25 = 39; using 25 instead of 26 for the new head-count.
