### Q43 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Mock Set B, Q7 (target 120 s): A committee of 3 is to be chosen from 5 men and 4 women. In how many ways can it be chosen so that it includes at least one woman?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); original mock-set problem, not taken from any company paper
**What the interviewer is checking:** Combinations, and the "at least one" shortcut (total minus the opposite case).
**Say this in the interview:** "At least one woman is everything minus the all-men committees. Total is 84, all-men is 10, so 74."
**Answer:** **74 ways**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. Total committees of 3 from 9 people = 9C3 = (9 x 8 x 7) / (3 x 2 x 1) = 84.
2. Committees with no woman means all 3 are men: 5C3 = (5 x 4 x 3) / (3 x 2 x 1) = 10.
3. At least one woman = 84 - 10 = 74.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Whenever you see "at least one", subtract the "none" case from the total. The direct route (1 woman + 2 men, 2 women + 1 man, 3 women) = 4x10 + 6x5 + 4x1 = 40 + 30 + 4 = 74, but it takes three lines instead of one.
**Target-time check (120 s):** the complement route takes about 40 seconds, so you bank about 80 seconds for harder questions.
**Check:** The direct route also gives 74. Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**
- nCr ("n choose r") counts groups where order does not matter, which suits a committee.
- "At least one" has many cases; "none" has only one, so subtract.
**Likely follow-ups:** At least one man in a committee of 3 from 5 men and 4 women? 84 - 4C3 = 84 - 4 = 80.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Using permutations (9P3 = 504) although order does not matter here; or forgetting to subtract and answering 84.

### Q45 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Mock Set C, Q3 (target 60 s): Statements: All analysts are graduates. Some graduates are managers. Conclusions: I. Some analysts are managers. II. Some managers are graduates. Which conclusion(s) follow?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); original mock-set problem, not taken from any company paper
**What the interviewer is checking:** Syllogisms: accept only what holds in every possible Venn picture.
**Say this in the interview:** "Only II follows. 'Some graduates are managers' reverses to 'some managers are graduates'. Analysts could sit entirely outside the managers, so I does not follow."
**Answer:** **Only conclusion II follows.**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. "All analysts are graduates": draw the analysts circle fully inside the graduates circle.
2. "Some graduates are managers": the managers circle overlaps graduates somewhere.
3. Conclusion II, "Some managers are graduates", is statement 2 read the other way. A "some" statement can always be reversed, so II is true.
4. Conclusion I, "Some analysts are managers": the overlap with managers may lie in the part of graduates that is outside analysts. Then no analyst is a manager. So I is not forced; it does not follow.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** "All A are G" plus "Some G are M" gives no certain link between A and M. Then check for simple reversals, which are always safe for "some".
**Target-time check (60 s):** about 25 seconds.
**Check:** Draw analysts inside graduates, with managers touching graduates only away from analysts: II is true and I is false, so I fails.
**Explain simply:**
- "Follows" means true in every possible picture, not just one.
- "Some X are Y" always means "some Y are X" too.
**Likely follow-ups:** Does "Some graduates are not analysts" follow? Not certain: analysts could be all the graduates.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Chaining All + Some into "Some analysts are managers" (the classic trap).

### Q54 ⭐⭐ 🔴 Using cyclicity of powers of 2 modulo 7, find the remainder when 2^50 is divided by 7.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Cyclicity (repeating remainders) so you never compute a huge power.
**Say this in the interview:** "Powers of 2 mod 7 repeat as 2, 4, 1 every three steps. 50 leaves remainder 2 when divided by 3, so the remainder is the second in the cycle, which is 4."
**Answer:** **4**
**Step-by-step working:**
1. 2^1 = 2, remainder 2. 2^2 = 4, remainder 4. 2^3 = 8, remainder 1.
2. Then 2^4 = 16, remainder 2; the pattern 2, 4, 1 repeats, cycle length 3.
3. 50 divided by 3 gives 16 groups and remainder 2 (3 x 16 = 48).
4. 2^48 = (2^3)^16 leaves remainder 1, so 2^50 = 2^48 x 2^2 leaves the same remainder as 2^2 = 4.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Find where the remainder first hits 1 (2^3). Then 2^50 = 2^48 x 2^2, and the first part is 1.
**Check:** Verified by script: 2^50 mod 7 = 4.
**Explain simply:**
- When a power's remainder equals 1, the cycle restarts.
- Only the leftover exponent (50 mod 3 = 2) matters.
**Likely follow-ups:** Remainder of 2^100 mod 7? 100 mod 3 = 1, so 2.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking the wrong position in the cycle and answering 1 or 2.

**Fast method (30 seconds):** Left part = number + deviation = 1012 + 12 = 1024; right part = deviation squared (12^2 = 144) written in 3 digits. Join: 1024 | 144.
**Check:** Verified by script: 1012^2 = 1,024,144.
**Explain simply:**
- The trick is (a + b)^2 with a = 1000, so 2ab and b^2 line up with the place values.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1007^2? 1014 | 049 = 1,014,049.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Writing the right part in two digits; for deviations under 10, forgetting the leading zeros.
