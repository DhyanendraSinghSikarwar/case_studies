### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Argument: "New hires score the same on the post-induction quiz after watching a 2-hour recorded video as after the 2-day classroom induction, so the company can replace the classroom induction with the video." Which is an assumption of the argument?

- (b) New hires enjoy videos more than classrooms.
- (c) The classroom induction has no social benefit at all.
- (d) The video will be watched in a group.

**Say this in the interview:** "The argument jumps from equal quiz scores to equal induction. That only works if the quiz captures what the induction is for. So the answer is (a)."
**Answer:** **(a)**
**Step-by-step reasoning:**
1. Premise: equal quiz scores after video and classroom. Conclusion: the video can replace the classroom.
2. The gap: a quiz is a proxy (a stand-in measure). The argument needs the proxy to cover the real purpose of induction (culture, people, process knowledge).

**Check:** Negating (b), (c) and (d) leaves the argument standing; negating (a) breaks it (hand-checked).
**Explain simply:**

- A proxy metric is an easy-to-measure stand-in (quiz score) for the thing you truly care about (readiness to work).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Which new fact would weaken it?* Hires from the video group make more process errors in month 3. 2) *What would strengthen it?* Equal job performance after 6 months for both groups.

### Q17 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Argument: "The app rating fell from 4.4 to 4.1 after the latest update, so the update made users less happy." Which of the following is NOT an assumption of the argument?
- (a) No separate event in the same period, such as a server outage, lowered the ratings.
- (b) The kind of users who left ratings before and after the update was broadly similar.
- (c) Ratings are a reasonable indicator of how users feel.
- (d) The update added more features than it removed.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you separate what the argument needs from a plausible-sounding extra detail ("NOT" question).
**Say this in the interview:** "I negate each option. (a), (b) and (c) each break the argument if false. (d) does not: the update could have removed features and still made users unhappy. So (d) is the NOT-assumption."
**Answer:** **(d)**

1. Argument: rating fell after the update, so the update caused lower happiness.
2. Test (a): if an outage lowered ratings, the update is not the cause. Needed.
3. Test (b): if different users (for example angrier ones) rated after the update, the drop shows a change in who rated, not in happiness. Needed.
4. Test (c): if ratings do not reflect feelings, "less happy" is unsupported. Needed.

**Common mistakes / traps:** Misreading the question as "which IS an assumption" and picking (a); always re-read the stem for NOT.

### Q21 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Quarterly revenue in Rs lakh: North 40, South 35, East 25 in Q1, and North 42, South 42, East 20 in Q2. Which statement can be correctly inferred?

- (d) Total revenue fell.

### Q25 ⭐⭐ 🟢 Why are absolute numbers often a poor basis for comparing two groups, and what should you ask for before accepting a claim such as "Branch A had more complaints than Branch B"?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you instinctively ask "out of how many?" and "over what period?".

### Q27 ⭐⭐ 🟢 A region's sales rose 20% in Q2 and then fell 20% in Q3. The manager says "we are back at the Q1 level". Is that right, and by what percentage is Q3 below Q1?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you know percentages apply to different bases, so a rise and an equal fall do not cancel.
**Say this in the interview:** "No. Take Q1 as 100. After +20% it is 120; 20% of 120 is 24, so Q3 is 96. That is 4% below Q1."
**Answer:** **No; Q3 is 4% below Q1.**
**Step-by-step reasoning:**
1. Q1 = 100. Q2 = 100 × 1.20 = 120.
2. Q3 = 120 × 0.80 = 96.
3. Change versus Q1 = (96 - 100) / 100 = -4%.
4. The fall of 20% was taken on the bigger base (120), so it removed 24, more than the 20 that was added.
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Multiply the factors: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96. Any "+x% then -x%" gives 1 - x², here 1 - 0.04 = 0.96.
**Check:** Script: 100 × 1.2 × 0.8 = 96.0.
**Explain simply:**
- A percentage is always "of the starting value of that step".
- To get back after a 20% fall you need +25% (1/0.8 - 1 = 25%); to undo a 20% rise you need a fall of 16.7% (1 - 1/1.2).
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What Q3 rise would bring it back to Q1?* From 96 to 100 is +4.17%. 2) *Rise 10% then fall 10%?* 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99, so 1% below the start.
