|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 95% | 9.50 | 9.00 |
| 12 | 88% | 10.56 | 9.12 |
| 14 | 80% | 11.20 | 8.40 |
| 16 | 68% | 10.88 | 5.76 |

**Check:** Verified by script.
**Explain simply:**

### Q39 ⭐ 🟡 You have 7 days before a reasoning-heavy online test and you are weak in everything. How do you triage topics and spend your daily time?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Prioritisation under a deadline.
**Say this in the interview:** "I would pick the topics most likely to appear and easiest to improve, practise them in short timed sets, and review mistakes daily. I would keep the last day for a light mock and rest."
**Answer:** **Our suggestion (not research): cover high-frequency, rule-based topics first, practise daily with a short timed set and error log, take a full mock midweek, and rest before the test.**
**Step-by-step reasoning:**
1. Day 1: find the format (see Q31), take a diagnostic set of about 20 questions, and rank topics by how many marks they carry and how weak you are.
2. Days 2 to 4: learn rules for the top 3 to 4 topics (for example syllogism, seating, series, blood relations) with 20 to 30 questions each.
3. Day 5: add one more topic and do a mixed timed set.
4. Day 6: full-length mock under test conditions; review every miss.
5. Day 7: light revision of the error log, check device and ID, sleep early.

**Explain simply:**
- Triage means sorting by importance and fixability, like in a hospital.

**Common mistakes / traps:** Studying everything shallowly; skipping the mock and the error log.

### Q40 ⭐ 🔴 You have 240 seconds left and no negative marking. Three unattempted questions need 40 seconds (success chance 90%), 90 seconds (80%) and 150 seconds (50%). Which questions should you attempt to maximise the expected number correct, and what is that expectation?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Choosing under a time budget by comparing value per time and total fit.
**Say this in the interview:** "I check which combinations fit in 240 seconds. 40 plus 90 is 130 seconds for 1.7 expected correct, which beats the others. Then I use spare time to check."
**Answer:** **Attempt the 40-second and 90-second questions; expected correct is 0.9 + 0.8 = 1.7.**
**Step-by-step reasoning:**
1. All three need 280 seconds, more than 240, so they do not all fit.
2. 40 + 90 = 130 s fits: 0.9 + 0.8 = 1.7.
3. 40 + 150 = 190 s fits: 0.9 + 0.5 = 1.4.
4. 90 + 150 = 240 s fits exactly: 0.8 + 0.5 = 1.3.
5. Best is 1.7, using 130 s; 110 s remain for checking.
6. With the spare 110 s, either check your two answers or, since there is no negative marking, put a guess on the third question rather than leave it blank (a random guess is a small bonus; the number of options is not given, so it is not counted in 1.7).
**Fast method (30 seconds):** Rank by success per second: 0.9/40 = 0.0225, 0.8/90 = 0.0089, 0.5/150 = 0.0033; take in that order until the time runs out. Here this order gives the optimum, but in general you should also check combinations (this is the "knapsack" problem).
**Check:** Verified by script: all 8 subsets enumerated.
**Explain simply:**
- Expected number correct means the sum of the success chances of the questions you attempt.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *If the 150-second question was 95%?* Then 40 + 150 gives 1.85 and beats 1.7 (hand-checked: 0.9 + 0.95 = 1.85, 190 s fits). 2) *With only 120 seconds?* 40 + 90 = 130 s no longer fits, so the best single question is the 40-second one with 0.9 (checked by script logic: no pair fits).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Picking the biggest success chance first without checking fit; attempting the long question at the cost of two short ones.
