## Practice answers

**Q1.** (a) Concerning: a bar chart starting at 40 exaggerates differences because bar length is read as the amount. Fix: start at 0 or print values. (b) Acceptable: a line chart shows change; the axis is clearly labelled and the range suits body temperature, where all values lie near 36 to 38. (c) Fine: bars start at zero.

**Q2.** Ask: the unit of the vertical axis (₹, units, percent?); what exactly is measured (revenue, profit, orders); the dates and period covered; how "best year" is defined (best of how many years); the data source and who prepared it; whether a longer history would change the picture; whether the numbers are counts or rates.

**Q3.** The slices add up to 30 + 25 + 25 + 30 = 110%, which is more than 100%. Possible causes: a rounding or typing error, or a question that allowed people to give more than one reason, in which case a pie is the wrong chart. Ask the author for the raw numbers.

**Q4.** Ask for a longer period (several months or the same weeks last year) and for the counts and order volumes. Four weeks may be a lucky stretch or a seasonal dip; a title with a conclusion needs the window explained. Also ask whether the weeks differ in the number of orders (a rate may differ from a count).

**Q5.** Area ratio = 3 x 3 = 9, so the coin looks nine times as big, though sales only tripled. This impression exaggerates the growth. Fair version: draw three equal-sized coins against one.

**Q6.**
(a) Sales: (25 minus 20) divided by 20 = 0.25 = 25%. Visitors: (2,500 minus 2,000) divided by 2,000 = 0.25 = 25%.
(b) No. Lines can coincide because someone chose the scales to make them coincide; and moving together does not prove cause (Chapter 24). Visitors and sales may both rise with season, advertising or festival.
(c) The visitors line would span a short axis range (800 units): it would start at (2,000 minus 1,800) divided by 800 = 25% of the height and end at (2,500 minus 1,800) divided by 800 = 87.5%, so it would look steeper than the sales line. That shows dual axes let the drawer decide how the lines look. Better: two separate charts or an index with the first month set to 100.

**Q7.**
(a) Sum = 30+32+33+35+36+38+40+42+400 = 686. Mean = 686 divided by 9 = 76.2 (thousand). Median: nine values in order, the 5th is **36**.
(b) The median (36) is typical; the mean (76.2) is pulled up by the single 400.
(c) Add the median, a note of the highest value or a spread (range 30 to 400), and the number of employees. Or show a dot plot of all nine salaries.

**Q8.**
(a) From 2 to 8 is an increase of 6; 6 divided by 2 = 3 = 300%. The number is right.
(b) Before: 2 divided by 4,000 = 0.0005 = 0.05%. After: 8 divided by 4,000 = 0.002 = 0.2%.
(c) "Complaints rose from 2 to 8 out of 4,000 orders (0.05% to 0.2%)." The jump looks dramatic in percent because the base was tiny; the numbers themselves are still small.

**Q9.**
(a) 10, 10 and 40 minutes wide.
(b) 0 to 10: 20 divided by 10 = 2 orders per minute of band; 10 to 20: 40 divided by 10 = 4; 20 to 60: 40 divided by 40 = 1.
(c) The 10 to 20 minute band (density 4). The equal-width bars made the last bar look as tall as the middle one although those 40 orders are spread across a band four times as wide.

**Q10.**
(a) State A is shaded darker, since 5,000 is more than 3,000.
(b) A: 5,000 cases divided by 10 million people = 0.0005 per person = 500 per million. B: 3,000 divided by 2 million = 0.0015 = 1,500 per million.
(c) State B has three times the rate. A fair map would shade by cases per million (or per 100,000) people, and ideally show the totals in a tooltip or table.

**Q11.**
(a) Only A, the bottom segment, sits on the zero baseline in both bars (0 to 40 in Quarter 1, 0 to 45 in Quarter 2). B and C float on top of the segments below them.
(b) Quarter 1 total: 40 + 30 + 20 = 90. Quarter 2 total: 45 + 20 + 40 = 105.
(c) B: (20 minus 30) divided by 30 = minus 10 divided by 30 = about minus 33.3%. C: (40 minus 20) divided by 20 = plus 100%.
(d) B and C do not start from the same height in the two bars: B starts at 40 in Quarter 1 and at 45 in Quarter 2; C starts at 70 in Quarter 1 and at 65 in Quarter 2. So the eye cannot compare their lengths against a common line. Use a grouped (side by side) bar chart or a separate small chart for each product, all starting from zero.

**Q12.**
(a) 10 to 100: (100 minus 10) divided by 10 = 9 = 900%. 100 to 1,000: (1,000 minus 100) divided by 100 = 9 = 900%. Each year the value is multiplied by 10.
(b) No. On a normal scale the line would be almost flat for the first year or two and then shoot upward, because the increases are 90 and then 900 units.
(c) On a log scale, equal distances mean equal ratios, not equal amounts. A reader who assumes a normal scale would misread a "straight line" as steady additions rather than multiplying by 10 each year.

**Q13.**
(a) With 0 at the top, higher values are drawn lower. The values fall from 9 to 5, so the line climbs towards the top of the page: it looks like it is rising.
(b) (5 minus 9) divided by 9 = minus 4 divided by 9 = about minus 44.4%. Resolution time fell by about 44%, which is an improvement.
(c) A viewer who glances at the shape may think resolution time is rising (getting worse), since lines normally rise with higher values. The unusual axis must be read, labelled clearly, or avoided.

**Q14.**
(a) Training index: 200 divided by 200 x 100 = 100; 220 divided by 200 x 100 = 110; 250 divided by 200 x 100 = 125; 300 divided by 200 x 100 = 150. Coffee index: 40 divided by 40 x 100 = 100; 44 divided by 40 x 100 = 110; 50 divided by 40 x 100 = 125; 60 divided by 40 x 100 = 150.
(b) The two lines are identical, 100, 110, 125, 150.
(c) No. Two series rising together does not show cause. A likelier explanation: both rise as the company grows (more staff means more training hours and more coffee machines). Compare per employee to test it (Chapter 24).

**Q15.**
(a) Heights above 117: 120 gives 3, 118 gives 1, 121 gives 4, 119 gives 2, 122 gives 5, 120 gives 3.
(b) Tallest 5 divided by shortest 1 = 5 times taller.
(c) Actual ratio highest to lowest = 122 divided by 118 = 1.0339, so **1.034**, about 3.4% higher. Average revenue = (120+118+121+119+122+120) divided by 6 = 720 divided by 6 = ₹120 lakh.
(d) Problems: truncated axis (makes a 3.4% range look like a fivefold difference); 3D bars (distort height comparison); no title, units or source; no indication that revenue is basically flat. Fair redraw: a plain 2D line or bar chart with the axis from 0 (or if a line, clearly labelled 110 to 130), title "Product A quarterly revenue, flat around ₹120 lakh", units in the axis label, values printed on the points, and source and period stated.
