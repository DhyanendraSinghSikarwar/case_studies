# Chapter 4 Answer Key: If-Then, Converse, Inverse, Contrapositive and If and Only If

## Self-check answers

1. **Only when p is true and q is false** (the condition happens but the result does not). In the other three combinations the rule is not broken.
2. **The contrapositive** (swap p and q and negate both: "If NOT q then NOT p"). The converse and inverse are not equivalent to the original.
3. **"If a refund is given, then the receipt was shown."** "Only if" points to what is required, so the requirement (receipt shown) is the result part.

## Practice answers

### Q1 🟢
The condition is the part after "if" and the result is the part after "then".
- p (condition): a candidate accepts the offer.
- q (result): HR sends a joining kit.

### Q2 🟢
Original: if p (parcel damaged) then q (courier files a report).
- **Converse** (swap): If the courier files a report, then the parcel is damaged.
- **Inverse** (negate both): If a parcel is not damaged, then the courier does not file a report.
- **Contrapositive** (swap and negate both): If the courier does not file a report, then the parcel is not damaged.

### Q3 🟢
A promise is broken only when the condition is true and the result is false.
- Asha: finished and got a certificate: promise kept.
- **Bilal: finished and got none: promise broken** (condition true, result false).
- Chitra: did not finish, got one anyway: the condition is false, so the promise says nothing; not broken.
- Dev: did not finish, got none: condition false, nothing promised; not broken.

**Answer:** only Bilal's case breaks the promise.

### Q4 🟢
p = "the lights are off", q = "the office is closed". Contrapositive (swap and negate both): **"If the office is not closed, then the lights are not off"**, that is, "If the office is open, the lights are on."

| p (lights off) | q (closed) | Original: if p then q | Contrapositive: if NOT q then NOT p |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | T |
| T | F | F | F |
| F | T | T | T |
| F | F | T | T |

**Answer:** the two columns are identical (T, F, T, T), so they are equivalent.

### Q5 🟢
"Only if" points to the requirement, so the rule is "If a refund is given, the receipt was shown."

**Answer: (a).** Option (b) is the converse (having the receipt does not force a refund). Option (c) says the opposite of the requirement.

### Q6 🟢

### Q7 🟢
Let p = "it is a leap year", q = "February has 29 days". February did not have 29 days (NOT q), so by the contrapositive ("If NOT q then NOT p") **the year is not a leap year.** We used the contrapositive.

### Q8 🟡
- If-then form: **"If a customer complains, then she gets a callback."**
- Converse: **"If a customer gets a callback, then she complained."**
- A customer who did not complain but got a callback is a case with the converse's condition true and result false, so the **converse is false** in that world.
- The **original rule is not broken**: that customer did not complain, so the promise says nothing about her.

### Q9 🟡
A person who **works in Delhi but is not the manager of the Delhi team** (for example, an analyst in the Delhi office). She makes the converse's condition ("works in Delhi") true and its result ("is the Delhi team manager") false, so the converse is false. She does not break the original rule.

### Q10 🟡
Read "unless" as "if not": **(a)** "If the maintenance flag is not set, then the server restarts."
- **(b)** The flag is set, so the condition ("flag not set") is false. The rule says nothing: the server may or may not have restarted.
- **(c)** Flag not set (condition true) and no restart (result false): **yes, the rule was broken.**

### Q12 🟡
Let p = "the invoice is paid", q = "the order is shipped".
(a) is the converse (if q then p), (b) is the contrapositive (if NOT q then NOT p), (c) is the inverse (if NOT p then NOT q).

| p | q | Original (if p then q) | (a) if q then p | (b) if NOT q then NOT p | (c) if NOT p then NOT q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | F | F | T | F | T |
| F | T | T | F | T | F |
| F | F | T | T | T | T |

**Answer: only (b).** Its column (T, F, T, T) matches the original. (a) and (c) both read T, T, F, T, which differs in rows 2 and 3.

### Q13 🟡
The rule holds only when "in group" and "can edit" have the same truth value.
- In group and can edit: **possible**.
- In group and cannot edit: **violation**; it breaks the half "if in the group, then can edit".
- Not in group and can edit: **violation**; it breaks the half "if can edit, then in the group".
- Not in group and cannot edit: **possible**.

### Q14 🟡
p = "the badge is not scanned", q = "the door stays locked". Contrapositive: "If the door does not stay locked, then the badge was not NOT scanned."
Tidy: remove the double negatives. "Door does not stay locked" is "the door unlocks"; "not NOT scanned" is "scanned".

**Answer:** **"If the door unlocks, then the badge was scanned."**

### Q15 🟡
p = "the shop is not open", q = "the till is locked".
- Converse (if q then p): **"If the till is locked, then the shop is not open."**
- Inverse (if NOT p then NOT q): **"If the shop is open, then the till is not locked."**
- The converse and the inverse are **equivalent to each other** (always true together; the inverse is the contrapositive of the converse).
- Neither is equivalent to the original. The equivalent form of the original is its contrapositive: "If the till is not locked, then the shop is open."

### Q16 🔴
Let p = "has a degree AND has at least 2 years of experience", q = "is shortlisted".
**(a)** Contrapositive: "If she is not shortlisted, then she does NOT (have a degree and at least 2 years of experience)", which is "If she is not shortlisted, then she has no degree OR she has less than 2 years of experience." (NOT of an AND becomes an OR of NOTs; Chapter 3.)

**(b)** Isha was not shortlisted, so at least one of the two is missing. She has a degree, so the degree is not the missing piece. Therefore **she has less than 2 years of experience.**

### Q17 🔴
Two parts: Part 1 "if score at least 40, then passes"; Part 2 "if passes, then attendance at least 75". A student breaks the rule if either part is broken.

| Student | Score at least 40? | Attendance at least 75? | Passed? | Part 1 broken? (score ok, not passed) | Part 2 broken? (passed, attendance low) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| S2 | Yes | No | No | **Yes** | No |
| S3 | No | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| S4 | Yes | No | Yes | No | **Yes** |
| S5 | No | No | No | No | No |
| S6 | Yes | Yes | No | **Yes** | No |

**Answer:** **S2, S4 and S6** break the rule. S3 does not: a score below 40 is not mentioned in either part, and attendance is fine. (The rule is not an "if and only if", so passing without 40 is allowed.) Note: the two parts together forbid any student who scores at least 40 but has attendance below 75 (Part 1 says she must pass, Part 2 says she must not), so S2 and S4 can never satisfy the rule. That is why a rule with two parts needs both parts checked.

### Q18 🔴
Let p = "overloaded", q = "stopped at the check post". S1 is "if p then q".
- S2: "stopped only if overloaded" is "if q then p": the **converse**. Not equivalent.
- S3: "not stopped, so not overloaded" is "if NOT q then NOT p": the **contrapositive**. Equivalent.
- S4: "overloaded only if stopped" is "if p then q": the same as S1. Equivalent.

**Answer:** **S3 and S4** say the same as S1; S2 does not. (Four-row check on the earlier trucks table: S1, S3, S4 all give T, F, T, T; S2 gives T, T, F, T.)

### Q19 🔴
Let p = "the test passes", q = "we release".
- **(a)** The release happened (q is true). Concluding "the test passed" goes from q back to p: that is the **converse**, which is **not valid**. They may have released despite a failed test, or for another reason. Priya's reasoning is invalid.
- **(b)** The release did not happen (NOT q). By the **contrapositive** ("If NOT q then NOT p"), **the test did not pass.** This is valid.

### Q20 🔴
Name the facts: A, B, C, D mean "attends".
1. Dev does not attend (D false).
2. "At least one of Chen and Dev attends" and D is false, so **Chen attends**.
3. "If Bela attends, Chen does not attend". Chen does attend, so by the contrapositive **Bela does not attend**.
4. "If Anil attends, Bela attends". Bela does not attend, so by the contrapositive **Anil does not attend**.

**Answer:** **only Chen attends.** A check of all 16 attendance combinations (done by code) gives exactly one combination that satisfies all four facts, so the answer is unique.
