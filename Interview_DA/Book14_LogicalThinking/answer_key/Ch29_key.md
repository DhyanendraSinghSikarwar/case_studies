# Chapter 29 Answer Key

## Self-check answers

1. Work backward, simplify, find patterns, casework, elimination.
2. Work backward. You know the end result, so you undo each step in reverse order with its opposite.
3. Casework. Split by one question (for example how many 50 notes are used), and count each case separately.

## Practice answers

### Q1 🟢 Leaving for the office (work backward)
Total journey: 10 + 7 + 28 + 5 = 50 minutes. Arrival 9:05 minus 50 minutes: 9:05 minus 5 is 9:00, minus 45 more is 8:15. Check forward: 8:15 + 10 = 8:25; +7 = 8:32; +28 = 9:00; +5 = 9:05. **Answer: 8:15.**

### Q2 🟢 Think of a number (work backward)
Forward steps: add 7, times 3, minus 9, result 30. Undo in reverse: add 9 gives 39; divide by 3 gives 13; subtract 7 gives 6. Check: 6 + 7 = 13; x 3 = 39; minus 9 = 30. **Answer: 6.**

### Q3 🟢 Ribbon cuts (simplify)
Simplify: a 10 m ribbon cut into 5 m pieces gives 2 pieces with 1 cut. So cuts = pieces - 1. Pieces for 60 m: 60 / 5 = 12. Cuts: 12 - 1 = 11. Check: 11 cuts make 12 pieces, 12 x 5 = 60. **Answer: 11 cuts.**

### Q4 🟢 Seats in the 12th row (patterns)
Row 1 has 4, and each row adds 3. Row n has 4 + 3 x (n - 1). Check small: row 2 = 4 + 3 = 7, row 3 = 4 + 6 = 10, matches. Row 12 = 4 + 3 x 11 = 4 + 33 = 37. Why 11 and not 12: from row 1 to row 12 there are 11 steps. **Answer: 37 seats.**

### Q5 🟢 Lunch combinations (casework by subtraction)
Count all, then remove the banned ones. All: 4 mains x 3 sides x 2 drinks = 24. Banned: fish with rice, with either drink: 1 x 1 x 2 = 2. Allowed: 24 - 2 = 22. Casework check: fish main has 2 allowed sides (salad, soup) x 2 drinks = 4; each of the other 3 mains has 3 sides x 2 drinks = 6, giving 18; 4 + 18 = 22. **Answer: 22.**

### Q6 🟢 Even multiple of 9 (elimination)
Multiples of 9 in the list: 45 (5 x 9), 63 (7 x 9), 72 (8 x 9). Others: 21, 34, 52 are not multiples of 9. Of the three, only 72 is even. **Answer: 72.**

### Q7 🟢 Petty cash (work backward)
End: 450. Wednesday one quarter was spent, so 450 is three quarters of the amount before: 450 / (3/4) = 600. Undo Tuesday: subtract 200 gives 400. Undo Monday: half was spent, so 400 is half: start = 800. Check: 800, half spent leaves 400; plus 200 is 600; a quarter spent (150) leaves 450. **Answer: 800.**

### Q8 🟢 Lift (simplify)
Floor 1 to floor 4 is 3 floor-gaps (1 to 2, 2 to 3, 3 to 4), not 4. 12 seconds for 3 gaps means 4 seconds per gap. Floor 1 to floor 8 is 7 gaps: 7 x 4 = 28 seconds. **Answer: 28 seconds.**

### Q9 🟡 Chairs in a triangle (patterns)
Totals: 1 row gives 1; 2 rows 3; 3 rows 6; 4 rows 10. Each new row adds its row number. For 12 rows: 1 + 2 + ... + 12. Pair from both ends: (1 + 12) = 13, (2 + 11) = 13, and so on, 6 pairs: 6 x 13 = 78. Check by adding on: 10 (4 rows), 15, 21, 28, 36, 45, 55, 66, 78. **Answer: 78 chairs.**

### Q10 🟡 Odd numbers over 300 (casework)
Split by the hundreds digit (must be 3, 4 or 5 to exceed 300).
- Hundreds 3: remaining digits 1, 2, 4, 5. Units must be odd: 1 or 5 (2 choices). Tens: any of the 3 digits left. 2 x 3 = 6.
- Hundreds 4: remaining 1, 2, 3, 5. Odd units: 1, 3, 5 (3 choices); tens: 3 left. 3 x 3 = 9.
- Hundreds 5: remaining 1, 2, 3, 4. Odd units: 1, 3 (2 choices); tens: 3 left. 2 x 3 = 6.
Total: 6 + 9 + 6 = 21. A computer count over all numbers agrees. **Answer: 21.**

### Q11 🟡 Pets (elimination)
- Ana: not cat, not dog, not bird, so Ana owns the fish.
- Ben: not fish, not bird, so cat or dog.
- Cara: not bird, not cat, so dog or fish; fish is Ana's, so Cara owns the dog.
- Ben then owns the cat (dog is taken).

Check all clues: every one holds. Brute force over all 24 assignments gives only this one. **Answer: Ana fish, Ben cat, Cara dog, Dev bird.**

### Q12 🟡 Upload deadline (work backward)
Total time: drafting 135 minutes + editing 65 + lunch 45 + approval 50 + upload 10 = 305 minutes = 5 h 5 min. Work from 17:00: minus 10 (upload) = 16:50; minus 50 (approval) = 16:00; minus 45 (lunch) = 15:15; minus 65 (editing) = 14:10; minus 135 (drafting) = 11:55. Check: 17:00 minus 5 h 5 min = 11:55. **Answer: 11:55.**

### Q13 🟡 Committee with at least one man (casework)
Cases by the number of men:
- 1 man and 2 women: 3 x 6 = 18 (there are 6 pairs of women among 4 women).
- 2 men and 1 woman: 3 pairs of men x 4 women = 12.
- 3 men: 1.
Total: 18 + 12 + 1 = 31. Check by subtraction: all committees of 3 from 7 people = 35; committees with no man (3 women from 4) = 4; 35 - 4 = 31. **Answer: 31.**

### Q14 🟡 Matchstick staircase (patterns)
Small cases: 1 column: one square, 4 sticks. Add the second column (height 2): 3 horizontal lines (bottom, middle, top) = 3 sticks, plus 2 sticks on its right side; its left side already exists except for 1 more stick on the part above the first column. New = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6, total 10. In general the k-th column adds (k + 1) horizontals + k on its right + 1 new left stick = 2k + 2. Totals: 4, 10, 18, 28, 40, 54, ... which equals n x (n + 3) (check: 3 x 6 = 18, 4 x 7 = 28). For n = 10: 10 x 13 = 130. Also the sum of (2k + 2) for k = 1 to 10 is 2 x 55 + 20 = 130. A computer count of unique edges for n = 10 also gives 130. **Answer: 130 matchsticks.**

### Q15 🟡 The machine (work backward, branching)
Rule: even gives half; odd gives plus 3. Undo each step. A result r came from either 2r (since 2r is even and halving gives r) or from r - 3 if r - 3 is odd and positive.
- After step 3 the number is 5. Before: 10 (even, halves to 5). Or 5 - 3 = 2, which is even, so adding 3 is not what the machine does to 2 (it would halve it). So only 10.
- Before reaching 10: from 20 (halves to 10) or from 7 (odd, 7 + 3 = 10). Both valid.
- Before reaching 20: from 40, or from 17 (odd, 17 + 3 = 20). Valid.
- Before reaching 7: from 14, or from 4 (even, so it would halve, not add 3). Only 14.
Starting numbers: 40, 17, 14. Check: 40 to 20 to 10 to 5. 17 to 20 to 10 to 5. 14 to 7 to 10 to 5. A computer search of 1 to 199 finds only these. **Answer: 14, 17 and 40.**

### Q16 🟡 Offsite day (elimination)
Start with Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. Not Monday: cross out Monday. Not Friday: cross out Friday. Board meeting is Tuesday, so not Tuesday: cross out Tuesday. Not the day after the board meeting, which is Wednesday: cross out Wednesday. Left: Thursday. **Answer: Thursday.**

### Q17 🟡 Paying 60 (casework)
Split by the number of 20 notes (1, 2 or 3; at least one).
- One 20: 40 left using 5s and 10s. Tens: 0 to 4 (5 ways), and the rest in fives. 5 ways.
- Two 20s: 20 left. Tens: 0, 1 or 2. 3 ways.
- Three 20s: 0 left. 1 way.
Total: 5 + 3 + 1 = 9. A computer count agrees. **Answer: 9 combinations.**

### Q18 🔴 Stamps 6 and 11 for 100 (casework with elimination)
Equation: 6a + 11b = 100, with a and b at least 1. Split by the number of 11-stamps b = 1 to 9.
- b = 1: 89 left, not a multiple of 6. b = 2: 78 = 6 x 13, yes (a = 13). b = 3: 67 no. b = 4: 56 no. b = 5: 45 no. b = 6: 34 no. b = 7: 23 no. b = 8: 12 = 6 x 2, yes (a = 2). b = 9: 1 no.
Two ways: 13 sixes and 2 elevens (15 stamps), or 2 sixes and 8 elevens (10 stamps). Check: 78 + 22 = 100 and 12 + 88 = 100. **Answer: 2 ways; the fewest stamps is 10 (two 6-stamps and eight 11-stamps).**

### Q19 🔴 Timing 9 minutes with 7 and 4 (work backward and trial)
Goal 9 = 7 + 2, or 8 + 1, or 4 + 4 + 1. Plan: aim to reach minute 8 (when the 4 timer finishes twice) with exactly 1 minute of work left on the 7 timer.
- Minute 0: start both timers.
- Minute 4: the 4 timer ends. Flip it so it runs again.
- Minute 7: the 7 timer ends. Flip it at once (it restarts as a full 7 minutes). The 4 timer has been running for 3 minutes of its second run, with 1 minute left.
- Minute 8: the 4 timer ends. At this moment the 7 timer has run 1 minute since it was flipped at minute 7, so it has 6 minutes in the top and 1 in the bottom. Flip it now: the 1 minute of sand in the bottom is now on top.
- Minute 9: that 1 minute of sand runs out. Exactly 9 minutes have passed.
A search over all flip choices at the end of each timer shows that flips at minutes 4, 7 and 8 reach minute 9. **Answer: start both at 0; flip the 4 timer at minute 4; flip the 7 timer at minute 7 and again at minute 8; stop when it ends at minute 9.**

### Q20 🔴 Interview order (casework / simplify)
Treat "E then D" as one block [ED], because D must come immediately after E. Now there are four units: A, B, C, [ED]. Orders of 4 units: 24.
- Keep only the orders where A is before B: half of 24 = 12.
- Remove those with C first: C first, then A, B, [ED] in the other three spots with A before B: 3 orders (3 spots for the block's position, A and B fixed in order). So 12 - 3 = 9.
- "A is not last" is already guaranteed (A must be before B), so it removes nothing.
List of the nine: ABCED, ABEDC, ACBED, ACEDB, AEDBC, AEDCB, EDABC, EDACB, EDCAB. Check a few against the clues: all have A before B, E immediately followed by D, and C not first. A computer count of all 120 orders gives 9. **Answer: 9.**

### Q21 🔴 Passcode (elimination)
Digits sets from the guesses (the code has 4 different digits):
- Guess 1234 has 0 + 2 = 2 digits in the code. Guess 5678 has 1 + 1 = 2 digits in the code. That makes 4 digits, so the code has no 0 and no 9.
- Guess 2580: 1 + 2 = 3 digits in the code; 0 is not, so 2, 5 and 8 are all in the code.
- 2 is from the first set, so one more digit from {1, 3, 4}; 5 and 8 are the two from the second set (so 6 and 7 are not in the code).
- Guess 9071: only 1 digit is in the code; 9 and 0 are out, 7 is out, so 1 is in. The code uses the digits 1, 2, 5, 8.
Positions. Guess 2580 has exactly 1 hit: one of 2 first, 5 second, 8 third. Guess 5678 has exactly 1 hit: one of 5 first, 8 fourth. Guess 5120 has exactly 1 hit: one of 5 first, 1 second, 2 third. Guess 1234: 1 is not first, 2 is not second.
- Case 5 first: then 8 is not fourth. Guess 2580 needs a hit: 2 first (no, 5 is there), 5 second (no), so 8 third. Remaining 1 and 2 for second and fourth: 2 is not second, so 1 second, 2 fourth: 5182. Guess 5120 then has 5 first and 1 second, 2 hits, but it must be 1. Fails.
- Case 8 fourth (5 not first): guess 2580 needs one hit from 2 first or 5 second. If 5 second (and 2 not first): 1 and 2 go to first and third; 1 is not first, so 2 is first, a contradiction. If 2 first (and 5 not second): 5 must be third, 1 second: 2158. Check guess 5120: 1 second is a hit; 5 and 2 are in the wrong places (5 first? no. 2 third? no): 1 hit, 2 nears. Correct.
Check all five guesses against 2158: 1234 gives 0 hits, 2 nears (1 and 2 present, in wrong places). 5678 gives 8? 8 is fourth in the code and fourth in the guess: 1 hit; 5 present elsewhere: 1 near. 2580 gives 1 hit? the code 2158 and guess 2580: first digit 2 matches (1 hit); 5 and 8 present elsewhere (2 nears). 9071 gives 1 near. All match. A computer search of all 5,040 codes finds only 2158. **Answer: 2158.**

### Q22 🔴 Six jobs, two people (work backward on the chain)
Durations: A2, B4, C3, D2 (after A), E3 (after B and C), F1 (after D and E).
- F is last and needs E; E needs B and C. The chain B (4), then E (3), then F (1) is 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 hours, and these must happen in order, so nothing can finish before hour 8.
- A schedule that achieves 8: Person 1 does B (hours 0 to 4), then E (4 to 7), then F (7 to 8). Person 2 does C (0 to 3), then A (3 to 5), then D (5 to 7). E starts at 4 (B ends at 4, C ended at 3). F starts at 7 (E ends at 7, D ends at 7).
- Check dependencies: D after A (A ends 5, D starts 5), E after B and C (4 and 3), F after D and E (7 and 7). All good. A computer search over all orders and worker assignments also finds 8 as the minimum.
**Answer: 8 hours (person 1: B, E, F; person 2: C, A, D).**

### Q23 🔴 PIN codes (casework)
Split by the set of four different digits that add to 10. The possible sets are {0, 1, 2, 7}, {0, 1, 3, 6}, {0, 1, 4, 5}, {0, 2, 3, 5} and {1, 2, 3, 4}. (Check: 0 + 1 + 2 + 7 = 10, 0 + 1 + 3 + 6 = 10, 0 + 1 + 4 + 5 = 10, 0 + 2 + 3 + 5 = 10, 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10. No other set of four different digits works.)
For each set count the arrangements with the first digit not 0 and the last digit even:
- {0, 1, 2, 7}: 10 arrangements.
- {0, 1, 3, 6}: 10.
- {0, 1, 4, 5}: 10.
- {0, 2, 3, 5}: 10.
- {1, 2, 3, 4}: 12.
(Example of how to count one set: {1, 2, 3, 4} has no 0, last digit 2 or 4 (2 choices), the other three digits in any order (6 ways): 12. For {0, 1, 2, 7}: last digit 0 gives 6 orders of the others; last digit 2 gives 3 orders with 0 not first among {0, 1, 7}: 6 - 2 = 4; total 6 + 4 = 10.)
Total: 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 12 = 52. A computer count of all 4-digit codes agrees. **Answer: 52.**

### Q24 🔴 Squares on a 7 by 5 floor (patterns)
Simplify: on a 3 by 2 grid, there are 6 squares of size 1 and 2 squares of size 2: 8 in all. Pattern: squares of size k can start in (length - k + 1) x (width - k + 1) positions.
For 7 by 5:
- 1 by 1: 7 x 5 = 35
- 2 by 2: 6 x 4 = 24
- 3 by 3: 5 x 3 = 15
- 4 by 4: 4 x 2 = 8
- 5 by 5: 3 x 1 = 3
- 6 by 6: does not fit (only 5 wide).
Total: 35 + 24 + 15 + 8 + 3 = 85. A computer count agrees. **Answer: 85 squares.**

### Q25 🔴 Token game (work backward)
Work from the end. Call a pile "losing" if the player to move from it will lose with best play by the opponent.
- Pile 0 (no tokens): the player to move has lost (the other took the last token). Losing.
- Pile 1: take 1 and win. Winning. Pile 2: take 1 leaves 1, a winning pile for the opponent; the only move is 1 (4 is too many). So pile 2 is losing.
- Pile 3: take 1 leaves 2 (losing for the opponent). Winning. Pile 4: take 4 and win. Winning.
- Pile 5: take 1 leaves 4 (winning for the opponent); take 4 leaves 1 (winning for the opponent). Losing.
- Pile 6: take 1 leaves 5 (losing). Winning. Pile 7: take 1 leaves 6 (winning), take 4 leaves 3 (winning). Losing.
The losing piles are 0, 2, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 22, ... (the remainder is 0 or 2 when divided by 5). From 23, taking 1 leaves 22, a losing pile for your opponent. Taking 4 would leave 19, a winning pile for the opponent (they take 4 to leave 15). After you leave a losing pile, every move your opponent can make leaves a winning pile, and from that pile you can move to a losing pile again (the list above shows how). Example: from 22, if the opponent takes 1 leaving 21, you take 1 leaving 20; if they take 4 leaving 18, you take 1 leaving 17.
**Answer: take 1 token, leaving 22, a losing pile for your opponent.**
