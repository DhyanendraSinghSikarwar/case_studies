# Chapter 1 Answer Key

## Self-check answers

1. **Conclusion:** "lunch will be late". The word "so" points to it; "the canteen is shut today" is the premise.
2. **False.** The conclusion can come first ("Rohan is late because the train was delayed"), in the middle, or last.
3. **Induction.** The premise lists past cases; the conclusion predicts the next one. A late payslip is still possible, so the premises do not guarantee it.

## Practice answers

**Q1** 🟢 Argument. Premises: (1) all sales team employees get a phone allowance; (2) Meera is in the sales team. Conclusion: Meera gets a phone allowance ("therefore" signals it). If both premises are true, the conclusion cannot be false: **deduction**.

**Q2** 🟢 Argument. Premise: every time the printer was refilled on a Friday it jammed by Monday; also it was refilled this Friday. Conclusion: it will probably jam by Monday ("so", "probably"). Past cases to a future case, with "probably": **induction**.

**Q3** 🟢 Argument. "Because" introduces the premises, so the conclusion is first. Conclusion: Rohan must be at the airport by 6 am. Premises: (1) the flight leaves at 8 am; (2) check-in closes two hours before departure. Two hours before 8 am is 6 am, so the conclusion is forced: **deduction**.

**Q4** 🟢 Argument. Premises: (1) no visitor may enter the lab without a badge; (2) Anil has no badge. Conclusion: Anil may not enter ("hence"). Forced by the rule: **deduction**.

**Q5** 🟢 **Not an argument.** It lists meal timings. Apply the test: no sentence is being defended and none needs a reason.

**Q6** 🟡 Argument. Conclusion (last, signalled by "It seems"): the calls work. Premises: (1) customers who got a call renewed; (2) most of those who did not get a call let their plans lapse. The word "seems" and the nature of the evidence (observed cases, not a rule) make it **induction**.

**Q7** 🟡 Argument. "Since" introduces premises: (1) the warehouse floods every monsoon; (2) this year's monsoon is forecast to be heavy. Conclusion: we should move the stock upstairs before June. Premises make the action sensible but do not guarantee a flood: **induction**.

**Q8** 🟡 Argument. Premise: 40 randomly chosen claims out of 500 all had receipts. Conclusion: nearly all 500 claims have receipts ("So"). Sample to whole group: **induction**. (The remaining 460 claims could differ.)

**Q9** 🟡 Argument. Premises: (1) either the payment failed or the bank is down; (2) the bank is not down. Conclusion: the payment failed ("So"). If both premises are true, the conclusion must be true (the only remaining option): **deduction**.

**Q10** 🟡 **Not an argument; an explanation.** "Because" is present, but the writer is not trying to convince you that the meeting moved (that is taken as known). The sentence explains why it happened. There is no claim being proved.

**Q11** 🔴 Argument. Stated premise: Priya has worked here eight years. Conclusion: she is eligible for the long-service award. **Unstated premise:** employees with eight years of service (or a threshold that eight years meets) are eligible for the long-service award. With that added, the conclusion is forced: **deduction** (any wording of the hidden rule that covers eight years is acceptable). Without it, the argument has a gap.

**Q12** 🔴 Argument with two steps.
- Premises: (1) a request is valid if filed within 30 days and the item is unopened; (2) every valid request is paid; (3) Sana filed within 30 days; (4) her item is unopened.
- **Intermediate conclusion:** Sana's request is valid. It follows from (1), (3), (4), and then acts as a premise for the next step.
- **Final conclusion:** Sana's request will be paid. It follows from the intermediate conclusion and (2).
- Each step is forced if the premises are true: **deduction**.

**Q13** 🔴 Argument. Conclusion: the vendor is probably not cheaper overall ("So I doubt"). Premise: the speaker tracked their own bills for two weeks and spent more than before. "Many people say the new vendor is cheaper" is **not a premise**: it is the opposing view that the writer pushes back against ("But"). Observations from one person's two weeks make the conclusion plausible, not certain: **induction**.

**Q14** 🔴 **Not an argument.** It is a short report of events in order: crash, restart, report. "So" here means "and then as a result", not "therefore this claim is proved". No statement is offered as a conclusion that needs support.

**Q15** 🔴 Argument. Conclusion: our next hire will probably accept the offer. Premises: (1) the last twelve candidates who got this offer all accepted; (2) the offer is now better than before. There is no clue word at the start; we recognise the conclusion because it is the only sentence that predicts something about the future (and says "probably"), while the others report facts that back it up. Past cases to a future case: **induction**.
