# Chapter 22 Answer Key: Data Sufficiency

Choices: A = I alone; B = II alone; C = both together only; D = each alone; E = not enough even together.
Every answer below was re-checked in review by code (see the verification note at the end).

## Self-check answers

1. "Sufficient" means the facts leave exactly one possible answer (one value, or an always-yes or always-no for a yes/no question).
2. No. "Sometimes yes, sometimes no" is not a single answer, so it is not sufficient. (Only "always yes" or "always no" is.)
3. Yes. All packs are identical, so one pack holds 60 / 5 = 12 pens. One answer.

## Easy

**Q1: A.**
1. I alone: price of the box = 12 x 15 = Rs 180. One answer. Sufficient.
2. II alone: the box cost at most Rs 500, so a pen could cost Rs 9 (box 108) or Rs 12 (box 144), and so on. Not sufficient.
3. Only I works: A.

**Q2: C.**
1. I alone: opening 12, closing unknown. Days taken could be 0, 3 or 5. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: closing 9, opening unknown. Days taken could be 1 or 10. Not sufficient.
3. Together: 12 - 9 = 3 days. One answer. C.

**Q3: B.**
1. I alone: 6 seniors plus an unknown number of juniors. With 0 juniors the team is 6 (not larger than 10); with 8 juniors it is 14 (larger). Not sufficient.
2. II alone: 11 juniors already makes at least 11 people, which is more than 10. Always yes. Sufficient.
3. B.

**Q4: D.**
1. I alone: 6,000 / 4 = Rs 1,500.
2. II alone: 15,000 / 10 = Rs 1,500.
3. Each alone gives one answer: D. (Same answer in both, which is fine; each is separately sufficient.)

**Q5: E.**
1. Each alone gives one end of the age range only. Not sufficient.
2. Together: the youngest is 21 and the oldest is 24; the other two ages are free between them. Ages 21, 21, 21, 24 give an average of 21.75 (sum 87). Ages 21, 24, 24, 24 give an average of 23.25 (sum 93). Two different averages, so not sufficient.
3. E.

**Q6: A.**
1. I alone: Rs 11,200 is more than Rs 10,000. Always yes. Sufficient.
2. II alone: higher than Friday's, but Friday's is unknown. Saturday could be Rs 9,000 (no) or Rs 12,000 (yes). Not sufficient.
3. A.

**Q7: B.**
1. I alone: the deposit is two months of the total rent, but no amount is given, so the rent is unknown. The statement is always true whatever the rent is. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: 4 rooms with equal rent, total Rs 28,000, so one room is 28,000 / 4 = Rs 7,000. Sufficient.
3. B.

**Q8: D.**
1. I alone: 30 boxes in 20 minutes = 1.5 boxes per minute.
2. II alone: 90 boxes in 60 minutes = 1.5 boxes per minute.
3. D.

**Q9: E.**
1. I alone: 20 employees; women could be 3 or 9. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: more men than women says nothing about the count. Not sufficient.
3. Together: 20 employees with more men than women allows women to be any of 0 to 9. For example 9 women and 11 men, or 5 women and 15 men. Not sufficient.
4. E.

**Q10: C.**
1. I alone: 5 days, hours per day unknown. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: 7 hours on Tuesday, so 7 hours every working day, but the number of days is unknown. Not sufficient.
3. Together: 5 days x 7 hours = 35 hours. C.

## Medium

**Q11: A.**
1. I alone: Tomas earns Rs 5,000 more than Lakshmi, so Tomas earns more, whatever the actual salaries. Always yes. Sufficient.
2. II alone: Lakshmi's pay is known, Tomas's is not. Not sufficient.
3. A. (You never needed to know the actual salaries.)

**Q12: C.**
1. I alone: sold for Rs 1,200, discount unknown. The marked price could be Rs 1,500 or Rs 2,000. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: the discount is Rs 300, but the sale price is unknown. Not sufficient.
3. Together: marked price = sale price + discount = 1,200 + 300 = Rs 1,500. C.

**Q13: B.**
1. I alone: three sold Rs 50,000 each = Rs 1,50,000. The fourth could sell Rs 40,000 (total Rs 1,90,000, short of target) or Rs 60,000 (total Rs 2,10,000, target reached). Not sufficient.
2. II alone: nobody above Rs 45,000, so the most the four could sell is 4 x 45,000 = Rs 1,80,000, which is less than Rs 2,00,000. Always no. Sufficient.
3. B.

**Q14: E.**
1. I alone: Chennai relates to Delhi, whose revenue is unknown; Pune is not mentioned. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: Pune is Rs 5 lakh, but Chennai is unknown. Not sufficient.
3. Together: Chennai is 1.2 x Delhi, and Delhi is still unknown. If Delhi is Rs 3 lakh, Chennai is Rs 3.6 lakh (below Pune's 5 lakh). If Delhi is Rs 5 lakh, Chennai is Rs 6 lakh (above Pune's 5 lakh). Not sufficient.
4. E.

**Q15: D.**
1. I alone: Rs 11.5 lakh is over Rs 10 lakh. Always yes.
2. II alone: Rs 2 lakh more than the budget is Rs 12 lakh, which is over budget. Always yes.
3. D.

**Q16: A.**
1. I alone: 35 of 50 = 70%. Sufficient (the total of 50 is in the question).
2. II alone: 10 blank tells you nothing about how many said "satisfied". Not sufficient.
3. A.

**Q17: C.**
1. I alone: day : night = 2 : 3. Night agents could be 3 (day 2), 6 (day 4), 30 (day 20), and so on. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: night = day + 10. Many pairs fit (day 5, night 15; day 20, night 30). Not sufficient.
3. Together: let day = 2k and night = 3k. Then 3k - 2k = k = 10, so night = 30 (day = 20). One answer. C.

**Q18: D.**
1. I alone: average = 350 / 5 = 70. Sufficient.
2. II alone: the scores add up to 60 + 65 + 70 + 75 + 80 = 350, so the average is 350 / 5 = 70. Sufficient.
3. D.

**Q19: B.**
1. I alone: an average of 30 means a total of 150 minutes, which does not fix the longest and shortest. For example 28, 29, 30, 31, 32 has range 4; 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 has range 20. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: the five times are listed, so the longest is 40 and the shortest is 22: range = 40 - 22 = 18. Sufficient.
3. B. (II's times add up to 150, so they also agree with I's average of 30; the two statements do not conflict.)

**Q20: E.**
1. I alone: Kiran > Omar, Sana unknown. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: Sana > Omar, Kiran unknown. Not sufficient.
3. Together: both beat Omar, so Omar is not the top. But Kiran versus Sana is unknown, so the top could be Kiran or Sana. Not sufficient.
4. E.

**Q21: B.**
1. I alone: length + breadth = 50. Length could be 30 (breadth 20) or 40 (breadth 10). Not sufficient.
2. II alone: breadth is 20 and length x 20 = 600, so length = 30. Sufficient.
3. B.

**Q22: A.**
1. I alone: 6 days at an average of Rs 12,000 gives 6 x 12,000 = Rs 72,000. Sufficient. (The 6 days comes from the question.)
2. II alone: the number of items sold says nothing about rupees. Not sufficient.
3. A.

## Hard

**Q23: E.**
Let F = full-time staff and T = total staff.
1. I alone: contract = two thirds of F, so F : contract = 3 : 2. Whole-number sizes include F = 6 (contract 4, total 10) and F = 30 (contract 20, total 50). Not sufficient.
2. II alone: F = 60% of T, so T could be 10 (F = 6) or 50 (F = 30). Not sufficient.
3. Together: II says contract = 40% of T and F = 60% of T, so contract / F = 40 / 60 = two thirds. That is exactly what I says, so I adds nothing new. Both T = 10 and T = 50 still fit. Not sufficient.
4. E. (Trap: two statements look different but say the same thing, and neither gives an actual head count.)

**Q24: A.**
1. I alone: 3a + 5b = 22 with a, b whole numbers, at least 1. Try b = 1: 22 - 5 = 17, not a multiple of 3. b = 2: 22 - 10 = 12, so a = 4. Works. b = 3: 22 - 15 = 7, not a multiple of 3. b = 4: 22 - 20 = 2, not a multiple of 3. b = 5 would need negative 3 kg boxes. So the only possibility is 4 three-kg boxes and 2 five-kg boxes. Number of 5 kg boxes = 2. Sufficient.
2. II alone: a + b = 6 gives b = 1 to 5. Not sufficient.
3. A. (Whole numbers can make a single equation enough.)

**Q25: B.**
1. I alone: Q2 = Q3 - 5. Q1 is not mentioned. If Q1 is large, Q2 may be lower; if small, higher. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: Q1 = 0.8 x Q2. Because revenue is positive, 0.8 x Q2 is less than Q2, so Q2 is higher than Q1. Always yes. Sufficient.
3. B.

**Q26: C.**
1. I alone: 18 returns. If there were 100 orders, the rate is 18% (above 5%). If there were 1,000, it is 1.8% (below). Not sufficient.
2. II alone: 400 orders. If 10 returned the rate is 2.5% (no); if 30 returned it is 7.5% (yes). Not sufficient.
3. Together: 18 / 400 = 4.5%, which is below 5%. Always no. C.

**Q27: D.**
1. I alone: a fee of Rs 48,000 on Rs 6,00,000 is 48,000 / 6,00,000 = 8%. Since the percentage is the same for every hire, the fee is 8%. Sufficient.
2. II alone: Rs 72,000 on Rs 9,00,000 is 72,000 / 9,00,000 = 8%. Sufficient.
3. D.

**Q28: E.**
1. I alone: the total is 5 x 45,000 = Rs 2,25,000, but the middle value is not fixed. Not sufficient.
2. II alone: only the top is known. Not sufficient.
3. Together: five salaries adding to Rs 2,25,000 with a maximum of Rs 80,000. Example 1: 80,000; 80,000; 55,000; 5,000; 5,000. Sum = 2,25,000, maximum 80,000, median 55,000 (above 40,000). Example 2: 80,000; 80,000; 40,000; 20,000; 5,000. Sum = 2,25,000, maximum 80,000, median 40,000 (not above 40,000). Different answers, so not sufficient.
4. E.

**Q29: C.**
The question is yes/no: is 7,20,000 / hires greater than 30,000? This is true exactly when the number of hires is below 24.
- A (applicants 400): applicants do not fix the number of hires. Not sufficient.
- B (more than 20 hires): 21 hires gives about Rs 34,286 per hire (up); 25 hires gives Rs 28,800 (down). Not sufficient.
- C (exactly 24 hires): 7,20,000 / 24 = Rs 30,000, the same as last quarter. So it did not go up. Always no. Sufficient.
- D (interviewer hours up 15%): says nothing about the number of hires. Not sufficient.
- E (at most 30 hires): 23 hires gives about Rs 31,304 (up); 30 hires gives Rs 24,000 (down). Not sufficient.
Answer: **C**. (Only one option settles it.)

**Q30: D.**
After training: 30 staff averaging 72, so a total of 30 x 72 = 2,160. The question is whether the average before was lower than 72, which is the same as whether the total before was below 2,160.
- A (best score after is 95): the average after is already known; this tells nothing about before. Not sufficient.
- B (highest before was 90): the average before is at most 90, which could be 60 (rose) or 85 (fell). Not sufficient.
- C (20 of the 30 averaged 65): those 20 total 1,300. The other 10 can total anywhere from 0 to 1,000. The overall total is then between 1,300 and 2,300, which includes values below and above 2,160. Not sufficient.
- D (30 scores add to 2,100): the average before is 2,100 / 30 = 70, which is below 72. So the average rose. Always yes. Sufficient.
- E (average before below the pass mark of 75): before could be 70 (rose) or 74 (fell). Not sufficient.
Answer: **D**.

## Verification note

All 30 questions, and the four worked examples that rest on search (Examples 1 to 4), were checked by a brute-force program that listed every situation allowed by the question's background facts, then kept those that obeyed each statement (I alone, II alone, both) and counted the distinct answers. Q29 and Q30 were checked option by option, and in each exactly one option leaves a single answer. Example 5 and Scenario 1 and 2 numbers were recomputed separately (36,000; 78,000; 18%; 11.25%; 18.75%; 28 x 7 = 196). Search ranges are finite (for example, salaries up to Rs 1,00,000), and the "not sufficient" answers were each confirmed by two explicit situations shown above.
