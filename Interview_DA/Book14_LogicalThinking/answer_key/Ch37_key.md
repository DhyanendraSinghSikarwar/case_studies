# Chapter 37 answer key: Building a Personal Error Log and Review System

Root causes and fix rules below are model answers. Yours will be worded differently; what matters is that the **error type** matches and the fix rule is a concrete action you could check. Types are chosen with the decision order in the chapter (first "yes" wins).

## Self-check answers

1. Any two of: the correct answer, the error type, the root cause in one sentence, the fix rule, the re-test date. Without the correct answer and a type you cannot see what went wrong; without a fix rule nothing changes next time.
2. Unit or convention mistake. The method may be fine; the quantity was in the wrong unit (grams instead of kilograms).
3. A fix rule is only a plan. The re-test date makes you solve a similar question again without help, so you find out whether the rule works; a Fail tells you to rewrite the rule.

## Practice answers

**Q1.** (a) The final answer: 18 is the number of late orders, not on-time ones. (b) **Misread the question** (decision rule 1: the answer is for another quantity). (c) Root cause: I used the one number I saw and did not check which group it described. (d) Fix rule: underline the asked quantity and tick it off before writing the answer. Correct: 120 - 18 = 102. *Why not another type:* the arithmetic and method are fine for the question the learner imagined.

**Q2.** (a) Step 2. (b) **Arithmetic slip** (rule 3: the method of 7 groups of 48 is right; only the product is wrong). (c) Root cause: I multiplied in my head and dropped a carry. (d) Fix rule: split two-digit multiplications (7 x 40 = 280, 7 x 8 = 56, 280 + 56) and add. Correct: 336. *Why not another type:* the right question was answered, with the right operation and units.

**Q3.** (a) Step 1 (the multiplication of 40 by 90 mixes hours and minutes). (b) **Unit or convention mistake** (rule 4). (c) Root cause: the speed is per hour but I used the time in minutes. (d) Fix rule: write a unit beside every number and convert to the same unit before multiplying. Correct: 90 minutes = 1.5 hours; 40 x 1.5 = 60 kilometres. *Why not arithmetic slip:* 40 x 90 = 3,600 is computed correctly; the inputs were incompatible. The reasonableness check (3,600 km by courier) also fails.

**Q4.** (a) The sign step: 140 - 112 is start minus final, but the question defines change as final minus start. (b) **Sign or direction slip** (rule 5). (c) Root cause: I subtracted in the order the numbers appeared and did not check that the sign fits the story. (d) Fix rule: write "final - start", then check that a fall gives a negative number. Correct: 112 - 140 = -28, a fall of 28. *Why not arithmetic slip:* 140 - 112 = 28 is right; what is wrong is the direction and the word "grew".

**Q5.** (a) The listing step: only 11, 12 and 21 were written; 22 is missing. (b) **Skipped case** (rule 8). (c) Root cause: I listed codes by hand and never checked all combinations, so one was left out. (d) Fix rule: reread every condition in the problem and make a quick table of all combinations (first digit by second digit). Correct: 11, 12, 21, 22, so 4 codes (2 choices for each of 2 digits). *Why not wrong assumption:* the learner allowed repeats (11 is listed) and imported no fact; one combination was simply never examined.

**Q6.** (a) The step "each group counts as one", then averaging the two averages. (b) **Wrong assumption** (rule 6: the learner treats 5 weekdays and 2 weekend days as having equal weight, which the problem does not say). (c) Root cause: I assumed the groups were the same size. (d) Fix rule: before averaging averages, write down the group sizes; average by total divided by number of days. Correct: weekdays 5 x 40 = 200 orders, weekend 2 x 100 = 200 orders, total 400 over 7 days = about 57.1 per day. *Why not arithmetic slip:* (40 + 100) / 2 = 70 is calculated correctly.

**Q7.** (a) The conclusion step: "fell right after, so caused". (b) **Fallacy missed** (rule 7: a conclusion drawn from facts given, using "after, therefore because"). (c) Root cause: I read "after" as "because" and ignored the billing fix named in the question. (d) Fix rule: before claiming a cause, list everything else that changed in the same period. Correct answer: we cannot tell; the billing bug fix may explain some or all of the drop. *Why not wrong assumption:* no extra fact was imported; the information needed was in the problem. *Why not misread:* the question was understood; the inference was weak.

**Q8.** (a) There is no working: the answer is the first number that came to mind (the mean itself). (b) **Tried to be too fast** (rule 2: no working and the answer fails a quick plug-back). (c) Root cause: I answered from first impression and never put my number back into the problem. (d) Fix rule: plug the answer back in before writing it. Check: 8 + 9 + 11 + 10 = 38, 38 / 4 = 9.5, not 10. Correct: total must be 4 x 10 = 40; 40 - (8 + 9 + 11) = 40 - 28 = 12; check 8 + 9 + 11 + 12 = 40, 40 / 4 = 10. *Why not wrong assumption:* there is no stated reasoning to judge, so the diagnosis rests on the missing working and the missing check.

**Q9.** (a) Step 1: only one of the two cases from the absolute value was written. (b) **Skipped case** (rule 8). (c) Root cause: |x - 3| = 5 allows x - 3 = 5 or x - 3 = -5 and I wrote only the first. (d) Fix rule: for an absolute value, a square or an "or", write "case 1, case 2" before solving. Correct: x = 8 or x = -2. Check: |-2 - 3| = |-5| = 5. *Why not sign slip:* nothing was turned around; a whole branch was missing.

**Q10.** (a) The final wording "2%". (b) **Unit or convention mistake** (rule 4: convention for reporting a change between percentages). (c) Root cause: I did not distinguish percentage points from percent. (d) Fix rule: when comparing two percentages, state the change in points and, if needed, the relative change; never write a bare "%". Correct: rose by 2 percentage points; relative rise is 2 / 4 = 50%. *Why not arithmetic slip:* 6 - 4 = 2 is right.

**Q11.** (a) Step 1: the mean was computed although the question asks for the median. (b) **Misread the question** (rule 1: the answer is for the neighbouring quantity). (c) Root cause: I used the average because it is the habit, instead of the median the question named. (d) Fix rule: circle the key word of the question ("median") and name the quantity before calculating. Correct: sorted 9, 12, 15, 18, 36; median 15; best day 36; 36 - 15 = 21. (The learner's mean 18 is right as a mean, and 90 / 5 = 18 checks out, so it is not an arithmetic slip.) *Why not unit or convention:* mean versus median is a different quantity, not a notation rule.

**Q12.** (a) Step 2: the assumption that every remaining day looks like the first 8. (b) **Wrong assumption** (rule 6: the learner brings in a fact nobody gave, and the data contradict it). (c) Root cause: I assumed a constant daily rate although the counts fall each day from 60 to 17. (d) Fix rule: write each assumption on its own line and test it against the data before using it. Correct: 900 is not supported. A rough estimate: if later days stay near the latest level (17 a day), 22 remaining days x 17 = 374 and 240 + 374 = 614. The honest statement is "about 600 if the trend stays level; the rest of the month is unknown". *Why not arithmetic slip:* 240 / 8 = 30 and 30 x 30 = 900 are right. *Why not fallacy:* the problem's facts are fine; an extra fact was imported.

**Q13.** (a) Step 2: dividing by a negative number must reverse the inequality sign. (b) **Sign or direction slip** (rule 5). (c) Root cause: I divided but kept the direction of the inequality. (d) Fix rule: whenever I multiply or divide an inequality by a negative number, flip the sign and test one value at the end. Correct: -2x > 6, so x < -3. Check: x = -4 gives 5 - 2(-4) = 13, which is greater than 11; x = -2 gives 9, not greater than 11. *Why not arithmetic slip:* -2x > 6 and 6 / -2 = -3 are right; the direction is wrong.

**Q14.** (a) Step 3: 747 + 350 is 1,097, not 1,087. (b) **Arithmetic slip** (rule 3: the method and the other steps are right; the later steps are right too, given Step 3). (c) Root cause: I added 747 and 350 quickly and wrote 1,087. (d) Fix rule: add large numbers in parts (700 + 300, 47 + 50) and re-add in the other order. Correct: subtotal 1,097; tax = 1,097 x 0.08 = 87.76; total 1,097 + 87.76 = 1,184.76. (The learner's total 1,087 x 1.08 = 1,173.96 is the right method with a bad input.) *Why not another type:* the question was read right, units are fine, nothing assumed or skipped.

**Q15.** (a) The final step: treating "90% of frauds are flagged" as "90% of flagged claims are fraud". (b) **Fallacy missed** (rule 7: a conclusion from given facts that confuses the two directions of a conditional probability, often called base rate neglect). (c) Root cause: I mixed up "the chance of a flag given fraud" with "the chance of fraud given a flag" and ignored how rare fraud is. (d) Fix rule: for tests and flags, count out a table with 1,000 cases: how many are really positive, how many are flagged from each group. Correct: fraud flagged = 90% of 10 = 9; honest flagged = 10% of 990 = 99; total flagged = 108; fraud share = 9 / 108 = about 8.3%. *Why not wrong assumption:* every needed fact was given; the error is in the inference. *Why not arithmetic slip:* no calculation was done.
