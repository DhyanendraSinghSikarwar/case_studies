# Appendix E: 12-week study schedule

**What you will find here:** a week-by-week plan for all 37 chapters and the 8 mock tests, a slower alternative plan, a practice budget per week, and rules for when you fall behind.

> Both plans below are **our suggestion, not research**. We have no evidence that one pace works better than another. Use them as a starting frame and change them to fit your life.

## 1. Plan A: 12 weeks (about 3 chapters a week)

### Daily routine (suggested)

| Day | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday to Friday | Study one block: read the concept, work the solved examples, then do practice questions | 45 to 60 minutes a day |
| Saturday | Catch-up day: finish anything left over from the week; if you are on time, redo the mistakes in your error log | Up to 60 minutes, flexible |
| Sunday | Rest | none |

- Study five days a week, with Saturday kept free as a catch-up day.
- A simple split for a chapter: Day 1 concept and solved examples, Day 2 and Day 3 practice questions, then the next chapter. Adjust as needed.
- Do not look at the answer key until you have tried a question and written your own answer.

### Weekly checkpoint (do it on the last study day of the week)

1. For each chapter of the week, **redo its 3 self-check questions** (the ones at the start of the chapter) without looking at your notes. Then compare with the answer key. (Chapter 35, the mixed cases, has no self-check section: for that chapter, redo one case you got wrong instead.)
2. **Review your error log** (the system taught in Chapter 37): read the week's mistakes and write one line on why each happened.
3. Tick off the chapters finished. If a chapter feels weak, put it on next week's catch-up day, not in the main block.

Chapter 37 comes in Week 12, but you do not need to wait for it. From Week 1, keep a plain notebook or sheet with three columns: question, what I answered, what was right and why. In Week 12 you will turn it into the full template.

### Mock tests (Weeks 9 to 12)

- Two mocks a week: one early in the week, one late in the week. Each is 30 minutes, 15 questions, timed.
- Take a mock in one sitting with a timer. Mark afterwards, not during.
- After each mock, add every wrong or guessed question to your error log with the reason.
- Mocks add about 60 minutes a week (two 30-minute tests) on top of chapter study in Weeks 9 to 12. Take them on the catch-up day or extend a study day.

## 2. Plan B: slower pace (about one chapter every 3 to 4 days)

The index suggests one chapter every 3 to 4 days, about 5 months. This is **our suggestion, not research**. We computed the length in Python instead of trusting the rounded figure.

| Pace | Days per chapter | Total days | Total weeks | Approx months (30-day months) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fast end | 3 | 111 | 15.9 | 3.7 |
| Middle (used here) | 3.5 | 129.5 | 18.5 | 4.3 |
| Slow end | 4 | 148 | 21.1 | 4.9 |

- At 3.5 days per chapter, 37 chapters take 129.5 days, which is 18.5 weeks, or about 4.3 months.
- The index says "about 5 months". The two numbers differ because the index is a round figure. If you add rest days and some slip, 5 months is a fair planning number. At the slow end (4 days per chapter) the total is 148 days, about 4.9 months.
- To use 3.5 days a chapter in practice, alternate: odd chapters get 3 days, even chapters get 4 days. That gives 129 days, about 18.4 weeks.

- Keep the same weekly checkpoint as Plan A (3 self-check questions per chapter and an error log review).
- Take the 8 mocks inside Chapter 36's slot and the weeks just before it: two a week is a good limit. Do not take all 8 in one week.
- Plan B suits you if you are working full time, have less than 45 minutes on most days, or want more rest. The content is the same; only the speed changes.

### Which plan to choose

| If this describes you | Try |
|---|---|
| You can give 45 to 60 minutes five days a week | Plan A |
| You can give 20 to 30 minutes on most days | Plan B |
| You are not sure | Start Plan A for Weeks 1 and 2; if you finish fewer than 2 chapters in a week, switch to Plan B without guilt |

## 3. Rules for when you are behind schedule

1. **Use the catch-up day first.** Saturday is there for exactly this. Missing one day is normal and needs no fix.
2. **Do not double up the next week.** Cramming 6 chapters into one week usually leads to weak understanding. Shift the whole plan one week later instead.
3. **Protect the order of chapters.** Parts 1 and 2 are the base for everything after them. If you must cut something, cut practice volume, never skip a chapter's concept and solved examples.
4. **Cut practice from the back.** In a chapter with many questions (for example 40), do the easy ones (🟢) and the medium ones (🟡) first. Do the hard ones (🔴) on a later catch-up day. Always do some of each level before moving on.
5. **Keep the weekly checkpoint, even if short.** If you are very short on time, do only the 3 self-check questions and a 10-minute error log review. This keeps the habit alive.
6. **Never skip the error log.** Writing down why you got a question wrong is how mistakes stop repeating. It takes only a few minutes.
7. **If you are more than 2 weeks behind**, switch from Plan A to Plan B and restart the calendar from the chapter you are on. Do not try to catch up by studying longer every day.
8. **Mocks are the last things to cut.** If behind in Weeks 9 to 12, reduce chapter practice questions first and keep at least one mock per week.
9. **If a chapter is hard**, give it one extra day and ask one question: which sentence in the concept section did I not understand? Re-read only that part.
10. **Stuck on one question for 10 minutes?** Look at the answer key, then rewrite the solution from memory the next day, and put it in the error log.

## 4. Reminder

These schedules are our own suggestion. We make no claim that following them gives a particular result. Adapt the times and order to your own energy and life.
