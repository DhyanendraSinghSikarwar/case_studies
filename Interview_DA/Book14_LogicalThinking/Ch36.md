## How to use these mock tests

Every mock test in this chapter has 15 questions and is built to be done in **30 minutes**. All questions are new for this chapter (none repeat the practice questions in Chapters 1 to 34). Names and numbers are invented and labelled illustrative. For interview-style timed sets, see Book 13 Part 11.

**A note on advice.** Everything below about timing, skipping and reviewing is **our suggestion**, based on how beginners can organise a short test. It is **not research** and we make no claim that it raises scores. Adjust it to your own style and to the rules of any real test you will take.

### Our suggested routine

| Step | What to do | Our suggestion |
|---|---|---|
| Set up | Clear a desk, use a timer, paper and pencil, no phone, no answer key open | 30 minutes, one sitting |
| Time per question | 15 questions in 30 minutes is **2 minutes per question on average** | About 1 minute for a 🟢, 2 for a 🟡, 3 for a 🔴 (roughly 30 in total) |
| Mark and skip | If you have no clear plan after about 3 minutes, write a "?" next to the question number and move on | Come back to marked questions after you have reached the end |
| Answer everything? | The mocks here have no negative marking, so a reasoned guess costs nothing | Real tests may differ: check the rules of the test you are preparing for |
| Stop | When the timer ends, put the pencil down | Keep your answers as they are, do not edit afterwards |
| Review | Open the answer key only now. Mark each question right, wrong or skipped. Read the full reasoning for every wrong or skipped one, and also for any right one you guessed | Redo the question without looking, a day later |
| Log errors | Write each wrong or skipped question in your mistake log (see below) | Chapter 37 gives the full error-log and review system |

### The difficulty emoji

- 🟢 easy: one idea, one or two steps.
- 🟡 medium: two ideas or a few steps; needs care.
- 🔴 hard: several steps, a small puzzle, or a trap to avoid.

Inside each mock the questions run from easy to hard, so the first questions are the quickest. A suggested order: do the 🟢 ones first and carefully, since they are the points you should not lose.

### Scoring

One point per correct answer, 15 per mock, 120 in total. For multi-part questions (marked (a), (b), (c) or "and"), count the question as correct only if every part is right.

### Logging errors (to be built out in Chapter 37)

For every wrong or skipped question, write one line: mock and question number, what went wrong, the type of error, and the fix. Useful error types: misread the question, wrong rule, arithmetic slip, ran out of time, guessed, did not know the method. A simple row looks like this:

| Mock and question | What went wrong | Error type | Fix for next time |
|---|---|---|---|
| M3-Q6 (example) | Treated "some" as "all" | Wrong rule | Re-read Chapter 12, redo with a Venn diagram |

## Mock 1

**Focus:** Parts 1 and 2 (logic foundations and organising information): statements, premises and conclusions, AND/OR/NOT, if-then, quantifiers, Venn diagrams, odd one out, analogies, series, tables, flowcharts and decision trees, valid and invalid arguments.

**Time:** 30 minutes. **Questions:** 15 (5 🟢, 6 🟡, 4 🔴).

**M1-Q1** 🟢 Which of these are statements (something that is true or false), and which statement is false? (a) "Please submit the form by Friday." (b) "The office has 4 floors." (c) "Is the HR desk open?" (d) "7 + 5 = 13." (e) "Tea is better than coffee."

**M1-Q2** 🟢 "Our last three newsletters each had an open rate above 40%, so the next one will probably also be above 40%." (Illustrative.) (i) What is the conclusion? (ii) Is the reasoning deduction or induction?

**M1-Q3** 🟢 Rule (illustrative): a leave request is auto-approved when the leave balance is at least 3 days AND the request is NOT in a blackout week. Five requests (balance in days, blackout week?): R1 (5, no), R2 (2, no), R3 (4, yes), R4 (3, no), R5 (6, yes). How many are auto-approved?

**M1-Q4** 🟢 Of 60 employees (illustrative), 35 use Excel, 28 use SQL and 9 use both. How many use neither?

**M1-Q5** 🟢 Find the odd one out: Invoice, Credit note, Purchase order, Warehouse.

**M1-Q6** 🟡 Find the next number: 11, 4, 14, 8, 17, 12, 20, ?

**M1-Q7** 🟡 Rule: "If an invoice is above 50,000, then a director must sign it." For each invoice say what, if anything, follows. (i) Invoice 1 is for 72,000. (ii) Invoice 2 has no director signature. (iii) Invoice 3 has a director signature.

**M1-Q8** 🟡 Negate: "Every candidate answered all the questions." Which is the exact opposite? (a) No candidate answered all the questions. (b) Some candidate did not answer all the questions. (c) Every candidate skipped some question. (d) Some candidate answered no question.

**M1-Q9** 🟡 Of 100 customers (illustrative), 50 bought product A, 40 bought B, 30 bought C; 15 bought both A and B, 10 both B and C, 12 both A and C, and 5 bought all three. How many bought exactly one product, and how many bought none?

**M1-Q10** 🟡 Fuel gauge : Fuel level :: Odometer : ? Options: (a) Speed (b) Distance travelled (c) Engine heat (d) Tyre pressure.

**M1-Q11** 🟡 A refund flowchart (illustrative) works like this. Step 1: was the order delivered? No: refund the full price. Yes: go to Step 2. Step 2: is it within 7 days of delivery (day 7 counts as within)? No: refund 0. Yes: go to Step 3. Step 3: is the item damaged? Yes: full refund. No: refund the price minus a 10% restocking fee. Orders: A (not delivered, price 1,500); B (delivered 5 days ago, damaged, 2,400); C (delivered 5 days ago, not damaged, 1,000); D (delivered 12 days ago, 3,000); E (delivered 7 days ago, not damaged, 2,000). What is the total refund?

**M1-Q12** 🔴 Four staff (illustrative names) Mira, Joel, Kavi and Lena each work in a different department (Sales, Legal, Ops, IT) and sit on a different floor (1 to 4). Clues: (1) Mira is not in Sales and not in IT. (2) The Legal person sits on floor 1. (3) Kavi sits on floor 4 and is not in Ops. (4) The IT person sits exactly one floor above the Ops person. (5) Lena is not in Legal. (6) Mira does not sit on floor 1. (7) Lena sits on a higher floor than Mira. Who is in IT, and on which floor?

**M1-Q13** 🔴 A workshop was run on two days for 90 staff (illustrative). 55 staff attended on Monday and 50 attended on Tuesday; some may have attended both days, and some may have attended neither. What is the smallest and the largest possible number of staff who attended neither day?

**M1-Q14** 🔴 Which of these four arguments are valid (the conclusion must be true whenever the premises are)? (A) If the van is late, the delivery is missed. The delivery was missed. So the van was late. (B) If the van is late, the delivery is missed. The delivery was not missed. So the van was not late. (C) If the van is late, the delivery is missed. The van was not late. So the delivery was not missed. (D) Either the van is late or the rider is absent. The van was not late. So the rider is absent.

**M1-Q15** 🔴 Each row follows the same rule using its first two numbers to make the third. Row 1: (4, 3, 13). Row 2: (5, 2, 23). Row 3: (7, 6, 43). Row 4: (6, 5, ?). Find the missing number.

**M2-Q13** 🔴 In a survey of 90 employees (illustrative), 40 like option A, 35 like option B and 30 like option C. Pair counts include people who like all three: 14 like both A and B, 11 like both B and C, 10 like both A and C. 10 employees like none of the three. How many like all three, and how many like exactly two?

## Mock 6

**Focus:** Part 4, critical thinking: fallacies, correlation versus causation, cognitive biases, evaluating claims and sources, misleading charts and statistics.

## Mistake log

Mistakes that often happen in timed tests (and what to do about each):

1. **Misreading the question.** Reading "which does NOT follow" as "which follows", or missing a part (b). Underline the question word before you start.
2. **Spending too long on one question.** A hard question can eat the time of three easier ones. Mark it with "?" and move on, as in our suggested rule.
3. **Skipping the check.** Many wrong answers can be caught by putting the answer back into the clues. For puzzles, test each clue once.
4. **Treating "some" as "all", or a converse as the original.** Slow down on wording questions; draw a quick diagram.
5. **Rushing the easy questions to save time for hard ones.** The 🟢 questions are the points you should not lose; a careless slip there costs as much as missing a 🔴.

## Recap

1. Each mock is 15 questions in 30 minutes, about 2 minutes per question on average (our suggestion, not research).
2. Do the easy questions first and carefully; mark with "?" and skip anything that has no clear plan after about 3 minutes.
3. Open the answer key only after the timer ends, and read the full reasoning for every wrong, skipped or guessed question.
4. Log every miss with an error type and a fix; Chapter 37 shows how to turn the log into a review routine.
5. Track your own trend from Mock 1 to Mock 8; compare yourself with your earlier self, not with anyone else.
