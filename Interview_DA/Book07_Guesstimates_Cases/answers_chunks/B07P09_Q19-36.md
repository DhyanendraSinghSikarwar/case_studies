### Q19 ⭐⭐ 🟢 Do you need a consulting background or an MBA to do well in case and guesstimate rounds?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a generic opener for career switchers, mainly HR or manager rounds (unverified). Internal research says Mu Sigma, Fractal, Flipkart and Meesho-type rounds score structure and explicit assumptions, not a degree.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you are honest about your background and confident that the skill is learnable.
**Say this in the interview:** "No. A case round tests a habit: clarify, structure, state assumptions, calculate, recommend. I built that habit through practice, and my HR work already trains me to look at people data in a structured way."
**Explain simply:**

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Then why do people say it helps?* More reps and more exposure to frameworks; both can be built. 2) *What is your gap?* Less industry exposure, so I read about one company a week.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Apologising for your background; claiming it is easy; memorising frameworks instead of practising.

### Q20 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you use AI assistants to practise cases and guesstimates without depending on them?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a modern prep question, unverified as an interview question.
**What the interviewer is checking:** That you use AI as a sparring partner, and that your own reasoning stays intact.
**Say this in the interview:** "I attempt the problem first on paper, then ask the AI to critique my structure and find missing branches. I never let it write the first answer."
**Explain simply:**
- Rule 1: solve first, ask second. Take 10 minutes alone, then compare with the AI.
- Rule 2: ask it to play interviewer: "Ask me clarifying-style follow-ups, do not give answers."
- Rule 3: ask it to find holes: "Is my tree MECE (no overlaps, nothing missing)? What assumption is weakest?"
- Rule 4: re-check every number yourself. AI can state wrong figures confidently, so treat its facts as unverified.
- Mini-example: you estimate daily absentees as 1,200 staff x 3% = 36. Ask the AI to challenge 3%, then decide yourself whether 2% to 5% is fair.
- Dependence check: once a week do a case with no AI, timed, and score it.
**Practice step:** Keep a log with two columns: "my answer" and "what the AI added"; the second column should shrink over time.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Would you use AI in the interview?* Only if allowed; I practise so I do not need it. 2) *What if AI disagrees?* I test both with a quick sanity check.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Copying its framework; skipping the solve-first step; trusting its statistics.

### Q21 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you find your weak spots in case solving and fix them week by week?

### Q22 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How would you prepare for a guesstimate and case round in two weeks?

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if you have one week?* Drop depth, keep one method per type and daily mocks. 2) *What do you skip?* Rare frameworks.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Reading frameworks for 10 days and practising on day 13; no mock before the real round.

### Q25 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you practise the answer-first (Pyramid Principle) style when explaining findings?

### Q28 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you check that your issue tree is MECE and not just a list of ideas?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can split a problem so nothing overlaps and nothing is missing.
**Say this in the interview:** "I split by one rule only, then check for overlap and gaps, and I test it with real data if I can."
**Explain simply:**
- MECE means Mutually Exclusive (no overlaps) and Collectively Exhaustive (nothing missing).
- Check 1: one splitting rule per level (tenure, or department, not both).
- Check 2: can one item sit in two branches? If yes, overlap.
- Check 3: do the branches add up to the whole? Mini-example: 240 leavers split by tenure: under 1 year 120, 1 to 3 years 80, over 3 years 40; 120 + 80 + 40 = 240. Complete and no overlap.
- Anti-example: "Pay, culture, manager, Bangalore office" mixes causes and a place, so it is only a list of ideas.
- Add an "other" branch if unsure, then try to shrink it.
**Practice step:** Take 5 trees from past practice and test each with the three checks.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Is perfect MECE needed?* Aim for it; useful and close beats perfect. 2) *Which splits are safe?* Numeric ranges and formulas (revenue = users x spend).
**Common mistakes / traps:** Mixing two rules at one level; ten branches; no numbers behind the tree.

### Q29 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you practise mental arithmetic with rounded numbers so that speed does not hurt accuracy in a guesstimate?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); round numbers and stated assumptions are standard guesstimate advice on prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Comfortable, fast arithmetic and awareness of how much rounding costs.
**Say this in the interview:** "I round to friendly numbers, keep track of which way I rounded, and check the error is small."
**Explain simply:**
- Friendly numbers: 25% = one quarter, 10% = shift a decimal, 1.4 billion = 140 crore.
- Mini-example: 1,380 x 24%. Round to 1,400 x 25% = 350. Exact is 331.2, so the rounding error is about 5.7%, fine for a guesstimate. Rounded up on both numbers, so the true value is a bit lower.
- Offsetting: round one number up and another down so errors cancel.
- Drills: 10 minutes a day, ten multiplications and divisions with Indian units (lakh, crore); then check against a calculator.
- Write the unit at every step.
**Practice step:** 10 minutes daily, tracking how many land within 10% of exact.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *How much error is acceptable?* Within about 10% for estimates. 2) *What is 15% of 840?* 84 + 42 = 126.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Rounding every number up; lakh/crore slips; false precision such as 331.2 on a guess.
