### Q51 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Assume an online grocery app has 8 lakh monthly users in a city, 40% order in a month, and an ordering user places 3 orders at Rs 600 each. What is the monthly gross order value in crores?
(These numbers are exercise inputs, not facts.)
**Asked at:** an online-grocery market question is listed on a practice platform as a Tredence business analytics practice item (search summary only, unverified); this version with inline numbers is an exercise.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Clean unit handling (lakh, crore) and a funnel chain.
**Say this in the interview:** "Users who order, times orders, times basket value. That is Rs 57.6 crore of gross order value a month."
**Definition in plain words:** Gross order value (GOV) is the total value of all orders placed before discounts, cancellations or returns are removed. Formula: GOV = ordering users x orders per user x average order value.
**Worked answer:**
1. Ordering users = 8 lakh x 40% = 3.2 lakh.
2. Orders = 3.2 lakh x 3 = 9.6 lakh.
3. GOV = 9.6 lakh x Rs 600 = Rs 57.6 crore (1 crore = 100 lakh; 9.6 lakh x 600 = Rs 5.76 crore x 10 = Rs 57.6 crore).

**Result:** 3.2 lakh ordering users, 9.6 lakh orders, Rs 57.6 crore a month.
**Check:** Per monthly user: 57.6 crore / 8 lakh = Rs 720 per user per month, which equals 40% x 3 x 600. Edge test: at 30% ordering, Rs 43.2 crore; at 50%, Rs 72 crore.
**Caveats:** GOV is not revenue (the app keeps only a margin or fee); "3 orders" is an average among orderers; "monthly users" may mean app opens, not buyers.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Annual?* Rs 57.6 crore x 12 = Rs 691 crore, ignoring seasonality. 2) *Which lever matters?* All three multiply, so a 10% gain in any one raises GOV 10%.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Treating 40% as 40 lakh; lakh to crore slips; applying 3 orders to all 8 lakh users.

### Q53 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you size a market when you can only estimate the number of potential customers and not their spending?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a method question.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can borrow a price from somewhere defensible.
**Say this in the interview:** "I size customers first, then get spend from a proxy: a similar product's price, what they pay now for the problem, or a willingness-to-pay survey, and present a low-base-high range."
**Explain simply:**

- Ways to get spend: (1) analogue, the price of a similar product or service; (2) current spend on the substitute, since people rarely pay more than they pay now; (3) value created (cost saved or revenue gained) and a fair share of it, say 10 to 20%; (4) a quick survey or price-ladder test with a sample.
- Give three scenarios and say which input you would verify first.

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Free product?* Size advertising or paid upgrade revenue instead. 2) *Which assumption matters most?* Price and adoption multiply, so test both.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Reporting only customers; using price of premium brands for all; ignoring adoption share.

### Q54 ⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you adjust a market-size estimate for seasonality, such as festival months?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a method question.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you avoid multiplying one month by 12.
**Say this in the interview:** "I split the year into peak and normal months with a seasonal index, then add them, instead of scaling one month by 12."
**Explain simply:**
- A seasonal index is a month's sales divided by the normal month. A festival month at 1.5 means 50% above normal.
- Steps: estimate a normal month; set an index for each month group; annual = normal month x sum of indices.
- If your data is from a festival month, divide by its index to find the normal month first.
- Say what the festival does: more volume, higher basket, or just shifting demand (pull-forward) from the next month.

**Worked mini-example:** Normal month Rs 100 crore; two festival months at 1.5 = 150. Annual = 10 x 100 + 2 x 150 = Rs 1,300 crore. Naive 12 x 150 = 1,800 (38% too high); naive 12 x 100 = 1,200 (about 8% too low). Illustrative numbers.
**Practice step:** Take any monthly sales or attendance series and compute each month divided by the yearly average.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Festival dates move?* Use a festival-based window, not calendar months. 2) *HR example?* Hiring and attendance dips around festivals.
**Common mistakes / traps:** Annualising a peak month; forgetting the post-festival dip; one index for all categories (sweets and cement differ).

### Q65 ⭐⭐ 🔴 How do you estimate the market size for a product that does not exist yet?
