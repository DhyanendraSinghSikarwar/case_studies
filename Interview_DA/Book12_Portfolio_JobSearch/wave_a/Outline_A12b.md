## Section 3: LinkedIn and Naukri profile optimisation

### 3.1 Purpose and honest limits (about 6 lines)
- What a profile does: it lets recruiters find you by search and lets people you message check you in 30 seconds. Role of each site: B0 Q101 (do not repeat; point to it).
- No reliable data found on: Naukri ranking formula, effect of headline wording, effect of Open to Work, callback rate by profile completeness. B12S "No reliable data found".
- Source access: Naukri/LinkedIn pages were not opened; only help/blog snippets (B12S). MS Section 2: Naukri 403, LinkedIn not fetched.
- Decision rule to teach instead of data: change one thing at a time, wait 2 weeks, read the tracker (Book 12 Section 6).

### 3.2 Keywords from job descriptions (core method, about 10 lines)
- Method (our own, not sourced): save 10-15 real JDs for one target title, list every tool/skill named, count, put top terms in headline, skills, summary, and project lines only where true.
- Table to include: skill frequency from MS pooled n=186 (dashboards/reporting 75%, communication 73%, Excel 60%, SQL 54%, stakeholder 44%, Power BI 40%, Tableau 33%, Python 32%, statistics 23%); caveat non-random, about +/-7 points (MS Addendum).
- Worked example to build: a table of 3 sample JD snippets (illustrative, labelled as such) turned into a keyword list and then into a headline.
- Role-title variants to search and to mention: MIS, reporting analyst, operations analyst, associate analyst (MS Section 6, B0 Q64; point, do not repeat).
- Rule: use only terms you can defend in an interview. No hidden keyword text, no copying skills you have not used (say this in the guide explicitly; ties to Book 11).

### 3.3 LinkedIn profile, field by field (about 14 lines)
- Headline: title-first plus 2-3 core skills; for a switcher, say direction honestly (e.g. "HR Reporting and People Analytics | Excel, SQL, Power BI"). Source: vendor guides, unverified (B12S).
- Build a before/after table with 3 headlines for the same HR person (weak: "HR Professional seeking opportunities"; better; best), clearly labelled illustrative.
- About: 3 short paragraphs (who you are, what you built, what you want); worked example for an HR-to-analytics switcher with a number from a public/synthetic project only. No employer data (rule).
- Experience entries: rewrite HR duties as reporting achievements without changing titles/dates/employers; cross-point to Section 1 translation table (A12a owns).
- Skills: LinkedIn help snippet says 100-skill cap in the Skills section (snippet only; check live). Pin the 3-5 that match JDs. Endorsements: no reliable data on effect.
- Featured/Projects: link GitHub, Power BI/Tableau Public pages from Section 2 (A12a owns projects).
- Photo, custom URL, location (Hyderabad/Bengaluru), banner: low-priority checklist; no data, so present as hygiene.
- "Open to Work": options all members vs recruiters only; LinkedIn says it cannot guarantee privacy from recruiters at your current company (official help snippet, B12S). Decision rule: if currently employed in HR, choose recruiters only or off, and say why.
- Posting content: optional; no reliable data found on effect; rule: post a project write-up once, not daily.

### 3.4 Naukri profile, field by field (about 12 lines)
- Resume headline, key skills, profile summary, employment and project entries, resume upload (parser-friendly format: simple layout, no tables/text boxes; third-party tip, unverified).
- Completeness: official blog advice to complete profile and update regularly (snippet only); "100% = 3x views" is unverified, do not use.
- Freshness: Naukri recruiter FAQ says resume freshness reflects when the candidate was last active and recruiters can filter on it (official snippet, 2016 page). Cadence tips (every 2-3 days, weekly) are third-party and unverified; official snippet says at least once in 6 months. Decision rule: genuine update weekly while searching; do not use automation tools (terms-of-service risk unverified).
- Privacy: Block Companies setting and "not searchable" option (official snippets); Naukri says it cannot guarantee full concealment. Table: setting, what it does, what it does not do.
- Notice period, current/expected CTC fields: how to fill honestly; point to Book 11 Part 8 (salary negotiation).
- Consistency: same titles, dates and employers on resume, LinkedIn, Naukri.

### 3.5 Search behaviour and diagnosis (about 8 lines)
- How recruiters search: keyword and filter-based; LinkedIn Recruiter has title/location/skills filters and an "Open to work" filter (LinkedIn marketing page; vendor-stated, B12S).
- Diagnosis table: symptom (no views / views but no calls / calls for wrong roles) -> likely cause (keywords, title mismatch, location, notice period) -> one change. All rules are ours, labelled "decision rule, not measured".
- Job alerts and saved searches: one setup walk-through per platform (labels may differ; check live).
- Worked example: reading a week of tracker rows to decide which keyword to add.

## Section 4: Referrals, cold outreach, recruiters, networking, consultancies

### 4.1 Purpose and honest limits (about 6 lines)
- Referral = an employee submits or recommends your profile for a named opening (concept; B0 Q74 covers "no network", do not repeat, point to it).
- No reliable data found on: referral conversion for switchers, cold-message reply rates, best follow-up timing, share of analyst hires via referral or consultancies. Trade-press examples (TCS, Infosys, Home Credit referral schemes, 2025) show that companies run referral programmes and often pay bonuses in stages (snippet only; amounts are for tech roles, do not quote as expected for analysts).
- Section is process and etiquette, not a promise.

### 4.2 Who to approach and in what order (about 8 lines)
- Tiers: (1) people you know (HR colleagues, recruiters you worked with, vendors, college alumni); (2) second-degree contacts in analytics at target companies; (3) strangers (cold). Our rule: start with tier 1, which an HR professional already has (advantage; ties to B0 Section 5 HR-as-advantage).
- Build a target list of 20 companies from saved JDs (MS Appendix A names as examples only; do not claim they hire switchers).
- Table: contact type, what to ask, what not to ask.

### 4.3 Message templates (about 16 lines; every template labelled illustrative and sized for a short message)
- LinkedIn connection note (check the live counter: vendor sources say 200 characters standard, 300 older/Premium, free-account note quota conflicting; B12S, unverified).
- Referral request to a known person; to a second-degree contact after one advice chat (B0 Q74 "Say this in your message" is the base; extend, do not copy).
- 15-minute advice request (two specific questions, no job ask).
- Cold email to a recruiter or hiring manager (subject line, 4-6 lines, attach resume, one link).
- Reply to an inbound recruiter message: thank, ask JD and pay band, state notice period honestly, offer two call slots. Source: general blog advice (unverified).
- Follow-up: one nudge after about 5-7 days, then stop (vendor/blog advice, unverified). Thank-you and outcome update message.
- Template rule list: name the role, one reason you fit (a real project), one clear ask, no mass copy-paste, no attachments in first cold message.

### 4.4 Recruiter calls and the "why switch" answer (about 6 lines)
- First recruiter call checklist: role, team, tools, pay band range, notice period, location. Script for "Why are you moving from HR to analytics?" using the B0 story framework; point to Book 11 Parts 1-2 and Book 0 translation work (do not duplicate).
- Salary and notice questions: honesty rule; point to Book 11 Part 8.

### 4.5 Consultancies and staffing firms (about 10 lines)
- How it works: employer-pays model (vendor-stated, unverified); a consultancy may place you on its own payroll (contract) or the client's payroll (permanent). Table: questions to ask (who is my employer, contract length, notice, background verification, conversion to permanent).
- Fee red flags: employers (TCS, Michael Page) warn that candidates should not pay registration/placement fees (official snippets). Legal status of candidate fees is conflicting (a 2025 bill and a Maharashtra act per one vendor source); verify before quoting. Fee ranges quoted online are unverified. Rule: do not pay anyone to get an interview or job.
- Verifying a consultancy: website, address, company registration, employer confirmation from the hiring company's careers page (checklist, our rule).
- No reliable data found on which firms place switchers.

### 4.6 Etiquette, volume, ethics (about 6 lines)
- Volume: our suggestion of 3 messages a week matches B0 Q74 (that figure is a suggestion, not research).
- Do not ask someone to vouch for skills they have not seen; give a short work sample first (project link).
- Referral bonus: employees may earn one; ask politely, never offer money or favours. Never misrepresent experience or share employer data to impress anyone.
- Do not spam hiring managers; one follow-up then move on.
- Respect no-reply; record in tracker (Section 6).
