## Section 5: Certifications (PL-300, Google Data Analytics, others)

### What the section contains
- Opening verdict, stated honestly: no certification is named in any in-scope JD of the 40-JD sample (MS Section 5, Addendum); named only as advantages in a few postings (Tableau/Power BI cert, Google DA, MS Data Analyst Associate, CBAP). Value in India: **No reliable data found.** Treat as optional signalling, never a hiring filter, never above projects (plan.md Book 12 row 5).
- Order of work: Excel, SQL, portfolio first; a certificate after (plan.md). Pointers: Books 1, 2, 4; Section 2 of this book.
- Fact table "what each certificate is", every cell with source and "snippet only" tag:
  - Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate (Coursera): 9 courses, about 6 months at 10 hrs/week, no degree or experience required (Coursera page; SRC). Google India page says many finish in 3-6 months. Price: US/India pages conflict ($20 vs about $49/month seen); **do not quote a price**, reader checks Coursera in rupees. Pace claims are the provider's own.
  - PL-300 Microsoft Power BI Data Analyst Associate: official page, price depends on proctoring country; no official rupee figure found, third-party guesses conflict (unverified). Passing score 700 and "skills measured as of April 20, 2026" (official snippet). Renewal free by online assessment before expiry; if expired, re-earn (official FAQ snippet; B0 Q55 says 12-month renewal, fetched earlier).
  - Tableau Desktop Specialist: official fee $100 (snippet), no prerequisites, no expiry; exam length not verified. Only relevant if target JDs name Tableau (MS: Tableau about 33%, Power BI about 40% in n=186 sample).
  - DP-600 (Fabric Analytics Engineer) and CBAP-type credentials: mention as out of scope for a first role; DP-600 is more advanced (blog, unverified); CBAP only appeared in a 4-6 yr BA posting (MS).
- Decision rule (since no data): count how many of your 10 target JDs name a certificate; if 0-2, spend the hours on a project; if the target role names Power BI as required and you have a finished dashboard, PL-300 is a reasonable add; budget = fee + study hours + retake risk.
- Worked examples (illustrative, own numbers): 2-week choice between certificate and project; cost arithmetic using a placeholder fee the reader replaces; how to list a certificate on a resume (below projects, with date, "in progress" only if honestly true) and in a LinkedIn profile (cross-link Section 3).
- Honest limits: placement and salary-uplift claims for certificates are vendor marketing; a certificate shows exam knowledge, not ability to build; AI-era change in exam content (PL-300 outline updated April 2026) means older prep material may be stale.
- Do-not list: never list a certificate not earned; no exam dumps/proxy testing (deceptive, risks the credential); no paid "guaranteed certificate" bundles; never use employer data in practice projects.
- Cross-book: B0 Q55-Q57 (certificate vs projects, placement guarantees, bootcamps) are pointed to, not repeated; Book 4 (Power BI) for PL-300 prep content; Book 2 SQL first; Book 11 for interview wording ("why this certificate?").

## Section 6: Application tracker and weekly job-search routine; job-search hygiene

### What the section contains
- Why track: not because a statistic says so (none found), but because a decision rule from B0 needs data: after 10 applications with no replies, change the resume, not the target (B0 own rule of thumb, not sourced).
- Tracker design (blogs agree on 6-10 core columns; SRC): date, company, role, source (Naukri/LinkedIn/referral/career page/consultancy), JD link, resume version, status, contact, next action date, notes (reason/feedback). Build it in Excel (Book 1) or Google Sheets; include a small summary pivot and response-rate formulas (our worked example: replies / applications, by source and resume version). All numbers in the example are illustrative placeholders, labelled as such.
- Status vocabulary (Saved, Applied, Referral asked, Screen, Interview round n, Offer, Rejected, Ghosted, Withdrawn) and a follow-up rule: first nudge about 1-2 weeks after applying, at most 2-3 follow-ups (convention from career blogs; unverified; not data).
- Weekly routine (our own design, not research): a template week for a working HR person (for example 5 weekday evenings 1 hr + weekend blocks): apply to a fixed number of well-matched roles, outreach (Section 4), study/portfolio (Section 2), interview practice (Books 7, 8, 11), review the tracker Sunday. Count is a planning variable the reader sets; **No reliable data found** on how many applications are needed.
- Where to apply: Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed, company career pages, referrals, consultancies (Section 4); public board names only. Naukri/Indeed pages are vendor sources; Indeed notes its India data skews to tech/management/sales. No source ranks boards by hit rate: **No reliable data found**; decision rule is to track response by source in your own tracker for 4-6 weeks then reallocate.
- Volume vs quality: tailor top-fit JDs (match 3 resume lines per JD, B0 Q83 pointer); do not mass-apply with an untailored resume; never hide keywords or falsify experience.
- Rejection handling: reframe, ask for feedback once, log the stage at which you fell (resume screen / screening call / technical / HR), then fix that stage with the right book. Career-changer note: some filters are rigid JD requirements, not a verdict on you (CompTIA blog snippet).
- Ghosting and silence: normal; use the follow-up rule; set a cut-off.
- Notice period overlap (HR reader likely employed): contract governs notice (blogs; Labour Codes leave it to contract per one blog, unverified); buyout is the current employer's discretion; some employers reimburse buyouts (Instahyre blog, unverified); ask the new employer for a later start; never leave without relieving letter. Say plainly: not legal advice; read your own offer and contract; as an HR person the reader knows the policy, use it.
- Scam hygiene: a recruiter who asks for money is a red flag; verify on the official careers page; no OTP/remote-access apps; WhatsApp-only offers with no interview; report via cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 (Indian company/media pages, snippet only).
- Confidentiality hygiene: do not discuss your search in your office, do not use official email/laptop, keep your employer's data out of portfolio and interviews; set LinkedIn "Open to work" to recruiters-only if you are employed (feature wording to be checked by A12b).
- Burnout and pacing: scheduled rest, cap on daily applications; evidence is general career-centre advice, not India data.
