### Q81 ⭐ 🟡 How do you keep a single document or thread so that everyone refers to the same version of the numbers?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Do you stop people quoting different versions of the same number?
**Say this in the interview:** "I keep one source of truth, put a version and date on it, and tell everyone where it lives. If a number changes, I say what changed and why."

**Explain simply:**
- The problem is not the number, it is five copies of the number in five emails.
- One shared file or one pinned message is the home. Everything else points to it.
- Every version gets a label: what it covers, as-of date, who owns it.
- A change log says what changed since last time, so nobody quotes the old figure by mistake.

**Plan / steps:**
1. Pick one place (a shared sheet, a shared folder, or one pinned thread) and tell everyone.
2. Name files plainly: "Attrition_Report_Oct2026_v2", with an as-of date inside the file.
3. Put a short "what changed" note at the top of each new version.
4. When you send a number by email or chat, link to the home, do not paste a different copy.
5. If an old version is wrong, say so openly and point to the new one.

**Worked example or script:** "Quick note: the attrition figure for October is now in the shared tracker, version 2, as of 5 Nov. It changed from 8.4% to 8.7% because two late exit entries were added. Please use the tracker, not the earlier email."

**Likely follow-ups:**
- *What if someone keeps using the old file?* Politely message them the link and the one-line change note; do not shame them in a group thread.
- *Which tool?* Whatever the team already uses; the habit matters more than the tool.

**Common mistakes:** Saving "final_final2" files, emailing attachments instead of links, and changing a number without saying so.

### Q84 ⭐ 🟡 How do you present a finding that has no clear recommendation yet, only a hypothesis?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Can you be honest about uncertainty and still be useful?
**Say this in the interview:** "I would say clearly it is a hypothesis, show why I think so, say what would confirm it, and suggest a small next step."

**Explain simply:**
- A hypothesis is a reasoned guess the data supports but does not yet prove.
- Say so in the first sentence, so nobody hears it as a conclusion.
- Give the evidence for, what we do not know, and a cheap way to test it.
- Do not force a recommendation you do not have; recommend the next step instead.

**Plan / steps:**
1. Lead with the pattern: "We see X."
2. Label it: "My working explanation is Y; it is not confirmed."
3. Give the evidence and the gap: "It fits A and B, but we have not checked C."
4. Propose the test: "Two weeks of exit-interview coding would tell us."
5. Ask for a decision on the next step only, with an owner and a date.

**Worked example or script:** "Exits among people with under one year of service are 14 out of 40, which is 35%. My working explanation is weak induction, but that is a hypothesis, not proven. It fits the exit reasons, but we have no induction feedback yet. I suggest we collect it from the last 40 joiners over two weeks before changing anything." (Invented numbers.)

**Likely follow-ups:**
- *Why present something unconfirmed?* Because the pattern is real and the next step is cheap.
- *What if they want a firm answer?* "I can give you a confident view in two weeks; today it would be a guess."

**Common mistakes:** Dressing a guess as a fact, hiding it in jargon, or offering no next step.

### Q85 ⭐ 🟡 What is your approach to writing a one-page summary of an analysis, and what do you put on it?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Can you put the point on one page, in the order a busy reader needs?
**Say this in the interview:** "Answer first, then the three numbers that matter, then what I need from the reader. Details go to the back."

**Explain simply:**
- One page means a reader can act without calling you.
- Order matters: answer, evidence, risks, action.
- Plain headings, short lines, one small chart or table at most.

**Plan / steps:**
1. Title that states the finding: "Overtime cost is concentrated in one plant."
2. Headline: one or two lines with the answer.
3. Key numbers: three or four, each with its comparison.
4. What it means: one or two lines.
5. Limits: data period, what is missing, how sure you are.
6. Recommended action and a named owner with a date.
7. Footer: source, as-of date, your contact.

**Worked example or script:** Title: "One plant drives about 40% of overtime cost." Headline: "Plant B accounts for Rs 3.1 lakh of Rs 7.8 lakh overtime this month (39.7%, about 40%)." Limits: "Two weeks of sheets still pending." Action: "Review Plant B shift roster by 30 Nov (owner: plant HR)." (Invented numbers, share computed by script.)

**Likely follow-ups:**
- *What if there is too much to say?* Cut to what changes a decision; move the rest to an appendix.
- *Who checks it?* Read it aloud to a colleague outside the project.

**Common mistakes:** Starting with method, putting five charts on one page, and burying the action.

2. Week 1 to 2: talk to five or six readers for 15 minutes each: "What do you use it for? What is unclear? What do you ignore?"

5. End of month: share a short note of what you learned and what you will change next.

**Worked example or script:** "In my first month I would interview five managers about the monthly attrition report. **Likely follow-ups:**
- *What if people are too busy?* Ask for 15 minutes or sit in on a meeting where the report is used.
- *How will you know it worked?* Fewer clarifying questions, and managers acting on the report.

**Common mistakes:** Redesigning reports alone, promising big changes early, and not asking users.

### Q88 ⭐ 🟡 What is the difference between informing, persuading and advising in data communication, and which one is your job?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Do you know the boundary between reporting facts, arguing for a view, and recommending action?
**Say this in the interview:** "Informing is what the data shows. Advising is what I would suggest, with reasons. Persuading is pushing for a decision. As an analyst, mostly I inform and advise, and I persuade with evidence when asked, but the decision stays with the business."

**Explain simply:**
- **Inform:** "Attrition rose from 6.0% to 8.7%."
- **Advise:** "I suggest we look at the two plants where most exits happened."
- **Persuade:** "I think we should act now, here is why." It needs strong evidence and openness to being wrong.
- Mixing them without saying which one you are doing confuses listeners.

**Plan / steps:**
1. Say clearly which part is fact and which is your view ("the data shows" versus "I recommend").
2. Always inform first; advise only after the facts are clear.
3. Persuade only on matters where you have solid evidence and the decision needs a push.
4. Respect that the decision belongs to the business owner.

**Worked example or script:** "The data shows [fact: 27 leavers on average headcount 310, so 8.7%]. I would advise checking exit reasons at two plants. If we do nothing, [risk, if supported]. The decision on action is yours." (Numbers from the Q24 exercise; 27/310 = 8.7%.)

**Likely follow-ups:**
- *Which is your job?* Inform and advise; persuasion only with clear evidence.
- *Can you advise without authority?* Yes, give a recommendation and reasons, then leave the decision to them.

**Common mistakes:** Presenting an opinion as data, or staying silent on a recommendation when asked.

### Q92 ⭐ 🔴 Someone in the room asks, "Why should I trust the data team's numbers at all?" What do you say?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Can you answer a challenge to your credibility without getting defensive?
**Say this in the interview:** "That is a fair question. I would not ask for trust; I would show how the numbers are checked and invite them to check one themselves."
