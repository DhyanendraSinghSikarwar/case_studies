**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if your manager was unavailable?* I would go to the next person or the process owner, and document it. 2) *Have you ever escalated too late?* Answer honestly and say what you changed.
**Common mistakes:** Escalating with panic and no facts; hiding a serious issue; blaming someone in the escalation.

### Q75 ⭐ 🟡 Tell me about a time you were given more work than you could handle. What did you do?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); workload and prioritising is a common prompt (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you prioritise, communicate early, and negotiate instead of silently failing or silently overworking.
**Say this in the interview:** "I list everything with effort and deadline, agree the priority order with my manager, and say clearly what will move."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I just worked late every day and did everything."
**Why it is weak:** It is not sustainable, and quality or health usually suffers.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I had [number] requests worth about [hours] against about [capacity] hours. I listed each with its deadline and impact, and showed my manager the list. We agreed that [A, B] come first and [C, D] move to [date] or go to [someone]. I told the people affected before the deadline, not after. I delivered [result]." Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** In one week I had 5 requests worth 12, 10, 9, 8 and 6 hours, so 45 hours in total, but only 30 hours were free: 15 hours too many. With my manager I kept the 12-hour payroll check, the 10-hour audit file and the 8-hour offer-letter batch (12 + 10 + 8 = 30), and moved the 9-hour and 6-hour reports to next week with a message to their owners.
**STAR breakdown:** Situation: more work than capacity. Task: deliver what matters. Action: listed, estimated, agreed priorities, communicated early. Result: key work delivered, others rescheduled with agreement.
**If you have no direct example:** Use exams plus a part-time job, or a course with assignments. Or describe the method: "I list tasks, estimate hours, rank by deadline and impact, and ask my manager to confirm."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if everything is urgent?* I ask the manager which one hurts most if late, and explain the trade-off. 2) *Did you ask for help?* Yes, early, with a clear request.
**Common mistakes:** Saying yes to everything; not giving hours or numbers; telling people only after missing a deadline.
