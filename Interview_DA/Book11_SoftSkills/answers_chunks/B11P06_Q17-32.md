5. Check the result against common sense and a known number.
6. Share early (a draft or a status), ask for feedback, then finish and write what you did.

**Worked example or script:** "Say a manager asks why overtime cost rose (invented). I would ask: which months, which sites, what is the decision? Then I would check the payroll and attendance files, split overtime by site and month, test one site first, check the total against payroll, and show a draft: 'Most of the rise is in one site; I need the shift roster to confirm why.'"
Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below: present the steps as your method, not as past events.

**Likely follow-ups:**
- What if you are not sure where to start? "I start with the data owner and the smallest testable question."
- When do you tell the requester? "Early, with a short update on what I have found and what I need."

**Common mistakes:** Jumping into the data without asking the real question; going silent until the deadline; no check of results; presenting guesses as findings.

### Q32 ⭐⭐ 🟡 Tell me about a time you noticed a problem before anyone else did.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found; "error before anyone else" prompts appear in prep-site banks, unverified)
**What the interviewer is checking:** Attention to detail, initiative and whether you raise issues instead of staying quiet.
**Say this in the interview:** "What I noticed, how I confirmed it, who I told, and what changed. Show you checked before you raised it."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I noticed the report was wrong, so I fixed it quietly and told no one."
**Why it is weak:** Quiet fixes hide the cause, and others may have used the wrong number.

**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "While [doing a routine task] I noticed [something that looked odd]. I checked it by [how you confirmed, for example compared to the source or recounted]. It turned out that [what was wrong] and it could have caused [risk]. I told [name] the same day with [the evidence and a suggested fix]. We [what was done]. Afterwards I added [a check or a note] so it would be caught earlier."

**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "While preparing a joiner list (invented), I saw 3 of 18 new joiners had no manager assigned, about 17 percent. I confirmed against the offer letters, and told the HR operations lead that day. The managers were assigned before day one, so the system access and induction were not delayed. I added a manager-assigned column to the checklist."
Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.

**STAR breakdown:** Situation: the routine task. Task: decide whether it was a real problem. Action: confirm, report, suggest a fix. Result: what was avoided and the check you added.

**If you have no direct experience:** Use a college or personal-project example. Or describe: "If I noticed something odd, I would confirm it, tell the owner with evidence, and suggest a fix". Do not invent a dramatic catch.

**Likely follow-ups:**
- What if you were wrong? "Then I say so, thank them for checking, and I learn what looked odd."
- Did you get credit? "It did not matter to me; the issue was fixed."

**Common mistakes:** Fixing silently; raising an alarm without checking; blaming the person who made the error; exaggerating the impact.
