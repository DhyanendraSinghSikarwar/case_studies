### Q49 ⭐⭐ 🔴 Role-play: we ask you to join in 15 days, but your notice is 60 days. Negotiate with me live.
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); joining date as a negotiation lever is discussed in notice-period guides (unverified blogs).

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, I can join in 15 days, no problem." (when it is not true) Or: "My notice is 60 days, I cannot do anything."
**Why it is weak:** The first is a promise you may break; the second ends the talk with no options.

**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise numbers only (invented Rs 40,000 monthly pay; I used a 30-day month and no rounding needed). Joining in 15 days means 45 days of the 60 must be released. Pay for 45 days is 40,000 / 30 x 45 = Rs 60,000. Whether the employer accepts a buy-out, and the exact rule, depends on your contract (from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal).
**STAR breakdown:** Point: I want the role and need an honest date. Reason: notice is in my contract. Options: early release, buy-out, partial release, handover plan. Link back: a firm answer by a set day.

**Likely follow-ups:** "Will you pay the buy-out yourself?" Say you would discuss it, and that you would rather ask the employer to agree to a shorter notice first. "What if your manager says no?" Then your honest date stands, and you offer to start remote or part-time handover only if your current contract allows it.
**Common mistakes:** Agreeing to a date you cannot meet; resigning before the written offer; treating the notice as only a number and forgetting the contract.

### Q56 ⭐ 🟡 You have a long commute or a relocation cost. Will you ask for a relocation allowance, and how?
**Asked at:** relocation and shift questions appear in anonymous Glassdoor reports for large companies (snippet level, unverified); otherwise commonly asked.

**Explain simply:**
