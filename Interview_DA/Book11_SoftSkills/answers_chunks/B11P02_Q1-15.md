### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why do you want to become a data analyst?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); appears in nearly every analyst question bank (unverified prep sites).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether your reason is real, specific and about the work, not just pay or fashion.
**Say this in the interview:** "I kept finding that the part of my work I enjoyed most was answering a question with numbers. I want to do that full time, and I have been building the skills for it."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Data is the future and analysts earn well, so I want to move into it."
**Why it is weak:** It could be said by anyone, names no work you enjoy, and sounds like a trend or pay chase.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "In my HR work, the tasks I liked best were [a true task, e.g. checking a report for errors or finding why a number moved]. I enjoyed [the specific part: cleaning the data, spotting the pattern, explaining it simply]. I realised I wanted that to be my main job, not a side task. So I have been learning [Excel / SQL / a BI tool] and practising on [a true project or dataset]. I like that this role lets me turn data into something a manager can act on."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "Every month I reconciled an attendance report for about 40 people by hand. It took three hours. I built a small sheet with formulas that did the same check in about 30 minutes, and I enjoyed finding the two rows that did not match. That is when I knew I liked this kind of problem."
Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.
**STAR breakdown:** Point: I like turning data into answers. Reason: one true task that showed it. Example: what I did and what changed. Link back to the role: why this job lets me do more of it.
**If you have no direct experience:** Use a project or course. "I started a small project on [a public dataset] and noticed I lost track of time cleaning and questioning it. That told me this is the work I want, so I am building skills in [tools]." Do not claim HR report-building you did not do.

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Why not stay in HR?* I am moving towards analysis work, not away from people topics. 2) *What have you done about it so far?* Name one true project or course (see Q21).
**Common mistakes:** Quoting pay or "data is the future"; giving a long life story; saying you "love numbers" with no example.

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why do you want to join our company?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); the "why us" question is common in Indian and global prep sites (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you researched them and picked them on purpose, rather than sending the same application everywhere.
**Say this in the interview:** "Two things stood out to me: [what the company does with data] and [what this team does]. I can see how my skills would help there."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "It is a big, well-known company with good brand value and a good location."
**Why it is weak:** Brand and location say nothing about the work or about you.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I read about [one true fact from their website, annual report, news or job post, e.g. what they sell or which markets they serve]. What interests me is [how data would matter there, e.g. tracking customers, operations or workforce]. This role works on [part of the job description], which matches what I have been practising in [your true skill or project]. I would like to grow here because [a true reason: learning from a larger team, real business data, a clear path]."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I saw that the company serves [a type of customer] in several cities. I wondered how the team decides where to add capacity. The job post mentions weekly dashboards, and I have been building dashboards like that on practice data. I would like to do it on real business data."
Say this only if it is true for you. Check each fact before you say it; if you could not find a fact, do not guess one.
**STAR breakdown:** Point: why this company. Reason: one researched fact. Example: link to the job post. Link back: what I bring and what I want to learn.
**If you have no direct experience:** Your research is the evidence. Spend 30 minutes reading their site, job post and recent news, and note two real facts. Stay honest: "I am still learning about the industry, but here is what drew me."

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What do you know about our products?* Answer only what you really read (Q4). 2) *Anywhere else you applied?* Be honest and tactful (Q39).
**Common mistakes:** Praising the brand only; reading the About page aloud; stating facts you did not verify.

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why are you leaving HR to move into data analytics?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); career-switch blogs say switchers will hear this (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you are moving towards something with a plan, or running away, and whether you will stay.
**Say this in the interview:** "I am not leaving because of HR. I found that analysis is the work I enjoy most, and I want to do it full time. My HR background helps me understand the business questions behind the numbers."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "HR is boring and stressful, there is no growth, and I want a change."
**Why it is weak:** It is all complaint and no pull; it also suggests you may leave this job the same way.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "In [number] years in HR, I kept being drawn to [a true task: reports, MIS, attrition numbers, payroll checks]. I enjoyed [the specific part]. Over [a true period] I learned [tools] and built [a true project]. So this is a planned move towards analytics, and I bring a good understanding of [people data, confidentiality, how managers ask questions]. I now want a role where analysis is the main job."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I was asked why attrition looked high in one team. I pulled the exit list, grouped it by tenure and saw most exits were within six months of joining. That finding changed how the team looked at onboarding. That question interested me more than any other task that month."
Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.
**STAR breakdown:** Point: moving towards analysis. Reason: what I enjoyed. Example: one true task plus my learning. Link back to the role: why this job fits.
**If you have no direct experience:** "I have mostly done HR operations work, so I started analytics on my own: [course, practice project]. That showed me I like the work, so this is a considered change."

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Was anything wrong in HR?* See Q10. 2) *Will you go back?* See Q26.
**Common mistakes:** Criticising your employer or HR; sounding apologetic; giving only "I wanted a change".

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What do you know about our company and what we do with data?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); "do you know us" probes appear in HR rounds in candidate reports (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Preparation and honesty, not a perfect recital.
**Say this in the interview:** "From what I could find, you [what the company does]. I am less sure how the data team works, so I would like to hear more, but I can guess it supports [a reasonable area]."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "You are a big MNC with many employees and good culture." Or inventing: "I know you use machine learning for everything."
**Why it is weak:** Generic, or a made-up claim the interviewer can check in seconds.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I read [source: their website, job post, a news story]. I understand you [business in one line] and serve [customers or markets]. For data, the job post mentions [tools or tasks, e.g. dashboards, SQL, reporting], so I expect the team supports [decisions, e.g. operations or finance]. I do not know the details of your data setup yet, and I would like to ask about it."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "Suppose the job post says weekly sales reports and an MIS dashboard. I would say: 'The post mentions weekly reporting, so I assume the team gives managers a regular view of performance. I would like to learn which decisions those reports feed.'"
Say this only if it is true for you. Use only facts you actually found; never guess a product or tool.
**STAR breakdown:** Point: what they do. Reason: where I learned it. Example: what the data team likely does, marked as my reading. Link back: one question I still have.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you found little, say so and lean on the job post. Admitting "this is what I could find" beats a bluff.

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Any recent news about us?* Mention one only if you read it. 2) *Which of our products do you like?* Describe one you have actually seen or used.
**Common mistakes:** Memorised Wikipedia lines; wrong facts; bluffing about their tech stack.

### Q13 ⭐⭐ 🟢 In your own words, what does a data analyst do all day?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a reality-check question for switchers (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you understand the real job, including the boring parts.
**Say this in the interview:** "Most of the day is getting data, checking it, answering a manager's question, and explaining it. Much less is fancy modelling than people think."

**Explain simply:**
- **Understand the question:** talk to the person asking and agree what decision the numbers will support.
- **Get and clean data:** pull it with SQL or Excel, check for duplicates, blanks and wrong values. This often takes the largest share of time.
- **Analyse:** group, compare, calculate rates, look for patterns.
- **Present:** a chart, a dashboard or a short note in plain words.
- **Maintain:** keep regular reports running and fix them when the data changes.
**Plan / steps:** A sample day (an illustration, not a fixed rule):
1. Check overnight reports and refreshes (about 30 minutes).
2. Clarify a new request with a manager.
3. Pull and clean data for it.
4. Analyse and build a chart.
5. Write two lines of "what it means" and send.
6. Note assumptions and anything to follow up.
**Worked example or script:** "A manager asks why returns went up. I ask which products and which dates. I pull the return data, check there are no duplicate entries, and compare returns by product. Say it shows one product has 5 of 20 returns, 25 percent. I explain that clearly and suggest checking that product's quality."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What share is cleaning?* It varies by team; I expect it to be significant. 2) *Which tools?* Name the ones in the job post you have used.
**Common mistakes:** Describing only dashboards; saying "I would build AI models"; ignoring the stakeholder conversations.

### Q15 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What do you expect to learn from us in your first year?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); a growth-mindset probe (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** That you are realistic, eager to learn and know what you do not yet know.
**Say this in the interview:** "In year one I expect to learn your data, your business and how your team works, and to get sharper on [tools]. I want to be useful quickly and reliable over time."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I expect to become a senior analyst and learn machine learning in a year."
**Why it is weak:** Unrealistic, and skips the basics of the business.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "First, your business and data: where it comes from and what the numbers mean. Second, how the team works: how requests arrive, how reports are checked. Third, skills: I want to go deeper on [SQL, a BI tool, statistics] from experienced colleagues. **Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "In the first three months I would learn the data and the existing reports. By six months I would take one report over fully. By twelve months I would suggest one improvement, such as automating a manual step."
Say this only if it is true for you. Pick learning goals you really hold.
**STAR breakdown:** Point: learn the business and craft. Reason: why this order. Example: a 3, 6, 12 month view. Link back: contribution to the team.
**If you have no direct experience:** This is future-looking, so none is needed. Keep it modest and specific.

**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What will you do if you struggle?* Ask early and show my attempt. 2) *What do you need from your manager?* Clear priorities and regular feedback.
**Common mistakes:** Overpromising; only technical goals; sounding passive.
