**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you handle reliability issues directly, kindly and with a clear process.
**Say this in the interview:** "I would make commitments specific and written, check in early, and talk to them privately about what is getting in the way."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I would call them out in the next meeting so everyone sees." Or: "I would just do it myself."
**Why it is weak:** Public blame damages trust, and doing it yourself hides the problem.

**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "After a meeting I write down who owns what and by when, and share it with everyone, so it is normal and not personal. I check in a little before the due date with a friendly question like, 'How is [task] going? Anything blocking you?' If it still slips, I talk privately: 'I noticed the last two items were late. What is making it hard?' Often it is overload or fear of saying no. I suggest realistic dates. If it affects delivery and does not improve, I tell my manager, with dates."

**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** A teammate agrees every week to send the new-hire data and delivers late twice (invented). I add owner and date to meeting notes and check in a day before. In a private chat, he says he has too many tasks. We set a Thursday date he can keep, and the next 3 weeks are on time.
Say this only if it is true for you. If you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.

**STAR breakdown:** Situation: promises not kept. Task: get reliable delivery. Action: written ownership, early check-in, private talk, escalate if needed. Result: realistic commitments met.

**If you have no direct example:** Use a college group-project example if true, or describe the steps.

**Likely follow-ups:**
- What if they keep missing? "I escalate to my manager with the dates and what I tried."
- Do you say anything in the meeting? "I confirm the date with them, politely, in the meeting."

**Common mistakes:** Public blame; silent rework; vague reminders; escalating before one private conversation.
