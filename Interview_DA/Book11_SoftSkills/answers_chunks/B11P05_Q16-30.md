### Q30 ⭐⭐ 🟢 What do you do when you disagree with a decision after it has already been made?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found). Related to Q24; this one is the general approach, Q24 is the story.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you can raise concerns properly and then commit, and know when a concern is serious enough to escalate.
**Say this in the interview:** "I ask to understand the reason, share my concern once with facts, and then support the decision. I escalate only if it is a serious ethical or legal issue."

**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I would continue to argue until they change it" or "I would just do it and complain to colleagues."
**Why it is weak:** One is stubborn; the other creates a negative team culture.

**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "First, I try to understand the reasoning: 'Can you help me understand why we chose this?' Often there is context I did not have. If I still have a concern, I share it once, privately or in the right forum, with facts, not feelings. If the decision stands, I commit and help make it succeed. I may suggest a check after [time], so we can review the result with data. If the decision seemed unethical or illegal, that is different: I would raise it through the proper route, such as my manager or HR or compliance. Specific rules differ by company and law: confirm with HR/legal."

**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** The team decided to report attrition quarterly, not monthly (invented). I asked why: the HR head wanted fewer, steadier numbers. I said monthly data would show spikes sooner. She kept quarterly, and I suggested a simple monthly internal check for the team. I supported the new format.
