### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What are your salary expectations for this data analyst role?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); Naukri, Internshala and Hirist style guides list it (unverified prep-site claims).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether your ask is realistic, whether you have researched the role, and whether you are flexible enough to work with.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give a researched range, tie it to the role and your skills, and say you are open to discussing the full package."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Whatever you think is fair." Or: "I want double my current salary."
**Why it is weak:** The first gives away your power and sounds unprepared; the second has no reasoning behind it.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Based on what I have seen for [role level, for example junior analyst] roles in [city] and my skills in [Excel / SQL / Power BI, only the ones you really have], I am looking at a range of [Rs X to Rs Y LPA]. I am moving from HR into analytics, so I care about the learning and the scope of the role too. I am open to discussing the whole package, including fixed and variable pay. Could you share the budget you have in mind for this role?"
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** All figures are exercise inputs. Say your current total is Rs 4.2 LPA (invented). A 20 to 40 percent step gives Rs 5.04 to Rs 5.88 LPA (computed by script, rounded to 2 decimals). So a spoken range of "Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA" is about 31 to 43 percent above current. Check that this range sits inside what the postings you have seen actually show. Our own research sample showed mostly Rs 3.5 to Rs 9 LPA (a non-random, unverified sample), so Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA is a reasonable middle, not a guarantee.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: a range, not one number. Reason: market research plus skills. Example: one line on your relevant work or project. Link back: open to the full package and to the budget they have.
**If you have no direct experience:** You do not need analyst pay history. Say: "I checked job postings and salary pages for entry-level analyst roles; the range I saw was about [X to Y]. I am placing myself at [lower to middle] of it because I am switching."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Why that number?* Link to the postings you read and your skills. 2) *Would you take less?* "I would look at the whole package and the learning; let us see what the budget is." 3) *Can you give one number?* Give the lower-middle of your range.
**Common mistakes:** Quoting a number with no reasoning; naming a figure far outside the posted band; refusing to give any range at all; saying market figures as facts when they are only estimates.

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your current CTC?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); salary-slip checks are mentioned in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you answer honestly and clearly, and whether the number matches your documents.
**Say this in the interview:** "State the total honestly, split it into fixed and variable, and say what you are looking for next."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** A rounded-up number, or "I would rather not say" with no explanation.
**Why it is weak:** A higher number can be caught by a salary slip or background check, and a flat refusal sounds evasive. Never inflate your pay.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My current total CTC is [Rs X LPA]. Of this, [Rs F] is fixed and [Rs V] is variable, which was paid at [percent] last year. I also get [benefits, if any, such as insurance or PF], which I count separately. I am looking at roles where the learning and scope are bigger, so my expectation is based on the role rather than only my current pay."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: fixed Rs 3.9 LPA plus variable Rs 0.3 LPA gives Rs 4.2 LPA total. Monthly fixed before deductions is Rs 32,500 (3.9 lakh divided by 12, computed by script). Saying "Rs 4.2 LPA total, Rs 3.9 LPA fixed" is clear, and it matches your payslips.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: total number. Reason: fixed plus variable split. Example: what last year's variable actually paid. Link back: what you expect next.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are a fresher or between jobs, give your last drawn pay or stipend as it was. If a company policy asks you not to share, say so politely, but do not give a false figure. Whether a company can legally demand your pay history, and whether it can ask for payslips, is "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal".
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you show payslips?* Say yes if you can, and have them ready. 2) *Is variable guaranteed?* Say what it paid, not what it could pay. 3) *Do you include bonus?* State what you include and keep it consistent with the form.
**Common mistakes:** Mixing fixed and variable; adding the expected hike into the current figure; quoting gross as take-home; differing numbers in the form and in the spoken answer.

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your expected CTC, and how did you arrive at that number?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); "expected CTC" is the standard pair of the current CTC question in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether your number has logic, is realistic, and leaves room to talk.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give a range, explain it with market research, your skills and a reasonable step from your present pay, and stay open."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I want 50 percent more because everyone else does."
**Why it is weak:** The reason is borrowed, the figure is arbitrary, and it ignores the role.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I am looking at [Rs X to Rs Y LPA]. I arrived at it in three steps. First, I checked postings for [role level] in [city], which showed about [range]. Second, I looked at my skills, which are [real skills], and I am moving from HR, so I placed myself at [lower-middle] of that range. Third, I compared it with my current pay of [Rs C], which makes it a step of about [percent]. I am flexible if the role and learning are strong."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: current Rs 4.2 LPA; market range seen Rs 4.5 to Rs 6 LPA (invented). Ask Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA, which is 31.0 to 42.9 percent above current (computed by script, rounded to 1 decimal). If that feels high for a switcher, a range of Rs 5 to Rs 5.5 LPA is 19.0 to 31.0 percent above current. Either is easy to explain; what matters is the explanation.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: a range. Reason: three steps (market, skills, current pay). Example: one real posting you saw. Link back: flexible if the role and learning are strong.
**If you have no direct experience:** Use only the market step and the skills step: "I do not have analyst pay history, so I based my number on postings and my skill level."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Is it negotiable?* "Yes, within a reasonable range, for the right role." 2) *Why more than your HR pay?* Link to the new skills. 3) *What if our budget is lower?* See Q9 and Q13.
**Common mistakes:** Quoting a percentage rule as if it were a law; giving a number below your own need and regretting it; not knowing how you got it.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your notice period, and can it be shortened?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds; HCLTech, Capgemini and similar HR-round reports on Glassdoor mention notice period (anecdotal, snippets only, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Your real joining date, and whether you will handle your exit professionally.
**Say this in the interview:** "State the exact period, say what is flexible and what is not, and promise a proper handover."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "It is 60 days but I can leave anytime, no problem." (when it is not true)
**Why it is weak:** It promises something you may not control and can damage trust if it is not true.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My notice period is [60] days as per my contract. I can ask my manager about an early release or a buy-out, but I cannot promise it, because it depends on my current team's handover. If it helps, I can [use pending leave, if you really have it, or negotiate a shorter date], and I will give you an update after I speak to my manager. I will also leave a clean handover."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: notice 60 days, monthly pay Rs 40,000 (invented). A buy-out for the full 60 days is about Rs 80,000 and for 30 days about Rs 40,000 (computed by script, assuming a 30-day month; real rules vary). If the buy-out is possible, the payer and the process depend on your contract and the new company's policy.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the exact period. Reason: contract and handover. Example: what you can negotiate (early release, leave, buy-out). Link back: a reliable joining date.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are not working, say "I am serving no notice and can join in [X days]", only if true. If you are a fresher, say the same.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you buy it out?* "If the company agrees and it is feasible, yes, but I would ask what is allowed first." 2) *Who pays?* Do not demand it; it is a discussion. 3) *When would you know?* Give a date you will update them by.
**Common mistakes:** Promising a date before asking your manager; resigning before the offer is in writing; treating notice-period rules as the same everywhere. Notice-period enforceability and buy-out rules are "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal"; the contract and company policy decide.

### Q5 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Can you join immediately, or how soon can you join if we make you an offer?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); "immediate joiner" honesty is mentioned in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Your honest availability and whether the date you give will hold.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give the earliest date you are sure about, and say what that depends on."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, I can join immediately." (while you have a 60-day notice)
**Why it is weak:** If it is not true, you will either break the promise or damage your name with the new employer and the old one.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My notice period is [X] days, so I can join about [date] if I resign the day I receive the offer. I will ask whether an early release is possible and let you know within [N] days. If you need a faster date, I will tell you honestly what is possible rather than promise something I cannot keep."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise: offer on 1 December, 60 days notice, so earliest date is around 30 January if you resign at once (computed by date script; adjust for weekends and holidays). If 15 days of leave can be adjusted (if your company allows it), the date moves to about 15 January. Do not assume this; ask.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the date. Reason: the notice period. Example: what could speed it up. Link back: a promise you will keep.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are not currently employed and truly can join immediately, say it plainly, and add any real constraint such as a two-week relocation.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you do it in 30 days?* See Q14. 2) *What if the offer comes late?* Date counts from resignation, not offer. 3) *Would you leave a handover half done?* No, I will complete it.
**Common mistakes:** Saying "immediate" to please; giving different dates in different rounds; forgetting to count holidays and weekends.

### Q6 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why are you moving from HR into data analytics, and why now, given you are leaving a stable role?
**Asked at:** commonly asked for switchers (no company-specific report found); "why change" questions appear in HR-round reports (anecdotal, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether the switch is thought through and lasting, not a reaction to a bad day.
**Say this in the interview:** "Show that the move grows out of what you already do in HR, name a real trigger, and show that you have already started preparing."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "HR is boring and analytics pays more."
**Why it is weak:** It is negative about your past and makes pay the only reason.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "In HR, I worked with [attendance, payroll, attrition or hiring data] and I enjoyed [finding the pattern, building the report]. What pulled me was [one true moment, for example a report that changed a decision]. Over the last [months], I have been learning [Excel, SQL, Power BI] and built [small project]. I am not leaving HR knowledge behind; I want to use it to make better data-based decisions. Now is the right time because [your true reason, for example you have reached the level where you can start the switch]."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "In my HR role I tracked monthly attrition in a spreadsheet for three teams. When one team's leavers kept increasing, I worked out the reasons by grouping exit data, and the manager changed the shift plan. That showed me how much I enjoyed it. I then completed a [course] and built an attrition dashboard on sample data." (An invented example for illustration only; use your own.)
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the switch is a growth of your work. Reason: one real trigger. Example: learning done and a small project. Link back: what you bring to this team.
**If you have no direct experience:** If your HR work had little data, say so honestly: "My HR work was mostly process, but I saw the data behind it and decided to learn," then describe the learning you have actually done.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Aren't you taking a risk?* "Yes, a calculated one; I prepared for it." 2) *Will you leave if it is hard?* Show your plan. 3) *Why not HR analytics?* "That is a good fit too; I am open to it."
**Common mistakes:** Complaining about HR or your manager; claiming a project you did not do; saying only "passion" with no proof.

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Do you have any other offers or interviews in progress?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); HR trade press on offer dropouts mentions recruiters asking this (SHRM India, People Matters, unverified snippets).
**What the interviewer is checking:** How serious you are, how fast they must move, and whether you are honest.
**Say this in the interview:** "Tell the truth in a calm, short way: what stage, and that this role is a strong interest."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "No, only you" (when you have others), or "Yes, I have three offers and I will choose the highest."
**Why it is weak:** The first is a lie that can surface; the second sounds like a bidding war.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Yes, I am [in the process with two other companies; one is at the final round and one at the first round], and I have [no offers yet / one offer pending]. This role is a strong match because [real reason], so I would like to know your timeline. I will share my decision date honestly once I have it."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I have one interview scheduled next week and no offers. If I get one, I will let you know right away so we can both plan." (If there are no other processes, say "No other offers at present; this is my main focus." Only if true.)
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: honest status. Reason: why this role matters. Example: their timeline versus yours. Link back: you will keep them informed.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you have none, say "No other offers at the moment, and I am focused on this."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Who are they?* See Q37; you may decline politely. 2) *What are the numbers?* You can say you prefer to discuss only this role. 3) *When do you need to decide?* Give a real date.
**Common mistakes:** Lying to look in demand or to look available; revealing everything too early; bragging or pressuring. Never hide a competing offer to gain an advantage.

### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Your notice period is 60 days but we need someone in 30. What can you do?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); notice buy-out and early release are covered in Instahyre, JobRise and Zoho guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you have practical options and will manage your exit responsibly.
**Say this in the interview:** "List real options: ask for early release, use leave, offer a strong handover, or discuss a buy-out, and promise to get back with a date."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I will just leave in 30 days, whatever happens."
**Why it is weak:** It risks a bad exit, a possible dispute, and a poor reference.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I would first speak to my manager and HR about releasing me in 30 days. I can offer a detailed handover, training of my replacement, and [if true: using my accrued leave]. If the company allows, I could discuss a buy-out of the remaining days. I will not promise 30 days until I have it in writing, but I will update you by [date]."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: notice 60 days, monthly pay Rs 40,000 (invented). Shortening by 30 days means 30 days to buy out: about Rs 1,333 a day, so Rs 40,000 for 30 days (computed by script, assuming a 30-day month; real formulas vary). Full buy-out of 60 days would be about Rs 80,000. Whether the employee pays, the employer waives, or the new company shares is a policy matter; do not demand it.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: options exist. Reason: manager and policy decide. Example: handover, leave, buy-out. Link back: a firm date after written confirmation.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say how you would do it step by step: ask the manager first, then HR, then get the agreement in writing.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *If your manager refuses?* See Q32. 2) *Can you start part-time earlier?* Probably not while employed; check policy. 3) *Can you work remotely for us?* Not usually allowed during notice; check.
**Common mistakes:** Promising the new date before the old employer agrees; quitting without paperwork; expecting the new company to pay. Enforceability of notice clauses is "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal".

### Q15 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 How do you decide between a salary hike and a role switch into analytics, if they pull in different directions?
**Asked at:** commonly asked for switchers (no company-specific report found); a gap-fill question (judgement).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you have thought about priorities and long-term growth rather than only the immediate pay.
**Say this in the interview:** "Describe a simple method: money need first, then growth over two to three years, then risk."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I will go wherever pays more." Or: "Money does not matter to me at all."
**Why it is weak:** One sounds like you will not stay; the other is not believable.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I decide in three steps. First, my minimum monthly need, so I know what I cannot go below. Second, where I will be in two to three years: the switch into analytics builds skills that grow my pay later. Third, the risk: is the team good at teaching, and is the company stable? If the dip is within my minimum and the learning is real, I choose the switch. I have chosen the switch for [true reason]."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs (invented): Option A, stay in HR at Rs 4.8 LPA (a 20.0 percent hike on Rs 4.0 LPA). Option B, analyst at Rs 3.8 LPA (5.0 percent below Rs 4.0 LPA). Year-one gap Rs 1.0 LPA. If analytics pay rises 25 percent in year two and HR 8 percent, B is Rs 4.75 LPA and A is Rs 5.18 LPA (computed by script, rounded). Growth rates here are invented to show the method, not forecasts. The question is whether the gap closes and whether you can bear the dip.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: three-step method. Reason: need, growth, risk. Example: your own comparison. Link back: why you chose the switch.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say "This is how I made the choice" and describe your honest weighing, without claiming a result.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What if the dip is large?* Set a limit. 2) *When do you expect to catch up?* Say "within [N] years", with uncertainty. 3) *Is pay not important to you?* "It matters; I weigh it with growth."
**Common mistakes:** Claiming pay is unimportant; promising the future pay as certain; not knowing your minimum.

### Q16 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Weak versus strong: how would you answer 'Tell me your expected salary' if you do not know the market range for a switcher?
**Asked at:** commonly asked (no company-specific report found); Naukri, Internshala and Blind style advice on deflecting versus ranges (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you handle uncertainty honestly and can still give a useful answer.
**Say this in the interview:** "Say that you have done some research, give a range, ask for theirs, and show you are flexible."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I do not know, you tell me." Or a random number such as "Rs 12 LPA."
**Why it is weak:** The first looks unprepared; the second can be far off and ends the talk.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "As I am moving from HR into my first analyst role, I looked at similar roles in [city]. The postings I saw ranged around [Rs X to Rs Y LPA], so I would place myself in the [lower to middle] part of that. Could you share the range for this role so we can see if it fits? I am open to discussion, and I care about the learning as well."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs (invented): postings seen Rs 4.5 to Rs 6 LPA; midpoint Rs 5.25 LPA (computed by script). Our own research sample showed mostly Rs 3.5 to Rs 9 LPA (a non-random, unverified sample), so check postings yourself before the interview. Before the interview: read 10 to 15 postings for your city and level, note the lowest, middle and highest, and place yourself near the lower-middle as a switcher.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: a researched range. Reason: place yourself honestly. Example: the postings you read. Link back: ask for their budget.
**If you have no direct experience:** Do the research before the interview; if asked on the spot and you have none, say "I am still gathering data; may I hear your range first?"
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *How did you get that range?* Name the sources, such as postings and salary pages, without claiming they are exact. 2) *What if ours is lower?* See Q9 and Q13. 3) *Can you give one number?* Give the lower-middle of your range.
**Common mistakes:** Saying "no idea"; quoting a number from a friend with no basis; giving a figure and refusing to move.
