### Q1 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What are your salary expectations for this data analyst role?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); Naukri, Internshala and Hirist style guides list it (unverified prep-site claims).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether your ask is realistic, whether you have researched the role, and whether you are flexible enough to work with.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give a researched range, tie it to the role and your skills, and say you are open to discussing the full package."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Whatever you think is fair." Or: "I want double my current salary."
**Why it is weak:** The first gives away your power and sounds unprepared; the second has no reasoning behind it.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Based on what I have seen for [role level, for example junior analyst] roles in [city] and my skills in [Excel / SQL / Power BI, only the ones you really have], I am looking at a range of [Rs X to Rs Y LPA]. I am moving from HR into analytics, so I care about the learning and the scope of the role too. I am open to discussing the whole package, including fixed and variable pay. Could you share the budget you have in mind for this role?"
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** All figures are exercise inputs. Say your current total is Rs 4.2 LPA (invented). A 20 to 40 percent step gives Rs 5.04 to Rs 5.88 LPA (computed by script, rounded to 2 decimals). So a spoken range of "Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA" is about 31 to 43 percent above current. Check that this range sits inside what the postings you have seen actually show. Our own research sample showed mostly Rs 3.5 to Rs 9 LPA (a non-random, unverified sample), so Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA is a reasonable middle, not a guarantee. (LPA means lakh per annum, a yearly figure; one lakh is Rs 1,00,000. CTC, used below, means cost to company: the total yearly pay package as the employer counts it.) Say this only if it is true for you: quote only a range you have actually checked.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: a range, not one number. Reason: market research plus skills. Example: one line on your relevant work or project. Link back: open to the full package and to the budget they have.
**If you have no direct experience:** You do not need analyst pay history. Say: "I checked job postings and salary pages for entry-level analyst roles; the range I saw was about [X to Y]. I am placing myself at [lower to middle] of it because I am switching."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Why that number?* Link to the postings you read and your skills. 2) *Would you take less?* "I would look at the whole package and the learning; let us see what the budget is." 3) *Can you give one number?* Give the lower-middle of your range.
**Common mistakes:** Quoting a number with no reasoning; naming a figure far outside the posted band; refusing to give any range at all; saying market figures as facts when they are only estimates.

### Q2 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your current CTC?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); salary-slip checks are mentioned in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you answer honestly and clearly, and whether the number matches your documents.
**Say this in the interview:** "State the total honestly, split it into fixed and variable, and say what you are looking for next."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** A rounded-up number, or "I would rather not say" with no explanation.
**Why it is weak:** A higher number can be caught by a salary slip or background check, and a flat refusal sounds evasive. Never inflate your pay.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My current total CTC is [Rs X LPA]. Of this, [Rs F] is fixed and [Rs V] is variable, which was paid at [percent] last year. I also get [benefits, if any, such as insurance or PF], which I count separately. I am looking at roles where the learning and scope are bigger, so my expectation is based on the role rather than only my current pay."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: fixed Rs 3.9 LPA plus variable Rs 0.3 LPA gives Rs 4.2 LPA total. Monthly fixed before deductions is Rs 32,500 (3.9 lakh divided by 12, computed by script). Saying "Rs 4.2 LPA total, Rs 3.9 LPA fixed" is clear, and it matches your payslips. Say this only if it is true for you: use your own real figures.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: total number. Reason: fixed plus variable split. Example: what last year's variable actually paid. Link back: what you expect next.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are a fresher or between jobs, give your last drawn pay or stipend as it was. If a company policy asks you not to share, say so politely, but do not give a false figure. Whether a company can legally demand your pay history, and whether it can ask for payslips, is "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal".
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you show payslips?* Say yes if you can, and have them ready. 2) *Is variable guaranteed?* Say what it paid, not what it could pay. 3) *Do you include bonus?* State what you include and keep it consistent with the form.
**Common mistakes:** Mixing fixed and variable; adding the expected hike into the current figure; quoting gross as take-home; differing numbers in the form and in the spoken answer.

### Q3 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your expected CTC, and how did you arrive at that number?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); "expected CTC" is the standard pair of the current CTC question in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether your number has logic, is realistic, and leaves room to talk.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give a range, explain it with market research, your skills and a reasonable step from your present pay, and stay open."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I want 50 percent more because everyone else does."
**Why it is weak:** The reason is borrowed, the figure is arbitrary, and it ignores the role.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I am looking at [Rs X to Rs Y LPA]. I arrived at it in three steps. First, I checked postings for [role level] in [city], which showed about [range]. Second, I looked at my skills, which are [real skills], and I am moving from HR, so I placed myself at [lower-middle] of that range. Third, I compared it with my current pay of [Rs C], which makes it a step of about [percent]. I am flexible if the role and learning are strong."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: current Rs 4.2 LPA; market range seen Rs 4.5 to Rs 6 LPA (invented). Ask Rs 5.5 to Rs 6 LPA, which is 31.0 to 42.9 percent above current (computed by script, rounded to 1 decimal). If that feels high for a switcher, a range of Rs 5 to Rs 5.5 LPA is 19.0 to 31.0 percent above current. Either is easy to explain; what matters is the explanation. Say this only if it is true for you: use your own current pay and your own research.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: a range. Reason: three steps (market, skills, current pay). Example: one real posting you saw. Link back: flexible if the role and learning are strong.
**If you have no direct experience:** Use only the market step and the skills step: "I do not have analyst pay history, so I based my number on postings and my skill level."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Is it negotiable?* "Yes, within a reasonable range, for the right role." 2) *Why more than your HR pay?* Link to the new skills. 3) *What if our budget is lower?* See Q9 and Q13.
**Common mistakes:** Quoting a percentage rule as if it were a law; giving a number below your own need and regretting it; not knowing how you got it.

### Q4 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 What is your notice period, and can it be shortened?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds; HCLTech, Capgemini and similar HR-round reports on Glassdoor mention notice period (anecdotal, snippets only, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Your real joining date, and whether you will handle your exit professionally.
**Say this in the interview:** "State the exact period, say what is flexible and what is not, and promise a proper handover."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "It is 60 days but I can leave anytime, no problem." (when it is not true)
**Why it is weak:** It promises something you may not control and can damage trust if it is not true.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My notice period is [60] days as per my contract. I can ask my manager about an early release or a buy-out, but I cannot promise it, because it depends on my current team's handover. If it helps, I can [use pending leave, if you really have it, or negotiate a shorter date], and I will give you an update after I speak to my manager. I will also leave a clean handover."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: notice 60 days, monthly pay Rs 40,000 (invented). A buy-out for the full 60 days is about Rs 80,000 and for 30 days about Rs 40,000 (computed by script, assuming a 30-day month; real rules vary). If the buy-out is possible, the payer and the process depend on your contract and the new company's policy. A buy-out means paying the old employer for the notice days you do not serve. Say this only if it is true for you: use your own contract terms.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the exact period. Reason: contract and handover. Example: what you can negotiate (early release, leave, buy-out). Link back: a reliable joining date.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are not working, say "I am serving no notice and can join in [X days]", only if true. If you are a fresher, say the same.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you buy it out?* "If the company agrees and it is feasible, yes, but I would ask what is allowed first." 2) *Who pays?* Do not demand it; it is a discussion. 3) *When would you know?* Give a date you will update them by.
**Common mistakes:** Promising a date before asking your manager; resigning before the offer is in writing; treating notice-period rules as the same everywhere. Notice-period enforceability and buy-out rules are "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal"; the contract and company policy decide.

### Q5 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Can you join immediately, or how soon can you join if we make you an offer?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); "immediate joiner" honesty is mentioned in prep-site guides (unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Your honest availability and whether the date you give will hold.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give the earliest date you are sure about, and say what that depends on."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, I can join immediately." (while you have a 60-day notice)
**Why it is weak:** If it is not true, you will either break the promise or damage your name with the new employer and the old one.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "My notice period is [X] days, so I can join about [date] if I resign the day I receive the written offer. I will ask whether an early release is possible and let you know within [N] days. If you need a faster date, I will tell you honestly what is possible rather than promise something I cannot keep."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise: offer on 1 December, 60 days notice, so earliest date is around 30 January if you resign at once (computed by date script; adjust for weekends and holidays). If 15 days of leave can be adjusted (if your company allows it), the date moves to about 15 January. Do not assume this; ask. Say this only if it is true for you: count from your own contract and leave balance.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the date. Reason: the notice period. Example: what could speed it up. Link back: a promise you will keep.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you are not currently employed and truly can join immediately, say it plainly, and add any real constraint such as a two-week relocation.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you do it in 30 days?* See Q14. 2) *What if the offer comes late?* Date counts from resignation, not offer. 3) *Would you leave a handover half done?* No, I will complete it.
**Common mistakes:** Saying "immediate" to please; giving different dates in different rounds; forgetting to count holidays and weekends.

### Q6 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why are you moving from HR into data analytics, and why now, given you are leaving a stable role?
**Asked at:** commonly asked for switchers (no company-specific report found); "why change" questions appear in HR-round reports (anecdotal, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether the switch is thought through and lasting, not a reaction to a bad day.
**Say this in the interview:** "Show that the move grows out of what you already do in HR, name a real trigger, and show that you have already started preparing."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "HR is boring and analytics pays more."
**Why it is weak:** It is negative about your past and makes pay the only reason.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "In HR, I worked with [attendance, payroll, attrition or hiring data] and I enjoyed [finding the pattern, building the report]. What pulled me was [one true moment, for example a report that changed a decision]. Over the last [months], I have been learning [Excel, SQL, Power BI] and built [small project]. I am not leaving HR knowledge behind; I want to use it to make better data-based decisions. Now is the right time because [your true reason, for example you have reached the level where you can start the switch]."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "In my HR role I tracked monthly attrition in a spreadsheet for three teams. When one team's leavers kept increasing, I worked out the reasons by grouping exit data, and the manager changed the shift plan. That showed me how much I enjoyed it. I then completed a [course] and built an attrition dashboard on sample data." (An invented example for illustration only; use your own.) Say this only if it is true for you; if you have no direct example, use the no-experience version below.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: the switch is a growth of your work. Reason: one real trigger. Example: learning done and a small project. Link back: what you bring to this team.
**If you have no direct experience:** If your HR work had little data, say so honestly: "My HR work was mostly process, but I saw the data behind it and decided to learn," then describe the learning you have actually done.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Aren't you taking a risk?* "Yes, a calculated one; I prepared for it." 2) *Will you leave if it is hard?* Show your plan. 3) *Why not HR analytics?* "That is a good fit too; I am open to it."
**Common mistakes:** Complaining about HR or your manager; claiming a project you did not do; saying only "passion" with no proof.

### Q7 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Are you willing to relocate to Bengaluru or Hyderabad for this role, and by when?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds; HCL, Deloitte and similar reports mention relocation questions (anecdotal, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether the location and the start date are workable, and whether you are serious.
**Say this in the interview:** "Answer yes or no clearly, say how long you need, and mention any real constraint without drama."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, anywhere" (when you cannot), or "I will see, maybe."
**Why it is weak:** A false yes ends in a drop-out, and a vague answer sounds uncommitted.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Yes, I am willing to relocate to [Bengaluru or Hyderabad]. I would need about [N weeks] after the joining date to find housing and settle, and I can [start remotely or from a temporary stay] during that time if that is useful. [Preference or constraint, only if true, for example I would prefer Hyderabad because of family.] I am happy to talk about the relocation support that exists in the policy."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I already know Hyderabad from my college years, so I can move within three weeks of the offer date. I will book a short stay first and look for a flat in the first week." (Invented; use your real situation.) Say this only if it is true for you.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: yes or no. Reason: your situation in one line. Example: what you will do. Link back: a clear timeline.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you have never moved cities, say what you have done to prepare: asked friends, researched rents, planned a budget.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Do you expect relocation support?* See Q56; ask politely. 2) *What if we want you in 2 weeks?* Say what is genuinely possible. 3) *Is your family moving?* Share only what you are comfortable with.
**Common mistakes:** Saying yes and not meaning it; not factoring in the cost of rent and moving; hiding a real constraint that will come up later.

### Q8 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Do you have any other offers or interviews in progress?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); HR trade press on offer dropouts mentions recruiters asking this (SHRM India, People Matters, unverified snippets).
**What the interviewer is checking:** How serious you are, how fast they must move, and whether you are honest.
**Say this in the interview:** "Tell the truth in a calm, short way: what stage, and that this role is a strong interest."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "No, only you" (when you have others), or "Yes, I have three offers and I will choose the highest."
**Why it is weak:** The first is a lie that can surface; the second sounds like a bidding war.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Yes, I am [in the process with two other companies; one is at the final round and one at the first round], and I have [no offers yet / one offer pending]. This role is a strong match because [real reason], so I would like to know your timeline. I will share my decision date honestly once I have it."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I have one interview scheduled next week and no offers. If I get one, I will let you know right away so we can both plan." (If there are no other processes, say "No other offers at present; this is my main focus." Only if true.) Say this only if it is true for you.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: honest status. Reason: why this role matters. Example: their timeline versus yours. Link back: you will keep them informed.
**If you have no direct experience:** If you have none, say "No other offers at the moment, and I am focused on this."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Who are they?* See Q37; you may decline politely. 2) *What are the numbers?* You can say you prefer to discuss only this role. 3) *When do you need to decide?* Give a real date.
**Common mistakes:** Lying to look in demand or to look available; revealing everything too early; bragging or pressuring. Never hide a competing offer to gain an advantage.

### Q9 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Would you accept this role if we offered a package lower than your expectation?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Your flexibility, your true priorities, and whether you will stay unhappy later.
**Say this in the interview:** "Say that you would look at the whole picture, name the gap you can live with, and ask what growth or review is possible."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, I will take anything." Or: "No, my number is final."
**Why it is weak:** The first undervalues you and risks resentment; the second closes the door without a talk.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "It depends on how far it is and what comes with it. For a first analyst role, learning and scope matter to me as much as the number. If the gap is small, I would consider it, especially if there is [a review in six months, learning support, a clear path]. If it is much lower than the market, I would have to think. May I ask what the structure is?"
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: ask Rs 6 LPA, offer Rs 5.4 LPA (invented). The gap is Rs 0.6 LPA, which is 10.0 percent of the ask and about Rs 5,000 a month before deductions (computed by script). A 10 percent gap with a six-month review might be acceptable; a 30 percent gap may not be (my suggestion, not a rule). Decide your own walk-away number before the call. Say this only if it is true for you.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: it depends. Reason: the size of the gap and the growth. Example: what you would ask for (review, variable, learning). Link back: you want this role.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say "I have not faced this, but this is how I would weigh it", and give the three factors: gap, growth, review.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What is your limit?* Give a range that you decided earlier. 2) *Would you ask for a joining bonus?* You could, politely. 3) *Will you stay at this pay?* See Q22.
**Common mistakes:** Saying yes and then regretting; saying a hard no; not knowing your own minimum before the call.

### Q10 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Are you comfortable working from the office on the days or shifts this team requires?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds; shift and work-mode questions appear in HR-round reports for service and GCC firms (anecdotal, unverified).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether the work mode fits your life and you will not leave over it.
**Say this in the interview:** "Answer yes or state a real limit early, with a reason and an alternative."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Yes, anything" (when you cannot), or "I prefer work from home only."
**Why it is weak:** A false yes leads to leaving later; a rigid preference signals low flexibility.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Yes, I am comfortable with [number] days in the office and with the shifts the team needs. [If true: I would only need notice for fixed commitments such as [one real thing].] I understand that a new analyst learns faster near the team. Could you tell me the usual schedule so I can plan?"
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "My commute is about 45 minutes each way; I have done the same for my current job, so three days a week in the office is fine." (Invented; use your own.) Say this only if it is true for you.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: yes or a clear limit. Reason: one line. Example: what you already do. Link back: you will be present when the team needs.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say how you have handled a fixed schedule before: college timings, previous job hours.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Late shifts?* See Q20. 2) *Hybrid important to you?* See Q54. 3) *What if the schedule changes?* "I will adapt and plan early."
**Common mistakes:** Saying yes and then complaining after joining; arguing over policy; hiding a health or family limit that will show later (see Q51).

### Q11 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟢 Why is there a gap in your resume between your last job and now?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); SHRM India, Naukri and Jobrise gap guides cover it (unverified snippets).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you are honest, whether you used the time well, and whether you are ready to commit.
**Say this in the interview:** "Give the true reason in one line, say what you did, and say you are ready now."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Nothing special, I was just at home." Or a made-up reason such as a fake job.
**Why it is weak:** It sounds like no effort, and a false reason can be caught in checks. Never invent employment.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "After leaving [company] in [month year], I took [N months] for [true reason: family, health, preparing for the switch]. During that time I [real activity: completed a course, built a project, freelanced]. I am now ready and focused on [role]. The gap is behind me, and I can show what I did in that time."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** "I left my HR job in March to look after a family matter for two months. After that I spent four months learning Excel and SQL through free courses and built a small attendance dashboard. I started applying in September." (Invented dates and activity; use your true ones.) Say this only if it is true for you.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: honest reason. Reason: brief. Example: what you did. Link back: ready now and committed.
**If you have no direct experience:** If the gap was because of a job search with little else, say it plainly: "I looked for roles in HR and decided to switch; I started learning in month three." Do not stretch.
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Can you prove the learning?* Show certificates or the project link. 2) *Any other gaps?* Answer honestly. 3) *Will it happen again?* "No; my situation is stable."
**Common mistakes:** Long apologies; inventing jobs or dates; claiming learning you cannot demonstrate. Background checks may compare your dates with records: "from memory, unverified, confirm with HR/legal".

### Q12 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 You are moving from HR into your first analyst role. Your current pay is higher than our budget for this role. How do you respond?
**Asked at:** commonly asked for career switchers (no company-specific report found); a gap-fill question built from the pay-negotiation guides (judgement).
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you understand a switch can mean a pay trade-off and can discuss it calmly.
**Say this in the interview:** "Acknowledge the gap, show why the switch is worth it to you, and ask about the full package and growth."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "I cannot take a pay cut." Or: "I will take anything, I just want the job."
**Why it is weak:** One is rigid and ends the talk; the other gives away your leverage.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "I understand it is a first analyst role, so I expected the pay structure to be different. I am committed to the switch for the learning, so I am open to a smaller step. I would like to understand the full package, including variable pay and review cycles, and what growth looks like in the first year. If we can come close to [Rs X], I would be comfortable moving ahead."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Exercise inputs: current Rs 5.0 LPA, budget Rs 4.2 LPA (invented). The budget is 84.0 percent of current, a gap of 16.0 percent (computed by script). A counter at Rs 4.6 LPA is Rs 38,333 a month against Rs 35,000 at budget (before deductions). Decide in advance what you can accept: your essential monthly expenses are the real limit. Say this only if it is true for you: use your own pay and the real budget.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: understand, and open. Reason: the switch matters. Example: ask for the package and review. Link back: a concrete figure to continue.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say how you would think: "I would calculate my monthly need first, then decide how much of a dip I can handle for how long."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *What is your limit?* Name a range you decided earlier. 2) *Will you leave when you get a better offer?* See Q22. 3) *Why not stay in HR?* See Q6 and Q15.
**Common mistakes:** Emotional answers; not calculating your own monthly need; asking for the same pay as an experienced analyst without reasons.

### Q13 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 You said your expected CTC is Rs 7 LPA, but our budget for this role is Rs 5.5 LPA. How do you react?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found). Both figures are exercise inputs.
**What the interviewer is checking:** Whether you stay calm, justify your ask, and look for a middle path rather than walking out or folding.
**Say this in the interview:** "Thank them for being direct, restate your reasoning, and look for movement on the structure and a review date."
**Weak answer (what not to say):** "Then I guess we cannot work together." Or: "Fine, I will take Rs 5.5 LPA" instantly.
**Why it is weak:** The first ends it with no discussion; the second shows that the number was not backed by reasoning.
**Strong answer (template, speak it in your own words):** "Thank you for being clear. My expectation was based on [market range, my skills and the scope of the role]. The gap is [Rs 1.5 LPA], and I would like to see whether we can bridge part of it. Could we look at the fixed and variable split, a joining bonus, or a review at six months? If we can get closer to [Rs X], I would be happy to move ahead."
**Illustrative example, replace with your own true story:** Computed by script, rounded: the gap is Rs 1.5 LPA; your ask is 27.3 percent above their budget; their budget is 21.4 percent below your ask. At Rs 7 LPA the monthly gross is about Rs 58,333, and at Rs 5.5 LPA about Rs 45,833; the difference is Rs 12,500 a month before deductions. The midpoint is Rs 6.25 LPA (about Rs 52,083 a month). Whether to move toward the midpoint depends on your minimum and your other options. Say this only if it is true for you: use your own ask and the real budget.
**STAR breakdown (structure that fits):** Point: calm and open. Reason: how you arrived at the ask. Example: ask about structure, bonus and review. Link back: interest in the role.
**If you have no direct experience:** Say "I would calculate the monthly difference first and decide my minimum before replying."
**Likely follow-ups:** 1) *Will you take Rs 5.5 LPA?* "Only if the role and review make it work; what can you offer on those?" 2) *Why did you say Rs 7 LPA?* Share the reasoning, such as postings in the sample showing up to Rs 9 LPA (non-random, unverified). 3) *What is your final number?* Give a range, not a bluff.
**Common mistakes:** Bluffing about other offers; reacting with emotion; agreeing without thinking; naming an ask that was never researched. Never invent a competing offer to push the number.

### Q14 ⭐⭐⭐ 🟡 Your notice period is 60 days but we need someone in 30. What can you do?
**Asked at:** commonly asked in HR rounds (no company-specific report found); notice buy-out and early release are covered in Instahyre, JobRise and Zoho guides (unverified).

### Q28 ⭐⭐ 🟡 An exercise: our offer is Rs 6 LPA CTC with Rs 50,000 as the annual variable. What is the monthly fixed component before deductions, assuming the rest is fixed?

- Fixed per year = 6,00,000 minus 50,000 = Rs 5,50,000.
- Fixed per month = 5,50,000 / 12 = Rs 45,833 (rounded to the nearest rupee).
- This is gross, before income tax, PF (provident fund, a retirement saving deducted from pay) and other deductions, so the take-home is lower. What counts inside CTC (employer PF, gratuity, insurance) varies: from memory, unverified, ask for the breakup.
- The variable is about 8.3 percent of CTC (computed), and may not be paid in full.

**Plan / steps:**
1. Subtract the variable from the CTC.
2. Divide by 12.
3. Ask for the full salary breakup (basic, HRA, allowances, PF, variable rules).
4. Ask how the variable is decided and paid, and whether it is guaranteed.

**Worked example or script:** "Just to confirm: of Rs 6 lakh, Rs 50,000 is variable, so the fixed is Rs 5.5 lakh, about Rs 45,800 a month gross. Could you share the breakup, including how the variable is calculated and when it is paid? I would also like to know if the PF share is inside the CTC."

**Likely follow-ups:**

**Common mistakes:** Dividing the whole CTC by 12 and calling it monthly salary (that gives Rs 50,000); forgetting deductions; not asking how the variable is decided.
