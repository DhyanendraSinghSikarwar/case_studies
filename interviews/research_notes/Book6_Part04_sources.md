## Found versus expert gap-check
- Found (snippet-level): SRM, peeking, novelty effect and network interference as the standard 'pitfalls' bundle; a flat or non-significant test is not proof of no effect and power or MDE comes first; guardrail regression needs a pre-agreed bound and a decision; switchback effective sample is the number of periods; geo designs trade power for independence; delta method for ratio metrics and CUPED covariate adjustment; DiD and synthetic control as the non-randomized options; bots as outlier traffic and winsorizing; LLM tests need conversation-level units and judge agreement checks.
- Expert gap-check (editor judgement, not sourced): segment-level SRM hidden by a passing overall check (Q43); post-treatment covariate in CUPED (Q42); ratio metric whose denominator moves with treatment (Q40); partial exposure and triggered analysis (Q32); crash-driven differential logging (Q18); cumulative effect versus the sum of launches (Q33); winner's curse and regression to the mean (Q44, Q48); hash-salt collision (Q54); uplift model validated without randomization (Q55); silent mid-test model update (Q53); redirect-only attrition (Q46); cross-device contamination (Q25).
- Arithmetic checked in qs.py: chi-square 160 for 52,000/48,000; CI of -0.3 to +0.5 points; 1 - 0.7^2 = 0.51; Simpson tables (8.8% versus 6.2%); required sample about 119,000 per arm for the 5% baseline and about 300,000 for the 2% baseline; design effect 1.98; 14 daily peeks give about 21% false wins in a simulation (the prompt says roughly one in five); 1 - 0.95^20 = 64%; 1 - 0.99^40 = 33%.
