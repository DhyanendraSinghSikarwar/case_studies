## Found versus expert gap-check
- Found (snippet-level): pricing is posed as a case with elasticity, experiments and guardrails; churn work should end in treatment choice (uplift, net of CLV and cost) not just a risk ranking; fraud thresholds follow the cost of a miss versus a false alarm and analyst capacity; CLV and CAC payback are standard growth-analyst prompts and the formulas need explicit assumptions; retail forecasts are trained on censored sales; take rate is revenue over GMV and raising it risks seller leakage; credit approval is an expected-profit rule; incrementality (holdouts) rather than attributed revenue is the test for promotions and ads; ETA models weigh late versus early errors asymmetrically; 2026 LLM-support ROI pages stress cost per resolution and re-contact rather than raw deflection.
- Expert gap-check (editor judgement, not sourced): the pull-forward and sister-brand cannibalisation arithmetic (Q17); reject inference from a random pilot (Q18); take-rate optimum from a two-point fit (Q52); cost of a false decline as retention loss on genuine customers (Q43); loyalty-programme selection bias and per-joiner effect (Q38); build versus buy (Q46); dark-store break-even (Q47); free-returns break-even (Q50); adverse selection in repricing (Q51); model cascade economics (Q54).
- Arithmetic checked in qs.py: all 55 prompts (for example break-even PD 20%, chargeback net 87,000, critical ratio 0.75 giving about 113.5 units, LTV-to-CAC 2.5 and 6.0, cascade cost 0.0411 versus 0.044, the reject-inference interval 16.3% to 19.7%).
