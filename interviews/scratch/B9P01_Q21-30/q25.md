**Say this first (1 line):** I give difficult feedback early, in private, about specific work, and with an offer to help; here is a real case and how it went.

**Structure:** Use STAR (see the STAR and CARL section above), with the wording of the feedback inside Action.
- Situation and Task: who, what was the problem, why it needed saying (impact on team or users).
- Action: how you prepared (facts, examples), where and when you said it, the actual words you used, how you listened, what you agreed.
- Result: what changed, how the relationship was afterwards, what you would adjust.
- Keep the person respected: no blaming language in the story.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"A teammate's analyses kept going to the business with no check on the data, and twice we found errors after the meeting. I decided to talk to him directly instead of raising it in the group.

I asked for ten minutes in private and said: 'I want to share something about the last two reports. Both had a join that dropped rows, and the numbers changed after. I think a quick check before sending would prevent this. Can I show you what I do?' I gave the two examples, not a general judgement.

He was defensive for a moment, then said he had been rushed. We agreed on a short checklist and I reviewed his next two reports as a second pair of eyes.

The errors stopped, and he later asked me to look at his work again. I now give this kind of feedback sooner."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I told him straight that his work was sloppy." It judges the person, not the work, offers no example and no help, and it shows poor feedback skill. The opposite, "I never gave feedback because I did not want conflict", fails too.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Courage and timing: you raise it early rather than avoid it or escalate first.
- Specific and kind: facts about the work, not labels about the person.
- Listening: you heard their side.
- Follow-through and the outcome for the person and the team.

**Make it yours:**
- Which real situation fits (code review, analysis, habits)?
- What was the specific example you used?
- What words did you actually say? Write the first two sentences.
- How did the person react, and what did you do next?
- If it went badly, what would you change? A partial result is fine.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
"Can I share something about [the specific piece of work]? I noticed [fact, with one example]. The effect was [impact on team or users]. I think [small change] could help. How does it look from your side? I can [offer: pair, review, share my checklist]."
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if they reject it?" I ask what they see, agree one small step, and check back; I only involve the manager if the impact stays and I have told them I will.
- "Have you given feedback to someone senior?" Describe the same method, with extra care on facts and a question as the opener.
- "How do you take feedback yourself?" Give a short real example.

**Common mistakes:**
- Attacking the person, not the work.
- Saying it in public or in writing first.
- No offer of help.
- A story where the feedback was easy.
