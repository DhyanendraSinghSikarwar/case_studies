**Say this first:** I follow a small, steady set of sources and I only count something as learned when I have tried it on a real problem; here is one example.

**Structure:** Use CARL (see the STAR and CARL section above), with the habit first.
- Habit (about 25 seconds): two or three specific sources or routines, and how you filter noise.
- Context: what you learned and the problem at hand, in one sentence.
- Action: how you tested it on a small, safe case before trusting it, what you compared against.
- Result: what changed, including if it did not help.
- Learning: how you decide what to learn next.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"I keep it small. I read a few release notes and papers each week, and I follow a short list of practitioners. Once a month I pick one thing and try it, because reading alone does not stick.

Recently I read about [a technique, for example a way to evaluate retrieval quality]. Our team was judging a search assistant by reading a few answers by hand. I built a small labelled set of [number of questions] from real queries and tried the new evaluation on it, next to our hand review.

It showed two failure types that we had missed. We changed how we chunk documents, and the share of weak answers on the set fell. It did not fix everything, and I said so in the team note.

The habit I take from it: I try the idea on our data before I recommend it, and I write down what did not work."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I read a lot of blogs and watch videos and I follow the latest models." It lists inputs, with no filter and no use. It sounds like consumption, and the follow-up "what did you do with it?" has no answer.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Curiosity that turns into action, not only reading.
- Judgement on what to ignore, since the field produces more than anyone can follow.
- Honesty about limits: you tested, and you report what did not work.
- Whether you can learn on the job in a field that changes.

**Make it yours:**
- Which sources do you actually return to every week? Name only those.
- Pick one thing from the last few months that you applied, even in a small way.
- What did you compare it against, and what was the result?
- What did you decide not to adopt, and why?
- AI-era note: if you used an AI assistant to learn it, say so plainly and say how you checked its explanations. Do not have AI write your story; live follow-ups on details will show it.

**Follow-ups:**
- "How do you decide what is hype?" I ask what problem it solves for us, whether it has been tried on data like ours and what it costs to test.
- "What are you learning now?" Give one real current topic and the next small step.
- "Did you ever adopt something that did not work?" Have one real case ready, with what you learned from it.

**Common mistakes:**
- A long list of names with no example.
- Claiming to follow everything.
- An example that is only reading, with nothing used.
- Presenting a tool's marketing claims as results.
