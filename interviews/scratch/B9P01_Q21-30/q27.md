**Say this first (1 line):** Without authority, I get action by making the benefit clear to the people who must act, lowering their effort and bringing evidence they can check.

**Structure:** Use STAR (see the STAR and CARL section above); this is the senior-level pattern in the level table (decided, aligned people, handled the fall-out).
- Situation and Task: the recommendation, who had to act, and why they had no reason to follow you.
- Action: what you learned about their goals, how you framed the recommendation in their terms, what evidence or small pilot you offered, who you brought in as an ally.
- Result: what they did, with a measure if honest, and what you could not get.
- Name the trade-off you accepted.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"I found that [an upstream team's] data export dropped a field our models needed. I had no authority over that team, and they had their own priorities.

First I asked what they were measured on. They cared about fewer support tickets. So I did not ask them for a favour; I brought a count of how many downstream issues came from the missing field, with three examples, and a small code change that I had already tested in a copy. That meant they only had to review it.

I also talked to a product manager who depended on both of us and asked her to see the case. The change shipped within [a short time], and our model errors from that cause stopped.

I learned to start from their goal, and to make the first step small."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I sent several emails and then escalated to my manager." This relies on pressure, shows no understanding of the other side, and the interviewer learns nothing about how you influence. Another weak form: "They just agreed because I was right."

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Understanding of the other party's goals and constraints.
- Evidence and low-effort options, not pressure.
- Using allies and escalation sensibly, and in the right order.
- A real result and what you gave up.

**Make it yours:**
- Which real recommendation did people have to act on without reporting to you?
- What did each person care about? How did you find out?
- What evidence or pilot did you bring?
- Who helped you, and when did you involve a manager?
- What did not go your way?

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if they still said no?" I ask what would change their view, record the risk and the decision, and check whether a smaller step is possible.
- "When do you escalate?" After I have tried the direct route, and I tell the other person I am doing it.
- "How do you build influence before you need it?" Help others earlier and be reliable on small things.

**Common mistakes:**
- Describing authority you did have.
- Escalating first.
- Presenting persuasion as a trick.
- No outcome or no cost named.
