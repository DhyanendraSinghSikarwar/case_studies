**Say this first (1 line):** I build trust with a sceptic by starting from their decision, not my model, by showing small checks they can verify, and by being open about what the model cannot do.

**Structure:** Use STAR (see the STAR and CARL section above). For numeric stakeholder-conflict scenarios with a live meeting, see Book 6 Part 6 (Stakeholder Conflicts and Trade-off Decisions); this question is a general story, not a calculation.
- Situation and Task: who the partner was, why they doubted data or ML (a past failure, a gut feeling, a fear of replacement), and what you needed from them.
- Action: listening first, using their language, a small test on a case they know, showing limits, regular short updates.
- Result: what changed in their behaviour (they used the output, asked for more, challenged it usefully).
- Learning on how trust is built.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"A head of operations told me that data projects had cost her team time and delivered little. She was polite but did not want to use our forecast.

I started by asking her to describe the last time it went wrong. It turned out earlier forecasts were wrong in busy weeks, which was the time she needed them most. So I tested our method on past busy weeks and showed her where it was weak and where it helped.

I also offered a two-week trial next to her own estimate, and I agreed that she could override it. We met for fifteen minutes weekly to compare.

By the end she was bringing me cases where her view and the forecast differed. She started using it for planning. The partial result: busy weeks stayed hard, and I told her that."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I explained how the model works and showed the accuracy, and she came round." It starts from the model, uses jargon and treats doubt as ignorance. It also gives no sign of listening.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Empathy for the partner's reasons, not just your own.
- Plain language and checks they can see.
- Honesty about limits.
- Change in the partner's behaviour as the result.

**Make it yours:**
- Which real partner doubted your work, and what was their stated reason?
- What did you ask them first?
- What small check or trial did you offer?
- What limit did you tell them about?
- What did they do afterwards? Keep to what really happened.

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if the model is wrong in front of them?" I say so at once, explain the cause and what I will change.
- "How do you explain a model without jargon?" Use an example from their own work; Book 9 Part 2 covers this in detail.
- "Does trust ever reach zero?" Sometimes; I would drop the model for a rule they trust and keep measuring.

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating the partner as ignorant.
- Showing only strengths.
- Leading with technical detail.
- A story where the partner was never actually sceptical.
