**Say this first (1 line):** I mentored a junior by setting a goal with them, giving them a real piece of work with support, and checking progress by what they could do on their own.

**Structure:** Use STAR (see the STAR and CARL section above); this question is level-sensitive, so see the leadership row in the level table: informal help is expected at mid level, open mentoring at senior level.
- Situation and Task: who the junior was, what they needed to grow in, how the mentoring began (asked or assigned).
- Action: the goal you agreed, how often you met, what real work you handed over, how you gave feedback, how you reduced your help over time.
- Result: evidence it worked (their independent work, quality of reviews, a task they now own), and what did not work.
- Keep the credit with them.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"A new analyst on our team found it hard to turn a business question into an analysis plan. We agreed one goal: by the end of the quarter she would write a plan and own a small project.

We met for thirty minutes every week. Early on I reviewed her plans with questions, such as 'what decision does this support?' I gave her a real request from a partner, not a practice task, and I sat in the first meeting without speaking.

How did I know it worked? By week eight she sent the plan before I asked, and my comments shifted from 'what is the question' to small details. She presented the final result herself.

One thing that did not work was my long written notes; she preferred a short talk. I changed that."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I showed him how I do things and he learned a lot." There is no goal, no evidence, and it reads as telling, not coaching. Another weak form is "I do not have the time for mentoring."

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Whether you coach (questions, goals) and not only instruct.
- Evidence of growth in the other person, not your effort.
- Adjustment to what this person needed.
- Level fit: at senior level, a pattern of growing others, not one case.

**Make it yours:**
- Which junior, intern or peer did you really help? If not formally, informal counts; do not call it more than it was.
- What goal did you set, even in an informal way?
- What real task did you give them?
- What is the evidence it worked in their work, not yours?
- What did you change halfway?

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if they did not improve?" I check the goal and my method first, then I share it with them and their manager if needed.
- "How do you split mentoring and your own work?" A fixed small slot per week and real tasks that also help the team.
- "What do you get out of it?" Say something real, such as clearer thinking from explaining.

**Common mistakes:**
- Claiming formal mentoring that was a few answers in chat.
- No measure of progress.
- Making it about your own brilliance.
- Describing only the first meeting.
