**Say this first:** I will tell you about a time a teammate was stuck on something that was not mine, what I did to help without taking over, and how it went for them.

**Structure:** Use STAR (see the STAR and CARL section above). The key is that the success belongs to your teammate.
- Situation and Task: who was stuck, why it mattered, and why it was not your own deliverable.
- Action: how you noticed, what you offered (time, review, a pairing session, a tool, an introduction), and how you left ownership with them.
- Result: what they delivered, what it cost your own work, and what they did next on their own.
- Close with one line on how you balance helping with your own deadlines.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (Illustrative; replace with your own.)
"A teammate was building her first model for a project of [her team], and she was stuck on a score that looked too good. It was not my deliverable, but I noticed in stand-up that she kept saying 'almost done'.

I offered an hour. We walked through her pipeline together and I asked questions rather than giving the fix. She found that a column was leaking the outcome. I did not touch her code. I shared a short checklist I use for leakage and offered to review her next draft.

She presented it herself a week later, and it passed review with fewer comments. It cost me about two hours, and I moved one of my own tasks by a day and told my lead.

What I took away is that asking questions helps more than fixing, and that the time cost is small if I say it out loud."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I stayed late and finished her part for her so the team would hit the date." It shows rescue, not support, the teammate learned nothing, and it can look like you hid a problem from the manager.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Team mindset: you notice others without being asked.
- Judgement: you help in a way that builds the other person's skill.
- Boundaries: you manage your own commitments and communicate the trade-off.
- Credit: you give the success to the teammate.

**Make it yours:**
- Think of a real teammate who was stuck, even on a small matter.
- What did you notice first, and what exactly did you offer?
- What did you decide not to do for them?
- What did it cost you, and who knew about that?
- What did they do afterwards that they could not do before?

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if helping made you miss your own deadline?" I tell my lead early and agree what moves, instead of absorbing it silently.
- "How did you know it worked?" Say what they did independently later.
- "Has anyone helped you like this?" Give a real example in a sentence; this shows you receive help too.

**Common mistakes:**
- Doing the work for the person and calling it help.
- Taking credit for their result.
- Choosing a story where you were the only expert and the help was a one-line answer.
- No cost or trade-off mentioned.
