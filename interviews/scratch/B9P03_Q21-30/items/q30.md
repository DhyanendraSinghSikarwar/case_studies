**Say this first (1 line):** With less than we wanted, I narrowed the goal to what mattered most, used what already existed, and was open about what we were not doing.

**Structure:** (STAR)
1. Situation: the goal and the real constraint (people, time or budget), honestly sized.
2. Task: your responsibility and what "good enough" had to mean.
3. Action: what you cut, what you reused, who you asked for help, how you tracked risk and told stakeholders.
4. Result: what was delivered, what was not, and what it cost the team.
5. Reflection: what you would ask for or protect next time.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace with your own story)
"We were asked to launch a first version of a recommendation model with two people and half the planned time. I asked the product owner which decision the model must improve, and we agreed on one use case. We reused an existing feature pipeline instead of building a new one, started with a simple baseline, and set up a weekly check on the single measure we cared about. I told stakeholders plainly that segment-level reporting would wait. We launched on time, and the baseline was useful enough to keep. We missed the reporting, and the team worked hard to hit it. Next time I would ask for a clear stop date for extra hours."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "We had no budget, so we just worked harder and got it done." It praises overwork, hides the choices you made, and does not show prioritising. Never claim you managed with nothing if you actually had more; keep the constraint real.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Prioritising: what you chose not to do.
- Resourcefulness without burning out the team.
- Honest communication of limits.
- A concrete outcome and its cost.

Company note: frugality is an Amazon-style principle name (editor knowledge; check the company's current list; Book 7 Part 1, Amazon, caveat on 14 versus 16 principles). The story works for any company.

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real project with a real constraint; state it plainly.
- List what you cut and why.
- Name what you reused or borrowed.
- Say what the outcome was and what you did not deliver.
- If your example involved long hours, say how you handled it, not just that you did it.

**Follow-ups:**
- *What would you have done with more resources?* "[Real answer], for example more testing and the reporting we postponed."
- *How did you decide what to cut?* "I asked which decision the work supported and dropped the rest."

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating overwork as the answer.
- Skipping the trade-offs.
- Exaggerating the constraint.
- No honest cost.
- Reciting the principle name instead of the story.
