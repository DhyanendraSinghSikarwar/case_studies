**Say this first (1 line):** I noticed that our work quality depended on individuals, so I introduced a shared practice and showed it worked.

**Structure:** (STAR)
1. Situation: the team and the quality gap you actually noticed (for example, inconsistent reviews, results nobody could reproduce).
2. Task: what you were asked or decided to fix, and your authority (often none).
3. Action: what you personally did (an example standard, a review checklist, a template), how you got the team on board, and how you made it easy.
4. Result: what changed, in the team's own measures or in observable terms, and what you learned.
5. Say what you would do differently.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace with your own story)
"On my team, model results were sometimes hard to reproduce and reviews were uneven. I did not have authority to set rules, so I proposed a small change. I wrote a one-page checklist for experiments: fixed seeds, saved data version, a baseline, and a short written summary. I used it on my own next project and showed the team how it caught an error before release. Then I offered to pair with two colleagues on theirs. Within a couple of months, most of us used it, and review discussions got shorter and clearer. What I would do differently is ask the team to shape the checklist from the start, so it felt like ours."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I told the team they needed to be more careful, and I always held myself to a high standard." It describes your personal standard, not a change you led; there is no action, no persuasion and no result. Do not take credit for work the team did, and do not invent a clean result.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Your own actions, not "we".
- Influence without authority.
- Making the better way easy for others.
- A result you can describe honestly and a lesson.

Company note: Amazon-style guides name the raise-the-bar principle (editor knowledge; check the company's current list). Book 7 Part 1 (Amazon, C3) says loops include a Bar Raiser interviewer (third-party and anecdotal) and notes that principle lists differ between guides.

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real moment when you improved how work was done, such as a review habit, a template or a test.
- Write what the old state looked like in observable terms (do not exaggerate).
- Say how you convinced people without authority, and one pushback you met.
- Describe the result you actually saw and who else could confirm it.
- If your example was small, keep it small; honest and specific beats grand.

**Follow-ups:**
- *Who resisted, and how did you handle it?* "[Real case]. I asked what worried them, trimmed the checklist, and kept the parts that mattered most."
- *How do you know it helped?* "Fewer reruns and faster reviews, and colleagues told me so. I do not have a precise measure."

**Common mistakes:**
- Describing your own high standards instead of a team change.
- Sounding as though others were careless.
- No evidence of impact.
- Taking sole credit.
- Having a story the follow-up questions cannot support; do not use AI to write it.
