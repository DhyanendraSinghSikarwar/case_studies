**Say this first (1 line):** I would like to start on [date], so I can complete a proper handover; here is what I will have finished by then.

**Structure:**
1. Express enthusiasm to join.
2. State a specific date and the honest reason (handover, wrapping up commitments).
3. Show what you will do before leaving, which signals reliability.
4. Offer a middle path if the team needs you sooner (for example, a call before the start date).
5. Ask the recruiter how notice and joining dates are usually handled; terms differ by employer and contract, and this book gives no legal advice.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace details with your own)
"I am really excited to join. My current team is relying on me for [your handover items], and I would like to leave things in good shape, as I would hope to be treated the same way. I could start on [date]. Before then I will document the pipelines and hand over the open items. If the team needs me earlier for planning, I am happy to join a call or two before I formally start. How are start dates usually set at your end, and is that date workable?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I will join whenever, it does not matter," or "I cannot leave before [date], that is final." The first suggests you do not care how you leave your current job, and the second sounds rigid with no reason. Never say your employer has agreed to something it has not, or claim a date you have not confirmed.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Reliability and professionalism in how you exit.
- Specificity: an exact date, not "soon".
- Flexibility and willingness to work around team needs.
- Honest, constructive tone.

**Make it yours:**
- Check your own contract and ask your current HR what the process is; do not assume.
- List the real handover items for your current work.
- Pick a date you can truly commit to and one earlier fallback.
- Write what you are willing to do before day one (calls, reading) and what you are not.
- If the new employer wants an earlier start than you can give, say so plainly.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Hi [recruiter name],
I am delighted to accept in principle and look forward to joining.
I would like to propose a start date of [date], so I can complete the handover of [your work items] to my team.
If the team needs input earlier, I am glad to join a call before [date].
Could you tell me how joining dates are usually confirmed, and what you would need from me in writing?
Thanks, [your name]
```

**Follow-ups:**
- *Can you start sooner?* "Let me check what is possible with my handover and come back by [date]. I will give you my earliest real date."
- *Why does it take that long?* "Because of [specific handover work]. I want the people I leave behind to be fine."

**Common mistakes:**
- Giving a vague timeline.
- Promising a date before checking your current terms.
- Treating the current employer badly in the conversation.
- Not putting the agreed date in writing.
