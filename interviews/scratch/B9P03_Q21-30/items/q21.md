**Say this first (1 line):** I am very interested, and I want to give you a considered answer, so may I have until [date] to decide?

**Structure:**
1. Thank them and say you are interested (one sentence).
2. Name the real reason you need time (for example, to read the written terms, or to finish another process that is already scheduled).
3. Ask for a specific date, not "a few days".
4. Offer something useful in return, such as questions you can send today.
5. If the deadline stays, ask what they can do, then decide honestly with what you know.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace the reason and date with your own)
"Thank you, I am excited about this role and the team. I want to make a decision I can fully stand behind, so I would like to read the written terms carefully and talk to one person at home. Could I have until [date] to give you a final answer? In the meantime, I will send my open questions today so nothing waits on me. If that date is not possible, could you tell me what the latest is on your side? I would rather be upfront than rush and get it wrong."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Can you give me some time? I need to think." It gives no reason, no date and no sign of interest, so the recruiter cannot tell whether you are leaving, bargaining or stalling. The opposite failure is to accept under pressure and regret it, or to invent a rival deadline to force their hand.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Clear interest stated before the request.
- A specific date and a believable, true reason.
- Respect for the recruiter's constraints, not pressure.
- Professional follow-through (sending questions promptly).

**Make it yours:**
- Write the real reason you need time (documents to read, a process you are honestly in the middle of, a family conversation).
- Choose the shortest date that really works for you.
- List the open questions about the role you would send today.
- If you are in another process, you may say so plainly and ask the other company about its timeline; never claim an offer or a deadline you do not have.
- AI note: a tool can polish the wording, but the reason must be yours; recruiters ask follow-ups live.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Hi [recruiter name],
Thank you for the offer. I am very interested in the [role] and the team.
To decide well, I would like until [date] to review the written terms.
I will send my open questions today: [question 1], [question 2].
If [date] is difficult, could you let me know the latest date that works on your side?
Thanks again, [your name]
```

**Follow-ups:**
- *Why do you need more time?* "I want to read the terms in writing and finish a conversation I committed to. I will give you a firm answer by [date]."
- *Do you have another offer?* "I am in other conversations and I will not name them. You will have a clear answer from me by [date]." (Say only what is true.)

**Common mistakes:**
- Asking without a date.
- Sounding lukewarm.
- Saying something untrue about other offers or deadlines.
- Going silent after asking for time.
- Whether and how a deadline can move is the employer's call; ask the recruiter how they handle it.
