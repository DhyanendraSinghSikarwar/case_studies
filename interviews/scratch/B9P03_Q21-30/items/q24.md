**Say this first (1 line):** I work best with clear goals and room to choose the method, and I do my best work with a manager who gives context and honest feedback.

**Structure:**
1. Describe your work style in 2-3 true, behaviour-based traits.
2. Give one short proof from real work for each.
3. Say what kind of manager and team help you (context, feedback rhythm, collaboration style).
4. Name one trade-off honestly and how you manage it.
5. Show flexibility: you have also worked well in other settings.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace the traits with yours)
"I like to start by understanding the goal and who will use the result, then work in small steps and share early drafts. For example, on [your project] I sent a rough analysis in week one, which saved us from building the wrong thing. I do best with a manager who explains the why, gives specific feedback regularly, and lets me own the method. I like a team that discusses openly and disagrees politely. The trade-off is that I can over-prepare, so I set a time limit and ask for early review. I have also worked well under more directive styles when the situation needed it."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I am a hard worker and a team player. I need a manager who does not micromanage." These are clichés with no proof, and the second line sounds like a warning about you. Do not copy a company's values back to them; do not claim a style you do not have.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Self-awareness backed by an example.
- Fit with how the team actually works (ask about it).
- Flexibility and a mature view of managers.
- A real trade-off, not a disguised strength.

**Make it yours:**
- Ask two or three friends or past colleagues how they would describe how you work.
- Pick one real moment for each trait you claim.
- Think about the best and worst manager relationships you have had, and say what differed, without naming or blaming.
- Name a real limit you are working on.
- Ask the interviewer how the team works day to day, and adjust your answer honestly.
- AI note: stock "work style" answers from a tool all sound the same; a specific example is what survives a follow-up.

**Follow-ups:**
- *Tell me about a time that style caused a problem.* "I once spent too long polishing before sharing. [Describe the real case]. I now share a draft at an agreed point."
- *What if your manager works differently?* "I would ask what they need and adapt my updates, and be clear about what helps me."

**Common mistakes:**
- Generic traits with no evidence.
- Criticising past managers.
- Describing a style that does not match the role.
- Saying you work equally well in every setting.
