**Say this first (1 line):** I am open to it for the right role, and here is what I would want to understand before I decide.

**Structure:**
1. Answer the question honestly: yes, no, or open under conditions.
2. Say what you are open to (a city, a timeframe, a hybrid pattern) without overpromising.
3. List the practical things you would need to know (location expectations, timeline, support offered, team, remote or hybrid options).
4. Say you will ask HR how relocation support works; it varies by employer.
5. Offer a timeline for when you could decide.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; use your own situation)
"Yes, I am open to relocating for the right role. [Or: I am based in [city] and prefer to stay, but I am open to a hybrid arrangement.] Before I decide, I would want to understand where the team sits, how often I would need to be in the office, what the timeline would be, and what relocation support the company usually offers. I would ask HR about that directly, since it varies by employer. I would also want to talk to someone on the team about day-to-day work. Once I know those things, I can give you a firm answer within [a number of days]."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Yes, anywhere, no problem," when you do not mean it, or "No." The first is a promise you may have to break; the second ends the conversation with no information. Never say yes just to get through the round and plan to back out later.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Honesty: your answer is one you can keep.
- Practical thinking (what you need to decide).
- Flexibility without recklessness.
- Clarity on timing.

**Make it yours:**
- Decide in advance, with family if relevant, which locations and arrangements are truly possible.
- Write your top three questions about the move.
- Check the employer's official policy page or ask HR how relocation support works; do not assume.
- Be ready to say what timing is realistic.
- If the answer is no, say so kindly and ask whether remote or hybrid options exist.

**Follow-ups:**
- *Would you relocate in [timeframe]?* "I would need roughly [time] to arrange it properly. I will confirm after I know the details."
- *What if there is no support?* "I would then weigh the whole package and my situation, and tell you honestly."

**Common mistakes:**
- Agreeing to everything and hedging later.
- Refusing without asking about options.
- Asking about relocation terms as if they were guaranteed.
- Treating relocation as only a pay question rather than also team, place and timeline.
