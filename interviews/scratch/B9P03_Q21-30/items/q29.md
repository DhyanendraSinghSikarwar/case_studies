**Say this first (1 line):** I gave a peer direct, specific feedback in private, focused on the work and its impact, and it improved how we worked together.

**Structure:** (STAR)
1. Situation: the peer, the behaviour and why it mattered (without blaming personality).
2. Task: why it was your place to say something.
3. Action: preparing (facts, one or two examples), choosing a private moment, asking permission, describing behaviour and impact, listening, agreeing a next step.
4. Result: what changed, how the person reacted, including discomfort.
5. Reflection: what you would say differently.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace with your own story)
"A colleague kept sharing model results in meetings without caveats, and stakeholders were making decisions on them. I asked if I could share some feedback and we spoke privately. I said, 'In Tuesday's review the forecast was shown without the range, and the product team planned around the single figure. I think it could cause trouble later.' They were defensive at first, so I asked how they saw it. We agreed they would add a short note on limits in future. They did, and later said they had not realised the effect. Our working relationship held, though it took a few weeks to feel easy again. I would now give such feedback sooner."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I told him he was sloppy and needed to improve." It judges the person, offers no example, and shows no listening. Equally weak: "I have never needed to give hard feedback." Do not stretch a small story to sound brave.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Directness with respect.
- Specific behaviour and impact, not labels.
- Listening and an agreed next step.
- Honesty about discomfort and about timing.

Company note: candour and hard-feedback prompts appear in Netflix-style culture guides (Book 7 Part 1, Netflix, evidence rated Thin and third-party; the careers culture page was the only official source) and Atlassian's values interview is reported similarly (Book 7 Part 3, Atlassian, Thin). Use the story either way; do not quote either company's culture back to them.

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real peer conversation, ideally recent.
- Write the exact facts you shared, not your judgement of the person.
- Recall how they reacted and what you did next.
- Say what the result was and what you would change.
- Choose a story where you were respectful; avoid examples where you vented.

**Follow-ups:**
- *How did they react?* "[Real reaction]. I stayed calm, asked for their view, and we agreed one concrete step."
- *Would you do it differently?* "I would raise it sooner and ask first how they wanted feedback."

**Common mistakes:**
- Feedback about character, not behaviour.
- Giving it in public or by message when a talk was needed.
- Making yourself the hero.
- Choosing a story with no discomfort in it.
