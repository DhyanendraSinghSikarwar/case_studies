**Say this first (1 line):** Yes, I can; I would choose people who have seen my work closely, and I will check with them before sharing their details.

**Structure:**
1. Say yes and that you will share references when asked, usually later in the process (ask HR when they need them).
2. Name the types of people: a recent manager, a senior colleague or a collaborator who saw your work directly.
3. Say why each one fits the role (what they can speak to).
4. Say you will confirm with each reference first and brief them on the role.
5. If you cannot use your current manager yet, say so and offer an alternative.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative; replace with your own people)
"Yes. I would suggest three people. My previous manager, [role], who saw my work on [project] and can speak to how I take ownership. A senior colleague from [team], who reviewed my models and can speak to my technical quality. And a stakeholder from [function], who used the results and can speak to how I communicate. I will check with each of them first and send them the job description so they know what to expect. I have not told my current manager yet, so I would prefer to share their contact only after an offer, if that works. How does your process handle references?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Sure, anyone you want." Then handing over a friend or a name you have not asked. The reference may be surprised or vague, and a casual reference hurts you. Do not list people who have not agreed or who cannot speak to your work.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Credibility of the people you choose.
- Professionalism: you ask first and brief them.
- Fit between the references and the role.
- Honesty about confidentiality of a current job.

**Make it yours:**
- List three real people who have seen your work at close range and would speak positively and accurately.
- Ask each for permission, tell them the role, and remind them of projects.
- Decide what to say about your current manager and when.
- Ask the recruiter or HR how and when references and background checks are done; check official sources; this book gives no legal advice.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Hi [reference name],
I hope you are well. I am interviewing for a [role] at [company]. Would you be comfortable serving as a reference for me?
It would help if you could speak to [project or skill].
I can send the job description and my latest summary. They may contact you around [date].
Thank you, [your name]
```

**Follow-ups:**
- *Why not your current manager?* "I have not told them yet. After an offer I can arrange that, if your process allows it."
- *What would they say about you?* "That I take ownership and explain things clearly. [Add one real example they would recall.]"

**Common mistakes:**
- Not asking permission.
- Choosing friends or relatives.
- Giving contacts with no briefing.
- Letting references go stale.
- Treating the check as a formality; it varies by employer, so ask.
