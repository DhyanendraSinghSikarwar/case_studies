### R2 Canada (North America)

**Evidence level:** Thin to Medium. No Canada-wide guide to data scientist, ML engineer or applied scientist loops appeared. The process picture is a scatter of single anonymous Glassdoor and Jointaro reports, mostly Toronto, plus two vendor guides for Shopify [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. Montreal has two or three small reports, Vancouver, Waterloo, Ottawa and Calgary have almost none for interviews [S11][S13]. CV conventions, the Quebec language rules and AI-in-hiring coverage are better sourced, but mostly from commercial blogs, law-firm notes and press [S14][S18][S24]. Snippets only; no page was opened. Borealis AI, Mila, Vector Institute and Cohere interview detail was searched for and not found [U].

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Hubs, by a 2026 CBRE tech-talent ranking summarised in press: Toronto third in North America, Waterloo Region tenth, Vancouver ninth, Montreal eleventh, Ottawa fourteenth, Calgary fifteenth [S28]. The same coverage says most Canadian AI jobs sit in Toronto, Montreal and Vancouver [S28]. Treat the ranking as one commercial report.
- Another guide names Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Ottawa, Calgary and Waterloo as hubs and Amazon among large Vancouver offices [S32]. An industry report says Ontario holds about half of Canada's tech workforce [S29].
- Employer types in the reports, Toronto: banks (RBC, TD) [S1][S8], e-commerce and platforms (Shopify, Instacart, Loblaw Digital) [S2][S7][S9], fintech and payments (Stripe, Affirm) [S6][S12], software firms (Kinaxis, Exadel) [S4][S10], big tech (Meta) [S5]. Montreal: an insurer (Intact) and a games and software firm (Unity, one review) [S13][S11].
- Market direction: one 2026 industry report describes modest growth and steady AI-skill demand [S29]. A single October 2026 vendor snapshot found very few entry-level titles among open Data and AI roles in Canada, and another source says AI and ML roles are usually mid-to-senior; weak, one-day evidence [S30]. No source here shows a broad slowdown [U].
- Hiring season: not found in sources [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or hiring-manager screen first in most reports (Exadel HR call, Kinaxis and Loblaw hiring-manager screens); one TD report had no HR screen and started with live coding [S4][S9][S10][S8].
2. Technical step, two shapes. Live coding: Meta phone screen then live coding, Affirm one LeetCode-medium problem, Instacart one easy coding round, Exadel SQL and Python [S5][S6][S7][S4]. Take-home: Loblaw Digital ML assignment with a review meeting, Kinaxis two-hour coding test, Unity take-home (Montreal) [S9][S10][S11]. Each is a single review (anecdotal).
3. Depth rounds: theory, statistics, A/B testing, system design (Exadel; Loblaw review meeting) and behavioural or director interviews (some RBC reports) [S4][S9][S1]. Montreal Intact reports describe coding then a technical and behavioural interview with a data scientist and a software engineer [S13].
4. Shopify data scientist: guides say five to six rounds over four to six weeks, varying by team [S2][S3] (vendor guides). RBC: phone interviews, one-on-ones, skills tests and presentations are the most-listed stages in a 29-review Glassdoor summary [S1].
5. Round counts in reports: four at Instacart; three stages after HR at Exadel [S7][S4]. Timelines: about four weeks (one RBC Toronto 2019 report) [S1]; Glassdoor shows an average near 49 days for Meta ML engineer from 13 submissions [S5]. Rough only.
6. Data scientist versus ML engineer: a vendor guide says ML engineer prep centres on production and pipelines, data scientist prep on analysis and experimentation [S31]. Canada-specific split not found [U].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- No source on Canadian interview etiquette or tone appeared; interview style is mostly [U].
- Amazon says its interviews use behavioural questions scored on its Leadership Principles and recommends STAR answers; that is company-wide, not Canadian [S33]. Job Bank advises practising with friends or family and bringing a resume copy and reference list [S17].
- One advice page says to be ready to explain every line of your resume with specific examples [S17] (third-party advice via summary).

**CV and application format:**
- Photo: sources agree on none for most roles [S14][S17]. Personal details such as age or marital status left off [S14][S17].
- Length: one to two pages in most guides; one says a single page under ten years' experience; a disagreement to check with the employer [S14].
- References: guides say drop "available on request"; supply when asked [S14]. Job Bank mentions bringing a reference list to interviews [S17].
- Cover letter: most guides say one page for most applications [S14].
- ATS: plain layout, no tables or text boxes; PDF versus .docx disagree, so follow the posting [S14].
- Tech: one summary says Vancouver tech values GitHub and portfolio links; weak [S16]. Tailor each application and volunteer work can be listed [S15].
- Quebec: a resume guide mentions bilingual rules and provincial differences, details not seen [S16].

**Language and location notes:**
- Outside Quebec, English is the working language in all reports [G]. No source gave an English-only statement for specific cities [U].
- Quebec / Montreal: law-firm coverage of Bill 96 (2022) says French versions of job descriptions are needed and an English requirement may need justification; I state no rule beyond that, and the current text is for the Office quebecois de la langue francaise or a Quebec lawyer to confirm [S18]. Tech leaders warned in 2022 it could hurt recruitment [S19].
- Practice: a general guide says some Montreal start-ups work mainly in English, large firms are strict [S20] (weak). Two Montreal ML postings list French and English fluency [S21]. Mix is unclear; read each advert [U].
- Toronto, Vancouver, Waterloo, Ottawa and Calgary: no language-specific interview source found [U]. Keep hubs apart: reports are mostly Toronto [S4][S5][S6][S7][S8][S9][S10], Montreal [S11][S13], Vancouver only through listings [S32].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official source: the Government of Canada "Work in Canada" page, https://www.canada.ca/work-in-canada [S35], and Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) through it. Job Bank, https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/findajob [S17], is the official job-search service.
- Verify there: which route fits your situation, what the employer must do, current timelines, and whether the employer appears on the official non-compliant list mentioned on the Work in Canada page [S35].
- Seen in results: several archived or third-party pages were old, so rely only on current official pages [U]. This book states no rules.

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- "Canadian experience": sources discuss it as a screening practice, not a rule. Ontario coverage says rules on advertised postings changed in 2026 [S22]; HR commentary says it may persist quietly in resume screening [S23]. Plan to show role evidence and local-format resume; verify any legal point with official Ontario pages [U].
- Expect a shorter, photo-free resume with no personal data, plus a cover letter more often than a CV-only process [S14][S17].
- Expect take-home plus review at some employers and live LeetCode-style rounds at others; both appear in the reports [S9][S10][S5][S6].
- Expect a behavioural component, with STAR at least at big employers [S33][S1].
- Expect AI-disclosure wording in some Ontario postings (employer side) [S24].
- Quebec postings may be French-first or bilingual, so check the language line [S18][S21].
- Round counts and timelines vary widely between employers [S2][S5][S7].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: one-to-two-page photo-free resume, a one-page tailored letter, and a project list [S14].
- Days 3-4: SQL and pandas drills: Book 3 Parts 3-4 [S1][S4].
- Days 5-6: LeetCode-style practice under time: Book 5 Parts 1 and 3 [S6][S7].
- Days 7-8: a timed take-home, with README and explained choices: Book 1 Part 12, Book 4 Part 1 [S9][S10].
- Days 9-10: stats, A/B, ML theory: Book 1 Parts 10 and 13 [S9][S13].
- Days 11-12: ML design (and GenAI design for those roles): Book 1 Part 11, Book 2 Part 12 [S4].
- Days 13-14: STAR stories, a 10-minute project presentation, and questions for the recruiter on format, language and AI policy [S1][S33][G].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one Glassdoor report or one Toronto employer as the Canadian norm [S1][S5].
- Preparing only LeetCode when a take-home and review meeting is possible, or the reverse [S9][S5].
- Adding a photo or personal data to the resume [S14].
- Skipping the language line on Quebec postings [S18][S21].
- Relying on blogs or recruiters for work-authorisation points rather than the official pages [S35].
