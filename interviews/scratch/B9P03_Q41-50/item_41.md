**Say this first (1 line):** I would own the miss in one sentence, say what the bet was and why it was reasonable at the time, then spend most of the answer on what I changed.

**Structure:** STAR plus a change step (editor guidance [G]).
- Situation: the decision and what was uncertain.
- Task: why you chose the risk, and who agreed.
- Action: what you did, including the safeguards you set up (a small pilot, a checkpoint, a stop condition).
- Result: what happened, stated plainly.
- What I changed: one habit or process you still use.
Do not blame the data, the team or the timing.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "On a [type of project] I proposed trying [new approach] instead of our usual method, because I thought it would [benefit]. I got agreement for a [short time] trial. I did not set a clear stop point, and by the time we looked, [time] had gone and the approach did not beat the baseline. I told my manager as soon as I saw the comparison, we went back to the usual method, and we still delivered, but later than planned. I own that: the bet was reasonable, my safeguard was not. Since then I write down what result would make us stop before I start any experiment, and I review it at a set date with the team. I have used that on [later project], and we stopped one idea early."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "It was a great idea, but the team was not ready and the data was bad." It blames others, shows no learning and no change. Another weak version is a fake failure, a small slip dressed up as a bet; interviewers hear the lack of risk.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Honesty about the miss, without drama.
- Reasoning behind the risk, not only the outcome.
- Safeguards and how quickly you raised the problem.
- A concrete change you actually kept.
- Seniority signal: the size of the bet fits the level you are applying for.

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real bet that did not work, not a safe one.
- Write what you knew and did not know when you decided.
- Name the change you made and where you have used it since.
- Be ready for: "what would you do differently with the same information?"
- Use your own story; AI-written stories collapse under live follow-ups.

**Follow-ups:**
- Would you take the same bet again? "Yes, with a stop point written down first; the idea was fine, my control was not."
- Who else was affected? "[Name the team]; I told them directly and what I would change."
- How do you decide which risks to take? "Small, reversible and with a defined way to stop."

**Common mistakes:**
- Choosing a failure that was really a success.
- Spending the whole answer on the story and none on the change.
- Blaming a person or a tool.
- Claiming a lesson you cannot show you applied.
