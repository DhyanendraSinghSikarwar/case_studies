**Say this first (1 line):** I would name what I want from my next role, say what I value in that type of company with an example from my own work, and show I would work well in the other kind too.

**Structure:** Compare, choose, qualify (editor guidance [G]).
1. Give your preference in one sentence.
2. Give two reasons from your own experience: scope, pace, mentoring, tooling, ownership, stability, learning.
3. Name one trade-off you accept.
4. Tie it to this company and role, with something you checked.
5. Avoid running down the other kind of company.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "I lean towards [large / early-stage] companies right now. In my last role I [real example, for example owned an end-to-end model from data to deployment / worked in a team with strong review habits], and that is where I learned fastest. At a [large company] I get [mentors, mature tools, data at scale]; at a [startup] I get [broad scope, quick decisions]. The trade-off I accept is [less structure / less breadth]. For this role, what drew me is [specific thing from their team or product]. I could be happy in either kind of company, so what matters most to me is [what you want to learn or own]. I would like to hear how this team works day to day."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Startups are chaotic and risky" or "big companies are slow and bureaucratic." It reads as complaint and stereotype, ignores the company in front of you, and gives no reason from your own work.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Self-knowledge: you know how you work best.
- Evidence from your own experience, not slogans.
- Fairness to both options.
- Fit with this company and team.
- Whether your reasons are about learning and impact, not only about status or safety.

**Make it yours:**
- List two moments when you worked best and what the setting was.
- Name one thing the other kind of company would have given you.
- Check this company's size, stage and team on its own site, and add one fact.
- Practise both versions: if they ask the reverse, you should answer without contradicting yourself.

**Follow-ups:**
- What would you miss at a smaller company? "[Specific thing, for example mentoring or tooling]; I would build it myself or ask for it."
- What if this role is not what you prefer? "I check the work and the team first; the setting is secondary."
- Where do you see yourself growing? "In [skill], by [real step]."

**Common mistakes:**
- Criticising the kind of company you are not choosing.
- Giving reasons about brand or comfort only.
- Contradicting your CV (all startups, then "I love big firms").
- Not mentioning this company at all.
