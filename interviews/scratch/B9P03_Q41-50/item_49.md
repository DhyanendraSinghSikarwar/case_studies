**Say this first (1 line):** I say it in one or two factual sentences, without blame, and move straight to what I did and what I want next.

**Structure:** Fact, brief, forward (editor guidance [G]; layoff-explanation guides advise one or two factual sentences without blame, snippet only).
1. State what happened: the company [reduced the team / closed the project], and it was a company decision.
2. Say one thing about your work or outcome that is true.
3. Say what you did since: learning, projects, interviews.
4. Link to why this role fits.
5. Stop and let them ask; do not over-explain.
Ask HR how references and reasons for leaving are recorded; this book gives no legal advice.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "In [month], [company] reduced the size of the [team] as part of a company-wide restructure, and my role was one of those affected. I was disappointed, but it was not about performance; my manager can speak to my work on [project]. Since then I have [real action: completed a course, built a project, helped a former colleague]. I am looking for a role where I can [what you want to do], and this role on [team] does that. I am happy to answer anything you would like to know about it."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** A long, bitter account of the company or leaders, or hiding the facts and giving a vague answer. Blame makes the interviewer wonder how you would speak about them; vagueness invites suspicion.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Honesty and composure.
- No blame, no bitterness.
- How you used the time since.
- Clarity about what you want next.

**Make it yours:**
- Write the facts in two sentences using your own dates and wording.
- Name one real thing you have done since, not something you plan to do.
- Check whether a former manager or colleague is willing to speak for you, and ask them first.
- Practise saying it calmly in front of a friend; the first time you say it aloud should not be in the interview.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
[Company] [reduced / closed] [team or project] in [month], and my role was affected.
Before that I had worked on [one line of real work].
Since then I have [real action].
I am looking for [what you want], which is why this role interests me.
```

**Follow-ups:**
- Were you the only one? "No, [a number of colleagues] were affected, as far as I know."
- Will your manager give a reference? "I will check with them; I can give other contacts as well."
- How did it change you? "I [real change, for example keep my skills current, keep a record of my work]."

**Common mistakes:**
- Speaking badly about the former employer.
- Over-apologising.
- A long answer.
- Stating facts you cannot support.
