**Say this first (1 line):** I made a call that some people disliked, I explained the reason and the trade-off openly, and I can show what it did for customers or the team.

**Structure:** STAR with a stakeholder step (editor guidance [G]).
- Situation: what was at stake and who would be unhappy.
- Task: why the decision was yours or yours to recommend.
- Action: the evidence you used, how you told people, what you offered in return (a plan, a delay, a workaround).
- Result: what happened to the customer or team, including the cost.
- Reflection: what you would do differently in communicating it.
Amazon-style prompts (have backbone; customer focus) and candour-style culture guides are third-party and skew to senior levels; check the company's current list of principles.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "We had a model ready to ship that improved [metric], but in my checks it performed worse for [a customer group]. The team wanted to launch on the planned date. I recommended we hold the launch for [short time], and I knew that would annoy people who had worked hard to hit it. I shared the numbers openly, proposed a fix with a date, and offered to run a limited launch for the groups where it worked. It was not a popular decision; a few colleagues were frustrated. We shipped [later] with the fix, and complaints from [group] did not appear. I now bring that kind of finding earlier, so a delay is never a surprise."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Everyone disagreed but I knew I was right, so I did it." It sounds arrogant, offers no evidence, ignores the people affected and shows no learning. A story with no real cost to you is also not an unpopular call.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Courage with evidence, not stubbornness.
- Empathy for those affected.
- Clear communication of the reason and trade-off.
- Accountability for the cost.
- Judgement about when to stand firm and when to change your mind.

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real decision that cost you some goodwill.
- Write the evidence you had, and what you gave up.
- Say how people took it, honestly, including anyone who still disagrees.
- Name what you would change about how you delivered it.
- Do not claim a result you cannot show.

**Follow-ups:**
- What if you had been wrong? "I would have said so and reversed it; I had agreed a checkpoint where we would review."
- How did you keep relationships? "I explained the reasons one to one first and gave people a say in the plan."
- Did anyone change their mind? "[Real answer.] Some did once they saw the data; others did not."

**Common mistakes:**
- Casting colleagues as villains.
- Using a decision that was popular after all.
- No data behind the call.
- No mention of the cost.
