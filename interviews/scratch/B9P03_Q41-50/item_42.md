**Say this first (1 line):** I would not treat the first offer as the only offer; I would ask for time, compare it on components, and ask for what I need politely, while being honest about my situation.

**Structure:** How-to, in order (editor guidance [G]).
1. Separate the pressure from the facts: a gap in work does not change what the role is worth to the company.
2. Say thanks and ask for the details in writing and a sensible time to decide.
3. Compare components: pay structure, role and level, location, start date, learning, team.
4. Decide your own walk-away point in advance, in private, and keep it in your notes, not in the conversation.
5. Ask once, clearly, for what you want; accept a calm no and decide.
Be honest throughout: do not say you have other offers if you do not.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "Thank you, I am glad to get this and I am excited about the team. Could you send the full details in writing? I would like to take [a few days] to review it properly. Having come through a layoff, I am keen to settle, and I also want this to be the right fit for both of us. Once I have it in writing I may come back with one or two questions on [the package or the role level]. I can give you my answer by [date]. Is that workable?" Then, in the follow-up: "Based on my research, I was hoping for something closer to [your target range]. Is there flexibility?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** Accepting on the call out of relief, or the reverse: bluffing about other offers to create pressure. The first leaves questions unasked; the second can collapse if the recruiter checks or asks for details, and it costs trust.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Calm under financial or emotional pressure.
- Professional, honest tone.
- A process: time, components, one clear ask.
- Realism: you can accept or walk away without resentment.

**Make it yours:**
- Fill in [your target range] from your own research and keep it private until needed.
- Decide your three must-haves before the call (for example role scope, location, start date).
- Practise saying "may I have until [date]" out loud.
- If you need an offer soon for personal reasons, you may say so honestly; you do not have to.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Thank you, I am glad to have this offer.
Could you send it in writing, with the full breakdown?
I would like until [date] to review it. 
My research suggests [your target range] for this role and level.
Is there flexibility on that, or on [other term]?
```

**Follow-ups:**
- Do you need this job urgently? "I want to settle, and I also want to choose well; I am looking for a good fit."
- Will you accept below your range? "I will consider the whole package and the role, and tell you by [date]."
- Should you mention the layoff? "Only if asked, in one factual line."

**Common mistakes:**
- Saying yes in the first minute.
- Inventing competing offers.
- Apologising for asking.
- Negotiating only one component and missing the others.
