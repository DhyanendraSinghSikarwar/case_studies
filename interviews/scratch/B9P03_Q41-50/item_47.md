**Say this first (1 line):** I would tell each company the truth about my timeline, ask the one with the later deadline for a little more time, and decide on the real facts, without inventing anything.

**Structure:** Process (editor guidance [G]; guides say be honest about competing offers and that a fabricated offer damages credibility, snippet only).
1. Write down both deadlines and what each company has actually put in writing.
2. Contact the company whose deadline comes later, say you have another process with an earlier date, and ask politely whether a faster timeline is possible.
3. Contact the earlier-deadline company, thank them, and ask whether the deadline can move by a short, specific period; give a reason that is true.
4. Do not claim an offer you do not have, and do not invent a deadline.
5. If neither moves, decide using your own priorities and accept the outcome.
Ask the recruiter how extensions are usually handled; policies differ.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "I would be straightforward. To [Company A, the later one] I would say: 'I am very interested in this role. I have another offer that needs an answer by [date]. Is there any way to speed up your process, or could we talk about where things stand?' To [Company B] I would say: 'Thank you, I am excited about this. I am finishing another interview process that ends on [date]. Could I have until [date] to give you a full answer?' I would not suggest that one has offered more than it has. If I cannot get the time, I will decide with what I have. I would rather lose an option than lose trust."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** Saying "I have a better offer" when there is none, or hinting at a figure that does not exist. It can be checked, and it ends the relationship. The other weak move is saying nothing and letting a deadline pass.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Honesty under pressure.
- Courtesy and clarity in a delicate message.
- Planning: you ask early, not on the last day.
- Willingness to make a decision with the information available.

**Make it yours:**
- Fill in [date], [Company A] and [Company B] with real details only.
- Rank what matters most to you (role, team, learning, location) before the calls.
- Write one honest sentence on why you need the extra time.
- Practise the message aloud; if you cannot say it truthfully, do not send it.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Hello [Name], thank you for the offer, I am keen on the role.
I am completing another interview process that ends on [date].
Could I have until [date] to respond?
If that is not possible, please let me know what the latest date is.
```

**Follow-ups:**
- What if they say no? "I thank them and decide by the date; the offer is still a good one."
- Will you reveal the other company? "Only if I am comfortable; I do not have to."
- Does the deadline change your answer? "It changes the timing, not my priorities."

**Common mistakes:**
- Bluffing about offers, figures or deadlines.
- Asking for an open-ended delay.
- Accepting and then withdrawing without warning.
- Asking on the final day.
