**Say this first (1 line):** I treat verification as a normal step after an offer: I give accurate documents, and if something will not match a record, I tell HR early and explain it, instead of hoping it is missed.

**Structure:** Process answer (editor guidance [G]; vendor and HR guides describe identity, education and employment checks after an offer, varying by employer, snippet only).
1. Ask HR what will be checked, which documents are needed, and the timeline.
2. Collect documents early: identity proof, degree and marksheets, experience and relieving letters, payslips if they are asked for, references.
3. Compare your CV, forms and records before submitting; fix errors on your side.
4. If a detail will not match (a name spelling, a date, a title, an employer renamed or closed), tell HR first, in writing, with whatever supports it.
5. Ask HR how mismatches are handled; this book gives no legal advice and rules differ, so check official sources and the employer's own guidance.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "I would first ask HR what they check and what documents they need, so I can send everything in one go. Before I submit, I compare my CV with my certificates and letters. If something does not line up, for example my [job title] on the CV is different from the letter, I would tell HR before the check, show them what I have, and explain the reason in two sentences. I would also ask how they usually handle a difference like that. I would rather raise a small gap early than have the agency find it. And I make sure my CV is accurate to begin with, so there are very few surprises."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I have nothing to hide, so there is no problem." It ignores real mismatches caused by records. Worse is hoping nobody checks, or changing a detail to match: that is a trust issue, not a paperwork one.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Honesty and early disclosure.
- Organisation with documents.
- Calm, factual explanation of a gap.
- Asking HR how the process works instead of assuming.

**Make it yours:**
- List the records a check might cover for you and note any mismatch today.
- Write a two-sentence explanation of each and the document that supports it.
- Collect old letters and contact details now, before an offer arrives.
- If a former employer is hard to reach, plan another document you can offer, and ask HR whether it is acceptable.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Hello [HR name], before the verification starts I want to flag one detail.
My [record] shows [A], while my [other document] shows [B].
The reason is [one sentence]. I have attached [document].
Could you tell me how this is usually handled and whether you need anything else?
```

**Follow-ups:**
- What if your old employer will not respond? "I tell HR early and offer other proof, such as offer letters or payslips, and ask what they accept."
- What if the offer depends on it? "I check the offer letter wording with HR; I do not assume."
- Would you change your CV to match? "No; if the record is wrong I get the record fixed or explain it."

**Common mistakes:**
- Waiting until the agency raises it.
- Rounding up a title or date on the CV.
- Treating verification as an insult.
- Giving legal opinions; ask HR instead.
