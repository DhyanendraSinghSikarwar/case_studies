**Say this first (1 line):** The summary number looked fine, so I looked at the raw data underneath, and that is where the real issue was.

**Structure:** STAR, with a detective flow.
1. Situation: the metric or behaviour that looked fine or odd.
2. Task: why you decided to dig rather than accept it.
3. Action: the steps you took, such as slicing by segment, reading raw records, tracing the pipeline.
4. Result: what you found, who was told, and what changed.
5. Reflection: the habit you kept.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** (illustrative template; replace with your own story)
"Our [model] showed good overall results on [metric]. But one [segment] kept raising support questions. Instead of accepting the average, I split the results by [segment] and read a sample of raw records. I found that [a data issue, for example a field recorded differently after a system change] made the model look better than it was for that group. I showed my lead the before and after, we corrected the data handling, and re-ran the evaluation. The honest number was lower, but it matched what [users] experienced. Now I always check results by segment before reporting an average."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I am very detail-oriented and always check everything." It claims a trait without evidence. A story of tedious effort with no finding, no impact and no follow-up also fails.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Curiosity: you did not accept a headline number.
- Method: a clear sequence of checks.
- Impact: the finding mattered and was acted on.
- Honest communication, including when the finding was awkward.

**Make it yours:**
- Choose a time you found a data, system or process problem others had missed.
- Write the steps in order, with the one check that cracked it.
- Say who you told and how they reacted.
- Add what changed afterwards, such as a test or a dashboard.
- If your example is about a model trade-off, see Book 6 Part 6 for worked data-science scenarios and do not repeat them here.
- Keep your own data and company details private if confidential; describe them generally.

**Follow-ups:**
- *How did you know where to look?* "A mismatch between the average and a user report; I narrowed it by segment."
- *What if you had found nothing?* "I would report that the check passed and how I checked."

**Common mistakes:**
- Claiming rigour without a specific finding.
- Describing only tools, not reasoning.
- Not saying what changed.
- Hiding that the finding made earlier work look worse.
