### R1 Singapore (Southeast Asia)

**Evidence level:** Medium-Thin. There is no official or employer-published guide to data or ML interview rounds. The process picture rests on anonymous Glassdoor-type reviews and aggregator guides (GovTech, Spectrum, Micron, HP, Gojek, ByteDance, Grab, Shopee, DBS), mostly single candidates across 2019-2026 [S1][S2]. CV, etiquette and AI-in-hiring points are covered by job boards, recruiter pages and news [S4][S5][S9][S10]. Snippets only; no page was opened.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types in results: government and government-linked technology bodies (GovTech, DSTA) [S8]; banks (DBS, UOB, OCBC) [S3]; regional and global tech (ByteDance, Grab, Shopee, Gojek) [S2]; a university (NUS) and multinationals such as Micron, HP, GSK, AIG, Dell [S1][S8]. One undated listing article says Indian-headquartered analytics firms have Singapore offices [S8] (weak).
- The city is Singapore itself; no further hub breakdown was found [U].
- Government data roles are described as asking for SQL, Python and, in some listings, LLM API experience and PyTorch [S8]. GenAI role postings list RAG, vector databases and agent frameworks, but no Singapore interview reports for these roles appeared; GenAI reviews found were for India, not used [S12][S11].
- Hiring season: not found [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen first, per a summary of reviews [S1].
2. Technical stage, most often a take-home: GovTech reports of a four-question take-home in about two days, and another of a coding and Tableau assignment of about two and a half days (anecdotal) [S1]. HP Inc: one take-home with ML questions on a dataset plus a Python question, then one live interview [S1]. Gojek: a model-improvement task with a one-page write-up [S1]. Micron: a computer vision take-home, then a manager discussion [S1].
3. Follow-up discussion or presentation of the take-home, with candidates asked to defend design choices (Spectrum, GovTech reports) [S1].
4. Big-tech ML engineer loops are described differently: data-structures coding at medium or hard level, a deep dive on your CV, and in some ByteDance reports an ML design prompt; one 2020 report said there were no ML questions (all anecdotal) [S2]. One aggregator quotes a pass rate from seven self-reported interviews, which is too small to use [S2].
5. Banks: aggregator guides describe an HR screen, technical rounds (SQL, Python, ML concepts, case study) and manager or panel rounds; a DBS review mentions a business case with data and tools; DBS's own page describes hiring only in general terms [S3].
6. Timelines: aggregator estimates of about three to six weeks (NUS), three to five weeks and four rounds (Maybank), two to four weeks and two rounds (Luxoft) [S1]; Shopee about 12 days on one aggregate of 14 reviews and a Grab report of about a week [S2]. Treat all as rough.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**

2. Interviews are often split into an HR interview and a "user" interview, where "user" means the business or hiring team side; panel interviews and group activities can also appear [S11][S13].
3. Psikotes (psychological or aptitude tests: logic, numerical, verbal, and personality such as DISC or MBTI) commonly sit in the process before an offer, per Indonesian career sites; whether tech employers use them for data roles is not shown [S14][S13] [U].
4. Reported data-role examples. Gojek: a guide for a data analyst role lists four rounds (phone screen, online HackerRank test, data scientist round, manager round) with deep A/B testing questions, advanced SQL and experiment design [S4]; another guide says Gojek platform teams add a code-pairing round, described as an engineering process [S3] (commercial).
5. Grab (Singapore-headquartered, regional; not necessarily Indonesia-based): guides describe a screen on past projects, a case study presenting an approach, and for analyst roles sometimes a take-home or presentation; Glassdoor reports mention a take-home SQL and data analysis task, SQL questions such as a day-over-day drop in completed rides, and SQL, statistics and root-cause analysis followed by a case study for a senior role (anecdotal, location of each report not confirmed) [S5][S6].

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Typical sequence in several reports: online assessment, then technical round(s), then a managerial round, then an HR round [S1][S2]. One TCS review says the managerial round is sometimes placed before HR [S1] (anecdotal).
2. Online assessment: coding, data manipulation and ML-concept questions on platforms such as HackerRank per one guide; a DSA-heavy test of three problems in about 90 minutes is reported at a software-role employer, which is not typical for data roles [S3][S21]. Services firms are described as using aptitude, logical and verbal tests (AMCAT, CoCubes, eLitmus named) before technical rounds [S22].
3. Technical round for data roles: Python and data manipulation, SQL, then questions on your own projects (Fractal data scientist review; a TCS review adds SQL on paper and a Databricks question) [S1][S3] (anecdotal).
4. Managerial round: communication, teamwork, experience and role suitability (TCS review) [S1]. HR round: culture fit and communication style per one guide; at PhonePe an HR call collected basic details and expectations (anecdotal) [S4][S3].
5. Round counts and timelines vary: about 4.7 rounds after screening at top product companies per one guide [S21]; a Fractal process of about a week and a TCS process of about two weeks in single reviews [S1][S3]; a Google-related report says two to three months [S3] (single review, role not data science). Treat all as rough.
6. Campus hiring: run through college placement offices on a calendar, with the IIT and IIM season in December and January and pre-placement offers from internships announced before it; the usual campus sequence is a machine test, a technical interview and an HR interview [S6] (HR-vendor glossary).
7. Lateral hiring: targeted at specific skills and experience, described by one vendor as the main route for specialised and senior roles [S5]. The share of lateral hires and the time-to-fill in vendor pages are unsourced; not used [U].
8. Campus market signals: a Deloitte 2024 survey press release reports a fall in campus placement budgets and pre-placement offers, and a 2025 report says IT campus intake improved in H1 FY26 (headline level) [S7][S8].
9. Referrals: a LinkedIn trends survey, reported by People Matters, said at least 65 per cent of Indian recruiters use employee referral programmes (older survey) [S16]. Naukri's recruiter site says about two in three companies hire through referral programmes (platform claim) [S16]. Percentages of referral hires for data roles: not found [U].
10. Channels named for data science roles in a community thread: Naukri, LinkedIn, iimjobs, Hirist and Indeed [S17]. Recruiter and consultancy fees and roles: not found in results [U].
11. Offer stage: offers are described as usually conditional, mainly on background verification and proof of previous employment; the offer letter carries headline terms and the appointment letter on or after joining carries the detailed contract (HR blogs) [S9]. Acceptance windows of a few days appear in one blog and one sample offer [S9] (weak).
12. Notice period and joining: there is no single standard; it depends on employer, industry and level and sits in the employment terms, so a joining date is agreed after the current notice period [S10]. Confirm the joining date in writing before resigning [S9]. A counter-offer from the current employer is named as a reason offers fall through, in one vendor article [S5].
13. Background verification (BGV): checks on identity, address, employment history, education and court records, with reference checks sometimes added; consent is collected first [S12]. Timing estimates differ: seven to ten business days for most technical hires in one guide, two to six weeks in another, so plan for a range [S11][S12]. Some firms check before the offer, others after, and some let you join while it runs [S12]. A relieving letter from the previous employer is a commonly requested document [S11].
