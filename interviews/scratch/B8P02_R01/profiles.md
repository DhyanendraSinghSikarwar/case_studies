### R1 United States (North America)

**Evidence level:** Medium for big-tech loops and CV conventions, Thin for every other segment. The US market is broad, so this profile is split by employer segment and no segment is generalised to the whole market. Big-tech data science and ML loops are described by several guides and anonymous candidate reports that broadly agree on shape [S1][S2][S4][S5]. Finance, healthcare, start-up, consulting and research-lab evidence is a few prep-site guides and single reviews each [S19][S20][S21][S22][S23][S27]. Government is sourced only at the application-portal level [S25]. Defence: not found [U]. Snippets only; no page was opened. Segment tags below: (big tech), (finance), (healthcare), (start-up), (consulting), (government), (research lab).

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Segments seen in results: big tech (Google, Meta, Uber, Amazon, DoorDash) [S1][S3][S4][S8][S17]; finance and fintech (Capital One) [S19]; healthcare and pharma (GSK, Pfizer, CVS Health) [S20][S21][S22]; consulting (McKinsey QuantumBlack) [S23][S24]; start-ups and AI-focused firms (general guidance, no named US firm confirmed) [S16]; federal agencies via USAJOBS announcements [S25]. Defence employers: not found [U].
- Role titles matter by segment. Product-focused data scientist loops (SQL, stats, product sense) are reported mainly at consumer big tech [S1][S3][S4]. Applied scientist and research scientist loops add research or science rounds [S17][S26][S27].
- Market direction (directional, vendor and blog sources): one summary of Indeed Hiring Lab figures says ML and AI engineer listings sit well above their 2020 level while general tech listings sit well below, and another piece says the generic data scientist title is shrinking in favour of specialists who ship production systems [S14][S15]. Treat these as commentary, not measurement.
- Hubs and hiring season: not found in sources [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. (big tech, data scientist) Recruiter screen, then a technical screen, usually SQL and sometimes a short product case, then a multi-round loop [S2][S3]. One Meta report describes a 45-minute video screen combining SQL and product questions (anecdotal) [S3].
2. (big tech, data scientist) The loop is typically four to five rounds across SQL, statistics, product sense, experimentation and behavioural (pattern across anonymous reports, not a fixed format) [S2][S3]. One Uber report lists five rounds: product sense, stats and experimentation, applied modelling, data processing, behavioural bar-raiser (anecdotal) [S4]. One Google product data scientist report lists stats/modelling, experimentation and a "Googleyness" round (anecdotal) [S1].
3. (big tech, ML engineer) Recruiter screen, one or two coding rounds, an ML system design round and a behavioural round; one guide gives four to six stages over three to six weeks [S5][S6]. Meta reports range from two coding rounds plus one ML design round plus one behavioural round (anecdotal) [S8].
4. (big tech, applied scientist) Amazon guides describe leadership-principle questions in every round, alongside coding, ML depth and breadth, system design and science application; stage counts differ between guides [S17][S18]. Amazon's own prep page for the role exists but only title-level text appeared [S17].
5. (research lab) Research scientist loops at DeepMind and Meta FAIR are described as including a research talk or job talk plus research-focused interviews, along with coding and behavioural rounds (guide and a few reviews; anecdotal) [S26][S27].
6. (finance) Capital One analyst and senior analyst reports describe a recruiter screen, an online assessment of messy-data work, then a final multi-interview day with case, data-challenge and behavioural interviews [S19]. This is a data analyst loop at one firm, not proof for all banks. Model-risk and quant-style data science loops: no interview guide found [U].
7. (healthcare) GSK, Pfizer and similar guides describe a recruiter conversation, a technical or behavioural interview, a take-home case on a healthcare-style dataset and a presentation to a panel [S20][S21]. CVS Health: one review of a long virtual onsite mixing behavioural, A/B testing and industry cases [S22].
8. (consulting) QuantumBlack reports describe an online coding and stats assessment, technical experience interviews, a pair-programming interview, and a data-science case of 45 to 60 minutes; forums disagree on round counts [S23][S24].
9. (government) Federal roles are applied for through USAJOBS announcements; each posting sets its own rules, a resume format and an online questionnaire in some cases [S25]. Interview stages for federal data roles: not found [U].
10. Timelines: a "three to six weeks" figure appears in one ML guide [S6]; Meta FAIR report cites about two months in one review (anecdotal) [S27]. No reliable market-wide timeline found [U].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Behavioural answers in structured STAR form (situation, task, action, result) are the standard advice in guides, with interviewers probing the depth and authenticity of stories through follow-ups [S18][S17].
- Candidate-side self-advocacy: specific results and your own contribution are expected in answers; this comes from prep guides, not a study [S18][G].
- Anything beyond this about tone or small talk was not found in sources and is not stated [U].
- Networking: see the CV and application section for referral evidence.

**CV and application format:**
- Length: most guides say one page for early-career candidates and two pages once experience is longer; one gives about seven years as the point for two pages (guides pooled in a search summary; commercial and university pages) [S11][S29][G].
- Photo, date of birth, marital status: guides advise leaving them off, and a university career page says the same [S11][S29]. One source gives anti-discrimination rules as the reason; this book makes no legal claim [U].
- Layout: plain single column, standard headings (Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education), no text boxes or images, because applicant-tracking systems read plain text better; file-type advice conflicts (some say PDF, some .docx unless the posting says otherwise), so follow the posting [S11][S12b][S29].
- Cover letter and references: no usable guidance appeared in the results [U].
- Federal applications differ: some announcements require the USAJOBS resume builder and an online questionnaire, and say outside resumes will not be accepted; read each announcement [S25].
- Referrals as a channel: one SHRM-hosted item is titled as saying referrals remain a top source of hires [S13]; a search summary reported an Ashby analysis putting referrals at roughly 18 per cent of hires and said the widely repeated 30-50 per cent figure is weakly sourced; the page carrying the Ashby figure is not identified, so treat it as approximate [S14b][U]. Referral share by employer size and sector: not found with certainty [U].

**Language and location notes:**
- English is the working language in all sources; no non-English requirement appeared [G].
- Hubs, remote versus office policy and hiring season: not found in sources [U].
- Google is reported to have reintroduced in-person rounds in response to AI-cheating concerns (secondary reporting) [S15b][U].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official bodies a candidate should check: the US Citizenship and Immigration Services, the Department of State and the Department of Labor; no URL for them appeared in the results, so find them through the official government site [U].
- For federal jobs, the USAJOBS portal and the individual announcement state who may apply: https://www.usajobs.gov/job/809634200 [S25].
- Verify on official pages: which route applies to you, what the employer must do, and timelines. This book states no rules and no figures [G].
- For questions about what employers may ask in interviews, check the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission's own pages; results only gave law-firm and career-site summaries, which are not used here [U].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect a recruiter screen, then a technical screen and a multi-round loop at one employer, rather than a single mass test; stage counts vary by team [S2][S5][G].
- Expect SQL, statistics, experimentation and product-sense rounds for data scientist roles at consumer tech, as separate rounds [S2][S3][S4].
- Expect an explicit behavioural round, with Amazon applying leadership-principle questions throughout [S17][S18].
- Expect a CV of one page, with no photo or personal details, and for some employers a plain layout tuned for applicant-tracking software [S11][S29].
- Expect referrals and networking to be a real channel for getting a recruiter's attention, share uncertain [S13][S14b].
- Expect some employers to ask for a take-home or presentation, and some to allow or forbid AI tools, so ask [S9][S12][S20].
- Segment differences are wider than country differences: a pharma panel, a consulting case and a big-tech loop have different shapes [S19][S20][S23].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: One-page plain CV, no photo or personal details; check the target segment's norms and ask for referrals from your network [S11][S29][S13].
- Days 3-4: SQL and pandas: Book 3 Parts 4, 6, 8 [S2][S3].
- Days 5-6: Stats and experimentation: Book 1 Parts 10, 13 [S1][S4].
- Days 7-8: Product-sense and business-case practice: Book 6 Parts 1, 5 [S3][S19].
- Days 9-10: ML system design for ML roles: Book 1 Part 11 [S5][S8].
- Days 11-12: Write STAR stories with results and follow-up depth [S18][G].
- Days 13-14: Mock loop; prepare a 10 to 15 minute project or case presentation if the segment uses one; ask the recruiter about AI-tool policy and take-home scope [S20][S12][S13b].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one segment's loop (big tech) as the US process for finance, healthcare or government [S19][S20][S25].
- Preparing only coding for data scientist loops that include stats, product sense and behavioural rounds [S2][S4].
- Generic behavioural answers with no result or follow-up depth [S18].
- Using AI tools in a round or take-home without checking the stated policy [S15b][S12].
- Relying on a referral share figure from a blog without checking its origin [S14b].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- Policies are mixed and changing, and sources disagree: a 2026 tracker says Amazon candidates acknowledge not using unauthorised tools, Anthropic banned AI in live interviews in 2025 reporting (current position needs checking), and Meta and Google are reported both as restricting AI and as piloting AI-assisted coding rounds (blogs and secondary reporting; check with the recruiter) [S15][S15b][S16b][S12][S13].
- Take-home debate: some posts argue AI has weakened take-homes and favour live rounds; others describe candidate choice between formats (blogs, one podcast) [S9][S10][S11].
- A data-engineering survey claims most companies ban AI on take-homes while most candidates use it; it is DE-specific and not used as evidence for ML roles [S16c][U].
- Market commentary on ML roles rising and entry-level pressure is vendor and blog material, not official statistics [S14][S15c].
