### R1 Singapore (Southeast Asia)

**Evidence level:** Medium-Thin. There is no official or employer-published guide to data or ML interview rounds. The process picture rests on anonymous Glassdoor-type reviews and aggregator guides (GovTech, Spectrum, Micron, HP, Gojek, ByteDance, Grab, Shopee, DBS), mostly single candidates across 2019-2026 [S1][S2]. CV, etiquette and AI-in-hiring points are covered by job boards, recruiter pages and news [S4][S5][S9][S10]. Snippets only; no page was opened.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types in results: government and government-linked technology bodies (GovTech, DSTA) [S8]; banks (DBS, UOB, OCBC) [S3]; regional and global tech (ByteDance, Grab, Shopee, Gojek) [S2]; a university (NUS) and multinationals such as Micron, HP, GSK, AIG, Dell [S1][S8]. One undated listing article says Indian-headquartered analytics firms have Singapore offices [S8] (weak).
- The city is Singapore itself; no further hub breakdown was found [U].
- Government data roles are described as asking for SQL, Python and, in some listings, LLM API experience and PyTorch [S8]. GenAI role postings list RAG, vector databases and agent frameworks, but no Singapore interview reports for these roles appeared; GenAI reviews found were for India, not used [S12][S11].
- Hiring season: not found [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen first, per a summary of reviews [S1].
2. Technical stage, most often a take-home: GovTech reports of a four-question take-home in about two days, and another of a coding and Tableau assignment of about two and a half days (anecdotal) [S1]. HP Inc: one take-home with ML questions on a dataset plus a Python question, then one live interview [S1]. Gojek: a model-improvement task with a one-page write-up [S1]. Micron: a computer vision take-home, then a manager discussion [S1].
3. Follow-up discussion or presentation of the take-home, with candidates asked to defend design choices (Spectrum, GovTech reports) [S1].
4. Big-tech ML engineer loops are described differently: data-structures coding at medium or hard level, a deep dive on your CV, and in some ByteDance reports an ML design prompt; one 2020 report said there were no ML questions (all anecdotal) [S2]. One aggregator quotes a pass rate from seven self-reported interviews, which is too small to use [S2].
5. Banks: aggregator guides describe an HR screen, technical rounds (SQL, Python, ML concepts, case study) and manager or panel rounds; a DBS review mentions a business case with data and tools; DBS's own page describes hiring only in general terms [S3].
6. Timelines: aggregator estimates of about three to six weeks (NUS), three to five weeks and four rounds (Maybank), two to four weeks and two rounds (Luxoft) [S1]; Shopee about 12 days on one aggregate of 14 reviews and a Grab report of about a week [S2]. Treat all as rough.

- A government career page advises confirming interview time and location in advance [S10].
- Generalisations about national character in etiquette blogs were discarded.

**CV and application format:**
- Length: one to two pages in most guides; reverse-chronological layout; PDF or Word file [S4].

- Personal details: guides advise keeping them minimal and avoiding age, NRIC and marital status [S4].
- References: sources differ between two or three named referees and "available upon request" [S4].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**

- Expect a short process at some employers (days to a few weeks) per reports, but wide variation [S1][S2].
- Interview language is English in guides, so no local-language paperwork is described [S10].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
