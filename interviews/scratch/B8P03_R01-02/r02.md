### R2 Malaysia (Southeast Asia)

**Evidence level:** Thin. No Malaysia-specific guide to data scientist or ML engineer rounds appeared. Process evidence is a few anonymous Glassdoor reviews for Kuala Lumpur employers (Grab, BAT, AirAsia, Petronas, Maybank), single candidates of mixed seniority and year, plus generic job-board advice [S1][S2][S3]. CV, language, hub and job-portal material is better covered but mostly from job boards, recruiters and news [S4][S5][S6][S7][S8]. Snippets only; no page was opened.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Hubs named in 2026 news and investment-agency pages: Kuala Lumpur and Cyberjaya grouped as the software, fintech, AI and shared-services core; Penang as semiconductor, IC design and electronics (a recruiter source ties Penang AI demand to computer vision); Johor as data centres [S6].
- Employer types in results: regional tech (Grab, Kuala Lumpur and Petaling Jaya) [S2]; a tobacco multinational's Kuala Lumpur office (BAT) and an airline-turned-platform (AirAsia) [S1]; a government-owned energy company (Petronas) and banks (Maybank, CIMB) [S3]; multinational data-centre and cloud investments (Microsoft, Google, AWS announced) [S6]. A promotional bootcamp blog lists Petronas, Maybank and AirAsia as AI employers (weak) [S2].
- One 2026 company announcement describes a Kuala Lumpur technology hub recruiting software, data and AI staff; it is the employer's own claim [S6].
- A recruiter hiring-trends overview for Malaysia exists but its content was not seen [S8].
- Hiring season: not found [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen or phone interview (BAT, AirAsia single-review stage breakdowns) [S1].
2. Technical stage. Grab Kuala Lumpur: a senior-role 2021 report of three rounds (SQL with a tech lead; ML theory and past projects with a manager; behavioural with a director), and a 2025 ML engineer report of four mostly technical rounds including live coding and explaining your thought process (anecdotal) [S1][S2]. BAT: phone, one-on-one and skills test from three reviews, about four weeks in one 2025 report [S1].
3. Banks and GLCs: Petronas's official careers page describes online application, then an assessment that may be in person or virtual, and says the process varies by country and position [S3]. A Petronas data analyst intern page lists a personality test, skills test and panel (two reports) [S3]. A 2026 Maybank prep guide lists recruiter screen, role-specific assessment where used, functional or manager round, HR discussion, document checks and offer confirmation; a data scientist guide mentions SQL and Python data-manipulation tasks, but most Maybank material found was for Singapore [S3].
4. Generic guides add a take-home or case study "sometimes" [S1]; no Malaysia-specific take-home report appeared [U].
5. Timelines: about four weeks in one BAT report; no consistent figure otherwise [S1][U].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Job-board and recruiter pages say to arrive on time, check the dress code in advance, greet the interviewer politely and silence your phone [S9]. These are advice articles, not studies.
- Hiring managers are said to expect candidates to attend alone [S9] (one job-board article).
- Behavioural and soft-skill questions are described as common, including "why this company" [S9].
- A job-board article says technical tests focus on how you approach a problem and communicate it, not only the final answer [S9].
- No Malaysia-specific data-science test guidance was found [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Length: one or two pages for a resume; guides distinguish a longer academic CV for research or specialist roles [S4].
- Photo: sources conflict. JobStreet advises leaving out photo, marital status and religion unless the employer asks; VisualCV says a photo and further personal details may be expected [S4]. Follow the advert [G].
- Personal details: name, mobile, professional email and city or state are described as customary; nationality can help foreign applicants [S4].
- References: employers are described as asking for them at a later stage, so a reference list on the CV is often unnecessary, though JobStreet suggests having several ready [S4].
- Cover letter: JobStreet suggests a letter that fits on one page, introducing yourself and adding context to the CV [S4].

**Language and location notes:**
- English is described as long expected in corporate hiring, and common in job-board adverts alongside Bahasa Malaysia [S5].
- Mandarin: a 2024 national newspaper report says more mid-senior listings include it as a requirement or a plus; a commentator argues it is often applied as a blanket checkbox [S5].
- A Deputy Minister was reported as saying interviews in all sectors should be in Bahasa Malaysia with English allowed for investors; this is a policy statement, current status not found [S5].
- A career blog suggests you may ask permission to use another language if stuck [S5]. Read each job advert's language line [G].
- Cities named: Kuala Lumpur, Petaling Jaya, Cyberjaya, Penang, Johor [S2][S6].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official bodies to consult: the Immigration Department's Expatriate Services Division (ESD), whose portal address given in secondary sources is esd.imi.gov.my/portal (not opened, verify it independently) [S10], and PERKESO's MYFutureJobs portal, the official job portal at https://myfuturejobs.gov.my [S7].
- Verify on official pages: which route applies to you, what the employer must do beforehand, and current timelines. Secondary sources date from several years back, so this book states no rules [U].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect a recruiter screen, one or more technical or manager rounds and an HR round, sometimes with an assessment, rather than one campus-style test [S1][S3][G].
- Expect behavioural questions, including "why this company", alongside technical ones [S9].
- Expect job adverts that name English, Bahasa Malaysia and sometimes Mandarin; check the language line [S5].
- Expect CV conventions on photo to vary, and references to be requested later [S4].
- Take-home assignments were not reported for Malaysia in the sources, unlike some Singapore reports; confirm with the recruiter [U].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: one- to two-page CV, minimal personal details, short one-page letter [S4].
- Days 3-5: SQL and Python data manipulation under time. Book 3 Parts 3-4 [S1][S3].
- Days 6-7: ML theory refresher. Book 1 Parts 1 and 3 [S1].
- Days 8-9: live coding with spoken reasoning. Book 1 Part 12, Book 5 Part 1 [S2].
- Days 10-11: three project stories and a "why this company" answer [S1][S9].
- Day 12: for GenAI roles, RAG design. Book 2 Part 12 [G].
- Days 13-14: ask the recruiter about stages, assessment, interview language and AI policy [G].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating a single Glassdoor review, often old or senior-level, as the process [S1].
- Assuming English only, or Bahasa Malaysia only, without reading the advert [S5].
- Skipping company research for "why us" questions [S9].
- Relying on blogs for work-authorisation facts instead of official pages [S7][S10].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- A Hays-based survey summary says most Malaysian employers do not intend to integrate AI into recruitment strategy this year even though most HR staff support AI tools; recruiter and vendor sources report resume screening and scheduling as the common uses [S11].
- A March 2026 recruiter blog says employers are cautious about over-automating and candidates worry about unfair AI screening [S11].
- Candidates are described as using AI interview-prep tools [S11].
- No Malaysian rule or employer policy on candidate AI use during interviews was found [U].
