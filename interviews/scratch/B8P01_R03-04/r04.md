### R4 Ireland (Europe)

**Evidence level:** Thin to Medium. Dublin ML and data scientist interview reports in the results are few, small and mixed across years (2020-2026), and several came from non-Dublin pages that a summary grouped together. The CV, competency-interview and AI-in-recruitment points are better covered by Irish recruiter and institutional pages. No official employer process page appeared [S1][S2][S7].

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Dublin is described as the centre, with Cork, Galway and Limerick described as emerging for fintech and AI (Cork), MedTech (Galway) and hardware engineering (Limerick) in a career-advice piece [S11].
- Employer types in the sources: US technology multinationals with Dublin offices (LinkedIn, Meta, Stripe, Workday, eBay, Indeed) [S1][S5][S3][S2], financial firms (Citi, Mastercard) [S4], and AI-lab and R&D expansions announced in news items such as OpenAI in Dublin and Genesys in Galway [S12].
- Ireland's skills body is quoted as saying demand is intensifying for AI and big data analytics skills [S13]. A one-day live-listing snapshot (October 2026) shows a small number of data and AI listings and should not be read as a trend [S14].
- Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter chat, then a technical phone or video screen, for example for an ML engineer role in Dublin [S1].
2. Loop or onsite: one Indeed Dublin data scientist report lists four rounds (math and stats, impact and prioritisation, coding and data wrangling, machine learning) [S2]; the LinkedIn ML engineer report describes a virtual loop with coding, ML design and behavioural rounds [S1].
3. Shorter loops elsewhere: eBay, two rounds on Python, SQL and ML [S3]; Citi, an hour-long phone interview with two lead data scientists [S4].
4. Competency-based questions ("tell me about a time when") are described as the most common interview type in Ireland across sectors, so a behavioural component is likely [S7][S8][G].
5. No consistent timeline appeared in the search [U].

Anecdotal throughout; one Indeed report cites more than 8 interviewers, which is not a norm [S2].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Irish sources on interviewing emphasise competency examples tied to the job description, at least two strong examples per key competency, and STAR structure [S7][S8].
- Showing enthusiasm and explaining why you applied is advised by one Irish tourism employer guide [S9].
- Small-talk and tone norms did not appear in the results; no claim made [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Length: two pages at most in most sources; one page can suit a graduate [S6].
- Photo: sources advise against it, and also against date of birth, marital status and nationality [S6][S10].
- Cover letter: views differ, from valued to optional unless requested; one page is a safe default [S6][S10].
- References: sources differ between "available on request" and named referees; tell referees in advance [S6][S10].
- Format: reverse-chronological, plain layout for applicant-tracking systems, Irish or UK spelling, usually PDF [S6].

**Language and location notes:**
- Working language is English in all the sources; Irish or UK spelling is advised on CVs [S6]. No claim about Irish-language requirements [U].
- Cork, Galway and Limerick are described as options besides Dublin; a single career blog is the source [S11].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official sources to consult: the gov.ie employment permits page of the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment [S15] and the Workplace Relations Commission page on coming to work in Ireland [S16].
- Verify there: whether you need an employment permit at all, which category fits the role, what the employer must satisfy, current processing times. Secondary guides were seen to disagree and to name outdated departments, so rely on official pages only [U].
- This book makes no claim about eligibility, thresholds or timing.

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- A behavioural, competency-based component is described as standard in Irish interviews, so prepare STAR-style examples even for technical roles [S7][S8] (compare the India baseline in Part 3 of this book [G]).
- Loops at multinationals may mix coding, stats and ML in separate rounds rather than one general technical round [S2][S1].
- CV conventions differ from some formats: no photo, no date of birth or marital status, two pages at most, named or on-request references [S6][S10].
- Cover letters are treated as tailored documents by some recruiters, not an afterthought [S6].
- Employers may use AI tools in screening or interview scheduling, and candidates are reported to be using AI when applying, so expect disclosure questions and ask what is used [S17][S18].

**How to use Books 1-7 for this market:**
- Stats and probability round: Book 1 Part 13 (statistics deep dive I) and Book 1 Part 14 (statistics deep dive II: probability puzzles, Bayes) [G].
- ML fundamentals: Book 1 Part 1 (stats, probability, ML fundamentals, from-scratch code) and Book 1 Part 3 (classification metrics, imbalance, calibration) [G].
- Coding and data wrangling: Book 1 Part 12 (NumPy/pandas/sklearn live-coding & AI-code-review rounds) and Book 3 Part 3 (subqueries, CTEs) [G].
- ML design: Book 1 Part 11 (ML system design) and Book 4 Part 6 (serving) [G].
- GenAI roles: Book 2 Part 6 (RAG basics) and Book 2 Part 12 (GenAI system design + coding) [G].
- Impact and prioritisation or behavioural: Book 6 Part 6 (stakeholder conflicts & trade-off decisions) and Book 6 Part 1 (product metrics & metric drops) [G].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-3: Probability, Bayes and hypothesis tests. Book 1 Parts 13 and 14 [S2].
- Days 4-5: ML fundamentals, tuning, over/underfitting. Book 1 Parts 1 and 3 [S2].
- Days 6-7: Python and pandas data wrangling in a plain editor. Book 1 Part 12 [S2][S3].
- Days 8-9: ML design and, for GenAI roles, RAG design. Book 1 Part 11, Book 2 Part 12 [S1][G].
- Days 10-12: Write two STAR examples for each competency in the job advert [S7][S8].
- Days 13-14: Trim CV to two pages without a photo; prepare referees; two mock loops [S6][S10].

**Common mistakes:**
- Preparing only coding for loops that include stats and an impact or prioritisation round [S2].
- Generic answers to competency questions with no concrete example [S7][S8].
- Putting a photo, date of birth or marital status on the CV [S6][S10].
- Treating one candidate's loop at one company as the standard for all of Dublin [S2][G].
- Relying on blogs for work-authorisation facts rather than the official pages [S15][S16].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- Employer-side: IrishJobs research reported by the press says 47 per cent of employers use AI to help recruitment and 35 per cent to schedule interviews, while another figure of nearly 8 in 10 appears in a different report with unclear definition; recruiter-sponsored surveys, not comparable [S17][S18].
- Candidate-side: Morgan McKinley reports that 71 per cent of candidates in Ireland use AI when applying, and Greenhouse is quoted saying 36 per cent of Irish job seekers have been interviewed by an AI, with non-disclosure a reason for dropout [S18][S19].
- IrishJobs advises prioritising in-person interviews and project tasks in 2026 [S17].
- No Dublin-specific report of AI-assisted technical rounds appeared [U].
