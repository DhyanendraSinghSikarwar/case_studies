### R3 Netherlands (Europe)

**Evidence level:** Medium. Several independent sources agree on a broad shape (recruiter or HR screen, a technical check, a take-home or case that is then presented, a final team or manager round), but the process-level evidence is mostly small-sample Glassdoor reviews and career-guide blogs, some years old, and ING itself is described as not standardised across teams. No official employer process page and no reliable AI-era interview source appeared [S1][S3][S5].

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Hiring is described as concentrated in and around Amsterdam, Eindhoven and Utrecht, with ASML, Philips, ING, Rabobank, Booking, Adyen and bol named as regular hirers (one guide; a commercial blog) [S1].
- Employer types in the sources: a large bank (ING, Rabobank) [S2][S4], a travel platform (Booking.com, Amsterdam) [S6][S7], a payments company (Adyen) [S8][S9], a semiconductor equipment maker (ASML, Eindhoven and Veldhoven) [S10], and recruitment agencies, where an intake interview with an external recruiter is described as common [S13].
- A January 2026 outlook describes AI acceleration and a market finding its balance, which is general commentary [S18]. One October 2026 live-listing count for Data and AI roles was very small and is a snapshot of one day, not a trend [S19].
- Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen, sometimes through an agency [S1][S13].
2. A technical check: a live coding session on a platform such as CoderPad, or a take-home, with the take-home called very common in one guide [S1]. At ING, an older report describes an online multiple-choice, SQL and Python assessment [S2].
3. A case or take-home that you present. One ING report describes a take-home case discussed in an hour-long interview [S2]; a more recent ING report describes a high-level technical talk, then a take-home project, then presenting it [S3]. Rabobank's Glassdoor summary lists a presentation stage in all three reviewed interviews, a tiny sample [S4].
4. Final conversation with the hiring manager or team, in some reports a more senior person [S2][S9].
5. Length: about 3-5 weeks and 3 rounds in one vendor guide [S5]; Booking.com reports range from about 2 weeks to about 2 months, with an average near 30 days on one Glassdoor aggregate for the ML engineer role [S6].
6. Not standardised: at ING each team is said to have its own hiring autonomy [S3]. Check with the recruiter.

**Round formats:**

| Format | How common as reported | What is tested | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Take-home assignment | Called very common in one guide; reported at ING, Booking.com and Adyen | Data cleaning, modelling choices, a documented notebook | [S1][S3][S6][S9] |
| Live coding or online test | Reported, varies by company | SQL, Python, ML and statistics basics | [S1][S2][S9] |
| Case study discussion | Reported at ING and Booking.com | Business framing, reasoning about a model | [S2][S6] |

Anecdotal throughout; a single review is not the norm.

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Guides describe interviews as direct, with blunt follow-up questions on items on your CV; one Eindhoven guide adds that this is not as harsh as it sounds [S13][S14]. These are expat-blog impressions, not data.
- Guides advise focused answers without tangents, punctuality and efficiency [S13].
- One guide says a clean, well-explained notebook can matter more than the best model score [S1].
- A decision is often described as coming within about a week, with a polite follow-up after that [S13].

**CV and application format:**
- Length: guides give one to two pages, with two A4 pages as a common ceiling [S11][S12].
- Format: reverse-chronological is described as the most common layout [S11].
- Photo: sources disagree. Some say it is not required, others say it is common; one source says no photo, birth date or nationality is needed [S11][S12]. Follow the job advert.
- Cover letter (motivatiebrief): described as expected with most applications and usually about one page [S11][S12].
- References: sources differ between "available on request" and listing two referees; diplomas and reference letters are typically asked for later [S11][S12].

**Language and location notes:**
- English is described as the main working language at international companies in Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Eindhoven, and data roles are listed with software roles as typically English [S1][S15].
- Some roles, particularly customer-facing or at traditional Dutch companies, ask for Dutch; the advice is to read each advert [S15].
- Hubs named: Amsterdam, Eindhoven, Utrecht; Delft and Groningen mentioned as growing by one report [S1][S19].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official sources to consult: the Dutch government page on foreign citizens working in the Netherlands (government.nl) [S16] and the Immigration and Naturalisation Service (IND) [S17].
- Verify there: which route applies to your nationality and situation, what the employer must do or be registered for, who files the application, and current timelines. Do not rely on blogs or recruiters for these points [U].
- This book makes no claim about eligibility, thresholds or timing.

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- A take-home plus a presentation round may be more prominent than a series of live algorithm rounds; expect to prepare a written, explained deliverable [S1][S3] (compare the India baseline in Part 3 of this book [G]).
- A cover letter and a motivation conversation are described as normal parts of the application, which a CV-only process would not require [S11][S12].
- Process steps can differ team by team within one employer, so ask the recruiter for the exact stages rather than assuming a fixed loop [S3].
- Direct, blunt follow-ups on CV items are described in guides; prepare a short evidence-backed line for each project [S13][G].
- Paperwork such as diplomas and references is described as requested at a later stage, not with the first application [S12].

### R4 Ireland (Europe)

**Evidence level:** Thin to Medium. **Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
