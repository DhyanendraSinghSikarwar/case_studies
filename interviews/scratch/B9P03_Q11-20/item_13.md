**Say this first (1 line):** I would politely steer back to the role and the process, say what I am looking for in general terms, and ask how the company handles this question.

**Structure:** A how-to in five moves [G, editor guidance; this book gives no legal advice].
1. Stay warm and brief; do not treat the question as an attack.
2. Check how it is handled: ask the recruiter or HR whether the company asks for current pay, why, and whether it is optional. Rules and practice differ by place and employer, so check official sources; no legal advice here.
3. Move to the role: ask about scope, level and the range for the position.
4. If you decide to share something, share what you are comfortable with and keep it true. You decide; never state anything untrue.
5. Offer your own expectation as a range placeholder you have researched, [your target range], once you know the role.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** "Thanks for asking. Before I get into that, can I check how this is usually handled here, and what the pay range for this role is? I would like to focus on the role first: the scope, the level and what success looks like. Once I understand that, I am happy to share what I am looking for, which is based on the work and my research, not only on my current package. If you need something from me in writing, tell me what form it takes and I will think about it. What range have you set for this position?" Delivered calmly, this is honest, open and keeps the conversation moving.

**Weak version (and why it fails):** Either blurting a figure before you know the role, or saying "that is private" sharply. The first gives away your position; the second sounds defensive and stalls the call.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Are you calm and professional on an uncomfortable question?
- Do you stay honest while not oversharing?
- Do you show you did your research on the role?
- Do you ask good process questions?
- Does the recruiter still feel you are easy to work with?

**Make it yours:**
- Decide in advance what you are comfortable sharing, and what you are not.
- Research your target range from sources you trust and keep it in notes.
- Write your own one-sentence redirect in your voice.
- Prepare the process question you will ask HR.
- Practise it aloud so it sounds relaxed.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Thanks for asking. Could you tell me how the company usually handles questions about current pay, and whether it is required for this step?
I would like to understand the role first. What is the pay range for this position and level?
Once I know the scope, I will happily share what I am looking for: [your target range].
```

**Follow-ups:**
- What if they insist? "I ask what the process needs and why, and I decide what to share. I stay polite and honest."
- Would you share it if asked in writing at offer stage? "Ask HR what form is used and how it is checked, and read official guidance first."
- What if they refuse to give their range? "Say I am flexible for the right role and ask when we can discuss it."

**Common mistakes:**
- Giving a figure before you know the role.
- Making up or inflating your current package; the honest version works.
- Sounding irritated or evasive.
- AI-era note: a tool can polish this wording, but it cannot know your situation; say it in your own voice so it holds up live.
