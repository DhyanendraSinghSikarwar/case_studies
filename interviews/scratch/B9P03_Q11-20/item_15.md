**Say this first (1 line):** I put both offers into one table of components, weight each by what matters to me, and compare the whole package over time, not one headline figure.

**Structure:** A comparison method [G, editor guidance; terms differ by employer, so confirm each in the written offer].
1. List the components: fixed pay, variable or bonus parts, equity or similar if any, benefits, leave, start date, level and title, location and remote days, team and manager, learning support, growth path, stability.
2. Mark each as fixed, variable or uncertain; ask the recruiter to explain anything unclear.
3. Give each component a weight from your own priorities (for example 1 to 3 stars, not money values).
4. Add the non-pay factors: the work, the people, the learning.
5. Sleep on it, then decide; use the table to find what to negotiate.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** "I would not compare the two headline figures. I would write both packages as a table: fixed pay, variable parts, benefits, leave, level, location, learning support and the team. I mark what is guaranteed and what is uncertain, and ask the recruiter to explain anything I do not understand. Then I add what matters to me beyond pay, such as the manager, the scope of the work and what I would learn in two years. I give each row a weight, and the table shows where the gap is. If one offer is stronger on pay and the other on growth, I decide which matters more now. I might also use the table to ask one employer for a specific change."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I would take whichever pays more." It ignores uncertain parts, growth, and fit, and shows no structured thinking.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you think in components, not one number?
- Do you separate fixed from uncertain?
- Do you include non-pay factors?
- Is your method clear and your priorities honest?
- Do you ask questions instead of guessing?

**Make it yours:**
- Build the table with your real offers or a practice example.
- Choose your own top three priorities and write why.
- List what you still need to ask each employer.
- Decide your walk-away conditions in words, not figures.
- Mention one trade-off you would accept.

**Follow-ups:**
- What if one offer has equity? "Ask how it works and the conditions in writing; treat it as uncertain until you understand it, and check official sources for any tax question."
- How do you decide if it is close? "Choose the one that matches my top priority, and ask one side whether a gap can be closed."
- Do you tell them about the other offer? "Only truthfully, and only when it helps the conversation."

**Common mistakes:**
- Ignoring variable parts you do not understand.
- Letting one number decide everything.
- Not asking the recruiter for details.
- AI-era note: a tool can build the table but cannot know your priorities; the weights must be yours.
