**Say this first (1 line):** I will tell about a time I spent my own time helping a teammate because the team's success mattered more than my credit.

**Structure:** STAR with a team focus [G, editor guidance].
1. Situation: the teammate's difficulty and why it did not help your own goals.
2. Task: why you chose to step in, and what you gave up (time, focus, credit).
3. Action: what you did, how you helped them learn or finish rather than doing it all for them, and how you kept your own work on track.
4. Result: the teammate's outcome, the team's outcome, and what you learned.

Company note: values-round guides for Atlassian mention team-first prompts (one snippet quotes "Play, as a team"; third-party; Book 7 Part 3, Atlassian, evidence Thin), and Book 7 Part 1 (Google, C1) lists collaboration among Googleyness attributes, third-party. Check the company's current values.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "A new teammate was struggling to build a [kind of analysis] before a team review, and my own deadline was a week later. Helping her would cost me a few hours and nobody would credit me. But the review mattered for the whole team. I sat with her for an hour, showed her how I structure the work, and let her do the steps, not me. I checked in twice that week for ten minutes. She presented well, and the team got its decision. My own work slipped by half a day, so I told my manager early. Afterwards she started helping others too. I learned that a small amount of time early prevents bigger problems later."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I always help everyone." Too general, no example, and it can sound like you ignore your own work.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you notice others' needs?
- Do you help without taking credit?
- Do you help them grow, not depend on you?
- Do you manage your own commitments honestly?
- Is the result shared and real?

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real time you helped someone with no benefit to you.
- Write what it cost you.
- Describe exactly how you helped.
- Say what the teammate and the team gained.
- Mention how you protected your own deadlines.

**Follow-ups:**
- What did you give up? "Be honest about time or focus, and how you told your manager."
- What if the teammate never improved? "Say what you tried, and how you passed the issue to the right person."
- Where do you draw the line? "I help within my capacity and say clearly when I cannot."

**Common mistakes:**
- A story where you quietly did all their work.
- Hidden self-interest presented as generosity.
- Making the teammate look weak.
- AI-era note: a tool-written story of kindness tends to be generic; use a real moment and its details.
