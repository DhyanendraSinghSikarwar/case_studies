**Say this first (1 line):** I will show how I learn fast on purpose: a clear target, a small first project, help from people, and a check that I really learned it.

**Structure:** STAR with a learning focus [G, editor guidance].
1. Situation: the work that required a skill you did not have, and the time you had.
2. Task: what you needed to be able to do, and by when.
3. Action: how you broke it down; the resources you used; who you asked; how you practised on something small and real; how you checked your understanding.
4. Result: what you delivered, what you could then do, and how you keep learning.

Company note: Book 7 Part 1 (Google, C1) lists curiosity, collaboration and user focus among Googleyness attributes, from a third-party guide.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "My team needed [a new tool or method, such as a streaming system] for a project, and I had not used it. I had [number] weeks. I first asked what the project actually needed, which was a small part of the tool. I read the main documentation, then built a tiny version using our own data. I asked a colleague who had used it to review my approach, and she pointed out a mistake in how I handled errors. I wrote a short note for the team on what I learned and what I was unsure about. The project shipped on time, and I now help newer colleagues with it. I learned to start small and ask for review early."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I am a fast learner; I watched some videos and figured it out." No example, no method, no result, no humility.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you have a learning method?
- Do you narrow the target?
- Do you use other people's knowledge?
- Do you apply what you learn on real work?
- Do you share it and stay honest about gaps?

**Make it yours:**
- Choose a real time you learned something for work, study or a project.
- Write the deadline and the target.
- List the steps you actually took.
- Note who helped you.
- Say what you can do now that you could not before.

**Follow-ups:**
- How do you know you really learned it? "I built something with it and had someone review it."
- What did you not learn? "Name one gap and how you handle it."
- How do you learn when no one around knows it? "Docs, small experiments, public communities, and clear notes."

**Common mistakes:**
- Saying you are a fast learner with no story.
- Claiming expert level after a short time.
- Learning alone and never asking.
- AI-era note: say honestly how you used AI tools to learn, if you did, and show you checked the results yourself.
