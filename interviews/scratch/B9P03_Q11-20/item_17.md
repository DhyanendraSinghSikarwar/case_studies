**Say this first (1 line):** I want to grow in skill and impact in this field, and I see this role as a good place to do that.

**Structure:** Three parts [G, editor guidance].
1. Direction: the skills and kind of work you want to be doing (deeper in ML, owning a product area, mentoring), not a title.
2. Link: how this role helps you get there.
3. Contribution: what you hope to give back to the team.
Keep it flexible: say your plans may change as you learn. Avoid "I want your job" and avoid saying you will certainly leave.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** "In five years I would like to be someone the team trusts with the hardest data problems, and to have helped others grow too. I want to get deeper in [your real area, such as applied ML or experimentation] and understand the business well enough to choose the right problems, not only solve the ones I am given. This role fits that, because [real reason from the job description]. Over time I would like to take on more ownership, maybe lead a project or mentor newer colleagues. I know plans change as I learn, so I stay open to where the team needs me. Could you tell me how people grow in this team?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I want to be a manager" or "I want your job" or "I have not thought about it." The first two sound ambitious for the wrong reasons; the third sounds uninterested.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Is your plan realistic and linked to this role?
- Are you motivated by growth, not only a title?
- Are you flexible and honest?
- Do you want to stay and contribute?
- Do you ask about growth paths?

**Make it yours:**
- Write the two skills you most want to build.
- Link each to something in the job description.
- Write one thing you want to contribute.
- Be honest about uncertainty.
- Prepare one question about growth in the team.

**Follow-ups:**
- Do you want to manage people? "Say honestly: curious about it, and want to try mentoring first; or prefer to grow as a specialist."
- What if the role does not offer that growth? "Ask how the team handles it, rather than assuming."
- What would make you leave? "Be honest and calm: no learning, no honest feedback; and say you would raise it first."

**Common mistakes:**
- A rehearsed 'perfect' five-year plan.
- Naming a title only.
- Saying you want to start your own company, without framing.
- AI-era note: a generic AI-written five-year answer sounds the same as everyone else's; use your own real direction.
