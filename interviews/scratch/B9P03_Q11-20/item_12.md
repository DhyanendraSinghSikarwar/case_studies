**Say this first (1 line):** I earned trust by listening first, being useful in small ways, and doing exactly what I said I would.

**Structure:** STAR with a trust focus [G, editor guidance].
1. Situation: who was sceptical and why (past experience, risk, a worry about your work). Do not blame them.
2. Task: what you needed their support for.
3. Action: ask what worries them; share your reasoning and limits openly; deliver a small early proof; keep every small promise; admit one thing you got wrong or did not know.
4. Result: what changed in how they worked with you, shown by something they did.

Company note: earn-trust is an Amazon-style principle name from editor memory (check the company's current list). Book 7 Part 1 (Google, C1) lists teamwork and collaboration in the Googleyness round, from third-party guides.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "A [role] on the [team] doubted our model because an earlier tool from another team had given wrong results. I needed her team's feedback to improve it. Instead of defending the model, I asked what had gone wrong last time, and she told me. I then showed her how we checked ours against that same failure, and I said clearly where it was still weak. I offered a small trial on one report, shared the results before she had to ask, and fixed a mistake I found, telling her the same day. After the trial she began sending me cases to test, and later asked her team to use it. What I learned is that trust came from being honest about limits, not from a better pitch."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I just proved I was good at my job and she came around." It gives no listening, no action, and implies the other person was the problem.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you understand the other person's concern, not only your own aim?
- Do you show humility, including admitting a limit or a mistake?
- Are your actions specific and repeatable?
- Is there evidence the relationship changed?
- Do you describe the colleague fairly?

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real colleague, client or stakeholder who doubted you or your work.
- Write their worry in their words, as fairly as you can.
- List the small things you did and when.
- Name the sign trust grew (a request, a decision, a handover).
- Include something you got wrong and how you handled it.

**Follow-ups:**
- What if they never came round? "I would say so, name what I learned, and describe how I kept the work moving."
- How do you handle a mistake in front of them? "Tell them early, say what I will do about it, then do it."
- Did you change your approach because of their doubt? "Yes, name one real change they influenced."

**Common mistakes:**
- Making the sceptic look foolish.
- Claiming trust with no evidence.
- Hiding the one mistake that would make the story believable.
- AI-era note: do not have a tool write the dialogue; interviewers probe the details of conversations, and invented detail falls apart.
