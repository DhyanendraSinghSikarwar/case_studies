**Say this first (1 line):** Thank them, show you are interested, ask for the offer in writing and the details, and give yourself time to respond.

**Structure:** Four steps [G, editor guidance].
1. In the moment: express thanks and real enthusiasm; do not accept, reject or negotiate on the spot.
2. Ask what you need to decide: the written offer, the full package parts, the level and title, the start date, any joining conditions and the response date.
3. Agree when you will reply, and put that date in your own diary.
4. After the call: send a short thank-you email recording what you understood; later, review the written offer calmly and bundle your requests into one conversation.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** "Thank you, I am really pleased to hear that, and I am excited about the work. Could you send the offer in writing, including all parts of the package, the level and title, the start date and any conditions? I want to read it carefully. When do you need my response? I would like to come back to you by [date]. If I have questions once I have read it, may I call you or email you? Thank you again." That is about thirty seconds, it commits to nothing, and it keeps the relationship warm. Negotiation comes after the written offer, through a calm single conversation.

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "Yes, I accept!" or "That is lower than I expected." The first closes your options before you have read the terms; the second sounds ungrateful and reactive, and neither was thought through.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you stay composed when surprised?
- Do you show genuine interest without rushing?
- Do you ask for the right information?
- Do you handle timing professionally?
- Would they enjoy negotiating with you?

**Make it yours:**
- Write three things you want to know from any offer call.
- Decide your response time and the words to ask for it.
- Write your own thank-you line.
- List the terms that matter to you, beyond pay.
- Practise with a friend playing the recruiter.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Thank you, I am delighted, and I am very interested in the role.
Could you send the offer in writing, with all parts of the package, the level, title, start date and conditions?
I would like to respond by [date]. Does that work for you?
```
Follow-up email:
```
Subject: Thank you, and next steps
Hi [name], thank you for the call today. To confirm: you will send the written offer, and I will reply by [date]. I am looking forward to reviewing it. Best, [your name]
```

**Follow-ups:**
- What if they press for an answer now? "I say I want to give a considered answer, and ask for a reasonable date."
- What if the number surprises you? "Stay neutral, say you will review the whole package, and respond in writing or a call later."
- Should you mention other offers? "Only truthfully, and only if relevant; see the competing offers questions."

**Common mistakes:**
- Accepting before reading anything.
- Negotiating on a first call without the written details.
- Showing disappointment.
- AI-era note: do not read a script from a tool during the call; a natural tone matters more than perfect wording.
