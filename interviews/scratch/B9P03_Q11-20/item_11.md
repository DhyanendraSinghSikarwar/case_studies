**Say this first (1 line):** Name the obstacle in one sentence, then spend most of the answer on what you personally did and what came out.

**Structure:** STAR [G, editor guidance].
1. Situation: the goal and why it mattered, in two sentences.
2. Task: your part of it, and the real obstacles (data, time, people, tools). Pick one or two, not five.
3. Action: three or four things you did, in "I" language, including how you chose between options.
4. Result: what was delivered, how you know, and what you would do differently.

Company note: Amazon-style loops tie questions to a list of principles and use real past examples (Book 7 Part 1, Amazon, C3; anecdotal and third-party). Google guides list a project-that-did-not-go-as-planned prompt. The principle names here are editor knowledge: check the company's current list, because older guides cite 14 and Amazon's own guide mentions 16.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):** Illustrative; replace with your own. "On a [project type], we had [number of weeks] to ship a [deliverable] when our main data source changed format halfway through. My task was the pipeline and the first report. The obstacles were the changed data and a teammate on leave. I first listed what the report truly needed and cut it to the two things the team would act on. I wrote a small converter for the new format and tested it on a sample before touching the rest. I asked [stakeholder] to confirm the cut list so nobody was surprised. We delivered on the date with the smaller scope, and the full version followed two weeks later. If I did it again, I would add a format check on day one, so a change is caught early."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "It was very hard but the team worked really hard and we finished on time." No obstacle you can picture, no personal action, no result you can check. The interviewer cannot tell what you did.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Is the obstacle real, and did you explain why it was hard?
- Can you separate what you did from what the team did?
- Did you make choices (cut scope, ask for help), not just work longer?
- Is the result concrete, and do you say what you learned?
- Do you stay honest about what did not work?

**Make it yours:**
- Choose a real delivery where something went wrong, even a course or internship project.
- Write the obstacle in one sentence, without drama.
- List two or three actions only you took.
- State the result in words you can defend with evidence.
- Practise a one-line answer to "what would you change?".

**Follow-ups:**
- What was your exact contribution? "I built X and decided Y; the team did Z." Be specific and fair to others.
- What did you give up to deliver? "Name the trade-off you chose and who agreed to it."
- What would you do differently? "One concrete change, such as an earlier check or an earlier conversation."

**Common mistakes:**
- Saying "we" for every step.
- Listing five obstacles and no choices.
- Claiming a result you cannot back up.
- AI-era note: live follow-ups expose a story written for you by a tool. Use your own real story and be ready to go two levels deeper.
