### R5 India (baseline for comparison)

**Evidence level:** Thin to Medium. Many pages exist, but almost none is specific to data scientist, ML engineer or GenAI hiring: the stage sequence rests on a few candidate reviews (several for data engineer or software roles), and the rest is HR-vendor, recruiter-blog and job-portal material with unsourced figures [S1][S2][S3][S21]. Background verification, notice period and offer steps are described consistently by several vendor pages, but timings differ [S9][S10][S11][S12]. This profile is the reference point for the other 12 profiles in this book. Snippets only; no page was opened. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types seen in results: IT services firms (TCS, CGI in candidate reviews), analytics firms (Fractal), product companies and start-ups (Flipkart, CRED, PhonePe, Groww), and global capability centres (GCCs) of foreign firms [S1][S3][S4][S18][S21].
- Product-company data science loops are described as longer and more case-driven; services firms as often opening with an aptitude test and focused on headcount; start-up loops as ad hoc (one guide, weak) [S21][S22].
- GCCs: city shares of GCC hiring differ by report (Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune named as the top three in a Q2 FY26 report) [S20]. A CBRE analysis of AI-tagged openings puts Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR and Mumbai at the top, with Hyderabad and Pune next [S18][S19]. The reports use different methods; treat shares as directional.
- Book 7 Part 2 covers Indian product companies (Flipkart, Swiggy, Zomato, PhonePe, Razorpay, CRED, Meesho, Ola, Paytm, Myntra); Book 7 Part 3 covers global firms with India centres (Walmart Global Tech India, Target in India, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, American Express, Visa, Mastercard, Intuit, Atlassian, Optum). Use those profiles for company detail; their claims are not repeated here [G].
- Hiring season: for campuses, see the campus note below [S6]; for lateral hiring, not found as a season [U].

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Typical sequence in several reports: online assessment, then technical round(s), then a managerial round, then an HR round [S1][S2]. One TCS review says the managerial round is sometimes placed before HR [S1] (anecdotal).
2. Online assessment: coding, data manipulation and ML-concept questions on platforms such as HackerRank per one guide; a DSA-heavy test of three problems in about 90 minutes is reported at a software-role employer, which is not typical for data roles [S3][S21]. Services firms are described as using aptitude, logical and verbal tests (AMCAT, CoCubes, eLitmus named) before technical rounds [S22].
3. Technical round for data roles: Python and data manipulation, SQL, then questions on your own projects (Fractal data scientist review; a TCS review adds SQL on paper and a Databricks question) [S1][S3] (anecdotal).
4. Managerial round: communication, teamwork, experience and role suitability (TCS review) [S1]. HR round: culture fit and communication style per one guide; at PhonePe an HR call collected basic details and expectations (anecdotal) [S4][S3].
5. Round counts and timelines vary: about 4.7 rounds after screening at top product companies per one guide [S21]; a Fractal process of about a week and a TCS process of about two weeks in single reviews [S1][S3]; a Google-related report says two to three months [S3] (single review, role not data science). Treat all as rough.
6. Campus hiring: run through college placement offices on a calendar, with the IIT and IIM season in December and January and pre-placement offers from internships announced before it; the usual campus sequence is a machine test, a technical interview and an HR interview [S6] (HR-vendor glossary).
7. Lateral hiring: targeted at specific skills and experience, described by one vendor as the main route for specialised and senior roles [S5]. The share of lateral hires and the time-to-fill in vendor pages are unsourced; not used [U].
8. Campus market signals: a Deloitte 2024 survey press release reports a fall in campus placement budgets and pre-placement offers, and a 2025 report says IT campus intake improved in H1 FY26 (headline level) [S7][S8].
9. Referrals: a LinkedIn trends survey, reported by People Matters, said at least 65 per cent of Indian recruiters use employee referral programmes (older survey) [S16]. Naukri's recruiter site says about two in three companies hire through referral programmes (platform claim) [S16]. Percentages of referral hires for data roles: not found [U].
10. Channels named for data science roles in a community thread: Naukri, LinkedIn, iimjobs, Hirist and Indeed [S17]. Recruiter and consultancy fees and roles: not found in results [U].
11. Offer stage: offers are described as usually conditional, mainly on background verification and proof of previous employment; the offer letter carries headline terms and the appointment letter on or after joining carries the detailed contract (HR blogs) [S9]. Acceptance windows of a few days appear in one blog and one sample offer [S9] (weak).
12. Notice period and joining: there is no single standard; it depends on employer, industry and level and sits in the employment terms, so a joining date is agreed after the current notice period [S10]. Confirm the joining date in writing before resigning [S9]. A counter-offer from the current employer is named as a reason offers fall through, in one vendor article [S5].
13. Background verification (BGV): checks on identity, address, employment history, education and court records, with reference checks sometimes added; consent is collected first [S12]. Timing estimates differ: seven to ten business days for most technical hires in one guide, two to six weeks in another, so plan for a range [S11][S12]. Some firms check before the offer, others after, and some let you join while it runs [S12]. A relieving letter from the previous employer is a commonly requested document [S11].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Sources describe the managerial and HR rounds as testing communication and fit, not technical depth [S1][S3].
- Interviewers are reported to start from the skills and projects listed on your CV, so every CV line may be questioned [S1][S3] (anecdotal).
- No source here described tone, address or hierarchy norms in interviews; not claimed [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Reverse-chronological layout is the most common; one page is advised for freshers and two pages are accepted for experienced candidates in several guides [S14][S15].
- Personal details: sources disagree. Several guides advise freshers applying to private-sector or IT roles to leave out the photo, date of birth and marital status; other guides still list date of birth, gender and marital status; one advises experienced candidates to add date of birth only if asked and to state notice period [S14][S15].
- Government, public-sector and some traditional employers are described as asking for date of birth and sometimes a photo on forms; multinationals are described as following Western conventions with no photo [S14][S15].
- The older word "biodata" for a personal-details-first document also appears in guides [S14].
- Cover letter and references: not covered in these results [U].

**Language and location notes:**
- Working language in interviews and offers: not found in these results [U]. English-language job portals and guides are the norm in what appeared, which is only a weak hint [S17][G].
- Hubs for data and AI work: Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR, Hyderabad, Pune, Mumbai and Chennai named in reports [S18][S19][S20]. Tier 2 cities are reported as the fastest growing GCC locations in one 2026 report (headline level, projection) [S20].
- Degrees and certifications as reported: a bachelor's in computer science, mathematics or statistics is described as the baseline, with master's or PhD more common for senior or research roles; one survey figure says half of data scientists hold a master's (survey source not checked) [S27][S28]. Certifications named include CAP, SAS and Cloudera credentials; unverified whether employers weigh them [S28][U].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Not applicable to domestic candidates applying to Indian employers [G].
- For foreign or returning candidates: no official source on this topic appeared in the results, so none is named here [U]. The only official body that did appear is the Ministry of Labour and Employment's National Career Service portal for job seekers (listing and counselling, not authorisation), via https://services.india.gov.in/service/detail/national-career-service-job-search [S29].
- Verify directly on official government pages and with the employer; this book states no rules [G].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
This section lists the baseline features the other profiles compare against.
- Stages: online assessment, technical round(s), managerial round, HR round [S1][S2].
- Campus versus lateral: campus is calendar-driven through placement offices and ends in offers before graduation; lateral is skill-targeted and continuous [S6][S5].
- Notice period: a notice period is part of the employment terms and the joining date follows it; ask other markets how they treat it [S10][G].
- Background verification: normally an after-offer or pre-joining step covering identity, address, education, employment and sometimes court records, with consent [S12][S9].
- Referral channels: employee referral programmes are widely used and job portals (Naukri, LinkedIn and others) are common [S16][S17].
- Offer process: conditional offer letter, short acceptance window, appointment letter at joining [S9].
- CV: reverse-chronological, one to two pages, personal details optional and mixed [S14][S15].
- Services versus product: aptitude-style first tests at services firms; case and metrics emphasis at product firms [S21][S22].

**How to use Books 1-7 for this market:**
- Book 3 Part 3 (subqueries, CTEs), Part 4 (window functions: ranking, top-N per group) and Part 6 (date/time: retention, cohorts) for SQL in online assessments and technical rounds [S1][S3].
- Book 1 Part 12 (NumPy/pandas/sklearn live-coding and AI-code-review rounds) for data-manipulation questions [S3].
- Book 5 Part 1 (arrays and hashing) and Part 2 (two pointers and sliding window) for DSA-heavy tests [S21].
- Book 6 Part 1 (product metrics and metric drops) and Book 6 Part 5 (business case studies) for case rounds at product companies [S21].
- Book 1 Part 11 (ML system design) and Book 2 Part 12 (GenAI system design plus coding) for senior and GenAI roles [G].
- Book 6 Part 6 (stakeholder conflicts and trade-off decisions) for managerial rounds [S1].
- Book 7 Parts 2 and 3 for company examples; do not repeat their claims here [G]. Book 9 is planned and not yet written; it is intended for behavioural and HR-round stories [G].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: write a one- to two-page reverse-chronological CV; decide on personal details by employer type [S14][S15].
- Days 3-5: SQL and pandas under time: Book 3 Parts 3-6, Book 1 Part 12 [S3].
- Days 6-7: DSA basics for assessment platforms: Book 5 Parts 1-2 [S21].
- Days 8-9: ML concepts and your project stories, since rounds start from your CV [S1][S3].
- Days 10-11: metrics and case practice: Book 6 Parts 1 and 5 [S21].
- Days 12-13: managerial and HR practice; read your notice terms and prepare joining-date and documents list (relieving letter, education and employment proofs) [S10][S11].
- Day 14: mock full loop; ask the recruiter about stages, BGV timing and offer deadline [G].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one review as the Indian process; services, product, GCC and start-up loops differ [S21][S22].
- Listing skills on the CV that you cannot discuss in depth [S1][S3].
- Resigning before the offer, joining date and verification steps are confirmed in writing [S9][S10].
- Assuming BGV is instant; timings in guides range from days to weeks [S11][S12].
- Putting a photo, date of birth or marital status on a private-sector CV by default [S14][S15].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- Employer side: a February 2026 report says over 70 per cent of Indian recruiters are using AI to find candidates; a LinkedIn survey reported by Careers360 says recruiters under pressure to explain how AI is used (recruiter-survey sources, weak) [S25][S26].
- Candidate side: one vendor that sells AI-interview tools reported that 38.5 per cent of candidates in its interviews were flagged for AI-cheating behaviour, and 48 per cent in technical roles; this is a flag rate from one vendor's detection, not verified cheating [S23]. An analysis notes confirmed 2026 cases are concentrated in exams rather than job interviews [S24].
- Press mention of recruiters using AI to catch candidates using AI in campus hiring appears, details not seen [S26][U].
- A 2026 HireRight blog on India covers employment risk, identity fraud and AI (title level only) [S30].
- No source here on rules for AI use in Indian hiring; not claimed [U]. A German-language guide on India data science applications exists but its content was not seen [S31].
