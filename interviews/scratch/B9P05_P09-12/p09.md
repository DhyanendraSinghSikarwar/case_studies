**What good looks like:** Good feedback handling is a loop, not a feeling. You listen without defending, you ask one clarifying question, you say back what you heard, you pick one or two changes, you do them, and you go back to the person to show what changed. In an interview that loop is your story: "I was told X, I changed Y, and here is how I know it worked." Interviewers are not looking for someone who never gets criticised. They are looking for someone who turns criticism into visible change without getting hurt or defensive.

**Self-assessment:** Score yourself 1 to 5 on each line (1 = rarely, 5 = almost always). Do this once now and again at day 30.

Total out of 50. Under 25: start with questions 1 to 5 only. 25 to 38: the gap is usually closing the loop (6 and 7). Above 38: work on asking early (8) and on telling the story naturally (10).

**Daily and weekly practice:**
- Daily (2 minutes): in your notes app, add one line to a feedback log with four columns: what I was told, who said it, what I will change, when I will check. Any small comment counts, including a code-review remark.
- Daily (2 minutes): when someone gives you feedback, practise the three-beat reply: "Thanks. What I heard is ... Can I check one thing: ...?" Do not defend in the first minute.
- Weekly (10 minutes, same day each week): read the log. Pick one item and do it. Mark the earlier ones done, dropped (with a reason) or still open.
- Weekly (5 minutes): send one short loop-closing message to a giver: "You said my summaries buried the answer. This week I put the answer first. Did you notice a difference?"
- Weekly: ask one person for feedback on one specific thing ("Was my update clear? What would you cut?"). A narrow question gets a usable answer.

**30-day plan:** 15 to 30 minutes a day.

| Week | Focus | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listen and log | Start the four-column log. Practise the three-beat reply in real conversations. Collect at least five items, from reviews, chats and past reviews you saved. No fixing yet. |
| 2 | Choose and change | Pick the two most repeated themes. Write a one-line behaviour change for each ("answer first in every message"). Do them daily. Ask an AI tool to critique a draft message for exactly that issue, then fix it yourself. |
| 3 | Close the loop | Go back to two givers. Say what you changed and ask what they notice. Log their reply. Practise saying one real example out loud in under 90 seconds. |
| 4 | Ask early and tell it | Ask three people for forward-looking feedback before a deliverable. Build your interview story from your own log: feedback, change, result. Do two spoken mock runs and ask a friend for follow-ups such as "What if you disagreed with it?" |

**How to show it in interviews:**
- Lead with the loop: what you heard, what you changed, what the person later said or what the result was.
- Practise the story against Book 9 Part 1 Q13 (critical feedback received), Q25 (difficult feedback you gave), Q7 (a mistake in an analysis or model); use Q37 (disagreeing with your manager's decision) when you did not fully agree.
- Part 4 S53 (blunt negative feedback in a team review) and S32 (a mistake found in code review) show the live-moment version; Part 2 Q17 (a senior leader says your analysis is wrong) shows composure in public.
- Part 3 Q29 (giving a peer hard feedback) pairs with it for the other direction.
- Use your own real feedback from your log. Never invent a criticism you did not receive; follow-ups will find it.
- AI-era note: use an AI tool to practise tough follow-up questions or to critique your draft, and say so if asked. Do not let it write the story; a live "tell me more about that conversation" exposes a rehearsed or generated one quickly.
- For data-science stakeholder trade-offs, see Book 6 Part 6 (Stakeholder Conflicts and Trade-off Decisions) instead of repeating them here.

**Common traps:**
- The humble-brag ("my weakness is working too hard"). Choose real feedback.
- Defending in the first minute, then agreeing later in the story. Say it was hard to hear if it was.
- A story with no change, only agreement. The change is the proof.
- Blaming the giver ("my manager was just unfair"). Even if partly true, show what you took from it.
- Collecting feedback you never read. The log only works if you review it weekly.
- Over-correcting on every comment. Sort it: true, partly true, or style, and say which you acted on.

**Measures of progress:**
- You have a log with at least ten dated entries by day 30.
- At least three entries are marked done with a date and a giver you reported back to.
- You can tell one feedback story in under two minutes with an observable change and a named outcome, without notes.
- A friend running live follow-ups cannot find a gap between your story and your log.
- You asked for feedback unprompted at least three times this month, and someone's reply changed something you did.
