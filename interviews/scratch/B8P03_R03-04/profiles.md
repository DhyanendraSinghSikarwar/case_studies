**Evidence level:** Thin. No Indonesia-specific guide to data scientist or ML engineer rounds from an official or neutral body appeared. **Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types named in results: e-commerce and marketplaces (Tokopedia), ride-hailing and super-apps (Gojek, Grab), banking and financial services, technology and e-commerce, and telecom and media as the main hirers of data science and AI talent per a training provider (vendor page) [S1][S4][S5][S18].
- Fintech engineers and AI and machine learning specialists are listed among the most in-demand roles in a talent summit report (title level only) [S19].
- Hubs: Jakarta is the centre; tech firms are described as clustered in South and Central Jakarta and around BSD, with some keeping Bandung engineering offices (SEO-style guide, weak) [S16].
- Market mood is mixed: one analysis cites tech layoffs at GoTo Group between mid-2023 and late 2024 alongside a gap in senior AI and fintech talent; another guide says hiring is selective, mainly for experienced people [S16][S17].
- Hiring season: not found in sources [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Application, then HR screening. Indonesian guides describe HR first, assessing background and behaviour [S12][S11].
2. Interviews are often split into an HR interview and a "user" interview, where "user" means the business or hiring team side; panel interviews and group activities can also appear [S11][S13].
3. Psikotes (psychological or aptitude tests: logic, numerical, verbal, and personality such as DISC or MBTI) commonly sit in the process before an offer, per Indonesian career sites; whether tech employers use them for data roles is not shown [S14][S13] [U].
4. Reported data-role examples. Tokopedia data scientist: one candidate account lists screening, an HR discussion, two user interviews with data science team leads, then the Head of Data Science, then offer and contract (anecdotal; the guide's description is pooled from a search summary) [S1][S2]. Other Tokopedia reports, mostly engineering, mention a HackerRank test, an HR interview and panel rounds [S2]. Gojek: a guide for a data analyst role lists four rounds (phone screen, online HackerRank test, data scientist round, manager round) with deep A/B testing questions, advanced SQL and experiment design [S4]; another guide says Gojek platform teams add a code-pairing round, described as an engineering process [S3] (commercial).
5. Grab (Singapore-headquartered, regional; not necessarily Indonesia-based): guides describe a screen on past projects, a case study presenting an approach, and for analyst roles sometimes a take-home or presentation; Glassdoor reports mention a take-home SQL and data analysis task, SQL questions such as a day-over-day drop in completed rides, and SQL, statistics and root-cause analysis followed by a case study for a senior role (anecdotal, location of each report not confirmed) [S5][S6].
6. Timeline: about three to four weeks from application to offer for Tokopedia data scientist in one guide; one Tokopedia Glassdoor report in Jakarta (July 2023) says about four weeks, another three weeks (2021) (anecdotal) [S1][S2].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Business-etiquette guides describe Indonesian workplaces as hierarchical: deference to seniority, titles with surnames until invited otherwise, and formal Bahasa Indonesia and "Bapak/Ibu" greetings in formal writing [S20]. These are expat and consultancy guides, not interview studies, and apply unevenly across employers [U].
- Punctuality is valued; guides mention that delays are common ("jam karet") and advise arriving early because of Jakarta traffic [S20].
- Conservative business dress is the usual advice; fashion norms vary by industry [S20].
- Some guides describe indirect communication and avoiding blunt criticism; treat as general orientation, not a rule for tech teams [S20] [U].
- Interview-specific style for data roles: not found [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Photo: described as common in Indonesian CVs, with a professional headshot; a 2018 academic study lists a photo and personal data among obligatory CV contents [S7][S8][S10]. Sources are mostly local career sites and one older study; international tech firms may differ [U].
