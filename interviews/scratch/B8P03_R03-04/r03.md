### R3 Indonesia (Southeast Asia)

**Evidence level:** Thin. No Indonesia-specific guide to data scientist or ML engineer rounds from an official or neutral body appeared. Process evidence is a handful of Glassdoor-type reports and commercial guides, mostly for Tokopedia, Gojek and Grab, of mixed years and roles, several for engineering rather than data roles [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. CV, stage-naming (HR interview, user interview, psikotes) and language material is better covered, mostly by Indonesian career sites and one 2018 academic paper [S7][S11][S14][S15]. Snippets only; no page was opened.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types named in results: e-commerce and marketplaces (Tokopedia), ride-hailing and super-apps (Gojek, Grab), banking and financial services, technology and e-commerce, and telecom and media as the main hirers of data science and AI talent per a training provider (vendor page) [S1][S4][S5][S18].
- Fintech engineers and AI and machine learning specialists are listed among the most in-demand roles in a talent summit report (title level only) [S19].
- Hubs: Jakarta is the centre; tech firms are described as clustered in South and Central Jakarta and around BSD, with some keeping Bandung engineering offices (SEO-style guide, weak) [S16].
- Market mood is mixed: one analysis cites tech layoffs at GoTo Group between mid-2023 and late 2024 alongside a gap in senior AI and fintech talent; another guide says hiring is selective, mainly for experienced people [S16][S17].
- Hiring season: not found in sources [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Application, then HR screening. Indonesian guides describe HR first, assessing background and behaviour [S12][S11].
2. Interviews are often split into an HR interview and a "user" interview, where "user" means the business or hiring team side; panel interviews and group activities can also appear [S11][S13].
3. Psikotes (psychological or aptitude tests: logic, numerical, verbal, and personality such as DISC or MBTI) commonly sit in the process before an offer, per Indonesian career sites; whether tech employers use them for data roles is not shown [S14][S13] [U].
4. Reported data-role examples. Tokopedia data scientist: one candidate account lists screening, an HR discussion, two user interviews with data science team leads, then the Head of Data Science, then offer and contract (anecdotal; the guide's description is pooled from a search summary) [S1][S2]. Other Tokopedia reports, mostly engineering, mention a HackerRank test, an HR interview and panel rounds [S2]. Gojek: a guide for a data analyst role lists four rounds (phone screen, online HackerRank test, data scientist round, manager round) with deep A/B testing questions, advanced SQL and experiment design [S4]; another guide says Gojek platform teams add a code-pairing round, described as an engineering process [S3] (commercial).
5. Grab (Singapore-headquartered, regional; not necessarily Indonesia-based): guides describe a screen on past projects, a case study presenting an approach, and for analyst roles sometimes a take-home or presentation; Glassdoor reports mention a take-home SQL and data analysis task, SQL questions such as a day-over-day drop in completed rides, and SQL, statistics and root-cause analysis followed by a case study for a senior role (anecdotal, location of each report not confirmed) [S5][S6].
6. Timeline: about three to four weeks from application to offer for Tokopedia data scientist in one guide; one Tokopedia Glassdoor report in Jakarta (July 2023) says about four weeks, another three weeks (2021) (anecdotal) [S1][S2].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Business-etiquette guides describe Indonesian workplaces as hierarchical: deference to seniority, titles with surnames until invited otherwise, and formal Bahasa Indonesia and "Bapak/Ibu" greetings in formal writing [S20]. These are expat and consultancy guides, not interview studies, and apply unevenly across employers [U].
- Punctuality is valued; guides mention that delays are common ("jam karet") and advise arriving early because of Jakarta traffic [S20].
- Conservative business dress is the usual advice; fashion norms vary by industry [S20].
- Some guides describe indirect communication and avoiding blunt criticism; treat as general orientation, not a rule for tech teams [S20] [U].
- Interview-specific style for data roles: not found [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Photo: described as common in Indonesian CVs, with a professional headshot; a 2018 academic study lists a photo and personal data among obligatory CV contents [S7][S8][S10]. Sources are mostly local career sites and one older study; international tech firms may differ [U].
- Personal details: guides differ; some say date of birth, marital status and religion are common, others suggest modesty for traditional employers [S8][S7]. Candidates can ask the recruiter or follow the advert [G].
- Cover letter: described as expected with the CV; a CNN Indonesia article (2016) says employers often read the letter first [S9].
- References: mixed; "available on request" is described as enough at first, another source suggests naming referees with their permission [S8].
- Format: chronological or combined layouts are recommended; translate the CV into Bahasa Indonesia if the job calls for it [S8][S15].

**Language and location notes:**
- One commercial guide says locally funded firms usually interview in Bahasa Indonesia, while foreign multinationals and tech companies often interview in English from the first round; confirm the interview language with the recruiter [S15].
- Knowing Bahasa Indonesia is called a plus, and some English-language listings also ask for it [S15][S16].
- Job boards named: JobStreet, LinkedIn, Glints [S16]. City named in sources: Jakarta, with BSD and Bandung as nearby tech locations [S16].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official body to consult: the Ministry of Manpower (Kementerian Ketenagakerjaan, kemnaker.go.id). Its job portal KarirHub is reported at karirhub.kemnaker.go.id, reached through the SIAPkerja account system, with listings from partner portals (domain from a news report; not verified on the ministry site) [S23].
- An official English translation of the ministerial regulation on foreign worker utilisation is hosted on peraturan.go.id (regulation text of 2018; it may have been replaced, so check the current rules) [S24].
- No official job-seeker page for foreign candidates appeared; the Indonesian immigration and embassy pages should also be checked [U]. Law-firm briefings and expat sites were seen but are not relied on [S24].
- Verify: whether you may work in your situation, which approvals the employer must obtain, and current timelines, on official pages only. This book states no rules [G].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect a staged HR interview then a "user" interview with the hiring team, which looks like a recruiter screen then manager round elsewhere [S11][G].
- Expect psikotes (aptitude or personality tests) at some employers, which may be unfamiliar as a stage [S14] [U].
- Expect online coding tests (HackerRank) at larger Jakarta tech firms, similar to an online assessment round [S2][S4].
- Expect a photo and fuller personal details on CVs at many local employers, and a cover letter [S7][S9].
- Expect the interview language to vary by employer, so confirm it [S15].
- Notice periods and background checks in Indonesia: not found [U].

- Book 3 Part 4 (window functions: ranking, top-N per group) and Book 3 Part 6 (date/time: retention, cohorts, MoM/YoY) for SQL questions such as day-over-day drops [S6][G].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: Prepare a CV (photo and personal details decision made per advert) and a short cover letter, in English and, if needed, Bahasa Indonesia [S8][S9][S15].
- Days 3-5: SQL practice including day-over-day and retention queries: Book 3 Parts 4 and 6 [S6][G].
- Days 6-7: A/B testing and statistics: Book 1 Parts 10 and 13 [S4].
- Days 8-9: Timed online coding practice: Book 5 Parts 1-2 [S2][G].
- Days 10-11: Two business case write-ups: Book 6 Parts 5 and 9 [S5][G].
- Days 12-13: Practise a project walk-through for HR and user rounds; practise a sample of logic and numerical aptitude questions [S11][S14].
- Day 14: Ask the recruiter about stages, interview language and any tests [S15][G].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating a Grab or single Tokopedia report as the Indonesian norm [S1][S5].
- Assuming English everywhere, or Bahasa everywhere, without asking [S15].
- Ignoring the psikotes stage where an employer uses it [S14].
- Using blogs, not official pages, for work-authorisation facts [S23][S24].
- Overusing first names or arriving late in formal settings [S20].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- A Jobstreet survey reported on databoks says 20 per cent of Indonesian companies use AI in recruitment, most often for screening; the article date is unclear, possibly 2025 [S21].
- Another report says 71 per cent of companies consider candidates' AI knowledge in recruitment, and a Goodstats summary says 69 per cent of employers are unwilling to hire without AI skills; these measure different things and conflict in framing with the 20 per cent figure; methodology not seen [S22].
- No Indonesia-specific report on candidate use of AI during interviews, or on interview-time AI policies, was found; a global 2026 AI-interview survey appeared but is secondhand and not Indonesian evidence [U].
