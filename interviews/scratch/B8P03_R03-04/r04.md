### R4 United Arab Emirates (Middle East)

**Evidence level:** Thin. Interview-process evidence is a few anonymous Glassdoor-type reports (BCG Dubai, IBM Dubai, Careem, G42 and a couple of Abu Dhabi bodies) and commercial guides; some are years old and several snippets showed no stage detail [S1][S2][S3][S4][S8][S9]. CV conventions and interview etiquette are covered by many recruiter and career blogs that disagree on photo and personal details [S10][S11][S12][S15]. Official job-portal pages are named in results [S23]. AI-in-hiring coverage is mostly press reports of surveys [S19][S20]. Snippets only; no page was opened.

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types named in results: a global consultancy (BCG, Dubai) [S1]; a technology multinational (IBM, Dubai) [S2]; a ride-hailing and super-app firm headquartered in Dubai (Careem) [S8][S9]; Abu Dhabi AI and research-linked bodies (G42, AI71, Technology Innovation Institute, MBZUAI) [S4][S5][S6][S7]. Banks and fintech: a jobs guide describes Dubai as the Middle East's financial centre with DIFC hosting banks and investment firms (low-quality source) [S14]. Specific interview reports for Emirates NBD, Mashreq, FAB and Noon were not found [U].
- GenAI roles: an Abu Dhabi AI engineer posting asks for LLM applications, agent systems, tool calling and memory management; another lists RAG pipelines and agentic workflows [S26].
- Press says nearly half of UAE and Saudi firms plan to hire more in 2026 (Cooper Fitch survey via trade press), and Gulf News lists sectors hiring most in Dubai and Abu Dhabi (title level) [S19][S18].
- Hubs: Dubai and Abu Dhabi, both named across sources [S1][S4][S18]. Hiring season: not found [U]. Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen first. Guides describe HR screen, then a technical or skills interview, then a final interview with senior management [S15]; a UAE data scientist guide says three to four rounds: recruiter screen, technical round, case study or take-home, final with hiring manager or director (commercial) [S3].
2. Reported examples. BCG Dubai (May 2026, one candidate): HR screen, online coding assessment, a pandas and data science case round, then three business case interviews including a culture-fit round (anecdotal) [S1]. IBM Dubai: one 2024 report of about one week with a SQL joins question and a Python data-structure problem; a 2020 report of about four months (anecdotal, pooled from a summary) [S2].
3. Careem: one review describes about three weeks and five rounds: four video rounds (three data science, one programming) and a machine learning assignment on metric selection, a practical case, dataset analytics and algorithm problems; reviews span 2018 to 2025, so format may have changed (anecdotal) [S9]. A prep guide describes analysing a dataset, choosing metrics and a case discussion [S8].
4. G42 (Abu Dhabi): reviews describe an HR screen, a technical interview with the lead scientist, a coding step, and in one 2023 report an assignment on coding and ML research; one candidate says four weeks (anecdotal, several reviews pooled) [S4]. AI71 and Technology Innovation Institute reviews exist but the snippets gave no stage detail [S5][S6]. MBZUAI ML engineer postings are on-site in Abu Dhabi; no interview detail found [S7].
5. Timelines vary from about one week to months across single reports [S2][S4][S9]. Document work such as degree attestation is advised by one guide to start early; check which documents an employer needs [S3] [U].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Etiquette guides stress respect, punctuality and modest dress [S16][S15]. They advise waiting for the interviewer to offer a handshake and sitting when invited, and treat accepting an offered drink as customary [S17]. These are recruiter and expat blogs, not interview studies [U].
- Interview panels can be mixed in background; one coaching site notes a very strong accent can hinder communication on a multinational panel, so speak clearly; commercial opinion [S13] [U].
- For remote interviews, guides advise testing audio and video and looking at the camera [S15].
- Culture generalisations beyond points repeated across several pages were discarded.

**CV and application format:**
- Personal details: UAE CV guides say nationality and often date of birth appear near the top; one says photo, date of birth and marital status are common but optional, and that no law requires them on a CV [S10][S11]. The legal aspect is not assessed here [U].
- Photo: sources conflict, from common but optional, to expected, to omit it for ATS versions; one guide advises keeping two versions, with and without a photo [S10][S11][S12]. Ask the employer or recruiter [G].
- Length: two pages in most guides, with some allowing two to three [S10][S11].
- References: "available on request" or two or three named referees; some say keep referees' details off a widely forwarded CV [S10][S11].
- Cover letter: sources disagree on whether it is expected; a short tailored one in English is the safe default per one summary [S10][S11].
- Section order often given as personal details, summary, reverse-chronological experience, education, skills, languages [S10][S11].

**Language and location notes:**
- A coaching site says interviews across the Gulf, including Dubai, are conducted in English even at Arabic-owned firms; commercial source [S13].
- Arabic is not always required; Arabic-speaking roles are labelled in postings, and some private banking roles say English and Arabic preferable [S14].
- Cities named: Dubai, Abu Dhabi [S1][S4]. Current share of data roles needing Arabic: not found [U].
- Some data postings ask for UAE work experience; one Dubai FMCG posting is an example (single posting) [S3].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official sources named in results: the UAE government portal page "searching for a job" at https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/find-a-job/searching-for-a-job [S23]. The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) job channel (Virtual Labour Market, at careers.mohre.gov.ae per press, a dated Gulf News article; confirm on the site) [S24]. The Abu Dhabi government's job portal under TAMM is mentioned by press and the u.ae page; dated, confirm [S25].
- The u.ae page also says job seekers are not required to pay a recruitment agency, as employers bear that cost; this is a statement from the page's summary; verify wording on the page [S23].
- Verify on official pages: whether and how you may work, which approvals the employer arranges, and current timelines. Nothing in the results covered Emiratisation requirements, and this book states no rules [U][G].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect an online coding or SQL assessment at some employers, comparable to an online assessment round [S1][S2].
- Expect business case rounds and assignments at consultancies and tech firms, which a coding-only loop would not include [S1][S9].
- Expect more personal details on a CV (nationality, sometimes date of birth, sometimes a photo) than a minimal CV, but conventions conflict [S10][S11].
- Expect interviews in English, with Arabic labelled in postings where needed [S13][S14].
- Expect HR, technical and senior-management stages in sequence [S15][S3].
- Expect to arrange documents (for example degree attestation) early where an employer asks; confirm what is needed [S3] [U].
- Notice periods and background checks in the UAE: not found [U].

**How to use Books 1-7 for this market:**
- Book 3 Part 2 (joins: all types, self, anti/semi joins) and Book 3 Part 3 (subqueries, CTEs) for SQL screens [S2][G].
- Book 1 Part 12 (NumPy/pandas/sklearn live-coding & AI-code-review rounds) for pandas and coding assessments [S1].
- Book 6 Part 5 (business case studies: pricing, churn, fraud, supply chain) and Book 6 Part 10 ("design a solution end-to-end" open scenarios) for case rounds [S1][S9].
- Book 1 Part 3 (classification metrics, imbalance, calibration, thresholding) for metric-selection questions [S9].
- Book 5 Part 1 (arrays and hashing) and Book 5 Part 2 (two pointers and sliding window) for algorithm problems [S9][G].
- Book 2 Part 6 (RAG basics) and Book 2 Part 8 (agents: ReAct, tool use, planning, memory, MCP, multi-agent) for GenAI roles listing RAG and agents [S26].
- Book 6 Part 9 (domain sets: fintech, e-commerce, healthcare, ads) for ride-hailing, banking and e-commerce employers [G].
- Book 9 for behavioural and culture-fit stories (planned, not yet written) [G].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: Two-page CV with a decision on photo and personal details per employer; English cover letter [S10][S11][S12].
- Days 3-4: SQL joins and CTEs: Book 3 Parts 2-3 [S2][G].
- Days 5-6: Pandas case work and metrics: Book 1 Parts 3 and 12 [S1][S9].
- Days 7-9: Three business cases written and spoken aloud: Book 6 Parts 5 and 10 [S1][S9].
- Days 10-11: One timed ML assignment with metric choice explained [S9][S4].
- Days 12-13: For GenAI roles, a mini RAG or agent design: Book 2 Parts 6 and 8 [S26].
- Day 14: Ask the recruiter about stages, language, assignment scope, any AI-tool policy, and documents needed [G].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one BCG, IBM or Careem report as the UAE norm [S1][S2][S9].
- Assuming a single CV convention on photo and personal details [S10][S11].
- Late arrival or casual manner in formal interviews [S15][S16].
- Ignoring the business-case element and preparing only coding [S1].
- Relying on blogs for work-authorisation facts instead of official pages [S23][S24].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- A LinkedIn survey reported in UAE press says 68 per cent of UAE talent professionals are not fully prepared for AI in hiring and 75 per cent worry AI-led interviews feel impersonal; press coverage of a vendor survey, original unseen [S20].
- A YouGov survey figure (HR decision-makers confident in spotting AI-assisted CVs) and identity-fraud findings appear in press coverage; original unseen [S21].
- At a 2026 careers fair, the telecom firm du reportedly used an AI avatar for initial interviews of fresh graduates, while experienced applicants met recruiters (press) [S22].
- Recruiters say employers are moving to practical assignments, scenario interviews and verification of claimed experience; Cooper Fitch notes AI used for preliminary interviews in some cases (press and trade press) [S19][S21].
- No UAE-specific policy on candidates using AI during technical interviews was found [U].
