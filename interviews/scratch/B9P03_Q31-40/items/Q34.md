**Say this first (1 line):** Staying current matters in this field, so could we talk about how the team supports learning?

**Structure:** Ask about support first, then make a small, specific request.
1. Ask what already exists: training budgets, conferences, courses, reading time, internal talks.
2. Name a real goal that helps the team (for example a skill the roadmap needs).
3. Make a bounded request: a course, a certification or conference you could name, plus time to do it.
4. Offer something back: a brown-bag talk, a short write-up, a training session for colleagues.
5. Ask for the arrangement to be noted in writing and how it is reviewed.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"I like to keep growing, and this role touches [area, such as evaluation of language-model systems], which I want to go deeper on. Does the team have a learning budget or study time? If not, I'd value support for [one specific course or conference], and I'd happily give a short session to the team afterwards on what I learned. Could we note that in the offer, or at least agree how requests are approved?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "What courses will you pay for? I want everything covered." It is vague and sounds like a list of perks. It does not show a goal or benefit to the team.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Is your interest in learning genuine and tied to the work?
- Is your request specific and reasonable?
- Do you offer to share what you learn?
- Do you ask rather than assume?

**Make it yours:**
- Name one real skill gap or goal and one resource you would use.
- Think of something you have already taught yourself, as proof you follow through.
- Decide what you would present back to the team.
- Check the company's own careers or benefits page for what is already offered.
- Do not claim certifications or study you have not done.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
"I'm keen to keep growing in [area]. Does the team have a learning budget or protected study time? If it helps, I'd like support for [named course or event] and would share what I learn through [talk or write-up]. Could we agree how this is approved and put it in writing?"
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What would you do with it?" Name the resource, the timing and what the team gets.
- "What if there is no budget?" Ask about time, internal mentoring or a review point later.

**Common mistakes:**
- Asking for generic perks with no plan.
- Treating learning support as a substitute for pay without saying so.
- Asking only after accepting.
- Forgetting to get approvals documented.
