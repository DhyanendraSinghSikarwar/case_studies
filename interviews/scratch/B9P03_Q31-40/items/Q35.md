**Say this first (1 line):** I am happy to move forward, and I would like to confirm all the details in writing before I resign.

**Structure:** Get the paper first, then act.
1. List what was discussed on calls: pay structure, title, start date, location or remote arrangement, anything else that matters to you.
2. Send a short, friendly recap and ask them to confirm or correct it.
3. Ask for the formal offer letter or contract, and read it fully; ask HR what is included and how conditions work.
4. Check that each agreed item appears; if something differs, ask politely and wait for a corrected version.
5. Resign only when the written offer matches what you need. Check conditions and any background checks with HR, and use official sources; this is not legal advice.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"I'm very excited to join. Before I resign, I'd like to be sure I've understood everything correctly. From our calls I noted [item one], [item two] and [item three]. Could you confirm those in the offer letter? I'm also happy to look at any conditions, like background checks, and understand how they work. Once I have the written offer, I'll review it and share my notice date with my current employer."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "They said it's fine, so I resigned." A promise on a call is easy to forget or change. If it is not written, you have little to point to, and resigning cannot be undone.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you take care with commitments?
- Is your tone professional and friendly, not suspicious?
- Do you understand conditions and process?
- Do you act in a sensible order?

**Make it yours:**
- Write your own list of items that matter and what was said about each.
- Note when and by whom each item was said.
- Ask HR what documents you will receive and when.
- Read the document slowly, and ask for help from someone you trust if unclear; check official sources for rules.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
"Hi [name], thank you again for the offer. Before I resign, could you confirm these points we discussed in the written offer?
- [item one]
- [item two]
- [item three]
Please let me know if anything differs. I'm keen to join and will share my notice date once I have the document. Could you also tell me how [conditions, such as checks] are handled?"
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "Why do you need it in writing?" Because it avoids misunderstanding for both sides.
- "Can we send it after you resign?" Politely say you would prefer to resign once everything is confirmed.

**Common mistakes:**
- Resigning on a verbal "yes".
- Sending a recap that sounds like an accusation.
- Skipping the conditions in the letter.
- Signing without reading the whole document.
