**Say this first (1 line):** I would listen respectfully, but I decided to leave for reasons that go beyond one conversation, so I would be unlikely to change my mind.

**Structure:** Be honest, decide your position before you resign, and stay professional.
1. Before resigning, write down why you are leaving. Check whether anything the current employer could offer would address those reasons.
2. If you are firmly leaving, say so kindly and thank them.
3. If you are open to staying, be honest with yourself and the new employer about it; do not use the new offer only as leverage.
4. Ask for any new terms in writing and compare against the written offer, using the same list as for any offer.
5. Keep relationships warm either way. Ask HR how notice and handover are handled and check official sources.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"I'd thank them, because it's good to feel valued. But I'd already thought hard about this before resigning. My reasons are about [real reasons, such as the type of work and learning], and those wouldn't change with a different package. So I'd politely decline, offer to hand over my work properly, and leave on good terms. I wouldn't make my decision around the counter-offer, because I've committed to this role and I'm excited about it."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "It depends what they offer. If they match it, I'll stay." This signals that the new role was only a bargaining chip, and it makes the interviewer wonder whether you will keep considering offers after you join.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you make decisions on reasons, not only on pay?
- Are you honest about your commitment?
- Do you handle exits professionally?
- Can you be trusted once you have said yes?

**Make it yours:**
- Write the real reasons you are leaving, in your own words.
- Decide before resigning whether anything could change your mind.
- Think about how you will thank your manager and offer a handover.
- Be honest with the new company; do not invent a position you do not hold.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
In the conversation: "Thank you, I appreciate that. I've thought carefully about this, and I've decided to move on because [your real reason]. I'd like to make the handover smooth and will [offer: document, handover meeting] before my last day."
If you are honestly unsure: "Thank you for the offer. I need a little time to consider it. I'll let you know by [date]."
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if they offered something you really wanted?" Be honest: it would have to address the reasons you are leaving, which usually sit beyond one change.
- "Have you ever stayed after a counter-offer?" Answer truthfully; if you have not, say so.

**Common mistakes:**
- Using the new offer only for leverage.
- Telling the new employer you will "see what happens".
- Burning bridges on the way out.
- Deciding before you know your own reasons.
