**Say this first (1 line):** I work best with some focused time at home and regular time with the team, so could we talk about how remote days would work?

**Structure:** Ask early enough to be useful, and show you have thought about the team.
1. Learn the default: what is the current policy, how is it applied, and does it differ by team?
2. State your preference in terms of the work (focus time for modelling, in-person time for planning).
3. Offer a specific arrangement, such as [number of remote days], with a way to adjust it after a trial period.
4. Show how you keep the team connected: overlap hours, written updates, demos.
5. Ask for the agreed arrangement to be written in the offer or an HR document, because policies can change.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"Could you tell me how the hybrid policy works for this team? For me, deep work like model development goes best with some quiet days at home, and I like being in the office for planning and reviews. I'd be comfortable with [your preferred pattern], and I'm happy to start with the team's default and review after a few months. In my current setup I keep people informed with short written updates and a weekly demo. If we can agree on something like this, could it be noted in the offer?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I will only work remotely, otherwise I cannot join." It makes your convenience the headline, ignores how the team works, and leaves no room to discuss. It may also surprise a recruiter late in the process.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Can you raise a need respectfully and with a reason?
- Do you think about team connection, not just yourself?
- Are you flexible and willing to trial?
- Do you check what is already policy before asking?

**Make it yours:**
- Decide what you truly need and what is merely nice to have.
- Describe a real stretch of remote or hybrid work and what made it work.
- Ask the recruiter how the policy is applied for this team; do not assume.
- Check official company pages for any stated policy, and treat them as current only as of the date you read them.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
"Could you share how remote and in-office days work for this team? I do my best focused work with [your preferred pattern], and I'd stay closely connected through [overlap hours, updates, demos]. Would you be open to [arrangement], reviewed after [period]? If so, could it be confirmed in writing?"
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if we need you in the office more often?" Say you will follow the team's needs and ask how much notice you would get.
- "Why does it matter?" Link it to the quality of your work, plainly and without exaggeration.

**Common mistakes:**
- Raising it only after accepting.
- Treating it as all-or-nothing.
- Ignoring travel or time-zone overlap needs of the team.
- Relying on a verbal "should be fine".
