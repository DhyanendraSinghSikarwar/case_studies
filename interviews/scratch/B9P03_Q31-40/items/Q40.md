**Say this first (1 line):** For me it is [value], and here is a real time it was hard and what I do differently now.

**Structure:** Use STAR with extra weight on the reflection.
- Situation: a real moment when the value was hard for you (for example candour, speed, ownership of unglamorous work, asking for help).
- Task: what the situation needed.
- Action: what you did, and honestly where you fell short at first.
- Result: what happened, and the habit you built since.
- Reflection: how you check yourself now, and where you still work on it.

Name a real weakness against a real value; avoid a fake flaw like "I care too much". Check the company's current values list, because wording changes; Book 7 Part 3 (Atlassian) says the values interview is a formal stage there, with Thin evidence from guides and the company's own material, so check the current values page and treat guides as secondary.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"The one I find hardest is giving direct feedback early. On a past project I noticed a colleague's analysis had a gap, and I waited a week hoping it would sort itself out. It didn't, and we lost time. Since then I raise concerns quickly and privately, with specifics, like 'I'm worried about [issue]; can we look at it together?' I also ask my manager to point out when I hold back. It's still not natural, but I've gotten faster, and I now see it as being kind to the team rather than critical."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I don't really struggle with any of them; I'm a perfectionist." It avoids the question, sounds rehearsed, and shows no self-awareness or growth.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Self-awareness: is the weakness real?
- Evidence of action, not just intention.
- Honest tone, without excuses or self-attack.
- A repeatable habit you built.
- Fit: do you value learning and growth?

**Make it yours:**
- Pick the value that is truly hardest for you, not the safest one.
- Recall one real moment, with specifics you remember.
- Write what you changed and how you know it worked.
- Name what is still hard.
- Practise telling it without AI polish; interviewers probe for details.

**Follow-ups:**
- "What would your teammates say?" Share feedback you actually received.
- "What are you doing now to improve?" Name one current habit and how you track it.

**Common mistakes:**
- Choosing a fake weakness.
- Skipping the action and result.
- Over-apologising, or defending yourself.
- Memorising a polished story that cannot survive follow-ups.
