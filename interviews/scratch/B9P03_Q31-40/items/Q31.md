**Say this first (1 line):** I would like to make this work, so here is what would get me to a yes, and I will explain why.

**Structure:** Counter in five steps, calmly and in order.
1. Pause: thank them, say you want to review it, and ask when they need an answer. Do not react on the call.
2. Compare: line up the offer against your own list (pay, role, learning, location, start date) and your researched [your target range] from sources you trust.
3. Pick one to three asks and rank them. Decide your [your minimum] privately, before the call.
4. Ask with a reason: name what you value, the evidence behind the ask (scope, skills, market research) and what you are asking for.
5. Listen, then settle in writing, or take time to decide. Stay warm throughout; you are solving a problem together.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"Thank you, I'm excited about the role and the team. I've reviewed the offer against my research and my other priorities, and the package is a little below what I was hoping for. Based on the scope we discussed, owning [the area] end to end, and what I've seen for similar roles, I was hoping for something closer to [your target range]. If the pay has limited room, I'm also open to talking about [another term, such as review timing or learning support]. I'd be glad to accept if we can find a way to bridge that. Is there flexibility there?"

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "That's too low. I have other offers. Match [their range] or I walk." It is a demand with no reason, it may claim leverage you do not have, and it ends the conversation instead of opening one. Do not mention offers you do not hold; the honest version works better.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you respond to news you dislike without defensiveness?
- Is your ask tied to reasons, not just a want?
- Do you stay collaborative and keep the relationship warm?
- Are you clear on priorities and flexible on how they are met?
- Do you follow the process (write it down, respect deadlines)?

**Make it yours:**
- Write your own research sources and [your target range] in your notes before the call, not in the message.
- Decide your top three priorities and [your minimum] while calm.
- List one real reason that supports your ask (a project, a skill, the scope of this role).
- Practise saying the ask aloud, ending on a question.
- Do not use AI to invent evidence or a script you cannot defend live; recruiters follow up on every reason you give.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
On the call: "Thank you, I'm excited about this. Could I have until [date] to review the details?"
Follow-up message: "Hi [name], thanks again for the offer. I'm keen to join. Based on [real reason: scope, skills, research], I was hoping for [your target range]. I'm also open to discussing [another term]. Could we find a way to bridge that? Happy to talk by phone."
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What is your minimum?" Say what matters to you and that you would like to hear what is possible; share [your minimum] only if you decide to.
- "If we cannot change the pay, would you still join?" Answer honestly: name what else would make it work, or say you need time.

**Common mistakes:**
- Reacting emotionally on the call.
- Asking for many things at once with no ranking.
- Bluffing about other offers or deadlines.
- Counting only pay and ignoring the rest of the package.
- Replying by text in the heat of the moment.
