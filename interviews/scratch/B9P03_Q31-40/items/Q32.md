**Say this first (1 line):** Before I accept, I want to be sure I understand the role and what success looks like, so may I ask a few questions about scope?

**Structure:** Treat it as shared clarification, not a demand.
1. Ask first: what are the main problems, what is in and out of scope, who are the partners and who owns the decisions.
2. Match to your goals: say which parts excite you (for example modelling, deployment, experiments, GenAI evaluation).
3. Propose one concrete adjustment, framed as how you add the most value.
4. Ask how scope is reviewed over time (for example at three months), and what success looks like.
5. Ask for key points in writing, such as the title and the main responsibilities in the offer.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"I'm really keen on this role. Before I decide, could I check how I'd split my time? From our talks I understood it was [how the role was described, for example mostly modelling with some analysis], and I'm most useful when I can also own [deployment or evaluation], because that's where I've seen results in my own work. Is there room for that, even starting small? And how does the team review scope after the first few months? If I can see that direction, I'm ready to move forward."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I will not do reporting work. I only want the modelling." A flat refusal sounds rigid and assumes you already know the role. It gives no reason and no path, and it makes a hiring manager worry about teamwork.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you ask thoughtful questions about the work, not only the perks?
- Can you link your strengths to team needs?
- Are you flexible about how scope grows?
- Do you ask for clarity politely and in writing?

**Make it yours:**
- Write the two tasks you most want and the one you would rather limit, and why, in terms of value to the team.
- Pick a real example where owning something end to end produced a result.
- Read the job post and note what is vague; those are your questions.
- Check the title and level wording in the offer against what was said on the calls.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
"Thank you for the offer. Before I confirm, could I clarify a few points about scope?
1. What are the first three problems I would work on?
2. How would my time split between [task A] and [task B]?
3. Would there be room for me to take on [area I want] after [period]?
Could the title and main responsibilities be reflected in the written offer? I'm keen to join once this is clear."
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if the role stays as described?" Say you can work within it and ask how growth is reviewed.
- "Why does scope matter to you?" Connect it to learning and impact, not status.

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating the role as fixed and negotiating only pay.
- Making demands before understanding the team's needs.
- Relying on verbal promises about scope later.
- Asking for a title without matching responsibilities.
