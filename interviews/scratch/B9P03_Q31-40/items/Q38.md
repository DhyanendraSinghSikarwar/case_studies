**Say this first (1 line):** I make my work visible on purpose, with short written updates, clear handoffs and regular check-ins.

**Structure:** Use STAR for one real remote or hybrid stretch, then add your habits.
- Situation: the team spread across places or time zones, and the project.
- Task: what you needed to deliver and keep others informed about.
- Action: concrete habits (written updates, shared docs, demos, agreed overlap hours, quick calls for blockers, camera on for key meetings).
- Result: what improved (fewer surprises, faster reviews, a delivered project), stated honestly.
- Reflection: one habit you would add.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own details.*
"On my last project the team was split between [places] with limited overlap. I owned the [model or pipeline]. To keep people informed I wrote a short update every [frequency] covering what I'd done, what was blocked and what I needed. I kept a shared design doc, agreed [overlap hours] for questions, and gave a short demo at each milestone. As a result, reviews moved faster and we had fewer surprises at handoff. I also started pairing with a colleague each week to stay connected on a personal level, which I'd keep doing."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I'm self-motivated, so I don't need much communication." It treats visibility as unnecessary and offers no evidence. Remote work depends on others being able to see your progress.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Do you communicate proactively?
- Do you have concrete habits, not slogans?
- Do you handle time zones and handoffs well?
- Do you build relationships, not just output?

**Make it yours:**
- Pick a real period when you worked with people at a distance.
- List the exact habits you used, and which worked.
- Recall one moment when poor communication caused a problem, and what you changed.
- If you have not worked remotely, use a comparable situation (a cross-team project) and say so.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
Situation: "On [project], our team was spread across [locations or time zones]."
Action: "I [update habit], kept [shared document], and agreed [overlap or check-in] with [people]."
Result: "As a result, [honest outcome]. I'd add [improvement] next time."
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "How do you handle a blocker when nobody is online?" Write down the context clearly, propose a default, and flag who can decide.
- "How do you build trust remotely?" Do what you say you will, and share progress early.

**Common mistakes:**
- Saying you are "great at communication" with no examples.
- Ignoring time zones and handoffs.
- Treating visibility as self-promotion.
- Describing remote work only as a perk.
