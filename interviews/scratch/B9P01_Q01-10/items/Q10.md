**Say this first (1 line):** When a problem was vague, I turned it into a question and a decision, agreed a small first slice with the people asking, and ordered the work by value and by what would teach us the most.

**Structure:** STAR; Action should show your sequence of steps. For turning a vague stakeholder request into something buildable with numbers, see Book 6 Part 6 (for example its question about a director who wants an AI that tells the team which customers are unhappy); this item is the general story version (framework in the STAR and CARL section above).
- Situation and Task: the vague or changing ask, who gave it, the deadline.
- Action: clarifying questions; defining the decision, the metric and the first deliverable; ranking options by value, effort and risk; agreeing what you would not do yet.
- Result: what you delivered first, and how the problem was clarified as you went.
- Learning: how you handle vague asks now.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own example.*
"My manager asked me to 'understand why customers are leaving', with no more detail and changing priorities. I started by asking what decision this would feed, and who would act on it. That narrowed it to the retention team's next campaign. I listed three possible first analyses, ranked them by how much they'd change that decision and how long each took, and picked the one that took four days. I wrote a half-page plan with what I would not do yet, and my manager agreed. The first analysis showed [a clear finding], which sharpened the next questions, so the vague brief became two defined pieces of work. I now start any vague task with a half-page plan."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "I just started with the data, explored everything I could find, and eventually found some insights." That is unprioritised exploration with no agreement on what success is. Interviewers hear a risk of wasted weeks, with no decision at the end.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Comfort with ambiguity: you did not freeze or flail.
- Asking the right clarifying questions (decision, audience, deadline).
- A prioritisation logic you can explain.
- Alignment with stakeholders on scope before building.
- Small first delivery that reduces uncertainty.

**Make it yours:**
- Choose a case where the request really was unclear or changed mid-way.
- Write the first three questions you asked, in your own words.
- List the options you considered and why you picked one.
- Note what you postponed and who agreed.
- Be honest if part of it went in the wrong direction; say what you learned.

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if the priorities changed again?" Revisit the plan in writing, state the cost of the change, and agree what moves.
- "How did you decide what to do first?" Value to the decision, effort, and how much it reduces uncertainty.
- "What if stakeholders disagreed on the goal?" Escalate to the decision owner with options and trade-offs, not a vote.

**Common mistakes:**
- Starting to model before asking what decision it serves.
- Trying to do everything.
- No written agreement on scope.
- Complaining that the request was bad rather than showing what you did.
- A story with no outcome.
