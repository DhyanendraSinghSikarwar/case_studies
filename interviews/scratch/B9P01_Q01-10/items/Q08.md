**Say this first (1 line):** I will describe a disagreement with a teammate where we both cared about the outcome, how I tried to understand their view first, and what we agreed.

**Structure:** STAR, with the weight on how you handled the person, not on who was right (see the STAR and CARL section above).
- Situation and Task: who, what the disagreement was about (a work issue, not personality), and what was at stake.
- Action: what you did to understand their view, what you proposed, how you moved from opinion to evidence or a decision rule, and whether you escalated.
- Result: what was decided, how the relationship is now, and what the project got.
- Learning: what you do earlier now.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; replace with your own real example.*
"A teammate and I disagreed on [a technical choice, such as how to split the data for evaluation]. I thought [my view]; she thought [hers], and the discussion was getting repetitive. I asked her to explain what she was worried about, and it turned out she'd seen a leak in an earlier project that I hadn't known about. That changed my view of the risk. We agreed on a quick test, ran both splits on a sample, and looked at the difference together. The results favoured a mix of both, so we used her split for evaluation and mine for faster iteration. We shipped on time, and we now write down the decision rule for splits at the start of a project. We still work together well."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "A teammate was lazy and kept missing deadlines, so I told the manager and he handled it." Or: "I convinced them I was right." The first blames and skips your own part; the second shows no listening. Neither shows how you resolve conflict.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Maturity: you disagree on the issue, not the person.
- Listening: you tried to understand before persuading.
- Use of evidence or a neutral decision rule.
- Knowing when to escalate and doing so respectfully.
- The relationship afterwards.

**Make it yours:**
- Choose a conflict that had real substance, with a result you can describe, not a trivial one.
- Write their position fairly, in the way they would put it.
- List the exact steps you took, in order.
- Include something you got wrong or learned from their side.
- Avoid stories that make a named colleague look bad; talk about the situation.

**Follow-ups:**
- "What if they had not agreed?" Say what the decision path was (manager, a test, a documented decision) and that you would commit to it.
- "Did you escalate?" Only after trying directly; say how you framed it neutrally.
- "Would you do anything differently?" Name one earlier step.

**Common mistakes:**
- Painting the other person as the problem.
- A story where you simply "won".
- No concrete actions, only feelings.
- Choosing a conflict with no real stakes.
- Skipping the outcome and the relationship afterwards.
