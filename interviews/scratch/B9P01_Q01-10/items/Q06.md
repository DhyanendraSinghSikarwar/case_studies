**Say this first (1 line):** I would ask about how the team measures success, what the first months look like, and what makes people struggle in this role, because those help me decide and show how I think.

**Structure:** Prepare five, ask two or three, and pick by who you are talking to. Not a STAR answer, so use categories (framework note: STAR and CARL above do not apply here).
- Work: what problems, what data, how decisions get made.
- Success: how this role is judged at 6 and 12 months.
- Team: how the team works with engineering and product, how feedback happens.
- Honest risk: what is hardest about the job.
- Process: what happens next and when.

**Strong sample answer (spoken):**
*Illustrative; adapt to the interviewer and the role.*
"Three questions, if that's okay. First: how would you know, in six months, that someone in this role has done well? I'd like to understand what good looks like here. Second: what does the path from an idea to something live look like for this team, and where does it usually get stuck? Third, and I ask this of everyone: what is the hardest part of this role that the posting doesn't say? I'm asking because I'm choosing as well as being chosen, and I'd rather know the real picture now than discover it later."

**Weak version (and why it fails):** "No, I think you covered everything." Or the opposite: "What is the pay? How many holidays? When do I get promoted?" The first signals low interest; the second leads with things better handled with the recruiter later and shows no interest in the work.

**What the interviewer scores:**
- Curiosity about the work and the team.
- Whether you prepared (questions that use something specific to the role).
- Judgement about what to ask whom (manager, peer, recruiter).
- Seriousness: you are evaluating the fit too.
- Respect for time: concise questions.

**Make it yours:**
- Write five questions after reading the posting and the team's public output.
- Tailor each to the interviewer: a peer for day-to-day, a manager for success and growth, a recruiter for process and timing.
- Cross out anything answered by the posting or website.
- Add one question that follows up on something said earlier in this interview; that shows you listened.
- Keep pay, notice and visa-type process questions for the recruiter or HR, and check official sources for rules; this book gives no legal advice.

**Script (copy and adapt):**
```
1. "What would success look like in this role after six months?"
2. "How does a model or analysis go from idea to being used by the business here, and where does it usually get stuck?"
3. "What is the hardest part of this job that people tend not to expect?"
4. "How does the team handle disagreement about what to work on?"
5. "What are the next steps in the process, and is there anything about my background you'd like me to clarify?"
```

**Follow-ups:**
- "Any concerns about your fit?" is not a question to ask nervously, but you can ask it: it invites objections you can answer.
- If the interviewer answers a question you prepared: ask a deeper follow-up instead of repeating.
- If time is short: ask one, the success-in-six-months question.

**Common mistakes:**
- Having no questions.
- Asking about pay or leave first.
- Asking something the website answers.
- Asking everyone the same question and ignoring their role.
- Treating it as a test of flattery instead of real curiosity.
