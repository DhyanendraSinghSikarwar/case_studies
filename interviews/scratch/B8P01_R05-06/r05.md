### R5 United Kingdom (Europe)

**Evidence level:** Medium-Thin. There is no official or UK-wide survey of data and ML interview stages in the results. The picture is built from a handful of Glassdoor and similar candidate reports for named UK employers (each one anecdotal), one general role guide, and UK-specific surveys on CV norms and AI in hiring. Stage counts differ a lot between reports [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5].

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types that appear in candidate reports: fintech and fraud analytics (Featurespace), a telecom (BT Group), pharma (GSK), AI hardware and autonomous driving (Graphcore in Bristol, Wayve), a bank-technology firm (Thought Machine), and healthcare data (Flatiron Health London) [S1][S3][S4][S10][S8][S9][S6].
- Big-tech and research-lab London roles show up as ML engineer and research or applied tracks, for example Google DeepMind [S19][S7].
- UK public-sector data science roles exist and are described on the official National Careers Service job profile, which lists alternative titles such as machine learning engineer and AI data scientist [S21].
- A job-board snapshot on 14 October 2026 counted open Data and AI listings on one site; it is a single-site snapshot, not a market survey [S22].
- City-level hub data (London versus other cities) was not found in the results [U].
- Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. Recruiter or HR screen first, covering background, motivation and timeline. This comes from a general role guide, not a UK-specific one [S5][G].
2. A take-home or case study is common in UK candidate reports: Featurespace (after a call with the hiring manager), Gener8 UK (a long project with analysis and model building), BT Group (take-home then a full technical interview) [S1][S2][S3]. These are single anecdotes each.
3. Online tests also appear: GSK reports mention a HireVue video step and an online test with behavioural and some SQL questions, then a final interview with the line manager; a longer GSK loop mentions a take-home case study and a panel presentation on it (few reports) [S4]. Flatiron Health London: a HackerRank test, a technical interview, then four interviews in one day [S6].
4. Startup processes can be short: Wayve reports (three reviews) list a phone interview, a skills test and a one-to-one interview [S8]. A general ML-interview text says big companies have standardised processes while startups make up the process as they go [S7].
5. Timeline: one Graphcore graduate ML report (January 2026) says the process took four weeks, anecdotal [S10]. No UK-wide timeline was found [U].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- UK careers guides describe competency-based interviews with STAR-style answers; large employers' interviewers score against them and probe with follow-ups on what you personally did and the outcome [S17][S18].
- For senior candidates, one guide suggests giving the action step more weight (decisions, alternatives, stakeholders) [S17].
- Claims about British understatement or modesty were not found in the results, so none are made here [U].

**CV and application format:**
- Length: guides put a UK CV at two A4 pages [S11][S12].
- Photo: guides say leave it off [S11][S12].
- References: sources differ; leaving them off or a short "available on request" line are both described as acceptable [S11][S12].
- Cover letter: one university guide says one page unless the application says otherwise [S13].
- Data roles: linking GitHub or a portfolio in the CV header is a common suggestion, not a verified norm [S12][G].

**Language and location notes:**
- English is the working language of the interviews seen in these reports; no local-language expectation appeared [U].
- No source compared locations within the UK, so check each posting for on-site, hybrid or remote terms [U].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official source to check: the UK government (GOV.UK) pages for the Skilled Worker route, for example the "Your job" page [S20].
- Verify there: which route fits your case, what the employer must hold or do, what the job itself must meet, the current rules and timelines. Rules change often, so read the live pages; this book states no rule [U].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect a CV of about two pages without a photo, and a tailored cover letter in many applications; check the posting [S11][S12][S13].
- Expect competency-style behavioural rounds written up against structured criteria at large employers, so prepare personal examples with outcomes [S17][S18].
- Expect a take-home or case study before or between interviews at some employers, which is uncommon as a fixed step in many Indian campus-style loops [S1][S2][S3][G].
- Expect online assessment steps (HackerRank, HireVue-style) in some processes [S4][S6][S10].
- Offer and notice timelines in the UK were not found in the results; do not assume either way [U].

**How to use Books 1-7 for this market:**
- Book 3 Part 12 (company-style mixed mock sets) and Book 3 Part 4 (window functions: ranking, top-N per group) for SQL screens that GSK-style online tests mention [S4].
- Book 1 Part 12 (NumPy/pandas/sklearn live-coding & AI-code-review rounds) for live coding with ML questions [S10][S9].
- Book 1 Part 11 (ML system design) and Book 4 Part 12 (cost, scaling, security, MLOps system design) for ML engineer and research-engineer deep dives [S19][G].
- Book 6 Part 5 (business case studies) and Book 6 Part 10 ("design a solution end-to-end" open scenarios) for take-home and case-study rounds [S2][S4].
- Book 6 Part 6 (stakeholder conflicts & trade-off decisions) and Book 9 Part 1 (STAR/CARL framework) for competency interviews [S17][S18].
- Book 2 Part 12 (GenAI system design + coding) for GenAI roles [G].
- Book 7 Part 4 (Europe/global company processes) for company-level detail, not repeated here [G].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-3: build six or so personal stories with situation, action, result, for competency rounds [S17][S18].
- Days 4-6: do two timed case-study or take-home mocks and write a short summary a manager could read [S1][S2][S9].
- Days 7-9: SQL, statistics and basic ML refresher for online tests [S4][S6].
- Days 10-11: live coding practice in a plain editor with ML questions [S10][S9].
- Day 12: trim the CV to two pages, drop the photo, and check the cover-letter ask [S11][S12][S13].
- Days 13-14: mock panel, presenting the case study in about ten minutes [S4].
- Throughout: read the target employer's recent reviews, since stage counts vary by company [S5].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one Glassdoor report as the standard process [G].
- Sending a long or photo-led CV [S11][S12].
- Giving "we" answers in competency rounds with no personal action or outcome [S17][S18].
- Over-engineering a take-home when the reviewer called the task long; stay within the stated time and explain trade-offs [S2][S9][G].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- Survey reports (recent, vendor or media sources): 47% of UK candidates surveyed use AI for applications and 30% reportedly walked away from a process that included an AI interview [S14][S15].
- A recruiter survey reports that 55% of UK employers had not adopted AI in hiring, and that UK HR decision-makers view candidates' AI use more negatively than the global share [S16].
- Opinion pieces argue live coding is replacing take-homes; this is not UK data [S23].
- No UK source on rule changes for AI use inside technical rounds was found [U]; ask the recruiter what tools are allowed.
