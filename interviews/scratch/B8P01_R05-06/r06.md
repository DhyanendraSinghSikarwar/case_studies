### R6 Switzerland (Europe)

**Evidence level:** Thin. No Swiss data-science or ML interview process page appeared. Process detail rests on a few old or small candidate reports (several for software or ML-team engineering roles, not data science), prep-site guides that are not Switzerland-specific, and expat or recruiter guides for CV, language and culture. Those guides are consistent on format but are commercial and partly disagree [S1][S2][S3][S6][S18][S19].

**Market snapshot for data and ML roles (as reported):**
- Employer types named in listings or reports: global tech (Google Zurich), banks and asset managers (UBS, Julius Baer, Zurcher Kantonalbank, Pictet), insurers and reinsurers (Zurich Insurance, Swiss Re), pharma and life sciences (Roche, Debiopharm, dsm-firmenich), trading and finance firms in Zug, and an ML firm in Lausanne [S1][S3][S4][S11][S12][S20].
- Hubs: a datacareer.ch post lists Zurich, Basel and Geneva as big centres and Bern and Lausanne as smaller, but it uses June 2017 postings and is dated; a later snapshot shows postings mostly in Zurich, then Geneva and Lausanne [S10][S11].
- ETH Zurich and EPFL as talent pipelines were not found in the results [U].
- Pay is out of scope in this book.

**Hiring process norms (as reported):**
1. A general 2026 guide says Swiss data scientist processes usually run three to four rounds; it is not a first-hand account [S6].
2. Another guide says two to three rounds for mid-level roles and three to five for senior ones, often with a case study or technical test; international organisations in Geneva may use more [S14].
3. Google Zurich/Munich reports (2020 and later, software-engineering and ML-team roles): recruiter-led phone screen, then a virtual onsite with graph coding, an ML round on deploying models and writing a train-test split, a design round, and a Googleyness round. Each is one anecdote [S1][S2].
4. Data scientist reports for Zurich employers: one Swiss Re report mentions statistics, coding and a question on calibrating ML models; one Zalando Zurich report (2024) describes a one-hour interview with a data scientist and a senior data scientist; each is one anecdote [S4][S5].
5. Timeline: Glassdoor data on Zurich Insurance data scientist gives an average of 56 days to hire from a small sample [S3]. No Swiss-wide timeline was found [U].
6. Guides for banks, reinsurers and pharma describe a take-home case study, cognitive or personality tests, or presenting a past project; these are prep-site descriptions and not Switzerland-specific [S18][S19][S20].

**Culture and communication style in interviews:**
- Guides describe Swiss interviews as punctual and formal, with arriving a few minutes early expected; one says business-formal dress remains standard in finance hubs [S14].
- Values commonly cited in the guides are precision and humility; these are generalisations from a recruiter guide, so treat them as a prompt to prepare rather than a rule [S14][G].
- A thank-you email within a day is described as well received [S14].

**CV and application format:**
- Length: most guides say about two pages [S7][S9].
- Photo: sources disagree. Some treat a professional photo as expected for German- or French-speaking employers, others as common but not mandatory; check the employer and the language region [S7][S8][S9].
- Cover letter: widely described as expected, one page, even if the advert does not ask [S7][S9].
- References and certificates: sources diverge on whether to attach employer certificates (Arbeitszeugnisse), diplomas and references, or offer them on request [S7][S8][S9].
- Dates: one guide says the numeric style (04.2022) is the local convention [S9].
- Write the application in the language of the job advert [S7][S9].

**Language and location notes:**
- Large international employers are described as working in English with German not a hiring filter for engineering roles; at smaller Swiss firms and client-facing roles German can be the working language, and mid-senior roles may ask for it. This comes from blogs and recruiter guides, not data-science data [S13][S6].
- German matters more in Zurich than in Geneva according to one guide, which also says English-only professionals can build careers in Geneva's international circle [S13].
- Candidate reports and the datacareer.ch snapshot place roles mainly in Zurich, Geneva and Lausanne, with Basel, Bern and Zug also appearing [S11][S12].

**Work authorisation: where to check:**
- Official source to check: the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM), which says it determines the conditions under which a person may enter Switzerland to live and work; it also has a work-topic page of links [S16][S17].
- Verify there: which route applies to your case, who applies (you or the employer), what the canton and federal offices each handle, what the employer must show, and current timelines. A vendor blog says the employer usually applies via the cantonal office; confirm that on the official pages. This book states no rule [U].
- Job-Room (job-room.ch) is named in cantonal pages as the federal online platform of the unemployment-insurance service; whether it suits your situation is for you to check there [S24][U].

**Process differences for candidates coming from India's process:**
- Expect a cover letter, a roughly two-page CV, a photo question to settle by employer, and supporting documents such as certificates in many applications; the paperwork is heavier than a typical one-page Indian resume plus form [S7][S8][S9][G].
- Expect a language-region question: the advert's language sets the application language [S7][S9].
- Expect two to five rounds depending on seniority, with a written case or technical test in some processes [S14][S6][S19].
- Expect presenting a past project or a case study in some processes [S20].
- Expect a formal, punctual setting [S14].
- Notice-period and offer timing norms were not found in the results [U].

**Preparation checklist (2 weeks):**
- Days 1-2: choose a language region and employer type, then check each advert for its language and its German or French ask [S7][S13].
- Days 3-4: build a two-page CV and a one-page cover letter; gather certificates and diplomas to hand [S7][S8][S9].
- Days 5-6: graph and algorithm practice [S1][S2].
- Days 7-8: statistics and calibration refresh [S4].
- Days 9-10: ML deployment and train-test split coding from scratch [S1].
- Days 11-12: one timed case study and a ten-minute project presentation [S18][S20].
- Days 13-14: formal mock interviews, with punctuality and a follow-up note planned [S14].

**Common mistakes:**
- Treating one 2020 report from Google as the Swiss norm [G].
- Applying in the wrong language for the advert [S7][S9].
- Leaving out the cover letter because the advert does not request one [S7].
- Assuming the photo rule without checking the employer, since sources disagree [S7][S8][S9].
- Arriving late or unprepared for a formal setting [S14].

**Recent changes and AI-era notes:**
- Michael Page Switzerland (Talent Trends 2026, 1,220 professionals) reports candidates adopting AI faster than employers; one summary quotes 86% of job seekers using AI in their search against 69% of employers using it in recruitment [S21].
- PwC's 2026 AI Jobs Barometer Swiss cut says AI skills are increasingly a hiring criterion in Switzerland [S22].
- Older: AlgorithmWatch research (2021-2022) found most large Swiss employers were not using AI recruitment tools, with Roche and Novartis the exceptions [S23].
- No Swiss source on AI inside technical interviews was found [U].
