# BOOK9_EXECUTION_PLAN.md — How Book 9 (Soft Skills, Scenarios and Skill-Improvement Playbooks, 5 parts) will be built

Follows plan.md §2 (Book 9), §4, §5 (Book 9 table), §11 and style_guide.md. Books 1-8 are complete. Output files: `Book9_SoftSkills/Book9_SoftSkills_PartNN.md`. Audience: data science, ML engineer and GenAI candidates preparing the behavioural, communication, leadership, negotiation-process and HR rounds, plus anyone building these skills over weeks.

## What is different about Book 9
Books 1-6 have runnable code. Books 7-8 are evidence-tagged profiles. Book 9 is written coaching: spoken sample answers, weak versus strong contrasts, scripts and practice plans. The risks are generic filler, invented company claims, stereotyping, legal or pay advice, and sample answers that candidates memorise word for word. So the book is built around structure (frameworks, what interviewers score), honest placeholders (the candidate's own story, never a fake achievement), and strict limits on claims.

Scope against earlier books: Book 6 Part 6 already covers data-science trade-off and stakeholder-conflict scenarios with numbers; Book 9 covers the general behavioural, communication, leadership, HR and workplace scenarios and the skill playbooks, and cross-references Book 6 Part 6 instead of repeating it. Book 7 profiles carry company-specific evidence; Book 9 may point to them by company name and Part but must not repeat their claims without their tags.

Environment limits: WebFetch fails here (ENOTFOUND), so research is WebSearch snippets only; label sources "snippet only". Most Book 9 content is editor guidance and says so; only factual claims (about a company's stated principles, a published framework, a survey result) need an evidence tag.

## Hard rules (the checker enforces what it can)
1. NO pay figures and no negotiation numbers anywhere. Pay and negotiation scripts use placeholders such as `[your target range]` and `[their number]`; never write an amount, a percentage raise, a "hike", or a currency symbol with a digit. The checker flags LPA, lakh, crore, CTC, salary, compensation, base pay, per annum, currency symbols and codes with digits, and digits near raise, hike, increase, bump or counter-offer. Use the word "pay" or "package", not the flagged words.
2. NO legal or regulatory claims stated as fact: nothing about what an employer may or may not ask, what is legally required, notice-period law, non-compete or contract enforceability, discrimination law, work authorisation, tax. When a point is legal-adjacent, say "ask the recruiter or HR how this is handled and check official sources; this book gives no legal advice". The checker flags the words illegal, unlawful, legally, by law, law requires and "right to" for manual review.
3. Sample stories are TEMPLATES with placeholders or clearly illustrative invented details ("illustrative; replace with your own"); never present an invented story as a real person's, never name a real company's internal event as a candidate's experience, and never imply that a fabricated achievement is acceptable: every answer has a "Make it yours" step that tells the candidate to use their own real example. No instructions to deceive an interviewer.
4. Company-specific claims (Amazon Leadership Principles, Google's behavioural round, what a company scores) must come from a Book 7 profile (cite company and Part, keep its caveat) or carry `[S#]` tags with a sources list; otherwise state them as general patterns `[G]` or omit. Leadership-principle names from memory are labelled editor knowledge and "check the company's current list" (Book 7 Part 1 notes that older guides cite 14 principles and Amazon's own guide mentions 16).
5. No stereotypes or claims about what people of a nationality, gender, age or background are like; no advice that depends on such claims. Cultural guidance is process-level only (formats, conventions, paperwork), as in Book 8.
6. Spoken-interview tone: plain language, short sentences, bullets and tables; sample answers are 60-200 words and read as speech. Scripts to copy (emails, messages, call openings) go in code blocks. No filler.
7. AI-era: include a short note where it matters (live follow-up depth when answers sound rehearsed or AI-written; do not use AI to write your story; how to disclose AI use if asked). Label policy claims by source quality.
8. Commit and push WIP often (stop hook); plain push, never force; never create PRs; if git add crashes remove a stale `.git/index.lock`; resume spend-limited agents with SendMessage.

## Part formats
**Parts 1-3 (question banks, `### Qn ⭐ 🟡 text` with `**Asked at:**` and `<!-- ANSWER_PENDING -->` while pending).** Answer labels in order: `**Say this first (1 line):**` / `**Structure:**` (the framework steps, e.g. STAR or CARL, with what goes in each) / `**Strong sample answer (spoken):**` (60-200 words, illustrative, placeholders allowed) / `**Weak version (and why it fails):**` / `**What the interviewer scores:**` (3-5 bullets) / `**Make it yours:**` (3-5 prompts for the candidate's own real example) / optionally `**Script (copy and adapt):**` (code block; negotiation and HR questions use it) / `**Follow-ups:**` (1-3 with short answers) / `**Common mistakes:**`.
**Part 4 (scenarios, `### Sn ⭐ 🟡 Category: situation`).** Labels in order: `**Situation:**` / `**Weak response:**` / `**Strong response:**` / `**Why it works:**` / `**What the interviewer scores:**` / `**Say it like this:**` (a short spoken script) / `**If they push back:**` / `**Common mistakes:**`.
**Part 5 (playbooks, `### Pn <Skill>`).** Labels in order: `**What good looks like:**` / `**Self-assessment:**` (a 1-5 rubric or 8-10 questions) / `**Daily and weekly practice:**` / `**30-day plan:**` (week by week) / `**Resources:**` (well-known books, frameworks and exercises by common name, labelled editor knowledge; URLs only if seen in a search result) / `**How to show it in interviews:**` / `**Common traps:**` / `**Measures of progress:**`.

## Pipeline
1. **Wave A (one research agent per part):** freeze the list in `Book9_SoftSkills/Book9_SoftSkills_PartNN.md` (questions with `<!-- ANSWER_PENDING -->`, or scenario/playbook skeletons), search public interview-question lists and guides for DS/ML/behavioural questions (snippet-only; mark found versus editor-designed), append `B9PNN-Qn` (or Sn/Pn) rows to `question_index.md`, write `research_notes/Book9_PartNN_sources.md`, `scratch/B9PNN_wavea/` scripts (qs.py, build.py).
2. **Wave B (4-6 answer agents per part, about 10-12 items each, own folders `scratch/B9PNN_xx-yy/answers.md`; progress to disk early):** the lead merges with `scratch/B9_merge.py` (by heading). Brief: `scratch/B9_brief.md`.
3. **Wave C (lead):** `python3 scratch/B9_check.py <q|s|p> <part file>` (labels in order, fences, pay and legal patterns, sample-answer length), spot-check three items per part against the hard rules, `review_notes/Book9_PartNN_review.md`, tracker and checklist updates, commit and push.
